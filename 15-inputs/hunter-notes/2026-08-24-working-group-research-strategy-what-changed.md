## 1. The HREC premise was wrong, and Eimear corrected it directly

MFC's prior working assumption, reflected in the pre-read and the HREC strategy record, was that MFC
might obtain ethics approval itself, whether free (Adelaide) or fee-for-service (South Western Sydney
LHD at $1,000, Bellberry at $4,125).

**Eimear, [23:23]:** *"They decide and then they apply to the ethics committee. Their own internal
ethics. You can't apply to the ethics committee unless you're in the university. This is one of the
biggest barriers to people doing research. You have to get a partner in the uni and then they go to
the ethics committee, which can take for ages."*

**What this changes.** The sequence is not "get ethics approval, then find a partner." It is **find
the researcher, and the researcher's institution runs the ethics application.** MFC is the partner
supplying the intervention and the participants, not the applicant.

**One honest tension, not resolved in the room.** MFC's own desk research (`00-hrec-and-data-strategy.md`,
31 universities checked 2026-08-12) did find non-university HRECs that explicitly accept external
organisations as applicants, notably South Western Sydney LHD and Bellberry. Eimear's statement is
about the university route, which is the route MFC actually wants. Both can be true: MFC could buy a
review from a non-university HREC, but that would not give it a university partner, publication
pathway, or academic credibility. **Do not treat this as a contradiction to be argued; treat the
paid non-university route as a different and weaker product than what the meeting was about.**

## 2. WHO-5 goes back in, and Eimear gave the reason MFC did not have

`D-0100` removed WHO-5 from the routine first-pilot core. The pre-read defended that removal on three
grounds, one of which was that wellbeing movement at three weeks would be uninterpretable.

**Eimear supplied the missing rationale, [26:45]:** *"you said, why would we bother doing the WHO-5?
Because how do you know if any of the changes are associated with anything? What you would do is you
would do the IMTAQ and the WHO-5, and then the stats program will compare against all these things
and go, oh, look, the people who enacted the skills more had a greater increase in their well-being
score. The people who did not enact the skills had no change."*

**Now formalised in `D-0109` (2026-08-25), which puts WHO-5 in the routine core for every participant
at baseline and the day-21/week-3 return, baseline only for the Introduction since it has no return.**

**Why this matters and why the pre-read got it wrong.** WHO-5 is not there to prove MFC caused
wellbeing change against an external benchmark. It is there as an **internal correlational
cross-check against skill enactment**: within the same sample, do the people who used the skills more
show different wellbeing movement from those who did not. That is a within-sample comparison, and it
does not require a control group. The pre-read treated WHO-5 as an outcome claim and moved it out on
timing grounds. That reasoning is superseded.

**Eimear, [27:25], on why a validated instrument is required for this to work at all:** *"Hence, you
wanted a validated research measure so it can do that in the stats program."*

**Debra, [27:52], on MFC-authored items:** *"We don't know the reliability. We don't know whether
it's measuring what it's supposed to measure."*

**Hunter accepted in the room, [28:32]:** *"I like the questions we're asking. WHO-5? I agree with
that."*

## 3. IMTAQ is the candidate instrument, not TYDQ

Hunter found IMTAQ the day before the meeting; it was not in the pre-read, which recommended TYDQ-15.

- **What it measures, Hunter [14:33]:** *"what they're testing for is, it's workshop intervention in
  the workplace... the effectiveness of that."* He judged it *"most applicable to what we're trying
  to do."*
- **When it would run, [16:05]:** *"it would be three months after, or three weeks after, three
  months, is when it would be implemented, because it's the effectiveness of the actual workshop."*
- **TYDQ, by contrast, [16:05]:** *"it's more about healthy mental health habits and behaviors. Had
  some sleep stuff and whatnot."* This matches the pre-read's own finding that TYDQ measures adjacent
  things.

**Superseded by KN-0006, which is more advanced than this section assumed.** The instrument is spelled
**IMTTAQ** in the controlling records, which is why an earlier search for "IMTAQ" wrongly reported it
absent from the vault. KN-0006 already specifies the intact six-item module (the three-item Training
Transfer subscale plus the three-item Opportunities to Integrate subscale), used alongside rather than
instead of MFC's own questions, at day-21/week-3 for Essentials and Foundations and repeated at three
months for case-study cohorts. It also records the limits: the items are workplace-worded and should
not be silently rewritten for family, school or community settings, and baseline or immediate-post
transfer scores are not meaningful because the items assume a post-training chance to use the
learning. **Read KN-0006, not this paragraph.**

## 4. Do not wait for the perfect instrument

**Debra, [21:06]:** *"in terms of finding the perfect measure, is that going to be the perfect measure
for what we're hoping for? May not be... It's not exact because the issue that we've all agreed is
that we're measuring something that hasn't been measured before. So we're trying to band-aid existing
measures that weren't really built to design this, but they might cover an aspect. So it's not 100%
yet, but it's still a starting point. So I personally wouldn't put any hold on anything. It's kind of
like starting, see what the data says, see where the mistakes are. Then when you do the next one,
build on that."*

This confirms the start-now sequencing, from a qualified voice, and removes the argument for delaying
delivery until the instrument is settled.

## 5. How to find a researcher, and what to say

**The search term is the key. Eimear, [25:04]:** *"you need to find researchers that have an area of
interest in... skill enactment. That's your key to open up."*

**Where to look:**
- University staff profiles, by school. **Eimear, [34:38]:** *"I would go to ACU School of Psychology
  and then go into the Staff Profiles."*
- **Debra, [32:16]:** *"they'll have research areas... they'll have all their citations, all their
  articles."* If interests are not listed, read the articles.
- **Or ask directly, Debra [33:45]:** *"if you just did a Google search or an AI search and said, who
  at ACU is leading compassion research?"*
- **The research coordinator route, Eimear [25:04]:** *"you find the research coordinator in the
  school of psychology where their job... is to oversee all of the research"* and who knows which
  supervisors have honours, masters and PhD students needing projects.
- **Debra, [38:08]:** *"all academics have a research component."* Not only designated researchers.

**What MFC actually offers, and it is not money. Debra, [40:14]:** *"You give them, you give them
participants. That's why the research is getting participants."*

**Eimear on the money reality, [29:17]:** universities like industry partners *"because industry
partners will give them money"* and co-fund grants. MFC cannot compete there, which is why alignment
of research interest plus participant access is the offer.

**Approach on their interest, not MFC's questions. Debra, [19:07]:** *"if they're reaching out to
organisations such as the Men's Sheds... and you approach them, not with the questions, but with the
collaboration question, with that other end goal in mind, wouldn't surprise me in the least if
they're far more likely to be interested."*

**Why a supervisor helps, Debra [26:00]:** *"they'll go in and bat for you. If they really like what
you're doing or they like the possible outcome of the question, they'll be interested... they can
publish it. It's kind of a win-win."*

## 6. A hard warning about AI-written outreach

**Eimear, [58:50]:** *"academics are very wary of AI... they are probably one of the best people to
see an email and go, that's AI, I'm not reading that. We just need to be really careful that it
doesn't read like something, like it's so disconnected. We want it to be really connected with you
and your passion and what you're about, rather than the AI version of you."*

**Binding instruction for MFC and for this system.** Researcher outreach emails must be written in
Hunter's own voice and must not read as AI-generated. Drafts prepared with AI assistance must be
rewritten by Hunter before sending, and both advisors offered to review before anything goes out
(Hunter, [30:57]: *"before I send anything out, I can see what your thoughts are"*).

## 7. Participant identification: the self-generated code is back

**Hunter, [28:14]:** *"I think we've got our own questions there, Eimear, with the identification
thing, what you said spot on. There's a way in which they can create their own code. It came up with
a system. And I'll send that back to you guys. That's the final approval on that aspect."*

`D-0100` preferred a **random system-generated evaluation ID** with a separately stored contact key,
and reasoned against participant-generated codes on the grounds that they can be forgotten, mistyped,
collide, and may embed personal information.

**Hunter's direction, 2026-08-25, resolves this.** It is not a choice between a random ID and a
self-generated code. It is a **structured self-generated code**: the participant builds their own
code from a fixed formula they can reproduce later, for example the first initial of a named thing
plus another fixed element, and the same prompt is shown on every form so they regenerate the same
code each time rather than having to remember an arbitrary string.

**Why this answers D-0100's objections.** Reproducibility is handled by asking the same prompt at
every timepoint, so the code is derived rather than recalled. Collisions are handled by choosing a
formula with enough combinations. The personal-information objection is handled by choosing prompt
elements that are not security-question material, which is the specific risk flagged in the earlier
privacy note against "mother's maiden name" style prompts.

**Still required before fielding:** the exact prompt wording and the collision rate, plus the
privacy read, since this changes the linkage description in the live forms. **This is now a
direction to implement, not an open question.**

## 9. Lead generation: the Mental Fitness Leadership Community

A new workstream, not previously in the measurement or committee records.

- **The shape, Hunter [45:15]:** a free fortnightly or monthly online session for managers and CEOs
  across Australia. *"When they come in, you get their form, you get their company, get who they work
  for, which creates a list."*
- **Debra named it, [52:34]:** *"something like a mental fitness leadership community."*
- **Debra's model, [46:26]:** NovaPsych host free sign-up webinars on services they already offer.
  Under an hour, pre-recorded, free, but requiring registration, which builds the email list.
- **Both formats work, Debra [48:56]:** *"You could start it as a live webinar... leave 10, 15
  minutes at the end, people are asking questions... And you just record it at the same time."*
- **Start small and free, Debra [50:56]:** *"if you target a couple of really small organisations
  that you think from a social media perspective are respected, then it may be worth to offer to do
  one or two free webinars."*
- **Also for individuals, not only managers, Hunter [53:17]:** free webinars for anyone.

**The strategic question Debra left open and nobody answered, [55:22]:** *"which is the strongest
starting point? Is the strongest starting point connecting managers?"* Versus reaching individuals
through the 60-minute Introduction. **This is an open decision, not a settled plan.**

**Eimear's caution against assuming who has budget, [57:11]:** *"you never know who's got the money.
Like some little charities will get a $5,000 grant... whereas you try to get $5,000 out of a
multi-billion dollar company, that's coming out of their money."*

## 10. The next review is a scoping review, and it was scoped in May

Hunter asked Debra about the rapid review, [41:36]. **Debra, [42:05]:** *"I have done a submission
because I thought there's no point doing any rewriting because I want it rewritten anyway... So just
waiting to hear back."* That is the existing rapid review, submitted for publication, awaiting a
response.

On a further review, Hunter [42:23] suggested *"probably not this year now, next year."* Debra noted
[43:25] there would be more papers now and *"the focal area's getting a lot more precise."*

**Important context neither raised in the room.** In the **Debra x Hunter meeting of 8 May 2026**
(`https://fathom.video/calls/664594773`), Debra proposed that the logical next step is a **scoping
review rather than another rapid review**, because it would map definitions, measurement approaches
and existing measures before the framework is tested. They refined the question together to:

> *"How can mental fitness be operationalised for real-world evaluation as a trainable capacity to
> think, feel, act, and connect? And what measures or indicators are appropriate for assessing first
> use, repeatability, application quality, and early capacity signals in adult populations?"*

**That question is precisely the problem MFC is now trying to solve with IMTAQ, WHO-5 and its own
items.** Debra committed in May to starting it immediately rather than waiting until June.
**Whether it progressed is unknown and unrecorded, and it is the single most useful thing to follow
up**, because a completed scoping review would answer the instrument question directly rather than
by trial.
