# K-08 — Claims and safety boundaries

## Core clinical lens

> "The framework should increase agency without increasing blame. It should offer skills without pretending that skills alone solve unsafe, clinical, structural, relational, or resource-based problems."

## Claim boundaries (Framework section 19)

| MFC can safely say | Use caution | Avoid saying |
|---|---|---|
| The framework is evidence-informed and evidence-aligned. | The framework may support mental health and functioning. | The framework treats, prevents, or cures mental illness. |
| Mental fitness can be strengthened through practice, support, repetition, and time. | Repeated practice may make useful patterns more familiar and easier to access. | Mental reps guarantee brain rewiring or guaranteed outcomes. |
| MFC teaches five public skills (Notice, Shift, Choose, Focus, Connect), each drawing on one or more of seven evidence-aligned deeper skills used directly by clinical advisors and the evidence page (`D-0081`, `D-0084`). | The skills connect to established evidence families. | MFC's exact model is clinically validated as a standalone protocol unless tested. |
| Mental fitness complements professional care. | Some people may need more support, adaptation, or clinical care. | Mental fitness replaces therapy, counselling, psychiatry, medication, or crisis support. |
| Access to skills is shaped by context, safety, support, body, brain, and conditions. | People may strengthen access where safe and appropriate. | Anyone can shift any moment if they try hard enough. |

## Clinical and inclusion safety rules (Framework section 20)

1. Use mental fitness as education and skill practice, not treatment.
2. Do not diagnose, pathologise, or interpret someone's mental health presentation through this framework.
3. Do not imply that struggle, distress, trauma responses, disability, neurodivergence, or difficulty accessing skills are personal failures.
4. Build in choice, opt-out pathways, pacing, and adaptation for workshops and public programmes.
5. Use "where safe and appropriate" for connection, vulnerability, exposure, reconnecting and difficult conversations. Name the actual action rather than relying on abstract shorthand.
6. Do not force calming, breathwork, eye closure, disclosure, touch, partner work, or public sharing.
7. Do not make the State Check mandatory, scored, public, or performance-based.
8. Do not centre emotional labelling or numbered scales.
9. Do not imply calm is always the goal. The goal is workable enough for this moment.
10. Use referral, safeguarding, and crisis pathways when distress, risk, or clinical needs are outside the scope of education.
11. Avoid discriminatory assumptions about culture, gender, sexuality, race, religion, disability, neurodivergence, socioeconomic position, age, mental health status, or family context.
12. Make delivery accessible: plain language, flexible participation, sensory considerations, inclusive examples, non-stigmatising wording.

### The evidence under rules 4, 6 and 10 (added 2026-09-24)

Rules 4, 6 and 10 above were written as caution. **They now have a published source behind
them, and it should be quoted rather than paraphrased**, because "we are being careful" is a
weaker position than "the authors of the review say this."

Boyd, Lanius and McKinnon (2018), reviewing mindfulness-based treatments for PTSD
(*Journal of Psychiatry and Neuroscience* 43(1):7-25), state that mindfulness-based approaches:

- **may increase distress or destabilise patients prone to flashbacks, rumination or easily
  triggered trauma memories**;
- **can be triggering for survivors of physical or sexual abuse**;
- may be difficult for people without emotion-regulation or distress-tolerance skills;
- **require a paced approach with fully informed consent.**

**What this changes in practice — nothing MFC does, and everything about why.** Contemplative
attention practices are already never forced (rule 6), calm is already never the goal (rule 9),
and opt-out and pacing are already required (rule 4). This section exists so that anyone asked
*why* can answer with the source instead of with an instinct.

**What it does NOT license.** MFC must not describe its own sessions as carrying this risk, and
must not describe them as free of it. Boyd is a narrative review of **treatment for a diagnosed
clinical population**, with no stated search method, no pooled estimate and no risk-of-bias
assessment, and MFC holds it as a documented exclusion for exactly that reason. It grounds a
precaution; it measures nothing about a non-clinical education session.

**A participant may be carrying a trauma history MFC does not know about and has no way to ask
about.** That is the real reason the rule is not "screen people out" but "never require the
practice, always offer the exit." It also converges with `AG-014` (Debra Fidler, 2026-07-20):
an anonymous pre-screening form cannot surface someone who is unwell without realising it.

**Owed to a qualified human.** This paragraph is drawn from a paper MFC screened out, not from
clinical advice. **Eimear Quigley should confirm the wording before it is relied on in any
participant-facing or buyer-facing document.** Until she does, it governs MFC's internal caution
only, and nothing here is clinical clearance.

## Category-confidence rule (added 2026-08-05, provisional, on Hunter product approval)

**Authority and status, stated precisely, same pattern as K-07's Radar and Stop-Shift-Do/Mental-Rep
additions.** Hunter approved this into the product on 2026-08-05, closing `IEQ-05`/domain
4/`R-01`/`R-03` (framework-integrity-observatory) under delegated product authority (`K-11`).
**Canonical Framework ratification remains with the committee**, as for this entire record.

> **MFC presents "mental fitness" as its own evidence-informed synthesis and communications
> category — never as an independently construct-validated scientific discovery.** No
> construct-validation literature currently distinguishes mental fitness from resilience training
> or mental-health-promotion/literacy work; that is a genuinely open external question, not settled
> either way.

| MFC can safely say | Avoid saying |
|---|---|
| MFC's approach draws together established psychological science — self-awareness, state regulation, flexible thinking, attention, values-based action, and relating to self and others — into a practical, teachable model. | Science shows mental fitness is a distinct, validated category. |
| "Mental fitness" is MFC's own name and architecture for this synthesis. | Mental fitness is a scientifically novel discovery. |
| The five-skill public architecture, and the seven deep skills beneath it, are built from well-established, individually distinct literatures. | This model has been independently validated as a whole. |

This does not change what MFC teaches — it governs how confidently MFC's own category name is
described in public, funder, and committee-facing copy. Whether "mental fitness" earns its own name
commercially or strategically remains a legitimate MFC choice, independent of and not conflated with
the unresolved science question above.

## "Life-changing" and founder conviction (added 2026-08-05, `D-0062`)

Hunter's own conviction that this work is "arguably life-changing" is genuine founder belief, not
public copy, and this record does not ask him to soften it in how he talks about MFC internally or
in conversation. The existing claim boundary already above ("Avoid saying": *"MFC's exact model is
clinically validated... unless tested"*; the four-position table's *"not a claim that one tool will
transform a person's life"*) governs what becomes **public-facing** copy specifically — "life-changing"
as a stated, evidenced outcome is not currently supported and should not appear as a claim in
website, funder, or workshop copy. Conviction and evidenced claim are different things, and keeping
them separate protects the conviction rather than undermining it — an overclaim that gets challenged
publicly costs credibility conviction alone doesn't.

## Additional prohibitions (Claims Register dashboard)

> "Do not claim treatment, prevention, diagnosis, symptom reduction, clinical validation, guaranteed outcomes, direct reductions in prevalence, suicide-risk reduction, or 'rewiring the brain'."

From the Commercial Model — MFC must not claim one engagement reduces absenteeism or attrition, prevents burnout, improves productivity, reduces workers' compensation, treats clinical distress, manages all psychosocial hazards, or transforms workplace culture. **Loosened by `D-0104`, 2026-08-19 (Hunter, founder decision, provisional pending committee ratification):** direct causal assertions of these outcomes remain barred exactly as above, but MFC may now use a **target-and-cite formula** — state what the programme targets, then cite what the general research shows, without asserting MFC's own programme produces the outcome (e.g. "this targets skills the research links to X, which the broader literature associates with Y" — not "this reduces Y"). Adopted directly from clinical director Eimear Quigley's own formula, MFC Committee Session 4, 2026-06-22 (Fathom 156941435, [50:36]). Full record, including the distinction between outcome and motivation Hunter drew (a possible outcome, never the stated reason MFC exists): `D-0104`.
