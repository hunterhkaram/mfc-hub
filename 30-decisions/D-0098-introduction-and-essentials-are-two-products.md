# D-0098 — Two short formats, two jobs

## The question

MFC has a **built and tested 60-minute delivery kit**. The offer ladder proposes a **90-minute
Essentials** as the paid entry product. Hunter asked whether Essentials should simply be the
60-minute kit that already exists, and delegated the call.

## Decided

**Both exist, and they are different products with different jobs. They are not two lengths of one
thing — and treating them as such is what caused the confusion.**

| | **Introduction — 60 min** | **Essentials — 90 min** |
|---|---|---|
| **Job** | Reach. People meet MFC and one person tries one thing | A paid product. Someone bought it and expects something delivered |
| **Where** | **Amended 2026-08-10 (D2):** adult community and workplace audiences, keynotes, conference and chamber slots. **Schools and youth are excluded until C-16 closes and a safeguarding lead is named** | On site, a single organisation's team |

## Why — structural, not preference

**The measurement does not fit in sixty minutes.** The forms draft protects **ten in-session minutes**
for Form 2. Put that into the 60-minute kit and you have **fifty minutes of teaching in a kit built
for sixty** — content cut to make room for data collection, in a format already tight.

At ninety minutes the same block is a far smaller proportion and **the built content survives intact.**

**But that only matters if the session is a paid product** — which exposes the real error: **a reach
format and a sellable format were being conflated.** Nobody paid for a keynote and nobody receives a
report from one, so it should not carry an instrument designed to produce one.

## The product argument, stated separately from the behaviour-change one

Hunter asked for the call on product grounds, not only outcomes.

- **An hour is what you give an audience. Ninety minutes is what someone buys.** Selling the
  give-away format creates exactly the ambiguity a founder hits walking into a local business — *is
  this a talk, or is this training?*
- **The extra thirty minutes is where the product stops being a demonstration.** It buys running all
  three steps on the participant's **own** chosen moment rather than generically, plus the five public
  skills. That is the difference between watching the procedure and owning a rep.
- **MFC has something sellable immediately.** The Introduction is built and tested, so it can be
  offered while the 90-minute build finishes. At zero paying customers, that matters.

## What this changes

1. The offer ladder gains an explicit line between the **free/reach hour** and the **paid ninety**.
2. **The measurement burden stops being forced into a format that cannot hold it** — the Introduction
   carries a light set only, consistent with the forms draft's own narrower bar for the short format.
3. Session-plan build work applies to **Essentials at 90 minutes**. The Introduction is not rebuilt.

## What this does NOT change

`D-0094`'s half-day Foundations is unaffected — its ~3 hours of content plus a break **is** the
existing 3-hour build repackaged, not a new design. `D-0016` still gates all participant-facing
delivery. `K-07` is untouched.

## Where this could be wrong, and how it gets tested

**If a small business owner says yes to an hour and balks at ninety minutes, the reach format has to
become the product and the measurement has to shrink to fit.**

**The test is free:** in the first three buyer conversations, **ask for ninety minutes and record
whether the pushback is about the length or the price.** If it is consistently the length, this
decision reverses and the instrument is redesigned for sixty.
