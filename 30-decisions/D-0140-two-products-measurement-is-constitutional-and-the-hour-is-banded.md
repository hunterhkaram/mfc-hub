# D-0140. Two products, and measurement stops being optional

## The decisions

**One. MFC goes to market with two products, not three.**

Jake, 17 September: *"we provide two services."* A half day for organisations, and a one-hour session
for talks, learning lunches and community groups. **They are for different rooms, not different
budgets.**

**Essentials is retired as a sold product.** Jake: *"just essentials isn't a thing now."* It remains
reachable as a configuration an organisation arrives at, and it is not advertised. **In six months it
may be a product again; it is not one now.**

**Two. MFC leads with the half day in every organisational conversation**, and handles the objection
rather than opening with a menu.

**Three. Measurement is not removable.** MFC delivers education and training **with measurement**, and
an engagement sold without it is not the thing MFC says it is. **The return session is required on the
half day.** The one exception is the Hour, which is a talk rather than an intervention, and whose
measurement may be cut on request.

**Four. Two things come off the half day, both with conditions.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The manager session** | Removable. **The managers then attend the half day itself**, which is a condition of removing it rather than a concession, and MFC says plainly that it is less likely to hold without their own session |
| **The report** | Removable. **2.5 hours come off, not 4**, because the cohort form review happens either way: that is where MFC's own evidence comes from |

**Reports are a half-day product only.** The Hour does not produce one.

**Five. The Hour is banded.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Up to 20** | **$600** |
| **21 to 50** | **$800** |
| **Over 50** | **$1,000** |

> **AMENDED, 2026-09-23, by Hunter Karam directly. The manager session is an hour, not 45 minutes**,
> and it is **never sold on its own**: it pairs with either product, and the managers attend that
> session too, as participants. Sold alone it would collapse into the Introduction, because the
> basics would have to be taught first. It is included in the half day, and adds **$300** to the
> Hour, so **$900** together.
>
> **The extra quarter hour moves the half day's costs**: delivery goes from 6.75 to 7.00 hours, the
> half day from 18.34 hours to **18.59**, its cost from $1,262 to **$1,279**, and the loaded floor
> from $1,767 to **$1,791**. Hunter's reason for the change: *"I'd rather have an extra 15 minutes."*
>
> **AMENDED, 2026-09-23. On delivery format.** The table above applies whether the Hour is
> delivered in person or online. **The Hour is sold online at the same $600.** Format is a delivery
> choice, not a product tier: same routine, same facilitator, same follow-up, and the cards are
> posted when MFC is not in the room.
>
> **An earlier form of this amendment priced online at $500.** That is withdrawn. A discount would
> tell an organisation in Perth that the only version MFC can bring it is the lesser one, and it
> would price on MFC's cost rather than on the session. **Where an organisation genuinely cannot
> meet the price, MFC discounts on request, not by default.**
>
> **What it costs.** Online takes **2.5 paid hours** against the in-person 4.93: booking, contract
> and invoicing at 0.75, preparation at 0.75, and the hour itself. With cards posted that is **$190**
> against **$351**, so at $600 it keeps **$410** rather than $249. The 2.43-hour difference is the
> travel hour, the 0.68 for setting up and the questions at each end, half an hour less quoting and a
> quarter less preparation.
>
> **The surplus is cross-subsidy, not markup**, and the record should be read that way: it funds
> in-person delivery to organisations where the trip costs more than the session earns.
>
> **What is not settled.** The 0.75-hour booking and preparation figures are founder estimates, not
> measured time. No online Hour has been delivered or quoted. **Fuel, parking and tolls are not
> costed anywhere in this record**, so in-person delivery costs more than the figures show. The
> bands above twenty were built on in-person cost and have not been reworked for online, where cost
> does not rise with headcount. **The rate is reviewed after the first ten delivered sessions, or by
> 30 June 2027, whichever is sooner.** Committee ratification remains owed, as for every price here.
>
> The current position is carried in
> `60-projects/committee-pack-oct-2026/THE-CONSOLIDATED-STRATEGY.md`, section 4 and Appendix C.

**$1,000 is a ceiling, not a band.** At a flat $600 the Hour fell below cost at a hundred people. The
ceiling holds above floor to about 312 people, sits under the nearest published comparator at $1,199,
and **keeps the Hour a door rather than a business**: at its best it returns about half a half day's
return per hour and an eighth of its margin per engagement.

**Six. Materials are costed from what a session needs**, replacing figures inherited from an older
document.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **The half day** | **$50 a session** | Material used in the room plus the cards. Participants take nothing away, so it barely moves with headcount |
| **The Hour** | **$25 a session**, bulk printed | An eight-sided sheet folded into a card. **This one does go home**, so it is the figure that moves if a room gets large |

**Founder estimate, not a quote.** It lowers the half day's loaded floor from **$1,907.18 to
$1,767.18**, a real $140 an engagement that was never MFC's to spend.

**Seven. Quarterly check-ins are offered, never sold, and MFC chooses who.** Out of contracted scope
and out of the floor, consistent with `D-0129`. **The criterion is whether the organisation is
actually doing it**, whether managers are behind it and the practice is visible. Where an
organisation wants it and is struggling, MFC returns anyway: that is the case-study relationship,
and it is where a discount or a mutual arrangement belongs.

**Eight. MFC is registering for GST.** Every published price is currently a final price because MFC is
not registered. **On the day it lands every figure is restated.**

## What the floor is now

> **(hours in that configuration × $66.10 + materials) × 1.4**

**Half day, everything: $1,767.18.** At ten people and $4,850 that is **2.74×** and MFC keeps
**$3,083**.

| Configuration | Floor | Price at ten | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everything | $1,767 | $4,850 | 2.74× |
| No manager session | $1,628 | $4,550 | 2.79× |
| No report | $1,536 | $4,150 | 2.70× |
| Neither | $1,397 | $3,850 | 2.76× |

**And a harder line underneath it.** Below pay plus materials with no overhead the facilitator is
working unpaid. **For a full half day that is $1,262**, and it is where a concession stops being a
concession.

## Why, in one line

**Three Essentials take 34.3 founder hours and three sales to close. One half day takes 18.3 hours and
one sale, for about the same margin.** Half the hours, a third of the selling, and nearly twice the
return per hour, because the cost of an engagement is the day rather than the depth.

**The margin comparison is close enough to be fragile and the case does not rest on it.** Essentials'
floor still carries an unchecked $60 materials figure; restated at $25 it would beat the half day on
margin alone. **The hours and the sales count hold at every assumption. The $55 gap does not.**

**The break-even follows:** moving from three products to two only has to convert **one** of three
Essentials buyers to leave MFC where it was.

## What this does not settle, and what it makes urgent

**The concession lane is now the only lever left.** Measurement cannot come off and the two removable
components total about $1,000. **When an organisation says the price does not work there is almost
nothing else to give.** The lane is recorded as being rebuilt after the previous version breached the
loaded floor.

**The cap of twenty blocks the Hour's bands.** Twenty is recorded as absolute on every product, set
with the committee and a clinical adviser. **Whether it applies to a talk is a clinical judgement, not
a pricing one**, and it goes to Eimear Quigley.

## One wording clash, recorded so it is not misread

`D-0132` says *"every cohort that buys a measured product receives a written report."* **That was
about not suppressing reports for small cohorts, not about a client declining one.** A client may
decline. **MFC may not withhold.**

## What is not claimed here

**None of this is tested.** MFC has delivered no cohorts, nobody has been quoted any of these prices,
and the conversion assumption underneath the two-product bet is a judgement rather than a finding.
**The first ten conversations are the test**, and what they should show is objections about time
rather than money.
