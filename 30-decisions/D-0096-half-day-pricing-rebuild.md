# D-0096 — Half-day pricing, rebuilt

## Why this exists

`04-product-pricing.md` now carries a supersession notice (added same day this record was written):
its entire cost model was built on a 3-hour session, and the flagship is now a half-day (`D-0094`).
This rebuilds it. **Structure is unchanged — base fee plus per-participant, capped at 20 — per
Hunter's explicit confirmation, 2026-08-09, that the existing base+per-person model stays, not a
flat fee.** The original document's own reasoning for that choice (a flat fee is structurally less
fair to smaller buyers than scaling with headcount) stands and isn't relitigated here.

## 1. The full line-item cost buildup

a headline number: *"Rob will most definitely want to see the behind and working out as to how we
landed there — how much it costs, time gone into it, after it (report we provide) etc."* This closes

## 2. Pricing — DECIDED, 2026-08-09 (raised same day, base fee only)

**Final: $2,500 base + $95/participant, capped at 20. Facilitator rate confirmed at $66.10/hour.**
This closes the pricing gap this record was written to fill — not a proposal any more.

**How it got here, in order, because the history is instructive, not just a rounding trail:**

1. **First pass, wrong rate.** Recalculated the cost basis at $52.88/hour → ~$800, proposed a
   $1,450 base. An independent review then caught that this "23%" proportionality claim was measuring
   the wrong thing (hours, not cost) and that the price undershot even the real cost increase at full
   capacity.
2. **Rate corrected.** Hunter confirmed $66.10/hour, not $52.88 — the true cost basis is **~$950**,
   not ~$800 (later revised again to ~$1,228 once the report/analysis work was properly costed, §1).
   Against that real number, $1,450 was genuinely too low, exactly as Hunter judged before the
   arithmetic caught up to him.
3. **Base fee decided at $2,000 directly by Hunter**, as a hard floor: *"I don't think people will
   expect low... e.g. half a day workshop and we get less than $2,000 is below the entire range."*
4. **Raised to $2,500, same day, after the full cost buildup landed.** Hunter, seeing that the base
   now properly includes data tracking, the Form 1/2/3 review, and the findings report — work MHFA's
   own product doesn't include at all — directly: *"we aren't charging because we believe it's
   important — there is just a lot of service and value we're providing."* This is the same kind of
   decision as step 3 — a founder pricing-strategy call about what the bundled service is worth, not
   a further cost-plus derivation. It moves the per-person price at full capacity from $195 to $220 —
   **exactly MHFA's own floor**, not below it, which is a meaningfully different market position than
   the $2,000 figure held, addressed directly in the market-comparison note below.

**Corrected 2026-08-09, after Hunter asked the duration question directly and the answer was run
properly through research and a second check.** Researched and checked: there is no general
professional-services rule that a shorter engagement commands a higher hourly rate than a longer
one — the like-for-like comparator (a consultant's day rate) actually discounts for length, not the
other way around. **MHFA was never a solid price anchor to begin with** — it is a subsidised,
accredited certification body, a different category of product from a fee-for-service half-day
workshop, and comparing MFC's price to it (matching or undercutting its floor) is not the reason
$2,500 is defensible. The sound comparison is commercial trainer/facilitator day rates
($1,500–$3,500/day, $5,000–$15,000 for some half-day corporate formats), against which $2,500 sits
comfortably inside normal range without needing any duration caveat. **Decided, Hunter, 2026-08-09,
directly ("yes agreed"): retire the MHFA comparison from external pricing messaging going forward.**
Commercial workshop/facilitator rates are the live comparison from this point on.

originally cited "$1,500–$3,500/day, $5,000–$15,000 for some half-day corporate formats" and

**Corrected 2026-08-09, after an independent check flagged this as not like-for-like.** MHFA's
product at that price point is a **multi-day course with formal certification** — a categorically
different, longer product, not a half-day workshop. Matching MHFA's per-person floor in dollar terms

stay visible rather than be smoothed into "matches MHFA." **The differentiator claim is also softened

does **not** support the stronger claim this section previously made, that MHFA "doesn't offer at any
price" — MHFA may offer add-on evaluation services outside its standard package that this vault has
not checked. The honest, sourced version of the value argument: **MFC bundles data tracking and a
findings report into its standard base fee, at a price matching MHFA's own standard-package floor** —

the same day. The only genuinely open input, per §4, is catering/materials cost.

### Original research, kept below for the record

Hunter asked directly for this to be evidence-checked, not decided on production-value instinct,
because a second facilitator roughly doubles the delivery-time cost line above. **It has been
checked, and the honest finding is that the evidence doesn't cleanly decide it either way — stating
that plainly is more useful than forcing a false answer.**

**What exists:** no meta-analysis or RCT compares solo vs. co-facilitated adult workshops on
engagement, learning, or satisfaction. The closest quantified analogue — K-12 co-teaching — has real
data, but it's contradictory (some comparisons favour co-teaching, g≈0.28–0.40; others favour solo
teaching, g≈−0.66) and the population doesn't map cleanly onto a single adult workshop session
(children with disabilities, ongoing classroom instruction over weeks, not a one-off half-day).
Facilitation-practice consensus (not research) puts close individual attention at 8–12 people,
degrading above ~15 — MFC's 20-person cap sits above that zone, which is *suggestive*, not evidence
about MFC's own format specifically.

**What Hunter's specific reasons for a second facilitator actually are, assessed separately:**
smoother pacing and fielding questions without derailing the room are **operational/logistics
claims**, not learning-outcome claims — real, but a different and smaller kind of benefit than "it
improves outcomes." "Mixing it up" is supported by real evidence (unbroken lecture loses attention
after 10–15 minutes) — but that's evidence for *varying activity types*, which one skilled facilitator
alternating between talking and structured activity already achieves. It is not evidence that a
*second person* is what produces the variation.

**Honest conclusion: this is a delivery-quality and operations judgement, not something the evidence
resolves for or against.** Recommend treating it as such rather than waiting for evidence that
doesn't exist: if Hunter's own experience running the room says pacing and question-handling
genuinely suffer solo at 20 people, that's a real operational reason on its own, separate from any
outcomes claim — and it should never be marketed to a buyer as "two facilitators produce better
results," because that specific claim isn't supported.

**Cost consequence, if adopted on operational grounds — figure corrected to match §1's rebuilt cost
model.** The delivery and Team Pulse aggregation hours (4.5 of the 11.0 in §1) roughly double for the
paid-time portion if two facilitators run (prep, travel, and the post-session report work may or may
not scale the same way, genuinely unclear until the actual delivery pattern is designed). That would
move total facilitator hours from 11.0 toward roughly **15.5**, and direct cost from $877.10 toward
roughly **~$1,175**, with the cost basis moving proportionally. Not the operating decision — staffing
is decided at one facilitator, §3 — kept only so the cost consequence of ever revisiting that isn't
based on a stale, now-superseded number. **Flagged 2026-08-27, not resolved here:** this recalculation
inherits §1's Team Pulse aggregation hour, which per `D-0110` is not a routine per-session cost — the
same caveat noted on that line applies here too.

## 3a. Why lower than MHFA was the right starting question, and where the answer actually landed

**Corrected, 2026-08-09, after §2's raise.** This section originally described MFC as pricing "below
MHFA's floor" and framed that as the deliberately-earned position for now. **At $2,500, MFC sits AT
MHFA's floor** ($220/person at full capacity, matching MHFA's cheapest published tier), not below it

there is just a lot of service and value we're providing"*) already answers most of what this
section originally planned to answer later, ahead of schedule. The remaining gap to close is between
MHFA's floor and its higher tiers ($220 vs. up to $462), not between MFC and MHFA's floor.

**The plan, made concrete — trigger is evidence, not a session count.** The original document tied
the price rise to a specific engagement count ("the first 5–8 paid engagements"); Hunter withdrew that
figure entirely in `D-0094` as an unfounded scarcity limit, and that withdrawal applies here too — the
trigger below is deliberately **not** a count.

| Stage | Condition | Approximate positioning |
