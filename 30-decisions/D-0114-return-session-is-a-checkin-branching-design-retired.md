# D-0114. The return session is a check-in

## The decision

**The return session exists because a skill decays if nobody comes back. It is
a check-in, not a second lesson and not a drill.**

**The first twenty minutes are identical for Essentials and Foundations:**
what today is, the room's own picture from session one (aggregate, no names, no
counts), warm reps with an opt-out, then whatever participants said they most
wanted to learn or were finding hard. **Then Essentials goes deeper into understanding and practice, and
Foundations goes into how the team implements it together.**

**Retired, in full:** the TEACH / PRACTICE / IMPLEMENT classification; the six
modules; the five allocation rules and percentage thresholds; the
eight-response floor; the fewer-than-five rule; candidate shared moments
composed offline at day 19 to 20; and the private paper slip vote with its veto
box and disposal protocol.

## Why

Hunter, 2026-08-30, verbatim: *"I don't agree with the eight response floor...
It should just be got four people. This is what we're doing with you. We're
here to help. This isn't about, like, we gotta keep a fuck because this is
crazy or really scary. We're not trying to capture. Is it a bad workplace? Tell
us, be honest. It's like, hey, what are the things that you wanna work on as a
group... If we can keep things digital, we can keep things simple."*

**The reasoning, stated so it is not lost:** the anonymity apparatus was built
for a disclosure question, and the question actually being asked is a positive
one. A secret ballot around *"what shall we practise together"* signals danger
the question does not carry, and so creates the fear it was meant to manage.
The scope rule is retained: anything really about workload, safety, staffing or
how one person behaves is not a shared moment and goes to the enabling-conditions
column as a category. **This record originally claimed the scope rule alone did
the protective work the ballot had been carrying. The 2026-08-30 safeguarding
review found that wrong** — see the addendum. A private route to remove a
candidate is retained, as a spoken costless exit rather than paper.

**Second reason, delivery.** The retired design asked one facilitator who has
never rehearsed the session to classify a cohort, choose among six modules
against five rules, compose candidates offline, run a paper ballot and sequence
a leader beat, inside seventy-five minutes. That is a protocol, not a service.
Every conditional branch is something that can fail live in front of a first
paying customer.

## Retained from the retired design

The reason the return exists; the leader beat and its single question
(*"which one of these can you make true, and by when?"*, asking for a
commitment and a date, not a verdict); the scope rule on what cannot be a
shared moment; aggregate-only reporting; and the rule that nothing an
individual wrote is visible when a leader enters.

## Two safeguards attached at the same time

1. **The pre-session form goes to MFC, never to the manager.** An employee
   telling their employer through MFC that they are struggling is a disclosure
   inside an employment relationship. The manager receives themes.
2. **Data shown back to the room carries no counts, at any group size.**
   *"Handovers came up, and a couple of people caught it at the time"* is fine.
   *"Two of you tried it"* is not, because in a small room the arithmetic
   identifies people whatever words surround it. **Corrected 2026-08-30 after the
   safeguarding review** — this originally read as a wording test, which cannot
   catch an arithmetic failure. See the addendum.

## Price and length consequence, modelled and deliberately not applied

Extending the Essentials return from thirty to sixty minutes adds about 0.75 of
a facilitator hour. The Pricing Book costs Essentials at 8.25 hours; `D-0113`
adds 0.25 for the longer manager session, giving 8.5; this change adds 0.75,
giving 9.25 hours, direct cost $671.43 and loaded $940.00 against the book's
$605 and $847. Retained margin at the middle band falls from 61% to 57%; roughly $100 across the bands
($1,350 / $1,650 / $1,900) would restore it.

**Not applied.** The committee is asked on 31 August 2026 to adopt the current
prices. This returns to them as a product improvement once built. **D-0113
continues to govern what the price includes**, and nothing here changes it.

Essentials moves from 2.0 to 2.5 hours of participant contact, closing the gap
to Foundations from 2.6x to 2.1x, so **Foundations earns its price on the depth
of its teaching, the on-site return and the written report rather than on
time.** `D-0110` forbids positioning the team-shared mechanism as an active
differentiator while its approval gate is open, so the collective piece is not
the commercial argument.

## What this decision does not do

It does not clear delivery. The escalation and distress-response procedures
still do not exist in cleared form, so no facilitator, including Hunter, can be
cleared to deliver. It is not evidence: no return session of any design has
ever been run or rehearsed, and whether one contact inside the decay window is
enough remains unevidenced.


## Addendum, 2026-08-30: what the safeguarding review changed

The retirement of the classification, the six modules, the percentage rules, the
paper ballot ceremony and the response floor on the team beat **all stand**.
Three things in this record's own reasoning did not survive an adversarial read
and are corrected in `121`:

1. **"A sentence test replaces the response floor" was wrong.** A wording test
   cannot catch an arithmetic failure. At three people, *"two of you tried it"*
   identifies the third by subtraction however carefully it is phrased. The fix
   is not a floor: it is **no counts at any group size**. Show what happened,
   never how many. That protects every room identically and never tells a small
   team it is too small for something.
2. **"The scope rule does the ballot's work" was wrong.** They protect different
   people from different things. The scope rule governs what may be proposed;
   the ballot let one person remove a candidate without arguing for it in front
   of colleagues. Without a private route, dissent in a team of four is fully
   attributable and the predictable outcome is silence, a surviving candidate
   and a scheduled commitment several people privately object to. Reinstated as
   a **spoken, costless exit** ("tell me at the break, I'll take it off and I
   won't say who"), which is lighter than the paper it replaces.
3. **The form routing rule was right but incomplete.** It needed a participant-
   facing boundary line, the restored promise that the organisation gets nothing
   about what anyone answered, a stated reader and deletion rule, and an honest
   statement that the form is not monitored. It also collides with `110`'s rule
   that the capture never reaches the organisation; that conflict is now stated
   rather than silently reconciled.

**Also caught, and not covered by any of the three claims:** simplification had
made answering aloud the default with no opt-out, which breaches `K-08` rule 6.
The leader-free requirement for the team beat had been dropped. The `D-0110`
approval gate was cited in metadata and contradicted in the body. And the
enabling-conditions column, which leaders see, had no de-identification standard
beyond "no names". All are fixed in `121`.
