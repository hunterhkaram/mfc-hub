# D-0094 — Half-day format, and how repeat revenue actually works

## 1. Format — decided by Hunter, 2026-08-09

**The two products are a one-hour Introduction and a half-day Foundations Session.** The 3-hour
framing is retired as the flagship's name and shape.

Hunter, directly: *"I think maybe with a one hour and a half day. I think the half day gives us more
time. There'll be breaks in the day, which means we can charge more... we may only do three hours of
actual work, but we go from, like, nine to one."*

**Working shape:** roughly 9:00–13:00 elapsed, with **approximately three hours of actual content**
and a substantial break (morning tea) inside it. The exact split is a delivery detail, not a
framework decision.

### Why this does not contradict `D-0093`'s evidence finding

`D-0093` concluded, from the strongest available evidence, that **adding more sessions or more
teaching content is not supported** as a way to improve outcomes. This decision does not do that.
**The intervention is unchanged — still approximately three hours of content.** What changes is the
container around it: elapsed time, breaks, and how it is sold. That is a delivery and commercial
decision, not a claim about efficacy, and it should never be presented to a buyer as though the
longer day itself produces better outcomes.

### Three things that independently support it

1. **The Committee already recommended exactly this.** `04-product-pricing.md` records the 20 July
   Committee session finding the ~$1,800 price *too low* and recommending repositioning toward
   **$3,000–$5,000 for a longer, half-day-style format.** Hunter's instinct here converges with a
   recommendation already on record, made independently and before this conversation.
2. **A half-day is a unit buyers recognise and price — but the benchmark partly cuts the other way,
   and that must be said.** `05-commercial-and-buyer-pathway-model.md` records **$500–$2,000 per
   half-day (entry tier)** and **$2,000–$7,500 per workshop (mid tier)**. **Corrected after
   independent review:** an earlier draft of this record called these "verified benchmarks." They are
   not. The source file marks them **`unknown`, vendor-published, single-sourced**, with an explicit
   instruction that *"none of it should be used in a funding application or board paper without
   independent confirmation."* Worse, read honestly: **the entry-tier half-day range tops out at
   $2,000 — below the $3,000 MFC would charge.** Only the mid tier reaches $3,000, and it is priced
   per *workshop*, a different unit. **The real support for a half-day here is that it is a
   recognised commercial unit, not that the benchmark validates MFC's price point.** It doesn't.
3. **Breaks are a genuine participant benefit, not just packaging.** A room that has eaten and
   moved is a better room. This is an honest quality improvement — it just isn't an outcomes claim.

### What this changes downstream — and the objection that has to be answered

The existing pricing model's 3-hour worked example (base coverage fee + per-participant, capped at
20) carries over structurally, but **every facilitator-time figure in it needs recalculating** —
delivery hours rise, and the current model's 6.5-hour estimate was built on a 3-hour session. Not
done here; flagged for the pricing update.

**The real value-for-money objection, named rather than avoided (added after independent review).**
"Same content, longer day, higher price" invites a buyer to see padding. Two things make that
objection sharper than it first looks:

- **MFC's own cost rises too** — more facilitator hours per session — so "we can charge more" is not
  free margin. How much of the increase is cost recovery versus genuine repricing is **unquantified
  until the pricing model is redone.**
- **The buyer's cost rises faster than the fee does.** Twenty staff released for a half-day is far
  more expensive to an organisation than twenty staff released for three hours — the fee increase is
  the smaller part of what they are being asked to absorb.

**The honest answer to give a buyer** is not "it's longer so it costs more." It is that a half-day
with proper breaks is a better-designed day for the people in it, and that MFC is pricing a
recognised commercial unit rather than an awkward three-hour block. **What must not be claimed is
that the longer day produces better outcomes** — `D-0093` establishes there is no evidence for that,
and this is the most likely place for that overclaim to slip in.

## 2. The repeat-engagement question — the finding that reframes it

Hunter's worry, repeatedly: MFC gets pigeonholed into one transaction per buyer, with no way back
in, no repeat revenue, no *"come back next year."*

**MFC's binding constraint is demand, not delivery capacity** — but the reasoning has to be the
right one.

**Corrected twice.** An earlier draft argued this from a Year 1 planning assumption of 5–11
engagements (`04-product-pricing.md`) against a much higher theoretical delivery capacity. An
independent review found that circular — a planning figure is not observed demand. **Hunter then set
the figure aside entirely, 2026-08-09: it originated as Jake's early estimate, not a founder decision
or a tested constraint — *"I want us to forget those initial claims."* No planning-volume figure
should be treated as a limit anywhere in this record or downstream of it.**

**The non-circular basis, which is stronger anyway:** MFC has delivered **zero pilots**, has
**zero paying buyers to date**, and its own commercial research marks the workplace **purchasing
pathway as `unknown`** (`05-commercial-and-buyer-pathway-model.md`) — nobody has established how
these buyers actually buy, or what triggers the purchase. That is a demand-side unknown of the most
fundamental kind, and it exists regardless of what any planning figure says.

**Why the reframe still matters:** if finding and converting a buyer is the expensive, unproven part,
then repeat engagement is not primarily about filling spare delivery capacity — it is about
**making each hard-won buyer relationship worth more.** That is the lens the options below are
judged against.

## 3. Three ways to earn repeat revenue, ranked

### Option A — Successive cohorts within the same organisation `RECOMMENDED, available now`

An organisation with 120 staff needs six half-day sessions at 20 participants each. One buyer
relationship, six engagements, sold once.

- **No *efficacy* claim required** — it is the same product delivered to different people, and
  nothing about it depends on `D-0093`'s rejected booster premise. **Corrected after independent
  review:** an earlier draft said "evidence claims required: none," which was too clean. Rebooking
  still requires the organisation to *believe session one worked* — and MFC's only outcome signal is
  a follow-up at day 21–28, which arrives **after** a rebooking conversation would realistically
  happen. Selling all six cohorts upfront avoids that timing problem but invites heavier procurement
  scrutiny, which lands on exactly the evidence MFC does not yet have. There is no version of this
  that is genuinely evidence-free.
- **Design work required: none.** It exists today.
- **Commercially natural:** start with one team or the leadership group, expand across the
  organisation. Ordinary land-and-expand, the standard way small providers grow inside large accounts.
- **Honest limit 1 — it defers the problem rather than solving it.** Six cohorts exhausts a
  120-person organisation. Then the pigeonhole returns, just later. This buys time and revenue; it is
  not the permanent answer to Hunter's worry, and should not be sold internally as one.
- **~~Honest limit 2 — concentration risk~~ — WITHDRAWN, 2026-08-09. This recommendation was wrong,
  and Hunter caught it.** An earlier version of this record recommended capping delivery at two to
  three cohorts per buyer to avoid concentrating revenue and evidence in one organisation.

  **Why it was wrong — it contradicts this record's own central finding.** §2 establishes that MFC's
  binding constraint is **demand, not delivery capacity**. Capping how much you sell to a willing
  buyer is a supply-side restriction, which only makes sense when supply is the scarce thing. It
  isn't. Hunter, directly: *"if we did six with them... we don't lose money for that, we gain money...
  if an organization's like, we want you to attend with ten teams, we'd never say no."* Correct. The
  cap would have meant declining revenue with no alternative buyer to redirect it to.

  **The "founding-cohort slots" framing that supported it is also withdrawn.** It rested on the 5–11
  engagements figure, which Hunter has identified as Jake's early planning estimate rather than a
  decided constraint, and has directed be set aside: *"I want us to forget those initial claims."*
  Treating an estimate as a scarcity limit was the error underneath the error.

  **What is actually true, and it points the opposite way:** multiple cohorts inside one organisation
  is the **best** case-study condition MFC could have for the thing it most needs to evidence.
  `D-0090`/`D-0091`'s environment and shared-norm work can only show up where enough people in the
  same organisation have been through the session — a single cohort of 20 in a 500-person company
  cannot demonstrate a norm shift; six cohorts might. **Six sessions in one organisation is a
  strategic asset for the evidence base, not a risk to it.** `reasoning, not evidence` — no MFC data
  exists either way yet.

  **Residual point, kept honestly and much smaller than the original claim:** case studies across
  several sectors are eventually more persuasive to funders than several in one. That is an argument
  to *also* pursue other buyers, never an argument to refuse a willing one.
- **Honest limit 3 — organisation size.** A 25-person business is one session and done. Option A does
  nothing for what may be a large share of MFC's realistic early market.

### Option B — Extended Pilot Partnership, correctly positioned `DESIGN IT, CAREFULLY`

Already named and priced in `04-product-pricing.md` (3–4 months, structured check-ins, up to −35%
discount for full participation), explicitly flagged there as *"the actual programme content is not
designed yet."*

- **What it legitimately is: measurement and implementation support.** The organisation gets real
  data about its own people; the shared commitment from the session stays visible in normal work
  rhythms. MFC gets the longitudinal evidence it currently has none of.
- **What it must never be sold as: a booster that improves outcomes.** `D-0093` establishes that
  claim is contradicted by the strongest available evidence. This is a `K-08` claims boundary, and
  it is the single easiest place in this whole model to overclaim by accident.
- **Low facilitator burden** — reminders, a short review conversation — so it does not consume the
  delivery capacity Option A needs.
- **Honest risk, named:** structured check-ins can quietly become de facto booster sessions, at
  which point the claim boundary is breached in practice even if the brochure is careful. Whatever
  gets designed needs an explicit line on what these check-ins are and are not.

### Option C — A genuinely deeper second product `LATER, NOT NOW`

`K-07`'s staged teaching arc already defines **Stage 3 "Deepening"** content that MFC does not
currently sell at all: the seven deep skills, the full Radar (My moments / Our moments), the evidence
layer.

- **This is real additional content, not a repetition of the first session** — which is precisely
  what makes it an honest answer to *"come back next year"* where a booster is not. The claim is
  "this is more, for people who want more," not "this makes the first session work better."
- **Already designed at framework level**, not invented here.
- **Why not now:** no pilot has run, `D-0016`'s safeguarding matters are open, and building a second
  product before the first has been delivered once would repeat the exact ordering error `D-0092`
  already made. This is the answer to the year-from-now version of Hunter's worry, and it should stay
  parked until there is delivery experience to build it on.

## 4. Recommendation

**Lead with Option A, uncapped. Design Option B deliberately. Park Option C — but know it is the real
long-term answer.**

Concretely: sell the half-day Foundations Session with an explicit multi-cohort pathway as the
default commercial conversation — not a single session with a hopeful follow-up. **If an organisation
wants six cohorts, or ten, sell them six or ten.** More cohorts in one organisation means more
revenue and a *better* evidence position for the environment and shared-norm work, not a worse one.
Offer the Extended Pilot Partnership to organisations that want depth and will participate in
measurement, positioned honestly as data and implementation support, never as a booster.

**Corrected 2026-08-09** — an earlier version recommended capping this at two to three cohorts per
buyer. Withdrawn; see Option A's limit 2 for why it contradicted this record's own finding.

**Stated plainly, because the independent review was right to push on it: this does not fully answer
Hunter's worry.** Option A defers the pigeonhole rather than removing it — it runs out when the
organisation runs out of people. **The genuine long-term answer is Option C** — a real second product
with genuinely different content (`K-07`'s Stage 3 layer), which is the only one of the three that
gives an *existing participant* an honest reason to come back. It stays parked because no pilot has
run and `D-0016` is open, not because it is the weaker idea. **Revisit it the moment there is real
delivery experience to build on.**

**What this recommendation does pass:** it requires no efficacy claim MFC cannot support, which is the
test `D-0093` set.

## 5. What is genuinely open, and one thing that constrains all of it

- **Every option above is gated behind `D-0016`'s six safeguarding matters**, unchanged by this
  record. Nothing here brings participant-facing delivery closer to approved; it describes what the
  commercial model should be once that gate clears.
- **The pricing model needs recalculating for a half-day** — facilitator hours, and therefore the
  cost basis and the base coverage fee. Not done here.
- **Multi-cohort pricing is undecided** — whether a six-session commitment gets a volume rate, and
  what it is.
- **No buyer has ever been asked** whether they would buy a multi-cohort pathway. Option A is
  strategically sound and commercially ordinary, but it is untested with a real MFC buyer, and the
  vault's own commercial research marks the workplace purchasing pathway as `unknown`.
- **Option A's limit is real:** it does nothing for small organisations, which may be a large share
  of MFC's realistic early market.
