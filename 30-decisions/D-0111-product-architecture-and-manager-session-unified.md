# D-0111 — Product architecture and the unified manager session

## Decision

### 1. ~~The three products are not one architecture at three lengths — Introduction is genuinely different in kind~~

> [!warning] **SUPERSEDED 2026-08-31 by Hunter's direct instruction. §1 no longer holds.**
>

> follow - the longer the session the more depth and info we can share (& practice)."* Shown this

> **What now holds:** all three products follow **one shared narrative** — the same beats, in the
> same order. Duration buys **depth, information and practice volume**, not a different structure.
> Introduction is the shortest expression of the same journey, not a separate design brief.
> Recorded in `60-projects/workshop-evidence-informed-adult/15-current-service-definition-v1/37-WORKSHOP-NARRATIVE-SPINE-v2.md`.
>

> three times at increasing dosage" characterisation is now closer to the intended design than
> this record's rejection of it was.

> **Superseded text preserved below**, per this vault's convention of superseding rather than

**Introduction (60 min)** is a standalone, portable talk — deliverable to any organisation, any team,
any ages, with no institutional infrastructure behind it (no return session, no data capture, no
environment work). Its design centre of gravity is **narrative and explanation**, not experience:
theory, story, and connection, structured to answer one question directly — in one hour, with no
follow-up of any kind, what gives someone the greatest realistic chance of starting a genuinely
independent, lifelong practice on their own. This is a different design brief from Essentials and
Foundations, not a shorter version of the same one.

**Essentials (90 min) and Foundations (half-day) share one core thesis, theory, and method.** They
are not different jobs — they are the same job at different depth and time. Foundations is Essentials
plus the additional element the half-day's extra time genuinely allows (per `D-0110`, that extra
element is deeper individual teaching, not routine organisational delivery). Within that shared
architecture: go deeper on some things in Foundations than Essentials allows time for (Hunter's own
example: more depth on Stop-Shift-Do specifically in Foundations than Essentials), but **the main
thesis and how it's taught stays the same across both** — this is a depth ladder between these two
specifically, not a redesign at each length.

**What Essentials/Foundations are actually for, stated in Hunter's own terms:** less time for the
slow, experiential pacing Foundations can afford — not fewer reps, not less practice, just less time
spent taking people through it slowly — traded for more explanation of *why it matters and where it
counts*, so the room can properly set up a real plan together. The point for the room as a whole is a
genuine "we all want this together" moment and a plan built collectively for that team/organisation,
not just individual practice sitting inside a shared room.

**This directly resolves finding 6 of the 2026-08-27 contrarian review** (three-product-content
pass): the products are not simply "one loop taught three times at increasing dosage." Introduction
is a genuinely different design brief. Essentials and Foundations *are* a depth ladder on a shared
thesis — that part of the contrarian's "dosage ladder" characterisation was correct for those two,
and is now confirmed as the intended design, not an unexamined default.

### 2. The manager/leader session is unified: one real ~1-hour session, shared across Essentials and Foundations

**This corrects work done earlier the same day.** The 45-minute, Essentials-only leader briefing
integrated into `workshop-package.html` (`id="leader90"`) this morning does not match this decision
and is **superseded, pending a rebuild** — see "What this supersedes" below.

The manager/leader session must be:

1. **~1 hour, not 45 minutes.** Hunter's own words: *"it needs its own product... we need them to
   fully be across it, to fully understand it, to be bored in [drawn in]... we can't just go tick in
   the box half an hour."* A shorter session reads, and functions, as a compliance formality rather
   than genuine engagement — Hunter is explicit that this is the wrong shape regardless of how well
   the content inside it is designed.
2. **One shared session for both Essentials and Foundations buyers**, not a separate design per
   product. The manager's job is materially the same regardless of which team session their people
   attend.
3. **Content emphasis, stated directly:**
   - What to actually watch for in the people they manage — signals, not just categories.
   - How this gets implemented concretely in their existing team rhythms: team meetings, whether a
     recurring slot (Hunter's example: a Friday session) can carry it, physical/environmental
     reminders (posters or equivalent), each built with real intention rather than bolted on.
   - Framed as **the manager's own responsibility to upskill on** — not only "this helps you and your
     own life," but explicitly "this is a responsibility you and your team carry, and here's what to
     be mindful of and how to build that skill."
4. **A proper meeting, a proper conversation** — the register Hunter wants is genuine engagement and
   dialogue, not a scripted briefing delivered at a manager.
