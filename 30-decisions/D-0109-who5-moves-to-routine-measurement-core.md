# D-0109 — WHO-5 moves to the routine measurement core

## Decision

WHO-5 is part of the routine participant measurement core for **every participant, in every
service that has a return timepoint** — not limited to selected case-study or research cohorts.

1. **Baseline:** every participant completes WHO-5, alongside the MFC mechanism items (and IMTTAQ
   where already proposed at later timepoints — see KN-0006 for the IMTTAQ position, which this
   record does not change).
2. **Day-21 return (Foundations) / week-3 return (Essentials):** every participant completes WHO-5
   again, alongside the core items.
3. **Introduction (no return session):** WHO-5 is fielded at baseline only, on the same logic
   already applied to every other instrument in this format — there is no second timepoint to ask
   it again at. This is Claude's reading of "every participant, baseline, and asked again at the
   day-21 return" applied to a format that has no day-21 return, not a separate founder decision;
   flagged here rather than silently assumed.
4. **Case-study/research cohorts:** continue to receive WHO-5 at their existing quarterly cadence
   (`D-0105`) in addition to the new routine baseline/return points — this record does not remove
   or narrow anything `D-0105` already established for that group.

## What this amends, and what it does not touch

**Amends `D-0100` point 4 directly.** `D-0100` states: *"Remove WHO-5 from the routine first-pilot
core"* and *"Preserve WHO-5 only as a possible future research module after [gates] exist."* This
record reverses that specific position: WHO-5 re-enters the routine core, decided by Hunter as the
same founder authority who made the original exclusion.

**Does not touch `D-0100`'s other six points** — the three-timepoint structure, the lean MFC
mechanism-item construct set, the past-14-day access item, the demographics/mission-discovery
carve-out, or the pseudonymous-linkage preference all remain exactly as `D-0100` set them.

**Does not resolve `D-0100`'s WHO-5 gates — it widens their scope.** A named wellbeing question,
confirmed licence position, an analysis plan, a clinical-response pathway, privacy review, and an
appropriate ethics determination were always required before WHO-5 could be fielded to anyone.
Those gates are unchanged in substance. What has changed is who they apply to: previously a small,
deliberately selected case-study subset; now every participant in every service with a return. This
is a materially larger exposure on every one of those open gates, most acutely the clinical-response
pathway — WHO-5 is a validated depression-screening-adjacent instrument, and D-0100's own reasoning
for keeping it out of the routine core was exactly this risk. **Nothing in this record makes WHO-5
field-ready.** It remains gated, now for a larger population.

## Claim boundary

Unchanged from `D-0100`: this record authorises what MFC intends to measure. It does not authorise
fielding, and it does not authorise any claim of wellbeing effect, improvement, causation, or
clinical outcome from WHO-5 data once collected. `K-08` continues to govern any external-facing
statement.
