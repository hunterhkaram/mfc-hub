# D-0105 — Touchpoint and case-study cadence strategy

## Decision

Extends D-0100's three-timepoint core (baseline, immediate post, day-21/day-28 close) with an
organisational touchpoint layer, separate from the per-participant instrument:

1. **Touchpoint two is the existing day-21 return session** (already built into Essentials and
   Foundations) — no new session is added for this purpose.
2. **Standard partner organisations** (i.e. not selected as a case study): **one** further check-in
   after the first quarter, once that quarter's data is compiled — a scheduled meeting, not a
   session, where the organisation completes a light form beforehand and MFC discusses it with them.
   This is **not** repeated indefinitely. It is a single, bounded follow-up.
3. **Case-study organisations** (selected, not every partner): the same light-form-plus-meeting
   format, but **continuing quarterly for a full year** (four touchpoints across 12 months).
4. **Case-study selection criteria**, in descending order of justification:
   - **Engagement/manager-support signal at the day-21 return** — the best-evidenced of the four,
     though the evidence is modest and must be stated as such. Blume et al. (2010), full-text
     verified elsewhere in MFC's own evidence corpus and already the basis for the leader-session
     design, found **modest** associations between real-world training use and workplace transfer
     climate (r = .27) and social support (r = .21) — measured entirely within organisational
     training samples, establishing correlation and **not** causation, and not tested outside
     workplace settings. **Correction, 2026-08-23:** an earlier version of this record described
     these as "among the strongest predictors of whether training transfers into use." That
     overstated the source, which MFC's own Stage 8 synthesis explicitly characterises as modest
     associations. Lead with this criterion when explaining selection logic, but describe it
     accurately — best available, not strong.
   - **Willingness to commit the full year** — feasibility, not evidence.
   - **Cohort size** — consistency with Foundations' existing delivery floor (6+), not itself a
     finding.
   - **Sector/context spread across the selected case studies** — standard case-study/qualitative
     research practice (maximum-variation sampling), not an MFC-specific result.
5. **Individual participant forms at 3 months feed the first quarterly discussion**, standard or
   case-study, and continue feeding subsequent ones for case-study orgs.

## Why this shape, and what was rejected on the way here

An earlier version of this design proposed a **monthly** organisational touchpoint for *every*
partner org, framed loosely as "a year-long case study." A bounded panel found this had two real
problems, both corrected in this record: (a) a monthly *participant*-facing touch would have
contaminated D-0100's unaided-recall measure — resolved by making the organisational touchpoint a
**manager-level** conversation, not a participant-facing repeat of the instrument; (b) framing
**indefinite ongoing contact** as evidence that anything survives independently was found to be
**backwards** — the vault's own RULER research explicitly warns against exactly this pattern
("build for graduation, not renewal"; RULER's own documented weakness is dependence on ongoing
provider contact for its effects). Indefinite quarterly contact for every organisation cannot answer
whether anything holds up without MFC's continued involvement, because MFC never actually withdraws.

**The decision below resolves this without adding process weight Hunter judged not worth carrying**
(his own words, in conversation: *"this is kind of too much work/effort in all honesty"*). Rather
than engineering a deliberate silence-then-check design (which would answer the durability question
more cleanly but cost more to run), the resolution is structural: **standard organisations get a
single bounded follow-up, not indefinite contact**, so the renewal-dependency problem simply does
not apply to them. **Case-study organisations knowingly trade durability-proof for richer ongoing
data** — a legitimate, bounded choice for a small, deliberately selected group, not a claim MFC
should make about organisations broadly.

## What this does not do

- Does not change D-0100's core participant instrument, timepoints, or claim boundary.
- Does not constitute a durability/sustained-effect claim for standard partner organisations or for
  case-study organisations — MFC continues to hold contact with case-study organisations throughout
  the year, so no version of this design shows effects surviving independently of MFC's involvement.
  If that specific claim becomes something Hunter wants to test later, it requires its own design
  (a genuine withdrawal period), not this one.
- Does not resolve any of D-0100's still-open gates (clinical-governance owner, legal/privacy
  sign-off, Eimear/Debra's advisor review, participant-testing rounds). Those remain open and
  unaffected by this record.
- Does not commit MFC to any specific quarterly-form instrument — the content of the quarterly
  organisational check-in form has not yet been designed.

## Claim boundary

This record does not authorise any claim that participation in the quarterly touchpoint programme,
standard or case-study, causes, sustains, or proves durable behaviour change. It authorises the
cadence and selection logic only. `K-08` continues to govern any external-facing statement.
