# Measurement System v3.1 — the advisor asks incorporated

## Why the advisors want these three things

Approving instruments whose purpose you can't see is how a form becomes thirty items nobody
can defend. So, plainly.

**MFC's own questions have one blind spot: they only ever ask about MFC.** C1–C6 ask whether
a moment came up, whether you noticed it, whether you acted, what got in the way. They are
all views of the same process from different angles. That is `AG-193`'s point (HARD): MFC's
bespoke questions have **unknown construct validity** — *"We don't know whether it's
measuring what it's supposed to measure"* — and cannot stand alone.

The three asks each do a different job.

### IMTTAQ — six items — "did their environment even let them?"

MFC's model has three parts (`KN-0006`): the teaching, the practice, and **the environment
that makes practice possible.** MFC measures the first two and *assumes* the third.

IMTTAQ's two subscales measure reported transfer and **Opportunities to Integrate** —
whether the workplace gave them a chance. That matters commercially as much as
scientifically: if enactment is low *and* opportunity is low, that is the organisation's
finding, not MFC's failure. Without it, every low number reads as "the workshop didn't work."

It answers Eimear's own critique (`AG-184`, her observation, not established fact): her read
of the meta-analytic picture is that workplace wellbeing programmes don't move outcomes and
that culture and workload do. The environment layer is MFC's response to that — and it is
currently the one layer with no measure attached.

### WHO-5 — five items — "does any of this connect to anything outside itself?"

`AG-171`, Eimear: *"how do you know if any of the changes are associated with anything?"*
And `AG-172`: a validated instrument lets a **statistics package cross-compare** against
enactment; MFC's own questions cannot.

With WHO-5 in, you can ask whether people reporting more Mental Reps also report better
wellbeing. **Association, not causation** — it proves nothing about what MFC caused, and the
report must never say otherwise.

**Two honest limits, stated here rather than discovered later by an analyst.**

1. **Common-method variance.** Both measures are self-report, same person, same sitting,
   minutes apart. A positive correlation is therefore not clean evidence that the MFC items
   are valid — shared method alone can produce it. WHO-5 makes the dataset *less*
   self-referential; it does not establish construct validity on its own. Anyone presenting
   it as the fix for `AG-193` is overselling it.
2. **A null is likely, and must be pre-registered as such.** R3's warning about TYDQ applies
   to WHO-5 with equal force: *"Adopting a measure your intervention has no mechanism to
   move is how you manufacture your own disconfirming evidence."* A half-day workshop should
   not be expected to move a 14-day wellbeing screener in three weeks.
   **Required protocol clause, written before fielding:** *WHO-5 is a context measure. It is
   not expected to move within three weeks, and a null result is not evidence the service
   failed.* Writing that down beforehand is what makes a subsequent null honest rather than
   embarrassing.

**And WHO-5 does not make this a clinical instrument.** `D-0100` imposed a "clinical-response pathway" gate on it; no advisor asked for that, and Eimear specified the opposite control on 31 August (`AG-004`, HARD): separate MFC from clinical risk **by disclaimer, not by clinical apparatus**. Closed by `D-0119`. What WHO-5 needs is a plain non-clinical disclaimer and a scoring note, served to everyone.

### Kirkpatrick — zero items — "what shape is this in?"

Onno's ask (`AG-206`). It adds **no questions**. It is a filing structure for measures MFC
already has, in a vocabulary buyers and committees recognise. Mapping in §4.

### The unifying question

`AG-182`, Eimear, tonight — the ask that ties all three together: *"I'd be super curious as
to whether it maps onto other things"* — do people scoring higher at the learning level
implement more moments, and show a different WHO-5 at follow-up? **That single analysis is
the reason to have all three rather than any one.** It is also the analysis that needs an
analyst and a sample size, neither of which MFC currently has (§3, §7).

| Job | Answered by |
|---|---|
| Did they learn and use the process? | MFC's own items |
| Did their environment give them the chance? | IMTTAQ |
| Does reported use connect to anything else? | WHO-5 (with the two limits above) |
| What shape do we report it in? | Kirkpatrick |

`AG-183` permits the addition specifically where measures *"test genuinely different things"*
and do not overlap **or overload the participant** — the register marks that position a
composite of three speakers and warns against quoting it to any one advisor. The
non-overlap test passes. The overload test is §3, and it does not pass cleanly.

## 2. The forms

Counting convention: **participant-facing items only.** Code fields (A0/B0/C0) and
system-captured fields are excluded from the count but are **retained on every form** —
without B0, Form B cannot be linked, and the pre→post linkage R3 rates at 98–100% is lost.

### Form A — before session

A0 code · A1 past-14-day noticing · A2 system-captured · **A3–A7 WHO-5, intact (NEW)**

**5 participant-facing items, ~3 min.**

### Form B — immediately after session

B0 code · B1–B6 unchanged · **B7 open question (NEW)**

> *Think of a moment coming up where you might use this. What is it, and what would you do?*

**7 items, ~4 min.** No WHO-5 (nothing to detect at zero elapsed time). No IMTTAQ — its
items assume a post-training chance to use the learning (`KN-0006`).

### Form C — day 21–28 return

| ID | Item | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C0 | Code | unchanged |
| C1 | Did a relevant moment come up since the session? | unchanged — gates C2–C6 |
| C2 | Did you notice it and intentionally choose what to do next? | unchanged |
| C3a–c | **Rep count:** (a) relevant moments in the last 7 days, (b) of those, how many did you run the steps in — even partly, even badly, even too late, (c) of those, how many did you complete | **REPLACES v3's C5.** Enactment = (b)÷(a) |
| C4 | Barrier list — **full v3 list retained, including `Prefer not to say`** and the `None`/`Prefer not` exclusivity rule | restored after draft 1 wrongly cut it |
| C5 | Did a situation arise where this did not feel appropriate or safe? | unchanged (was v3 C4) |
| C6 | Product improvement checklist | **restored** — see §5 |
| C7–C12 | **IMTTAQ intact (NEW)** | Transfer ×3, Opportunities ×3 |
| C13–C17 | **WHO-5 intact (NEW)** | |
| C18 | **Open (NEW):** *Think of the most recent time a moment on your Radar came up. What happened, and what did you do? (2–3 sentences. Don't use anyone's name.)* | `R3` §6.3 |

**19 participant-facing items + 1 open, ~8–9 min.**

**Data rules that must not be lost in any rewrite:**
- **`a = 0` rule.** C3a asks about the last 7 days; C1 gates on the whole 21–28 day window.
  A participant gated in by a moment on day 4 with none in the last week yields `a = 0` and
  the enactment rate is **undefined, not zero**. Record as `no_recent_opportunity`, exclude
  from the rate's denominator, report the count separately.
- **Replacement for v3's `C2_C5_inconsistent` control.** If C2=Yes and C3b=0, or C2=No and
  C3b>0, retain both, flag `C2_C3b_inconsistent`, keep C2 in its own denominator, exclude
  from the rate numerator. **Do not silently recode.**

## 3. The decision that actually matters: response rate

`AG-176` (Eimear, HARD): *"the power is in the numbers"* — roughly 15% of workshop
attendees complete a post-workshop form.

`D-0115` caps Essentials and Foundations at **twenty participants, full stop.** At 15%, a
capped cohort returns **three responses.** Every analysis in §"Why the advisors want these"
— the WHO-5 cross-comparison, `AG-182`'s mapping question, any enactment rate with a
denominator — is uninterpretable at n=3.

**This, not item count, is what decides whether the design produces anything.**

| Option | Effect | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Complete Form C inside the return session** | The return session **exists for both Essentials and Foundations** — `D-0114`, founder direction, 30 August. Protected time, in the room, no cold send. Plausibly the largest single lift available | **Not blocked.** Draft 1 wrongly described this as an undecided product question. Constraint: `D-0114`'s **no-counts rule** governs what is shown *back to the room* — collecting counts and displaying none are compatible, but the session design must keep them apart |
| **B. Protected return time written into the organisation agreement** | Buyer commits to it as a delivery condition | Supported by `AG-201` (Gina, HARD): assessment outcomes negotiated into every client engagement |
| **C. Recurring organisation check-in** | `D-0115` §3 adopts a monthly-or-quarterly check-in with each partner organisation — a standing contact channel that did not exist when v3 was written | **New, uncosted.** Not a participant channel, but the natural home for the manager/implementation record |
| **D. Pool across cohorts** | MFC's own evidence base reaches usable N even when no single cohort does | No per-organisation participant section for small cohorts |

**Recommendation: A + B as standard, D for MFC's own evidence base, C for the
organisation-side record.** A is the cheapest and was wrongly presented as the most
expensive in draft 1.

## 4. Kirkpatrick mapping — the report spine

| Level | MFC items | Report may say |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 — Reaction** | B5, B6 | Experience, agency, friction. Never a proxy for learning |
| **L2 — Learning** | B1, B2, B7 | Process discrimination at immediate post. **Recognition, not unaided recall** |
| **L3 — Behaviour** | C1, C2, C3a–c, C4, C18, IMTTAQ **Transfer ×3** and **Opportunities ×3** | Participant-reported use and reported opportunity, with denominators |
| **L4 — Results** | *Deliberately bounded* — delivery counts, response coverage, component implementation | No attribution |

**Opportunities to Integrate sits at L3, not L4.** In Kirkpatrick's own model, opportunity
to perform is a *required driver* of behaviour, not an organisational result. Draft 1 put it
at L4 — an error against the model, in the section written to answer Onno.

**Outside the ladder, stated explicitly rather than left unfiled:** A1 (baseline noticing),
A3–A7 and C13–C17 (WHO-5, a cross-comparison measure), B3/B4 (offer and participation
route), C5 (safety), C6 (product improvement). Reporting WHO-5 as an L4 result would be
exactly the attribution error the boundary paragraphs exist to prevent.

**Two mandatory verbatim paragraphs, in every report.**

> **Level 3.** These are participant self-reports of their own use. No behaviour was
> observed, no individual was assessed, and no manager or colleague was asked to rate
> anyone. Percentages describe respondents only.

> **Level 4.** These are context measures for the organisation. No change here is attributed
> to the MFC service. There is no comparison condition, so any movement described is
> descriptive only.

The L3 paragraph matters more than the L4 one. A buyer reading *"Level 3 — Behaviour: 40%"*
reads observed behaviour; `K-08` bars objective behaviour observation. L4 must be present
and bounded rather than blank — a blank reads as an omission and invites a request for a
number MFC has framed itself as owing.

## 5. What does not change, and must not

- **No manager or peer report at any level.** `R3` §6.7, `AG-194`. Kirkpatrick's
  field-standard L3 method is paired learner/manager surveys; MFC refuses it because it
  destroys the confidentiality promise and there is little externally observable to rate.
- **The three separate records**, never joined. **The claim ladder A–D** — Kirkpatrick is a
  reporting *shape*; the claim ladder governs what may be *said*. Standard reports stop at B.
- **Every privacy, linkage, accessibility and support rule in v3.**
- **C4's full barrier list and C6.** Draft 1 cut both, wrongly. `Capacity or time` and
  `Needed support or change beyond me` are **per-moment, multi-select attributions about one
  occasion**; IMTTAQ Opportunities is a **3-item Likert subscale about the general setting**.
  Different referent, different time frame, not substitutable — and cutting them would have
  deleted precisely the two barriers that point at the organisation, which is the argument
  IMTTAQ was added to support. C6 is the only accessibility-and-adaptation signal at day 21
  and the only participant input to v3's report-template item 6 (*"what MFC will keep,
  investigate, adapt or stop"*); C18's coding rubric has no product-improvement dimension,
  so the claim that C6 was recoverable from it was false.

## 6. Reporting floor — draft 1 was wrong

Draft 1 recommended adopting `R3` #8's N≥20 report / N≥10 breakdown thresholds.

**`D-0115` §4 retired the headcount reporting floor by founder direction on 31 August:**
*"the under twenty reporting rule has gone, mate, ignore that. We don't need that anymore."*
What remains is `D-0114`'s **no-counts rule**. With a hard cap of twenty (`D-0115` §1),
N≥20 respondents would require a 100% response rate in every cohort forever — the
recommendation was not merely against a decision, it was arithmetically impossible.

**Withdrawn. No headcount floor is proposed.**

**Resolved by `D-0119`, 2026-09-01.** Founder direction: *"no counts is for the room only, reports keep denominators."* `D-0114`/`D-0115`'s no-counts rule governs **in-room display** in the return session. The **written organisation report retains denominators** and v3's claim ladder stands unchanged, with small-cell suppression as already specified. No rebuild of the claim ladder is required.
