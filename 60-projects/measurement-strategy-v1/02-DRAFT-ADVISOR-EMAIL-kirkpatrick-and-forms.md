## Where this landed

Short version: **we've adopted it, and it changes less than the name
suggests.**

Kirkpatrick isn't a set of questions — it's a **filing structure**: four
things worth evaluating (reaction, learning, behaviour, results), and each
organisation writes its own questions for them. So adopting it doesn't add
anything to what we ask. It gives our existing questions a shape you'll both
recognise, and gives me a shared vocabulary with Onno for what each part of
our forms is doing. (That last part is my own read, not something I've
checked against a published Kirkpatrick source — worth saying plainly rather
than presenting it as more settled than it is.)

**One correction on my end first:** I'd been thinking of Kirkpatrick as a
third validated measure alongside WHO-5 and a training-transfer measure.
It isn't — Kirkpatrick is the structure, not an instrument. And on that
second measure: Eimear, this is one for you specifically — I've been calling
it "IMTTAQ" since your working session with Debra, but going back to the
actual recording, I can only find it as "IMTAC," and I can't independently
locate a published instrument under either name. Before I put a name to it
anywhere outside this email, can you tell me exactly what it is and where
it's published? Until then I'm treating it as unconfirmed rather than as our
second validated measure.

## The four levels, and what we already had approved at each

1. **Reaction** — how the session felt. We already ask this at the end of
   every session.
2. **Learning** — did the core distinction land. Forced-choice recognition,
   pre and post — not a claim of unaided recall, and we say so in every
   report.
3. **Behaviour** — did people actually use it. This is the return-session
   layer, and it's where WHO-5 (and the second measure, once confirmed) come
   in (below).
4. **Results** — did anything change for the organisation. Important to get
   right: this is **never** a participant score. It's a separate,
   organisation-level record — delivery counts and what we hear back from
   the manager/implementation side. No individual wellbeing number ever gets
   reported as an organisational result.

**Scope:** the four-level structure applies wherever there's a return
session — currently Essentials and Foundations. **Introduction stays
pre/post only** — before and right after, checking understanding. No return
session exists for it, so nothing past Level 2 applies, and there's no WHO-5
or the second measure on it either.

## WHO-5, and the measure I need Eimear to confirm

- **The training-transfer measure** (name pending Eimear's confirmation,
  above) — the intended six items would answer a question we couldn't
  otherwise ask: did the person's *environment* actually give them a chance
  to use what we taught. Held pending confirmation.
- **WHO-5** — five items, used intact and confirmed. The only currently
  confirmed measure that reaches outside our own vocabulary — it lets us ask
  whether reported use tracks with anything else, as an **association, never
  a claim that we caused it.**

WHO-5 is used exactly as published, never adapted, and reported with the same
limit every time: participant-reported, not observed behaviour, no causal
claim. The second measure gets the same treatment once it's confirmed.

## What this lets us say over time — and what it never lets us say

What it earns us: a defensible answer, cohort after cohort, to "did people
understand it, did they use it, and did anything change on the
organisation's side" — with a named validated instrument behind at least one,
and a second pending Eimear's confirmation, of those three questions.

What it never earns us: proof that MFC caused a change. No cohort we run has
a comparison condition, so Level 3 and Level 4 are always described as
**reported**, never observed or attributed.
