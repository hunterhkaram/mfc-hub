Key — how to read every item below

IMTTAQ — working name, not yet confirmed as a real citable instrument (blocker 1). Six items measuring reported transfer and whether the workplace gave them the opportunity. Treat as unvalidated until the source is confirmed — do not present externally as "validated" until then.
WHO-5 — validated instrument. Five items, well-established wellbeing index with published norms. Used as a context measure, never as an outcome MFC claims to have moved.
MFC-designed — our own questions. Not validated. Unknown reliability and construct validity — which is exactly why the two above are there.

2. When Kirkpatrick applies — and where the four levels actually live

D-0120: the four-level structure applies wherever a return session exists. Essentials and Foundations, today. Not a fixed product list — if MFC ever builds a return touchpoint for another service, the same structure extends to it.

Introduction has no return session, so it gets Levels 1–2 only, pre/post — see §1.

Your read was that three check-ins carry the four levels, with the third capturing the last two. Half right, and the correction matters.

Levels 1–3 are the learner's evidence. Level 4 is the organisation's. L4 does not come from the participant form at all — it comes from the delivery/fidelity record and the Radar/manager implementation record, which close in the same window.

If L4 sat on Form C, the only thing available to fill it would be WHO-5 — and reporting a wellbeing score as an organisational result is precisely the attribution error our claim boundary forbids. Keeping L4 on the organisation side is what makes it safe to have at all.

Two paragraphs appear verbatim in every report.

Level 3. These are participant self-reports of their own use. No behaviour was observed, no individual was assessed, and no manager or colleague was asked to rate anyone. Percentages describe respondents only.
Level 4. These are context measures for the organisation. No change here is attributed to the MFC service. There is no comparison condition, so any movement described is descriptive only.

The Level 3 one matters more. A buyer reading "Level 3 — Behaviour: 40%" hears observed behaviour.

7. What everyone is told — served to everyone, not triggered by any score

About these questions

This is a workshop evaluation, not a health check. MFC is not a clinical service and we are not assessing you. No one reviews or scores you individually, nobody follows up with you, and no one at your workplace sees your answers.

If anything here brings something up for you, or you'd like to talk to someone, your GP is a good place to start. You can call Lifeline any time on 13 11 14. In an emergency, call 000.
One thing worth knowing about those five questions

Those five questions come from a well-known wellbeing questionnaire called the WHO-5. It is widely used and it is not a diagnosis of anything.

One thing is worth saying plainly: when people score low on it, that is sometimes a sign it is worth having a proper conversation about how you have been going. We do not see your score and we cannot tell you yours — these answers are not linked to you, and no one at MFC or at your workplace can look them up.

So this is for you, not from us: if the last couple of weeks have felt flat, heavy or harder than usual, that is worth mentioning to your GP. It is a normal thing to bring up and they will know what to do with it. Lifeline is on 13 11 14 any time.

Both drafted for Eimear to react to, not to be adopted as-is. The scoring note is deliberately softer than "indicates a need for further depression screening" — accurate to a clinician, alarming to a general adult. That calibration is hers to make.

8. Open blockers — why this is not field-ready

  • 1. IMTTAQ may not be a verifiable instrument at all — not just unheld wording. Traced to source: the only primary record is an auto-transcript of Eimear speaking, rendered "IMTAC," not "IMTTAQ." The name and expansion ("Integrated Model of Training Transfer and its Associated Questionnaire") appear only in a non-verbatim extract of that meeting. No published instrument under either name has been found. Do not call this "validated" or "IMTTAQ" externally until it is confirmed — via Debra's Measures Table, then Eimear directly, asking her to name and cite the actual instrument.
  • 2. WHO-5 master wording not yet obtained, and licence position unconfirmed. The five items shown are a standard published rendering, not verified against the master. Same defect class as blocker 1 — do not build from the text in this document.
  • 2a. Insurance not yet checked. MFC's professional-indemnity exclusion (endorsement BUP026) excludes cover for anything connected to rendering professional advice or service — administering a validated wellbeing instrument at scale is exactly the class of activity that exclusion targets. Ask the broker whether this is a notifiable change in use before fielding, not after.
  • 3. Free-text screening rule for B9 and C19 must exist before first use — identifiability lives in free text, not in the linkage field.
  • 4. Response rate. At a cap of 20 and ~15% completion, a cohort returns 3 responses — below our own 10-respondent reporting floor. The design would suppress its own output. Fix is delivery mode, not item count: complete Form C inside the return session.
  • 5. Form length selects against the people we most need to hear from. Low literacy, English as an additional language, shift work, no private device — these are the participants who drop out of a long form first. A measure only the articulate and desk-based complete is not representative, and in-session completion only partly fixes it. No EAL route, no screen-reader route and no named no-device route currently exist.
  • 6. No analysis plan and no named analyst. The cross-comparison is the entire point of adding WHO-5, and nobody can currently run it.
  • 7. No hosting platform has been selected. The entire no-contact, code-only linkage design is privacy-protective on paper only until a platform is checked for hidden metadata (email/login/IP/device/analytics). If a platform gets chosen by default rather than decision, it can silently break the anonymity promise this whole design rests on.
  • 8. Advisors have never seen this architecture. They were sent a review pack on 13 July — now superseded — and it has zero comments from anyone, 50 days open. They have never seen Measurement System v3 or anything resembling these forms. The gap is larger than "awaiting a reply."

None of these blocks your review of the architecture. Blockers 1–3 and 5 block fielding. Blocker 7 blocks showing this to advisors as a finished thing — though sending it to get their review is exactly the point.

9. Beyond the workshop — the quarterly and year-later check-ins

D-0105 (21 Aug) already decided the cadence for this — one Q1 follow-up for every standard partner; quarterly for a full year (four touchpoints) for selected case-study organisations — but explicitly left the content undesigned: "the content of the quarterly organisational check-in form has not yet been designed." That's what this section builds.

Conflict, surfaced not resolved. D-0115 §3 (31 Aug) separately adopted "a recurring organisation check-in, monthly or quarterly" for every partner org — cadence unfixed, uncosted, no reference to D-0105's already-decided split. Two decision records now say different things about who gets checked in on and how often. This section builds content against D-0105's structure, since it's the more specific, deliberated one with a stated claim boundary — but the two records need reconciling, and that's yours to call.

The boundary that governs everything below, from D-0105 itself: MFC never actually withdraws from a case-study organisation during its year of quarterly contact — so no version of this design can show an effect surviving independently of MFC's continued involvement. D-0105 cites RULER's own documented weakness directly: dependence on ongoing provider contact is what you're checking for, not proof against. "RULER-style" below means matching that shape — a light pulse, then a deeper annual read — not adopting RULER's own proprietary instrument, which MFC has neither licensed nor verified. Same discipline as the IMTTAQ finding: unconfirmed content is never presented as real.
