## Proposed learning

**Proposed lesson: a rejected recurring commitment returning under a new name must be re-tested against the ground it was rejected on.**

D9 rejected webinars on capacity grounds. The same object has returned as "free monthly Zoom session" with the capacity argument neither addressed nor contradicted — not through anyone's bad faith, and the strategic intent genuinely did change, which is a legitimate reason to reopen. The defect is that nothing in the system *required* the reopening to engage the original ground. The rejection was recorded as a table row and bound to no check.

This is `L-025`'s pattern once more (a control that is never invoked fails exactly as hard as one that does not exist), with a specific twist worth naming separately: **the rename is what defeats retrieval.** A grep for "webinar" finds D9; nobody greps for "webinar" when the packet says "free monthly session."

Binding control I would propose, kept deliberately small so it does not become the compliance overhead `L-064` warns against: **any proposal that creates a recurring, externally-advertised commitment must state (a) sustaining hours per month, (b) a named owner, and (c) whether a prior record rejected a similar commitment — before adoption.** Three lines. It is not a governance layer; it is a field on a form, and it would have caught this one.

Whether that is worth a numbered lesson under `L-008` is a call for the routing/learning owner, not mine. I flag the cause rather than assume the remedy.

WHAT BREAKS FIRST

**Not the session. Not the tooling. Not the money. The follow-up layer breaks first** — the post-session 1:1 outreach to attendees, which is the only step in the entire model that converts an attendance into a conversation into a sale.

Why that one: it is the least visible (nobody sees it not happening), the most deferrable (no advertised date), the most emotionally costly (it is the closest thing to selling in a plan whose first principle is "nothing is sold cold"), and it collides directly with the first paid delivery. Sessions 1 and 2 will run on founder energy and look like success. The follow-up will quietly not happen.

The failure state is specific and worth naming, because it is the current failure reproduced at greater cost: MFC accumulates registrations, attendance, a database and visible goodwill, reports them as progress, and closes zero sales. The topic register already says `STRATEGY_FINALISED_EXECUTION_UNPROVEN` — *"the strategy defines three funnels and referral channels; it has not produced a sale."* This model, unmitigated, makes that sentence bigger rather than untrue. And it will feel like it is working the entire time, because the vanity numbers are the ones that move first — which the packet's own hard boundary already prohibits reporting as impact.

**The leading indicator, ~3 weeks early: median time-to-first-human-contact after registration.**

Measure the hours between a person registering and receiving a genuinely human, non-automated touch. Two thresholds, either of which trips the alarm:

- median time-to-human-touch exceeds **5 business days**, or
- **more than 30%** of the previous session's attendees have no logged follow-up by the day promotion opens for the next session.

This runs ~3 weeks ahead of any visible damage because registration for session N+1 opens roughly three weeks before it runs — so the degradation is observable a full cycle before attendance dips and a full quarter before anyone notices a flat sales line. It is also one CRM field and one report, costing minutes. **This is the number the committee should look at first every month**, ahead of registrations and attendance.

Today, the seven referral relationships live substantially in one person's memory; if that is still true in 90 days, an unavailable month is an unrecoverable one.
4. **A named volunteer with CRM access and standing authority to send the "we'll be back next month" note** — not to facilitate, just to keep the audience warm. Jake or Nabill. Fifteen minutes of work; must be arranged before it is needed.
5. **The V5/T5 fixed-structure format**, so that a trained facilitator or the psychologist Hunter mentioned can eventually run it. Not deliverable inside 90 days — but foreclosed permanently if the format is built bespoke in week 3.

What survives with 1–5 in place: the list, the database, the relationships, the newsletter, the automated capture, and the audience's belief that MFC still exists. What still does not survive: the session itself, and all conversion. That is an acceptable one-month gap. It is not acceptable as a permanent structure, and item 5 is the only route out of it.

STRONGEST OBJECTION TO MY OWN VIEW

Nabill has actually run free masterclasses for small-to-mid-reputation organisations and reports they work well with resources at this scale (25:00, 31:10). **He has operating evidence; I have a bottom-up estimate.** Where those conflict, the person who has run the thing should generally win.

Three specific ways I may be wrong. First, my per-cycle session figure of 15.5 hours may hold for cycle 1–2 and then collapse to 8–10 as content, promotion copy and follow-up templates stop being rebuilt each month — which would take steady state to roughly 40 hrs/month and change the verdict from "at the ceiling" to "tight but real." Second, my "follow-up breaks first" claim assumes follow-up is manual; a properly built Brevo sequence with genuine segmentation does more of that work than I have credited, and the human touch may only be needed for the 10–20% who engage. Third, and most uncomfortably: my whole return is shaped by an instinct to protect a founder from over-commitment, and `L-064` is explicit that MFC has real human governance for that and does not need me duplicating it. A capacity specialist who always says "too much" is not calibrated, he is just a brake. The proposal may genuinely be the best available use of Hunter's hours precisely because the alternative — seven referral channels that have produced no sale — is not obviously a better one.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY VIEW

- **A measured timesheet from cycle 1.** Actual hours, logged, on promotion / prep / facilitation / follow-up. If the total lands under 10 hrs, my estimate is wrong and I would revise the whole capacity finding rather than defend it.
- **Nabill or another volunteer committing named hours per month in writing**, with a specific owned workstream (most usefully: social + comment-to-DM, which is 14 of the 50 hours and is the most delegable block in the table). Advisory input does not change the arithmetic; an owned workstream does.
- **Evidence of a comparable one-operator charity sustaining a monthly free session past six cycles.** D9 §-final already records `Documented comparable-charity playbook | NOT FOUND` — if that gap were filled, it would be decisive either way.
- **A cycle-1 attendance number.** If session 1 draws two people, the capacity question is moot and the problem is demand. If it draws sixty, my safeguarding and follow-up findings get sharply more urgent, not less.

PROPOSED MFC LEARNING

**Proposed lesson: a rejected recurring commitment returning under a new name must be re-tested against the ground it was rejected on.**

D9 rejected webinars on capacity grounds. The same object has returned as "free monthly Zoom session" with the capacity argument neither addressed nor contradicted — not through anyone's bad faith, and the strategic intent genuinely did change, which is a legitimate reason to reopen. The defect is that nothing in the system *required* the reopening to engage the original ground. The rejection was recorded as a table row and bound to no check.

This is `L-025`'s pattern once more (a control that is never invoked fails exactly as hard as one that does not exist), with a specific twist worth naming separately: **the rename is what defeats retrieval.** A grep for "webinar" finds D9; nobody greps for "webinar" when the packet says "free monthly session."

Binding control I would propose, kept deliberately small so it does not become the compliance overhead `L-064` warns against: **any proposal that creates a recurring, externally-advertised commitment must state (a) sustaining hours per month, (b) a named owner, and (c) whether a prior record rejected a similar commitment — before adoption.** Three lines. It is not a governance layer; it is a field on a form, and it would have caught this one.

Whether that is worth a numbered lesson under `L-008` is a call for the routing/learning owner, not mine. I flag the cause rather than assume the remedy.
