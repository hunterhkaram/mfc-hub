## 1. Purpose, scope and how to use this pack

This pack collects MFC's language authority for website copy in one place: what is locked and must be reproduced exactly, what is approved and usable with care, what is provisional, and what is genuinely unresolved. It exists so a drafter or reviewer does not have to re-derive wording judgements from first principles on every page.

**What this is not.** It approves nothing: nothing moves from provisional to approved, or from awaiting-committee to confirmed, by appearing here. It changes no MFC authority and adjudicates no open conflict (C-01, C-13, C-14, C-20, C-22, C-23 and others named below); where the corpus has not settled something, this pack says so and names who must. It does not design pages, write page copy or propose user experience; illustrative wording is always labelled under Section 4's status system, never presented as approved. It does not touch Wix or any Drive or repository file outside itself.

**How to use it.** Before drafting or reviewing a page, check Section 3 for every MFC term the page uses. Before publishing a claim, check it against Section 5's claims boundary and the mental-health claim ladder. Before sign-off, run Section 7's category integrity tests over the whole draft, not only sentences that quote MFC terms directly, since drift usually enters through paraphrase. Where Section 8 records a decision as genuinely open, do not resolve it by picking the version that reads best; escalate to the named decision-maker. Where Section 2's hierarchy does not resolve an apparent disagreement (three such cases are named as control-state warnings), treat the passage as unsettled.

**Provenance.** Drawn from the orchestrator-retrieved `AUTHORITY-INPUTS.md` (retrieved 31 July 2026 from Drive, the MFC repository kernel, and a read-only Wix inspection) and, for added precision, direct reads of `00-foundations/K-02-vision.md`, `K-03-mission.md`, `K-04-public-definition.md`, `K-06-retired-language.md`, `K-07-framework-architecture.md`, `K-08-claims-red-lines.md`, and `60-projects/founder-decision-rationale/32-website-verified-baseline.md` and `42b-website-technical-appendix-v3.md`. Nothing here was re-retrieved independently or contradicts those sources. Quotations are exact; summaries are marked as such.

## 3. The core deliverable, term-by-term language bank

Forty entries, in the brief's order. Each carries the same fields. Where a field does not apply, that is stated rather than left blank.

### 3.1 Mental Fitness Collective
- Term: Mental Fitness Collective, or MFC. Proper noun, always capitalised.
- Source/status: organisational identity, uncontested.
- Meaning: the organisation stewarding the mental fitness category, the Framework and this website.
- Locked wording: none at passage level; the name itself is fixed.
- Public line: "MFC" after first use as "Mental Fitness Collective (MFC)."
- Evidence-facing: same.
- Paraphrase: no, for the name; surrounding description varies by audience.
- Prohibited drift: presenting MFC as sole owner rather than steward. Hunter, 27 July: "a person should be able to go to our page and go, well, I want to understand this more" is invitation, not gatekeeping.
- Claims boundary: do not claim MFC invented mental fitness; its claim is a definition, an educational structure and a practical pathway (`HOME-06-CONTRIBUTION`).

### 3.2 Mission
- Term: "mission," lowercase in running text, capitalised only at a sentence start or as a heading.
- Source/status: rank 1, locked as `HOME-01-BODY-01`. Kernel mirror K-03, provisional. See warning 3, C-14.
- Meaning: what MFC exists to do.
- Locked wording: "Our mission is to help people understand and strengthen their mental fitness through education and lifelong practice."
- Public line: the locked sentence is already public level.
- Evidence-facing: same; none separate stated.
- Paraphrase: no for the locked instance; elsewhere, only if "education" and "lifelong practice" are both retained.
- Prohibited drift: drafting from a Strategic Direction file (two superseded formulations exist, warning 3); presenting it as a heading styled "Our Mission Is."
- Claims boundary: a purpose statement, not an outcome guarantee; do not follow it with an implied promise.

### 3.3 Vision
- Term: "vision," lowercase in running text.
- Source/status: rank 1, locked as `HOME-01-H1`. Kernel mirror K-02, provisional. See warning 2, C-01.
- Meaning: the world state MFC works toward.
- Locked wording: "A world where building mental fitness is a normal part of life for everyone."
- Public line: as above; no shorter approved form.
- Evidence-facing: same.
- Paraphrase: no for the locked instance; elsewhere only with "for everyone" retained.
- Prohibited drift: the competing wording without "for everyone" is the outlier form and must not appear on the site.
- Claims boundary: aspirational; do not let copy imply population-level normalisation has already happened, or commit to a timeline.

### 3.4 Mental fitness
- Term: "mental fitness," lowercase always, generic term.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2. Public definition locked 20 July 2026; evidence-facing definition in K-04, awaiting committee.
- Meaning: a developable human capacity reflected in how a person navigates everyday moments, not a tool, activity, mood or judgement of worth.
- Locked wording: "Mental fitness is how well we navigate life's everyday moments." Expansion: "It shows up in how we think, work with what we feel, act on what matters, and connect with ourselves and others."
- Public line: the locked definition and expansion together.
- Evidence-facing: "MFC conceptualises mental fitness as a developable capacity to access and coordinate relevant human skills across changing situations: to think flexibly, recognise and work skilfully with internal states, direct attention and action towards what matters, and relate constructively to ourselves and others." Both forms describe "the same construct at different levels of resolution," not competing definitions.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints generally; no for the locked instance.
- Prohibited drift: the retired "how well we feel" must never define it again; K-06 records this as a retirement, not a style choice. Also prohibited: "use mental fitness" where the meaning is "strengthen" or "build" it, see 3.23.
- Claims boundary: not a fixed identity, a judgement of worth or a promise of control; access "changes with health, state, previous learning, habits, relationships, support, environment, power, safety, and the demands of the situation" (K-04). Not identical to wellbeing; not a diagnostic construct.
- Unresolved: whether the evidence-facing definition may appear in committee papers, Section 8.

### 3.5 Mental health
- Term: "mental health," lowercase always.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, via the distinction (K-05, locked).
- Meaning: on the website, defined only relationally through the distinction; no standalone clinical definition is authored here.
- Locked wording: none standalone; appears only within the distinction (3.6) and claims (Section 5).
- Public line: "Mental health is how we are doing," never used alone without its pair.
- Evidence-facing: same; K-04 labels the distinction "an educational distinction, not a complete clinical definition."
- Paraphrase: no for the half-sentence; the site should not attempt a fuller clinical definition at all.
- Prohibited drift: collapsing mental health and mental fitness into synonyms; describing mental fitness content as diagnosing, treating or assessing mental health.
- Claims boundary: never claim treatment, prevention, cure, diagnosis or symptom reduction of a mental health condition.

### 3.6 The mental-health/mental-fitness distinction
- Term: no fixed short name, "the distinction," lowercase.
- Source/status: rank 1, locked, used verbatim at `HOME-04-DISTINCTION`.
- Meaning: related but distinct constructs, one describing state, the other capacity.
- Locked wording: "Mental health and mental fitness are related, but they are not the same." "Mental health is how we are doing. Mental fitness is what we are able to do."
- Public line: the two locked sentences together, not shortened to one, since "related, not the same" prevents readers hearing synonyms.
- Evidence-facing: same, with "an educational distinction, not a complete clinical definition" carried alongside it.
- Paraphrase: no for the two sentences; yes with constraints for material built on them, provided direction is not reversed.
- Prohibited drift: swapping the two halves; dropping "related" or dropping "not the same."
- Claims boundary: do not extend into a claim that mental fitness work has no bearing on mental health, or conversely guarantees mental-health outcomes; see the claim ladder, Section 5.

### 3.7 The physical-health comparison
- Term: no fixed short name, "the physical-health comparison" or "the fitness analogy," lowercase.
- Source/status: rank 2. Hunter names it directly, 27 July, as his preferred way to teach the definition.
- Meaning: physical health and fitness relate the way mental health and mental fitness relate; the comparison teaches shape, not mechanism.
- Locked wording: "The comparison is explanatory, not literal: the mind is not a muscle, and practice does not solve every problem."
- Public line: introduce the comparison, then move to mental-fitness-specific wording; do not let it run on.
- Evidence-facing: same constraint, made more explicit.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints; the non-literal caveat must travel with it wherever used prominently.
- Prohibited drift: saying or implying the mind is a muscle; implying effort guarantees outcomes.
- Claims boundary: do not imply anyone can achieve any outcome through sufficient effort; this contradicts the K-04 boundary directly.

### 3.8 The four-position relationship model
- Term: "the four-position relationship model," lowercase. Avoid "quadrant" outside internal shorthand, Gate 10 names quadrant labels as always material.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, locked, full table in K-04.
- Meaning: two independent dimensions, doing well or struggling, and relevant skills easier or harder to access, producing four non-fixed positions, not four types of person.
- Locked wording (table): doing well with skills easier to access: "Doing well and drawing on relevant skills." Doing well with skills harder to access: "Doing well while some relevant skills remain harder to access." Struggling with skills easier to access: "Struggling while still drawing on significant skill." Struggling with skills harder to access: "Struggling with fewer skills, supports, or safe options available." Caveat: "These positions are not fixed categories, diagnoses, or person-level scores. A person can move between them across time, situations, and areas of life."
- Public line: present as something that moves, using a concrete example rather than a static grid.
- Evidence-facing: same table.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints, provided both dimensions stay independent and the non-fixed caveat is retained wherever shown.
- Prohibited drift: "skilled" and "unskilled" as axis labels are explicitly prohibited by the Glossary in favour of "relevant skills easier to access" and "relevant skills harder to access"; "mentally fit" and "mentally unfit" as whole-person labels; rendering the model as a quiz result assigning a visitor permanently to one box.
- Claims boundary: not a diagnostic instrument or scored assessment. Live-site Finding H2 notes a rendered version already exists off-lock; any such table must carry the caveat.

### 3.9 Moments
- Term: "moment(s)," lowercase always.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 4, central to `HOME-02`, corroborated as genuine founder language throughout the 27 July meeting.
- Meaning: a specific part of life as it is happening.
- Locked wording: "We do not experience a year, a relationship or a life all at once. We experience conversations, decisions, reactions, pauses, distractions, connections and ordinary moments as they happen." Callout: "The lives we want are shaped through these moments."
- Public line: use concrete, recognisable examples over the abstract definition. Approved founder-generated examples: phone-scrolling, late-night Netflix, saying something regrettable to a friend.
- Evidence-facing: same plain-language device throughout.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints, keeping examples everyday, not clinical or extreme.
- Prohibited drift: coining a capitalised label for "moment" instead of plain phrases such as "one moment that matters." The "snapping at someone in traffic" example is not from the 27 July transcript and should not be attributed to Hunter or Alysse.
- Claims boundary: a unit of everyday experience, not a crisis event; keep illustrative moments away from content needing a safeguarding response.

### 3.10 Five foundational skills, the set
- Term: "five foundational skills," lowercase in running text, capitalised only as a heading.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, locked as the homepage's primary skill language. The lock itself discloses mixed Framework usage of "five public skills" and "foundational skills" as a separate, unreconciled issue.
- Meaning: the five plain-language skills MFC teaches as its primary public architecture, Notice, Shift, Choose, Focus, Connect.
- Locked wording: "MFC focuses on five foundational skills. They work together, and different moments may draw on one or several of them."
- Public line: the five, each with one locked line (3.11 to 3.15); the seven deeper areas are not introduced on general public surfaces.
- Evidence-facing: the seven-skill mapping, see 3.16.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints for the introductory sentence; no for the individual skill lines.
- Prohibited drift: implying strict sequence or that every moment draws on all five; the Decision Gate prohibits implying "every rep must visibly use all five skills or all three cues."
- Claims boundary: not a complete inventory of human function; mastery of all five does not guarantee any outcome.
- Unresolved: FD-04, foundational versus public naming, Section 8.

### 3.11 Notice
- Term: "Notice," capitalised as a named skill.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, locked at `HOME-05-SKILL-01`. **Wording updated `D-0086`/`D-0087`, 2026-08-06.** Verified directly against the Drive-authoritative framework document, which now reads as below; this is a restoration, not a new decision, closing the gap this section's own "prohibited drift" note had already flagged (see next line).
- Meaning: becoming aware of what is happening within us, around us and in our interactions.
- Locked wording: "Notice what is happening inside you, around you, and between you and others." (Previously "Notice what is happening." Dropped the tri-locus; restored to match Self-Awareness's own definition below.)
- Public line: as above, already simplest form.
- Evidence-facing: maps to "Self-Awareness," "Noticing what is happening inside you, around you, and between you and others in real time."
- Paraphrase: no for the locked line; yes for surrounding sentences.
- Prohibited drift: narrowing Notice to feelings only. **Now resolved by the wording itself**, which explicitly spans within, around and interactions rather than only implying it.
- Claims boundary: does not alone resolve a difficult situation; usually paired with other skills in a rep.

### 3.12 Shift
- Term: "Shift," capitalised as a named skill and within Stop, Shift, Do; lowercase "a shift" for a single instance.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, locked at `HOME-05-SKILL-02`. One of the highest drift-risk terms in this pack. **Wording updated `D-0086`/`D-0087`, 2026-08-06.** Verified directly against the Drive-authoritative framework document, closing the gap this section's own "public line" note had already flagged (see next line).
- Meaning: changing something that may help state or situation fit the moment better; "a shift" is one action within Stop, Shift, Do, not the process's name.
- Locked wording: "Create enough space to become workable — through your body, or through your thinking." (Previously stopped after "workable." Named neither door; restored so a reader cannot take Shift as body-only.) A shift: "An intentional change involving the body, attention, perspective, connection or surroundings."
- Public line: as locked. **The wording itself now names both routes**, reducing but not eliminating the need to separately explain the four categories (body, attention, perspective, connection) so readers do not infer breathing alone.
- Evidence-facing: maps to "State Regulation" and "Flexible Thinking."
- Paraphrase: no for the locked line; yes with constraints elsewhere, without reducing the four tool categories to one.
- Prohibited drift, highest risk in the pack: "calm" is not the goal of Shift, K-06 verbatim, "Workable is not always calm." Breath is not THE Shift strategy, K-06 verbatim, "Breath is one Shift option, not the Shift strategy... it should never be required." No Shift technique may ever be made mandatory (safety rule 6).
- Claims boundary: a shift changes access to state or perspective, not the external situation; no outcome guarantee.

### 3.17 Mental Rep
- Term: "a mental rep" / "mental reps," lowercase. Note: this pack's brief capitalised it, but every locked and approved source uses it lowercase; treat lowercase as controlling.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, locked at `HOME-05-REP-DEFINITION`, identical in Glossary and K-07.
- Meaning: catching a moment and choosing your next move, on purpose, toward what matters, regardless of timing or outcome.
- Locked wording: "A mental rep is catching a moment and choosing your next move, on purpose, toward what matters. You may catch the moment before it happens, while it is happening, or afterwards. The outcome does not determine whether it counts." Repetition line: "Repeated mental reps can make the skills, the Stop, Shift, Do process and possible responses more familiar over time."
- Public line: as locked.
- Evidence-facing: what does not count, K-07, "A moment by itself, a healthy habit done entirely on autopilot, or reading about mental fitness without applying anything in a moment."
- Paraphrase: no for the locked definition; the outcome clause must never be dropped.
- Prohibited drift: implying every rep visibly uses all five skills or all three cues; any streak or quota framing, which contradicts outcome-independence directly. An open divergence exists elsewhere ("more intention" versus locked "with intention," C-20); do not import it into website copy.
- Claims boundary: no claim of a specific result from a specific number of reps; no "rewires the brain" language, use Section 5's substitution instead.

### 3.18 Stop, Shift, Do
- Term: "Stop, Shift, Do," all three words capitalised.
- Source/status: rank 1 to 2, locked at `HOME-05-SSD-INTRO` and `HOME-05-SSD`.
- Meaning: a simple, repeatable recall process for using relevant skills while life is happening, without a long sequence.
- Locked wording: "Stop, Shift, Do gives us a simple recall and application process. Instead of having to search through a long list of skills in the moment, it helps us retrieve and use what is relevant while life is happening." Steps: "Stop: Catch the moment. Shift: Create enough space. Do: Make the next move with intention."
- Public line: as locked.
- Evidence-facing: K-07 gives Shift a fuller qualifier, "create enough space where useful and available," since Shift is not mandatory every pass.
- Paraphrase: no for the three step-lines; yes for framing around them.
- Prohibited drift, flagged not corrected: the Glossary's own entry currently reads "Do means choose the next move with more intention," not matching the locked "with intention," no "more." Draft new copy from the locked line, not the Glossary paraphrase. Also prohibited, verbatim from the Decision Gate: "do not describe Stop, Shift, Do as only a practice tool for future performance." Named misunderstandings: Stop is not freezing; Shift is not always calming down; Do is not the perfect action; it is not another set of skills or the whole framework.
- Claims boundary: does not guarantee a good outcome from any moment.

### 3.19 State Check
- Term: "State Check," both words capitalised.
- Source/status: rank 2, defined in K-07 and the Glossary; no permanent homepage ID yet.
- Meaning: a simple, optional reflection on whether current state fits the moment.
- Locked wording: two current, approved phrasings: "A simple reflection: What state am I in, and does this state work for this moment?" and "Is my state too much, too little, or workable for this moment?" "For this moment" is the fixed anchor in both.
- Public line: too much, workable, too little, never a numbered scale.
- Evidence-facing: same; no separate clinical version exists or should exist.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints, keeping the anchor phrase and the three-way framing.
- Prohibited drift: State Check must never be a fourth Stop, Shift, Do step, a mandatory ritual, a Mood Meter replacement, an emotional-labelling task, or a numbered scale, all named directly in K-06.
- Claims boundary: never scored, mandatory, public or performance-based, safety rule 7.

### 3.20 Workable
- Term: "workable," lowercase always.
- Source/status: rank 2, Glossary and K-07's State Check table.
- Meaning: a state or situation with enough energy, steadiness or clarity for what the moment requires.
- Locked wording: "A state or situation that gives us enough energy, steadiness or clarity for what the moment requires. Workable does not mean perfectly calm, comfortable or in the middle."
- Public line: as locked.
- Evidence-facing: same.
- Paraphrase: yes with constraints, keeping the "not perfectly calm" caveat.
- Prohibited drift: equating workable with calm, one of the two highest-risk substitutions in this pack; "Workable is not always calm" (K-06).
- Claims boundary: not a required precondition before a person may act; a lens, not a gate.

### 3.21 Radar

## 4. The language-status system

House style fixes six labels. Every significant passage in a draft should carry exactly one.

**EXACT LOCKED WORDING.** Carries a permanent ID and a stated character-for-character rule; only Gate 10 can alter it. Example: `HOME-01-H1`, "A world where building mental fitness is a normal part of life for everyone." The control rule: "the words may not be rewritten, shortened or replaced without a recorded copy decision."

**APPROVED WORDING.** A sanctioned Framework, Glossary or kernel formulation, correct and safe, without a per-page lock. Example: the Glossary's Shift definition, "The skill of changing something that may help our state or situation fit the moment better," usable wherever Shift needs defining.

**APPROVED MEANING, OPTIMISED EXPRESSION.** A fresh form of an already-approved meaning, explicitly labelled, never used as if already approved. Example: the three items in Section 3's proposed optimised expressions, each with its meaning impact and change control step stated.

**EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED PROPOSAL.** A recommendation resting on named evidence, offered to a decision-maker, not decided. Example: FD-07's recommendation to move the Human Operating System off general surfaces, resting on the stated finding that disclaimers only partially reduce mistaken literal belief in lay audiences.

**PLACEHOLDER PENDING DECISION.** Copy depends on a live, unresolved decision with no safe default. Example: the Radar's entry count, FD-01, marked "DO NOT SETTLE. Design so the count can change without redesigning the experience."

**CONTENT REQUIRED FROM MFC.** A named gap; do not invent detail to fill it. Example: the individual definitions of Learn It, Use It and Live It, which K-07 states were "not elaborated in the sections read."

**Choosing between them.** Does the passage carry a permanent ID or exact-reproduction rule? Use EXACT LOCKED WORDING. Is it a sanctioned formulation without a per-instance lock? APPROVED WORDING. Rewording something approved, meaning unchanged? APPROVED MEANING, OPTIMISED EXPRESSION, always stating meaning impact and change control. Recommending on evidence MFC has not adopted? EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED PROPOSAL, naming the evidence. Depends on an undecided live question? PLACEHOLDER PENDING DECISION, written to survive either answer. No source material exists? CONTENT REQUIRED FROM MFC, naming the gap and who must supply it. Never leave a significant passage unlabelled; never assign a higher-certainty label than it has earned.

## 5. The claims boundary set, as a website-usable instrument

This turns K-08's claim table and safety rules into four practical instruments. Per warning 1, none of this is board-approved; it is MFC's own stated design position, real for drafting purposes but a lower tier than a ratified red line.

### 5.1 What any page may say
The framework is evidence-informed and evidence-aligned. Mental fitness can be strengthened through practice, support, repetition and time. The five public skills simplify seven deeper foundational human skills. Mental fitness complements professional care. Access to skills is shaped by context, safety, support, body, brain and conditions.

### 5.2 What requires qualification, and the exact language to use

| Statement type | Required qualifying language |
|---|---|
| Mental health or functioning benefit | "may support how you function in moments," not "will fix" |
| Repeated practice changing patterns | "may make useful patterns more familiar and easier to access over time," not "guarantees" or "rewires" |
| Skills connecting to external research | "connect to established evidence families," not "clinically validated" |
| Alongside professional care | "some people may need more support, adaptation, or clinical care," paired with the locked care-boundary sentence |
| Ability to shift or access a skill | "may strengthen access where safe and appropriate," never an unqualified "you can always" |
| Connection, vulnerability, reconnecting | "where safe and appropriate," naming the actual action |
| The Framework's evidence status overall | "the complete MFC synthesis has not yet been independently validated," not implied whole-framework validation |
| State Check or a state lens | anchored to "for this moment," never scored |

### 5.3 What no page may say
That the framework treats, prevents or cures mental illness; that mental reps guarantee brain rewiring or guaranteed outcomes; that MFC's exact model is clinically validated as a standalone protocol unless tested; that mental fitness replaces therapy, counselling, psychiatry, medication or crisis support; that anyone can shift any moment if they try hard enough. Commercial-context extension, binding on Workshops and similar copy: no claim that one engagement reduces absenteeism or attrition, prevents burnout, improves productivity, reduces workers' compensation claims, treats clinical distress, manages all psychosocial hazards, or transforms workplace culture.

## 7. Category integrity tests

Run over a complete draft page, not only sentences quoting MFC terms directly. Drift enters mainly through paraphrase and worked examples.

**Generic-wellbeing drift.** (1) Search for "how well we feel" or close paraphrases used to define mental fitness. Fail if mental fitness is defined by mood rather than navigating moments. (2) Search for "wellbeing" used as a synonym for "mental fitness." Fail if used interchangeably anywhere. (3) Check whether "mental fitness" is used as a general positive adjective ("a mentally fit team") rather than a developable capacity being practised. Fail if found. (4) Check any "what mental fitness helps with" list for scope creep toward "everything good in life." Fail if the term is stretched beyond recognisable, moment-level examples.

**Clinical-implication drift.** (5) Search for treats, prevents, cures, diagnoses, or reduces symptoms of, applied to mental fitness, the Framework or a programme. Fail if any appear. (6) Check whether the care-boundary sentence, or a link to it, appears on any page discussing distress or difficulty. Fail if absent. (7) Check any Human Operating System content for language read as literal brain anatomy or a diagnostic tool. Fail if the components read as structures rather than metaphors. (8) Check whether State Check is mandatory, scored, numbered, public or performance-framed. Fail if any apply. (9) Check reconnection, disclosure or repair-adjacent content for "where safe and appropriate" and a named action. Fail if missing, or if "repair" appears unexplained as a public label. (10) Check whether any page implies a working referral or crisis pathway exists before the urgent-support utility is actually built and live. Fail if implied.

**Category-dilution drift.** (11) Check whether mental fitness is presented as MFC's exclusive property rather than a stewarded category. Fail if copy implies no one else may define or use the term. (12) Check whether the five skills are presented as a complete account of psychological life rather than MFC's teaching architecture. Fail if implied. (13) Check rep or moment examples for "always" or "every time" language around the five skills or three cues. Fail if found. (14) Check the physical-health comparison for literal physiological claims. Fail if found. (15) Check evidence language for citation volume implying MFC's complete framework, not its underlying principles, has been validated. Fail if a reader could reasonably conclude MFC's own model is proven rather than its principles well evidenced. (16) Search for "skilled/unskilled" or "mentally fit/unfit" applied to a person, and check any quiz-like feature for a permanent typing into one of the four positions. Fail if either appears.
