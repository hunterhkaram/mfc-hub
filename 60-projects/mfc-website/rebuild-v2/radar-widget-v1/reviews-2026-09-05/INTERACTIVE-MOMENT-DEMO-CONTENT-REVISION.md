# The Radar demo: six example moments

**Status:** converged content, corrected 2026-09-05 after Hunter caught a real error. Not
published, not logged into the Moment Bank. Two genuine Hunter questions at the end, both about
supply of real moments, not about design.

**Correction, 2026-09-05.** The first version of field 7 used K-07's old locked Shift names
(Move it / Zoom in or out / Ask a different question / Bring someone in) and merged "attention"
and "thinking" into one route. Hunter pointed at the wrong source: those names were superseded on
the live website itself, on his own instruction 2026-08-25 ("Please fix the shift tools!"). The
actual copy lives in `60-projects/mfc-website/rebuild-v2/mfc-site.html:1642-1699` — four separate
categories, **Through your body / Through your attention / Through your thinking / With other
people**, each written as concrete, example-rich prose ("A few that tend to work:..."), with the
category names themselves never shown as jargon labels (the site build note records that framing
being deliberately deleted as "internal design vocabulary"). Field 7 below is rewritten against
that source for all six scenes. This also means K-07 itself is stale against what is actually
shipping and awaiting Hunter's read-and-approve before a decision record locks the rename in.

**Correction, 2026-09-06.** Three further changes, all at Hunter's direction: (1) scene setups for
Scenes 1, 2 and 5 were too staged/cinematic to feel like a moment a reader recognises in themselves;
simplified to plain, direct address. (2) Scene 4 was rebuilt end to end — "the driver who didn't
wave" is too small a spark to plausibly still be running twenty minutes later; it is now a near-miss
cut-off, which earns the replay honestly. (3) The outcome mechanic changed from a re-run (same move,
different landing, built to avoid any hint of causation) to a Caught/Not-caught split that shows the
moment play out both ways, including an uncaught moment turning up again later in a different part
of the day. This sits closer to the AG-001/AG-008 causation line than the old design and is guarded
accordingly: see "The outcome: caught / not caught, not a re-run" below for the four-point rule that
keeps it honest, including that not every Caught card resolves cleanly and not every Not-caught card
ripples.

## SHORT VERDICT

**What is strong.** The six territories are the right six: they are broad, ordinary, and none of
them is a crisis. Two of them (walking in the door, one more episode) come straight from Hunter's
own verbatim bank entries, which is why they are the two strongest sentences in the set. The
Radar to optional Shift to possible next move spine holds all the way through without a rival
framework creeping in. And the flow now genuinely refuses to score anyone.

**What needed correction, and did not survive review.**

1. **The outcome screen was the whole risk and it was empty.** The draft recorded "show the outcome
   on both paths" as the fix for the causation problem and then wrote no outcome copy at all. It is
   written now, eighteen strings, and the re-run mechanic was inverted: it holds the move constant
   and varies the landing, because varying the *choice* and showing two endings is the textbook way
   to *demonstrate* causation, not disconfirm it.
2. **Every structural protection lived in a field the visitor never sees.** Disability, caring load,
   unsafe homes, night shifts, no ten minutes available: all correctly written, all in the
   authoring-only "must not be implied" field. They have been promoted into shipping copy.
3. **Breath had quietly become the Shift strategy.** It appeared in four of six body options with
   the "optional" carve-out on only one. Cut to one.
4. **Twenty-four hedge clauses.** Every Shift option ended by telling the visitor it probably would
   not work. That is Gina's caution-repeated-throughout failure (AG-028) at maximum volume, at the
   point of maximum interest. Deleted, replaced by one licensed line, once.
5. **Batch tells at four levels.** The moment sentences ran the same contrast formula 6/6 against a
   threshold of 4. So did the "what this moment is" lines, the scene setups, and the thought
   fragments (the resigned turn landed third in all six). Broken.
6. **A consolation prize.** "The moment was still caught" appeared only on the do-nothing option, in
   two of six scenes, which ranks the three moves and reads as a pity line. It is now one
   flow-level sentence above all three moves, applying equally.
7. **Screen count.** Seven screens down to five, six states. Not four: four measures at ~130 words
   and three controls on the last screen, roughly 2.5 mobile viewports, and does not fit.

**One thing stated straight rather than buried:** all six moments are solo and domestic, and none is
a win. That breaches a written boundary in MFC's own moment-writing standard ("do not make every
positive direction a problem"). The six territories are Hunter's, so none has been swapped out. It
is the first of the two questions at the bottom.

## THE SOURCE CONFLICT, RESOLVED

MOMENT-WRITING-STANDARD-001 says moments begin with "When / Before / After / As", 8 to 22 words.
The `moment-drafting` empirical analysis of the fifteen confirmed-real entries says
present-participle opening dominant, "you" never the opening subject, 5 to 25 words.

**Ruling: the empirical analysis governs these six. No supersession marker on either document.**

Not on authority, on scope. The two documents govern different objects. MOMENT-WRITING-STANDARD-001
governs the widget's **608 starter moments** in a pick-list, where parallel "When..." openers exist
to make a long list fast to scan. The empirical analysis governs **Moment Bank entries** met one at
a time and meant to land as a memory. These six are the second kind, and two of them are derived
from bank entries directly.

Reading it as "the empirical analysis beats the standard, full stop" would license rewriting 608
starter moments into present participles, which would be expensive and would make the pick-list
harder to scan. The ruling is bounded to the six.

**Action, not a question:** both files need a one-line scope statement, which neither currently has.
Neither is superseded. Separately, the live widget instructs visitors on screen to write their own
moments as "When I...", so the demo and the builder three clicks later teach different sentence
shapes. That is a real seam and it resolves the same way: the demo's six are recognition artefacts,
the builder's instruction governs what a person writes themselves.

Hunter's 2026-08-27 correction (a moment may state a want and the passive reality that resulted, but
must never narrate a deliberately executed coping action) binds either way, and all six sentences
pass it. The skeptic checked specifically and found none of them narrates an executed coping move.

### SCENE 2 — The unclear message

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Direction** | Handle messages that could be read more than one way. |
| **2. Radar moment** | "Three reads in, and the tone of a short message still won't settle." |
| **3. Why it is recognisable** | The cue is the re-read, not the message. |
| **4. Scene setup** | You've read the message three times now, and you still can't tell what they meant by it. |
| **5. Three thought fragments** | "Can't tell what that one's doing." / "They type like that when they're flat out." / "The reply's half-written already." |
| **6. What this moment is** | The gap where a short message hasn't landed one way or the other. |
| **7. Optional Shift options** | **Through your body:** put the phone face down and stand up for a second. · **Through your attention:** back to the cup in your hand, the room, the noise behind you. · **Through your thinking:** both readings are still available. Neither has been confirmed. · **With other people:** read it to someone you trust and see what they hear. |
| **8. Three possible next moves** | (a) Leave it. The reading stays open and the message will still be there. (b) Answer the part you're sure about, and leave the tone alone. (c) Ask plainly: "How did you mean this?" |
| **9. What must not be implied** | That the sender's intent is knowable from the text. That re-reading is a symptom, a trait, or evidence of anxiety. That a message that reads badly is one reader's problem to manage rather than something two people can sort out. |

**Ships:** Through your thinking, With other people. **Profile:** `connection` (Connect + Notice).

**Five tests:** 1 pass · 2 pass · 3 pass · 4 pass · 5 pass, conditional on the build rule below.

**Unknown intent, specifically.** The first draft preserved it about the sender and abandoned it
about the reader, three times over: the direction presupposed blank-filling, the cold coffee was
visual shorthand for rumination, and the "what this moment is" line delivered a verdict on the
reader's ratio of inference to evidence. All three are gone. Nothing nudges toward the sender being
cold either. The symmetric line, "both readings are still available, neither has been confirmed",
is the cleanest sentence in the set and should not be trimmed later.

**Build rule, and it is not optional.** **Never show the message body.** Any wording shown becomes a
claim about tone that the visitor then judges, and half will read it as obviously fine, which kills
the scene. Render it as an unreadable grey bubble and depict the *re-read*: thumb scrolls up, bubble
returns to the top, twice, with a beat between. A message bubble inside a depicted phone at 375px is
illegible at any acceptable type size, so this is also the only version that works on a phone. The
repetition is the whole moment and it is fully depictable wordless.

### SCENE 4 — Cut off *(reworked 2026-09-06, at Hunter's direction)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Direction** | Let a jolt like that pass instead of carrying it. |
| **2. Radar moment** | "Someone cutting in without warning, and the sharp jolt of it still running twenty minutes later." |
| **3. Why it is recognisable** | The body reacts before the moment is even understood; the replay starts after the danger has already passed. |
| **4. Scene setup** | A car cuts in without indicating and you brake hard. Nothing happens. Twenty minutes later, hands still a bit tight on the wheel, still running it back. |
| **5. Three thought fragments** | "Could've hit them." / "Didn't even see me." / "Still feel that in my chest." |
| **6. What this moment is** | The near-miss is over. The body hasn't caught up yet. |
| **7. Optional Shift options** | **Through your body:** unclench your hands on the wheel, roll your shoulders once. · **Through your attention:** the road in front of you now, not the one behind. · **Through your thinking:** they probably didn't see you, not deliberate. · **With other people:** mention it to whoever's in the car, or text someone when you're stopped. |
| **8. Three possible next moves** | (a) Say it out loud once, to the car or to someone later, and leave it there. (b) Get where you're going and start the next thing. It may follow you in; it may not. (c) Stay rattled for a bit. A near-miss is a real jolt and doesn't need fixing on a timetable. |
| **9. What must not be implied** | That the other driver was reckless, careless or deliberate. That being shaken by a near-miss is a fault to be corrected. That the moment must resolve before doing anything else. |

**Ships:** Through your attention, Through your thinking. **Plus a third state on this scene only:**
*"Some moments don't need any of this. This might be one."* This is the only place in the set that
shows a Shift is not always called for, and it is why the demo does not teach that every moment
needs one.

**Why this changed.** The prior version, a driver who didn't wave, was too small a spark to carry a
scene about something replaying twenty minutes on: an ordinary person is more likely to shrug that
off in the moment it happens than still be turning it over at the car park. A near-miss earns the
replay honestly, without needing rudeness or intent from anyone. It also gives the caught/not-caught
split below something real to work with, since a jolt that's still live in the body is exactly the
kind of moment that plausibly colours what comes right after it.

**Profile:** `focus` (Focus + Notice). The direction is about how long the jolt persists, which is
attentional return, not intensity.

**Five tests:** 1 pass · 2 pass · 3 pass · 4 pass, (c) legitimises staying rattled with no
consolation attached · 5 pass, conditional on the build rule below.

**Build rule.** Depict the near-miss itself briefly (a car entering frame too close, a hard brake),
then cut to stopped and stationary, hands on the wheel a beat too long. The cut from moving to still
is the moment; an exterior wide shot loses the hands. Interior-tight at 375px.

## THE FLOW

**Five screens, six states.** Four was tried and measured; screen 4 came to roughly 130 words and
three controls, about 2.5 mobile viewports, and could not be trimmed into one.

| # | Screen | What it does | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Choose** | Lead line, six tiles: image crop plus a 3 to 5 word situation label | <=36 |
| 2 | **The scene** *(2 states)* | Setup and three thought fragments arriving in sequence, then the frame freezes and the moment is named **on the image** | <=55 on screen at once |
| 3 | **What now** | Three equal tiles, no visual hierarchy. Shift collapsed beside them. | <=86 |
| 4 | **The outcome** | Caught card, then Not-caught card, side by side or swipe-between, then the fixed hedge lines | <=90, revised 2026-09-06 (word count not yet re-measured against build) |
| 5 | **Your turn** | Transfer prompt, one CTA, the disclaimer once in footer type | <=90 |

**Screens removed and why.** The old "this is now on the Radar" screen came *before* the scene,
which spent the recognition payoff before there was anything to recognise, and set up the causal
chain in advance. The old separate "this is the moment" card was the same beat a second time. Naming
the moment *on the frozen frame* is what recognition means; a separate card turns it into a caption.
The old summary screen was a recap of a story that ended well, which is a moral, and it generalised
a single fictional instance by the act of summarising it.

**Six tile labels.** Shoes on, still not started · A message you keep re-reading · Home, and still at
work · Still annoyed, engine off · Sat down, picked up the phone · One more episode, again.

Lead line: **"Six ordinary ones. Pick whichever is closest."** No label names a direction, a state or
a fix. None contains an adjective about the person. That matters: it makes selection recognition
rather than self-diagnosis.

**The Shift is not a screen.** It is collapsed beside the three moves, closed by default: **"There's
a Shift option here too, if you want it."** A dedicated Shift screen between the moment and the
moves makes the Shift the road and skipping it the exit ramp, and "straight to next move" framed
not-shifting as the shortcut. Two options ship per scene, not four, and no two scenes carry the same
pair in the same order.

One line, once, below the options: **"It's a capacity, not perfection. You will miss moments."**
That replaces twenty-four separate hedges.

**Above the three moves, in every scene:** *"You already caught the moment. What comes next is a
separate decision, and these are three of them."*

**The outcome: caught / not caught, not a re-run.** *(Redesigned 2026-09-06, at Hunter's direction:
"maybe there is a way where we show the moments and how it plays out if we intervene, and if we
don't", including how an uncaught moment can carry into something else later in the day.) This
replaces the previous re-run mechanic (same move, different landing), which answered a narrower
question than the one Hunter actually asked for.*

Screen 4 becomes two linked cards instead of a re-run.

**Card A, Caught.** What happens right after the move chosen on screen 3. One line, tied to that
specific move. Worked example, Scene 4 (cut off), having chosen "mention it to whoever's in the
car": *"You say it out loud. Someone says 'close one', and by the next set of lights it's mostly
gone."*

**Card B, Not caught.** The same moment, but the jolt goes unremarked rather than moved. Then, one
line showing it turning up again later, in a different, unrelated moment: the ripple Hunter asked
for. Worked example, same scene: *"You don't say anything. An hour later someone taps their horn at
a green light, half a second late, and you're angrier at them than it deserves."*

**Why this sits closer to a causal claim than the old design did, and how it stays honest anyway.**
Showing that catching a moment plausibly changes what happens next is nearer the AG-001/AG-008 line
than the previous "same move, different landing" construction, which existed specifically to avoid
implying causation. Four things hold the line:

1. Both cards keep the same fixed hedge, worded to fit the new construction: *"This is one way it
   could go, not the only way and not a guarantee."* and *"Noticing didn't switch anything off. It
   meant there was a choice available where there might not have felt like one."* The subject of
   the second line is **noticing**, never the Radar, never MFC, and the claim is about a choice
   existing, not about an outcome being produced.
2. **Parity, both directions.** Not every Caught card resolves cleanly and not every Not-caught card
   ripples. At least one scene's Caught card still carries some residue (Scene 6's "you let it play,
   and it's still a late night, just a chosen one" is exactly this), and at least one scene's
   Not-caught card has **no** ripple (Scene 5: nothing dramatic follows from checking the phone once;
   most days it's simply a slightly shorter sit). Without this, the pair reads as a morality tale
   with a right answer, which breaches the moment-writing standard's own boundary against implying
   there is a correct response.
3. **The ripple must land as ordinary, not as harm.** Scene 4's example, snapping slightly at an
   unrelated driver, is the right size: recognisable, mildly costly, not catastrophic. A ripple that
   reads as a damaged relationship or a lost job would be fear marketing, breaches the brand's
   non-preachy register, and risks an implicit negative-outcome claim (that *not* using this causes
   harm) with exactly the evidentiary weight problem AG-008 names for the positive direction.
4. **Single fictional instance, not a population claim.** The cards describe what happens to this
   character on this occasion. Nothing generalises it to "people who do this" or "research shows."

Scene 3 keeps its own honest exception rather than forcing it into this shape: the moment where **no
move was available at all**, and the rep counted anyway: *"There was no gap. The door opened onto
someone already talking... There was nothing available to do with it. Noticing was the part that was
yours."* That case has no meaningful Caught/Not-caught split, since nothing was chosen either way.

**The other five, written against the same parity rule.**

| Scene | Caught | Not caught |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Exercise | Chose (a), start now: "You start, and five minutes in it's easier than it looked from the porch." | No decision either way. "By evening you're telling yourself you'll definitely go tomorrow, the same thing you told yourself yesterday." |

Scene 6's Caught card is deliberately not a clean win (parity point 2: an outcome can be chosen and
still cost something), and Scene 5's Not-caught card is deliberately not a loss (parity point 2 in
the other direction: an uncaught moment can simply be nothing). Between them the two parity
exceptions promised above are now both written, not just asserted.

**The transfer step, which the first draft did not have anywhere.** Picking a move for a fictional
situation is comprehension. Naming your own is the capability. Screen 5: *"There'll be a moment like

No "you" in that sentence, deliberately: "you" means the person inside the moment everywhere else in
the widget, and switching it to mean the visitor on the last screen breaks the grammar and turns an
observation into an instruction.

## CROSS-SCENE DUPLICATION AUDIT

The honest finding, and it is sharper than the profile tags admit. **The six territories are three
underlying shapes:**

| Shape | Scenes | How each stays distinct |
|---|---|---|
| **An intention that didn't start** | 1 exercise, 5 meditate, 6 bedtime | Distinguished by *what intervenes*: internal reluctance (1), an automatic device reach before any decision is felt (5), an environment that starts the next thing for you (6). Also by duration: 1 is open-ended, 5 is a few minutes, 6 is about ten seconds. And by whether the moment is at the start (1, 5) or mid-flow (6). |
| **Arrival with the previous context still running** | 3 home, 4 car park | Distinguished by *who else is present*: 3 has people about to be affected, 4 has nobody. That is why 3 is `state` (Notice + Shift, then go in) and 4 is `focus` (attentional return with nothing to protect). 3 is a repeating daily threshold; 4 is a one-off residue. |
| **Ambiguity in someone else's words** | 2 message | The only genuinely distinct territory. Keep it. It is also the only scene where a second person has agency. |

**Profile duplication:** `action` on 1 and 5, `state` on 3 and 6. Both are defensible and neither
profile is wrong. `support`, `systems`, `safety` and `default` go unused. `safety` is correctly
omitted (a public demo handing a visitor three next moves on a safety scene is the highest-risk thing
this build could do). `support` overlaps `connection` and six slots cannot carry eight profiles.
**`systems` and `default` are real gaps** and are the substance of question 1 below.

**Batch-level tells, all now under threshold.** Moment sentences: two bare noun-phrase, one
participle-plus-still, three participle-plus-and, down from 6/6 on one formula. Scene setups: the
editorial capper broken in three. Thought fragments: the resigned turn moved off slot three in three
scenes. Field 6: the terse-counterweight formula broken in three.

**Acts no longer duplicated within a scene.** Scene 2 shipped "read it to someone" as both a Shift
option and a next move on the same screen. Scene 3 shipped "ask for ten minutes" twice, with the
structural hedge on one copy and not the other. Both replaced.

## QUESTIONS HUNTER MUST DECIDE

Two. Both are about supply of real moments, which only Hunter holds. Everything else is decided.

**1. All six are solo, domestic, and about friction. Trade one out?**

Two things sit on this. MFC's own moment-writing standard says "do not make every positive direction
a problem: preserve ordinary enjoyment, relief, progress, connection and contribution as cues", and
there are zero wins in the set, when M-03 in the bank is exactly that and Hunter has said wins
deserve the same visible recognition as a Strava post. Separately, not one of the six is at work, and
the buyer is an organisation.

No scene has been swapped, because the six territories are Hunter's and because the standing rule
says no session invents a bank moment from scratch. Recommendation, at **moderate confidence**: keep
all six for launch and add a seventh later rather than losing one now. The set is coherent, the
domestic register is a lower-threat first contact, and a workplace scene without a real captured
moment behind it would be the weakest thing on the page. If Hunter disagrees, the scene to replace
is 5.

**What is needed to fix it now:** one real moment, in Hunter's own words, that is either a win or is
at work. Not a description of one.

**2. M-06, "the running one", is a known-lost bank entry and Scene 1 is where it belongs.**

The vault records it as referenced but never captured, living only in a session that cannot be
retrieved, with a standing instruction not to reconstruct it and to ask Hunter instead. Scene 1's
territory is exercise. If the real running moment says something different from "the stretch between
ready and started", the real one should win.

**What is needed:** either the moment as Hunter actually said it, or "treat it as lost" so it stops
sitting open in the bank.
