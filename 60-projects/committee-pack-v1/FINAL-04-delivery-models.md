# Delivery Models: What MFC Delivers

## Executive Summary

MFC delivers through a ladder of formats — from one hour to four hours — all built around the same core measurement. The measurement is the constant; the format is the variable. In-person delivery is the stated preference and the current focus. Virtual delivery is designed next. Everything else is tested against a single design rule: **any format that cannot run the core measurement is not an MFC offering.**

This document describes what's built today (Foundations and Essentials), what's being built next (the one-hour digital session), and why other approaches don't survive testing.

---

## 1. The Core Design Principle

> The measurement is the constant. The format is the variable. A format that cannot carry the measurement is not an MFC offering.

This protects the evidence base while we build multiple formats. It's what makes a ladder one thing, not a catalogue.

### What "The Constant" Means

Every MFC offering runs this minimum measurement:

- **Baseline (before):** Can you retrieve this skill when you notice you need it? — the primary matched item
- **Then-test (at follow-up):** Rate that same question again, to catch bias in how your awareness changes
- **End of session:** Does practice in the room move it? — a post-session repeat of the primary matched item
- **Matched retrieval (baseline):** A second matched item, also captured at baseline, — to measure whether noticing itself improves
- **Three weeks later:** Does it stick? — the primary matched item at follow-up, plus the then-test above
- **The harm item:** Did this make anything worse?
- **The referral line:** If distress came up, where could you go? — Routing only, no data recorded

**This runs on every format.** No exceptions. No opt-out. It takes ~2.5 minutes per timepoint.

Longer formats (Foundations, Essentials) add:

- In-session protected block with situational judgement, linkage code, and the full measurement-instrument design
- Follow-up at three weeks in depth
- E-items measuring whether the client commitment obligations held

## 2. What's Built Today

### V1 — Foundations Half-Day, In-Person

**What it is:** 4 hours in-person, same location, MFC-delivered to a group typically 15–20 people from the same organisation (working assumption — founder-set operating parameter, not externally sourced; see §7 for the reasoning).

**The method** *(corrected 2026-08-13 — this description had drifted from the actual built sequence; see [[90-meta/controlled-automation-v1/topic-control/canonical-topic-register.yml|workshop-delivery topic record]] for the current canonical build)*: the Human Operating System (three-part model), Stop-Shift-Do, the five skills (Notice, Shift, Choose, Focus, Connect), a State Check, paired/guided practice, a contrast demonstration, one Mental Rep chosen per participant to use after the session, and Radar — run either as an individual reveal, or as a team-aggregate reveal only when an approved, privacy-reviewed team module was actually collected. "Board narrowing," a "full-pass sheet," and a "manager briefing" are not part of the current build and should not be cited elsewhere as current MFC method.

**Measurement:** Full instrument, protected in-session block, baseline, post, three-week follow-up.

**Who delivers:** Hunter, the founder, one-facilitator capacity limit.

**Founder hours:** 11 hours direct (prep, delivery, analysis, report), plus materials/setup (source: the pricing research).

**Loaded cost:** ~$1,228 per session | **Standing price:** $2,500 base + $95/person | **Margin:** 2.18×–3.22× depending on headcount (source: the pricing research)

**Why this works:** This is the full system in the full time. Pair beats work. Reading the room works. The live rep on your own moment actually happens. The evidence of transfer is cleanest here because the environment is intact (you're with your colleagues, you're going back to the same workplace, the manager is in the building).

**What breaks it:** Travel distance >1 hour (the 1.0 hr travel line becomes unrealistic). Organisations without a room. Groups under 6 (compliance risk, consent complexity).

### V2 — Essentials 90-Minute, In-Person or Virtual

**What it is:** 90 minutes, taught in-person or digitally, to a group 6–15 people (working assumption — founder-set operating parameter, not externally sourced; see §7 for the reasoning).

**The method:** Stop-Shift-Do, three public skills (not five), one paired practice, the board narrowing, one mental rep, manager briefing.

**Measurement:** Full instrument, protected in-session block, baseline, post, three-week follow-up.

**Founder hours:** 4.5 hours in-person (or 3.2 hours virtual, reflecting ~1.3 hours of reduced travel/logistics) (source: the pricing research).

**Loaded cost:** ~$500 in-person, ~$324 virtual | **Standing price:** $700–$1,200 in-person (by size band), $1,000 virtual | **Margin:** 1.10×–2.18× depending on band and format (source: the pricing research)

**Why this works:** Shorter entry point for small teams. Still carries the full measurement. Virtual version reaches people who can't release everyone at once or aren't co-located.

**What breaks it:** Groups under 6 (same consent/compliance risk as V1). Organisations without a named manager commitment (the method depends on follow-through).

## 3. What's Being Built Next

### V5 — One-Hour Live Digital Session, Individually Bookable

**What it is:** 60 minutes live, delivered digitally, in MFC-scheduled cohorts of 6–12 strangers **or** to individuals alone. The bottom rung of the ladder — enterable by one person.

**The method:** Stop-Shift-Do, one public skill (retrieval under load), one live rep on the participant's own moment, the mental rep, no manager layer.

**Measurement:** Core measurement only (the constant, no protected block). Baseline, post, three-week follow-up.

**Founder hours:** Estimated 1.5 hours direct per session + ~30 hours build/design (build once, run many times) (estimate — the delivery-models research flags V5 as not yet built, ~20–30 founder hours estimated).

**Loaded cost:** ~$255 per session across 20 seats (source: the lead-generation funnel research) | **Standing price:** $25–35/seat (source: the delivery-models and lead-generation research) | **Why this works:** Reaches individuals who have no organisational sponsor and no 90 minutes to release. Community events, conference hallways, people scrolling LinkedIn. Honest message: education plus a practical tool, not a full workshop. Produces evidence in the form of "reached and measured individuals who used the skill," not "changed an organisation."

**The safeguarding exception:** This is the most safeguarding-exposed configuration in the entire ladder. A participant sits alone online, with no colleague beside them, no manager to escalate to, no employer with duty-of-care structures. This format requires:

- Jurisdiction check at booking (Australia only)
- Named referral pathway shown *before* the session, not after
- Facilitator protocol for a distress disclosure with no employer to escalate to
- A decision about whether MFC collects enough contact detail to act on a disclosure at all (which creates tension with the anonymity the format promises)

**These are preconditions of offering V5** — not of building it. The build can proceed. The individual-booking channel cannot open until the safeguarding annex exists and the safeguarding review's items 1-2 (clinical-governance input on distress response and legal review) have qualified input.

## 4. What Doesn't Survive Testing

### E1 — Asynchronous / Self-Paced Only

**Why rejected:** No baseline that is not self-administered on an unknown day. No protected moment for the harm item to be seen by anyone. No referral line that reaches a human. A format where a distress disclosure lands in an unmonitored form is worse than no format at all.

### E2 — Cue Placement in the Environment Alone

**Why rejected as a standalone offering:** Not a delivery vehicle. It teaches nothing and measures nothing.

**Why retained as a free add-on:** Cues (signage, prompts, nudges where the moment happens) are genuinely well-matched to retrieval under load. Added to every in-person and virtual format at no additional charge.

### E3 — Long Format (Essentials or Foundations) Sold to Individuals

**Why rejected:** Essentials and Foundations both depend on a team that goes back to the same workplace. You cannot design a follow-up commitment for an individual who came to a ticketed event. (The short V5 format works for individuals because it never claims there will be follow-up.)

**Partially reinstated:** V5 (the one-hour digital session) *is* sold to individuals, but the short format that never claims environment support, not a longer one.

## 5. The Five Tests Every Format Faces

| Test | What it means | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **T1: Does the taught procedure survive?** | Stop-Shift-Do, the five public skills, the mental rep survive intact. Most at risk remotely: paired practice, reading the room, the live rep on your own moment | MFC's framework-architecture note |
| **T2: Does the measurement survive?** | The core constant at minimum. Anonymity, linkage code, N ≥ 20 reporting floor must hold | the measurement research |
| **T3: Founder hours per session** | Reported as prominently as dollars, because hours are the binding constraint | the pricing research — Hunter can deliver max twice a day, not every day |
| **T4: Which segment does it serve, and what's its role in the funnel?** | Different entry points bring different organisations in. Assessed as demand mechanism, not just a delivery menu | the demand-generation and free-offer research, founder input |
| **T5: How delegable is it?** | A short, well-specified format is the one someone else can eventually run. This is the first real answer to founder dependency | Founder input |

## 6. In-Person Is the Stated Preference

Hunter, directly: *"we would rather do it in person because that's the best intervention from what we've seen."*

The hedge ("from what we've seen") is his and is correctly placed — it's experience, not evidence. But the evidence on remote skills transfer is actually mixed: meta-analyses don't show remote as categorically worse, but don't show it as equivalent either. The safest read is: **every remote option is a weaker version offered so people who cannot be reached in person still get something, not an equivalent.**

**In-person Foundations is the aspirational product.** It's where the full system comes alive, where you read the room, where the live rep on your own moment actually happens. But virtual exists because a delivered-in-your-own-setup 90 minutes is better than zero.

## 7. The Room-Size Decision

**Essentials: 6–15 people.** Foundations: 6–20 people.

*(Working assumption — founder-set operating decision, not an externally sourced figure; reasoning below is the source.)*

These are hard caps. Why:

- **Below 6:** Consent gets complicated (every person identifiable), anonymity gets impossible.
- **Above 15 (Essentials):** The method depends on reading the room, paired practice with space to move, the board-narrowing exercise working at scale. At 20, Essentials becomes a presentation, not a workshop.
- **Above 20 (Foundations):** Per-head margin drops (more people, same facilitator hours), and the measurement load grows (you're collecting from more participants, follow-up becomes harder to manage, the report gets harder to interpret).

## 8. The Client Commitment — Measured, Not Just Claimed

Every session requires the organisation to make three named commitments:

1. **Voluntary attendance** — demonstrably so. Form asks: were you directed to attend, or did you choose to?
2. **One named real opportunity** — named before the session, in the next three weeks. Form asks: did that situation actually happen?
3. **One named manager asks once** — within three weeks. Form asks: did anyone ask whether there was anything you wanted to use?

**These are measured at three-week follow-up and reported split by whether they happened.**

This is not an environment layer (the facilitator leaves; the manager stays, but only for these three things). It's a delivery precondition measured as a covariate. If the preconditions don't hold, MFC does not accept a second cohort to that organisation until they're renegotiated.

**The consequence is deliberately small:** No penalty. No refund. No clause with teeth. A one-facilitator charity's only real enforcement is whether it agrees to come back.

## 9. Scaling — When and How

MFC is one facilitator. One facilitator can deliver:

- Twice per day maximum (one morning, one afternoon) (source: the pricing research's founder capacity note)
- 5 days a week = ~10 sessions per week at absolute maximum (derived from the above)
- Realistically, ~2–3 sessions per week (estimate, unverified — accounting for prep, analysis, report writing, travel)

**So the question is not "how do we scale delivery" but "what does a working first cohort look like, and how do we prove it?"**

The ladder is designed so that **V1 (Foundations) establishes credibility, V2 (Essentials) builds evidence volume, and V5 (the digital one-hour) reaches cost-effectively without requiring a room.** It's not a growth plan. It's a diversification plan around one person's capacity.

**Delegability (T5 above) is the real answer to founder dependency.** A short, well-specified format (like V5 or V2 Essentials) is something someone else could learn to run. A 4-hour workshop that depends on reading the room and the live rep is harder to hand over. So the order is: build proof with Foundations (hard to delegate), systematise Essentials (medium difficulty), test the one-hour digital (most delegable). Once V5 is proven and systematised, someone else can run it, and suddenly the capacity problem changes shape.

**That's in year two or beyond.** Year one is one facilitator proving the method works.

## 10. Delivery Timeline and What Needs to Close First

Nothing is delivered to a participant until:

1. **The safeguarding review has named owners and dates.** All six safeguarding matters must have someone responsible and a target date, not "TBD."
2. **V5's safeguarding annex exists** (if V5 is offered to individuals).
3. **Clinical governance and legal review have run** on the distress-response protocols.

Once those close, the timeline is:

- **Month 1:** Foundations and Essentials delivery begins (grant-funded and corporate case-study cohorts)
- **Month 2–3:** Build V5 in parallel
- **Month 3–4:** V5 launches to individuals and organisations
- **Month 4+:** Accumulate measurement data, begin publishing findings

This is aspirational and depends on grant funding arriving and the safeguarding review closing. If either doesn't happen, the timeline slips.
