# What we are doing, why, how, and by when

This is MFC's product and commercial strategy. A broader organisational strategy, covering
governance, administration and marketing, does not yet exist and is best written after the review
date named in section 8.

## 1. What MFC is, and what it is not

**We teach mental fitness the way physical fitness is taught** (Source: MFC's product-category framework, §1.1): somebody
explains how it works, then you train, on your own real situations, until you can reach the routine
under pressure. **The statement we are asking you to adopt:**

> **Mental Fitness Collective delivers skills-based mental fitness education and training with
> measurement. What it can do** is teach a practised routine people can use in real moments, and
> measure honestly whether they used it.
>
> **It is not** clinical care or therapy. **It is not** a substitute for legal or work-health-and-safety
> advice. **It is not** a standalone psychosocial risk management system, and **it is not a control an
> employer can rely on to discharge a duty.**

**Rationale, plainly.** Workplace mental-health law is about the conditions people work in, and
training removes none of them; Victoria has written that into regulation, where training **"must not
be the predominant measure (subject to reasonable practicability)"** (SR 103/2025)
(`Source: research/market-psychosocial-compliance-landscape.md`). Wellbeing programmes, Mental Health
First Aid and therapy each do a real job well; **we are not a better version of any of them, we do a
different job**, training a person on their own state without needing anything to be wrong.

| | Stated plainly |
|---|---|
| **On duties** | Nothing an organisation buys from us changes its own legal duty, and we never say that what we sell discharges a duty, satisfies a Code obligation, or counts as consulting workers |
| **The regulatory context** | What we sell belongs to the class of measure regulators have demoted (`Source: research/market-psychosocial-compliance-landscape.md` §3.6). Research does not separate mental fitness from resilience training as a distinct, measurable thing, so we present the category as our own way of organising the work, never as a validated discovery (Source: MFC's product-category framework, §4) |
| **The gate on the wording** | **No lawyer and no work-health-and-safety practitioner has read any of our positioning**, and it cannot go in front of a buyer until one has. The statement above is a working boundary, not an approved one |
| **A conflict we are not resolving here** | Our Framework says we do not claim to prevent mental illness; our Constitution names it as a principal activity. That needs qualified legal advice, and no legal conclusion is drawn here |

**We never say** that we prevent or treat mental illness, that we replace therapy or crisis support,
that any other provider fails, or that a single session produces lasting behaviour change.

**What makes this different, stated honestly.** The loop is not new: parts exist in DBT, ACT and CBT.
What we have not found elsewhere in the category is one three-step procedure, cued to any moment, run
identically every time, with nothing to diagnose first (`Source:
13-founder-voice-v2-category-and-competitors`). **Not claimed as proven**: zero cohorts run, and
nobody in this category has funded the proactive, universal version of it. That gap is the
opportunity.

## 2. What's in it for me

*Why should a person care, why should an organisation spend money on it, and what will we be able to
prove.*

### The human lens

**Everyone has these moments, and they are not only the hard ones.** The sentence said in anger, the
silence when somebody needed them to speak, being somewhere else entirely at the end of a hard day.
And just as much, the good ones: showing up fully for someone, connecting with intention instead of
by accident. We are all human, we all have moments like this constantly, at work and everywhere else,
and we expect ourselves to handle them well. Almost nobody is taught how, let alone trained and given
the chance to practise. That is what we are trying to solve. The gap between knowing what to do and
doing it is not a character flaw. It is a skill nobody was ever taught, and it is trainable.

**What a person gets, specifically.** Not another method they are simply told to do. An actual
grounding in what is happening inside them in a moment: their thoughts, their feelings, what their
body is doing, and what they can start to do about it. From there, a loop they can actually recall
and use in the moment itself, not just understand afterwards, because knowing what to do and being
able to reach it under pressure are two different gaps, and the second one is the one we see missing
most. Practice at using it, in the room, on their own real situations. And afterwards, a Radar: simply
a short list of moments they choose to keep practising, kept alive by a reminder tied to something
already in their day, since a skill with no cue attached to real life does not stick on its own. And
a way back after getting something wrong that does not turn into a verdict on who they are.

**It is theirs, not their employer's.** Nobody has to identify as unwell, disclose anything, or
accept that something is wrong with them in order to take part. That matters, because it is the
single biggest reason people do not use what workplaces already offer them.

### The business lens

**What an organisation is actually buying**, stated as levers rather than promises:

| The lever | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **A practised skill, not an awareness session** | People leave having used the routine on a real situation of their own, not having watched a presentation about it |
| **Evidence for a psychosocial initiative** | A documented training initiative that sits alongside your own controls, with measurement attached, at a time when regulators expect organisations to show what they are doing |
| **Data about your own people** | A short measurement before, immediately after, and again at day 21, reported back with the numbers underneath, including when the answer is that nothing changed |
| **Reach across a whole team at once** | Everybody gets the same routine, so a team can use it with each other rather than a few individuals having a private skill |
| **A shared Radar, not only an individual one** | Individual change does not hold if the environment stays the same. A team agrees its own short list of moments and norms, the same tool at group level |
| **A cost you can size before you commit** | Fixed prices, published here, from $600 to $3,900 plus $95 a person (`Source: 110`) |

**The return-on-investment question, answered honestly.** For a team of ten, Foundations (section 3) is
**$4,850** (`Source: 110`), which is **$485 a person** `[Source: derived]`, once. **What that buys is
five things, and they should be seen before the number is compared to anything:** a session for
whoever leads the group, a half day with the team, a facilitated return three weeks later, a written
report back to the organisation, and measurement taken at three points (`Source: 110`).

**What we can honestly say is the mechanism, not a measured outcome:** a person who has this skill and
reaches for it, difficult moment or good one, is better placed to handle it than a person who does
not. That is the actual selling point; what we have not measured is that we cause it. What we cannot
honestly do is convert any of it into a payback figure. **MFC cannot and will not
claim improvements in engagement, retention, absenteeism, productivity or claims costs.** We do not
have access to an organisation's HR data, we have run no cohorts, and any such number would be
invented.

**So here is the exact line between what we will show you and what we will not.**

| We will be able to show | We will not be able to show |
|---|---|
| Whether people could remember and use the routine at three weeks, with the number it was asked of | That the training reduced turnover, absence or claims |
| What changed in people's own reported ability to use it, before and after | That any change would not have happened anyway |
| Whether a team kept using it once we left | A financial return per dollar spent |
| Whether clients continued, and the reasons given by those who did not | Anything at all about clinical outcomes |

shape matches that.

## 5. How we reach organisations, what we measure, and by when

### 5.1 How we get top-of-funnel: three routes to market

**How we get top-of-funnel is a relationship question, not a marketing-spend question.** Nothing is
sold cold; every route starts with someone who has already met Hunter or been introduced.

| Route | What it is, and why it is one of the three |
|---|---|
| **Safety consultants, brokers and peak bodies** | Borrowed trust, no sales function required: named consultancies, brokers and bodies approached directly, in ranked order |
| **Warm direct sales** | Organisations we can already get a meeting with, sold as our first case studies. Costs nothing, and can start this week |
| **Community talks and events** | A short talk or activity, 5 to 15 minutes, at an existing community event. Already run before; it reaches an organisation and grows the charity's own base of supporters at the same time |

**Rationale.** One person who delivers everything can open a small number of doors properly, not a
large number badly, so this is sequenced by trust already built. **Social media is active now, not
deferred**: over **9,000 followers** already (`Source: founder-reported, 2026-08-17, not
independently verified`), and consistent posting is the cheapest lever we have. An **ambassador with
a large, genuinely connected following** is a lever we are exploring for the same reason: what we
teach is simple and quick to learn, which makes it easy for someone else to carry. Once we have real
traction, a roving MFC community across different parts of Sydney is worth building; not before.

**On an executive-network route, which we have looked at.** Genuinely a good idea; we have considered
partnering with an existing group over building one. Not one of the three we start with: founder
hours are the constraint. If we are genuinely struggling to reach people at 3 to 6 months, this is
the first thing we pick up.

### 5.2 What we measure, and when

We measure three times: before, immediately after, and again at day 21. The questions separate
whether a person **could not remember** the routine from whether they **could not use** it, because
those two failures need different fixes (Source: the measurement research). A recognised evaluation framework,
Kirkpatrick, has been proposed; **we have not adopted it**, and it goes to our measurement advisers
first (`Source: founder record, 2026-08-16`).

**What cannot be concluded from it.** We do not claim behaviour change at day 21, and a three to four
month follow-up is not designed, costed or funded **for standard delivery**. Organisations choose
their own product, so **we cannot say the longer product works better, or that the monthly sessions
cause anything**, and we do not control the environment somebody returns to (Source: MFC's product-category framework, §1.2).

**Our founding case studies add a three-month wave**, checking unaided recall, still no claim the
training caused anything (`Source: B5c`). **This starts to turn a session into evidence.** Using it
beyond our own report needs ethics approval (HREC) before collection, so a university or research
partner is a live, unstarted task, not a formality (`Source: 00-hrec-and-data-strategy`); data storage
is designed and cleared the same way.
