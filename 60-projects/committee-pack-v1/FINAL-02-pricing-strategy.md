# Pricing Strategy: The Complete Offer and Price List

## Executive Summary

Every MFC offering is priced to clear its direct cost plus a 1.4× overhead allocation. Nothing is sold below cost. The half-day Foundations session is the revenue engine and is priced to carry the organisation. Shorter formats (Essentials, Introduction, the new one-hour digital) are priced to break even or slightly above, funded from Foundations margin and access levy where they do not.

For the first three organisations that book Foundations, we offer a case-study rate (source: the founding case-study pricing research; 26-29% discount, computed from that research — see §4 for how this relates to the 25–30% target range) in exchange for full measurement participation and permission to reference them. That rate still clears cost and holds a real margin. It ends after three sales or four months, whichever is first.

Beyond the corporate channel, community organisations access a tiered community rate, and applications for pro bono (free) delivery are considered from community groups or micro-businesses using a dedicated access fund.

## 1. The Pricing Framework

### Cost Model

**Loaded cost** = Facilitator hours × $66.10/hour (founder time, not salary) + materials + 1.4× overhead multiplier (source: the pricing research and the half-day pricing decision).

- Facilitator rate $66.10/hour is the imputed founder hourly rate from the half-day pricing decision (source: the pricing research)
- Overhead 1.4× is a standard charity proxy for facilities, insurance, governance, unfunded time (source: the pricing research, using the half-day pricing decision's proxy)
- **This is loaded cost, not cash cost.** The real cash commitment for a free session is ~$600 materials + travel, and 45 hours of facilitator calendar — not the $4,570 the loaded figure might appear to show (source: the pricing research)

**Every figure below shows direct cost, loaded cost, and margin.** The margin exists to fund access, not to profit. MFC is not GST-registered, so every price shown is a final price.

### Standing Principle

> The half-day Foundations workshop is the revenue engine. Everything else is access and evidence, priced to clear its own cost or above, funded from corporate margin where it doesn't.

This means:

- **Foundations is never discounted except in the founding case-study window.** After three cohorts or four months, the price is $2,500 base + $95 per person, period.
- **Essentials and Introduction are reach and evidence, not revenue.** They're priced as low as they can go while still not consuming runway.
- **Community and pro bono are explicitly subsidised** by corporate margin, not treated as business lines.

## 3. The Nine Foundational Answers

**Figures throughout §3 are sourced from the pricing research's nine-answers section unless marked otherwise.**

### 1. Foundations — Repackage or New Design?

**Repackage.** The half-day format decision already settled this. The arc works, the teaching blocks hold. Restores two breaks that the contracted version removed. No new design needed. Reverses only if a delivered half day shows the repackaged arc cannot hold four hours without new material.

### 2. Reporting — What Does Each Size Cohort Actually Receive?

**Revised 2026-08-19, Hunter, direct decision — corrected same day after a first pass wrongly kept a
two-tier structure he explicitly rejected.** There is **one report format, given to every
organisation MFC works with, regardless of group size.** No size threshold, no "fuller" version that
unlocks at a headcount, no separate barrier-diagnostic report reserved for larger cohorts. Hunter,
directly, correcting the first pass: *"the detailed barrier level report isn't and can't be gated at
20+... we have a report for every organisation that's the same regardless of numbers - a report that
gives the team value and shows that it was worthwhile."*

**What the one report contains:** whether the session landed, whether people generally reported
learning something and finding it valuable — framed to demonstrate the programme was worthwhile, not
to diagnose individual or team-level barriers. **Hard limits, at every size, with no exception:** no
individual figures, no sub-group cuts, no verbatims, nothing traceable to one specific person even in
a room of 3. Example: *"the session was well received; most participants said they took away
something they intend to try."*

**What this replaces:** the earlier two-tier idea (a coarse report below 20, a separate "fuller
participant-data report" with barrier splits unlocked at 20+) is withdrawn. There is no second,
larger report waiting behind a headcount gate — the same value-focused report is what every
organisation gets, always. Data capture itself was never gated by group size and is unaffected —
MFC still captures full measurement at every size; only what's given back changes, and it no longer
varies by size at all.

**Not yet resolved by this change:** whether this report format is genuinely safe at the smallest end,
n=2–3, remains the open referral to Eimear and Debra (item 3 below) — that referral is now specifically
about the report's safety at extreme small numbers, not about whether a two-tier system exists (it
doesn't) or whether small groups get a report at all (they do, the same one everyone gets).

This was clarified in response to earlier pricing that promised reports to groups MFC's own privacy rules forbid reporting on.

### 3. Virtual — When Does It Launch?

**Withdraw virtual pricing from all buyer-facing materials until the online variant is fully designed.** Prices (§2) show what virtual *will* cost; it is not offered until it exists. This restricts MFC to driving distance of one facilitator and deletes access to interstate/regional buyers until the variant is built. Reverses when: an online variant with captions-by-default, no registration gate, and redesigned pair beats exists and is reviewed.

### 4. Room Size — What Are the Caps?

**Essentials: cap at 15 (was 20).** Foundations: cap at 20. These are hard limits. Reverses only if the committee explicitly overrides.

### 5. K6 (Knowing–Doing Gap Outcome) — Is It in Scope?

**Yes, approved.** The construct is already measured (B-06 designs it, K-E2 is a seven-minute beat). Leaving it measured but unapproved is inconsistent. Reverses only if a later review finds it duplicates an existing outcome.

### 6. Schools, Youth, Public Audiences — Offered?

**No.** Not until C-16 (the youth scope question) is reviewed and a safeguarding lead is named. The Introduction also narrows to adult community and workplace audiences only. Reverses when: safeguarding lead is named and C-16 closes.

### 7. Introduction Light Form — What's Measured?

**The primary matched item, the harm item, and the referral line. Nothing else.** One matched item, one harm item, no linkage code, no follow-up. And its booked price is $600 (source: the pricing research; was $400, which was 1.05× loaded and did not cover a single cancellation). Reverses if the first three bookings price out.

### 8. Anonymity at n = 2–5 — How's It Handled?

**The anonymity claim is removed at n=2–5.** We don't claim "we can't work backwards from the code to you" because we can, at that size. Instead, what runs at that size is session-level, pooled measurement only, with no individual linkage code. Foundations is not offered below six people at any price. This decision goes to qualified privacy advice before implementation (Eimear and Debra).

### 9. Leaders' Session — Paid Add-On or Included?

**Included, never sold separately.** (Source: the pricing research.) Charging $350 on top invites the buyer to decline the mechanism MFC says the product depends on. Its $139 loaded cost is carried in every line above. Reverses only if buyers routinely decline the briefing anyway, which means it's a product problem, not a pricing one.

## 4. The Founding Case-Study Mechanism — Fully Specified

**Figures throughout §4 are sourced from the founding case-study pricing research unless marked otherwise.**

### What the Buyer Gets

(Source: the founding case-study pricing research.) $1,750 base + $75/person (not $2,500 + $95) for the half-day workshop. Same delivery, same measurement, same Leaders' Session. The discount reflects that this is the first cohort, and MFC is building proof.

### What MFC Gets

In exchange for the discounted rate, the organisation agrees to:

1. **Full measurement participation** — pre-session, post-session, three-week follow-up on MFC's standard measures, at the participation rate MFC requests
2. **A testimonial** — written or recorded, from a nominated representative, within four weeks
3. **Reference permission** — MFC names the organisation, sector, and headcount band (never individual results) in marketing, grant applications, and funder reporting (unless anonymisation is requested in writing)
4. **Case-study review** — MFC shares any material naming or attributing findings to the organisation at least 10 business days before external use, will not publish over an unresolved factual objection
5. **Understanding of publication standards** — any pooled or aggregate finding published is subject to MFC's commitment to publish null results with equal prominence to positive ones; case-study participation is evidence-generation, not a guaranteed positive write-up

### The Trigger Back to Full Price

**Three case-study cohorts delivered, OR four months from the first case-study sale, whichever comes first.** After the trigger:

- The case-study rate is withdrawn
- Every subsequent Foundations sale (including to an organisation that inquired but hadn't yet booked) is at the standing rate
- No cohort loses its rate retroactively
- No automatic extension is granted if the time limit fires before three cohorts have sold

### Why This Mechanism Works

- **It directly answers the core gap:** "we're sold on promise, not proof." This is the fastest legitimate way to get proof without abandoning the premium price generally.
- **It's self-funding:** every sale clears loaded cost with a real margin (1.55×–2.38×), unlike the earlier founding-rate attempt which required an undocumented subsidy.
- **It's bounded:** three cohorts or four months creates natural discipline against indefinite drift.
- **It reuses proven evidence infrastructure:** MFC's existing N ≥ 20 threshold and measurement instrument, not invented criteria.

### Why It's Difficult

- **It creates a visible seam:** (source: the founding case-study pricing research) cohort 3 pays 26-29% less than cohort 4 for what looks like the same product. A procurement-literate buyer will ask why. The answer is transparent: it's limited to early adopters, and it ends automatically.
- **It sits close to breaking the "nothing below loaded cost" rule in spirit, even though it doesn't in letter.** The rule protects MFC's floor; a rule that technically holds but is deliberately engineered down to the thinnest defensible margin for three sales in a row reads, to a skeptical reader, as finding the exception rather than respecting the rule.
- **It adds a fourth live pricing mechanism** (standing, community tier, pro bono, case-study) to a document set that revised pricing twice in one day. That complexity is real and is a reputational cost, not hypothetical.

**These difficulties are manageable because they are named in the offer itself.** §5's copy says plainly: "first 3 organisations, then it ends." The margin is shown, not hidden. The purposes stay visibly distinct.

## 5. Marketing the Case-Study Rate

**Founding case-study rate — first 3 organisations**

We're a new charity building the evidence base for mental fitness training at work. The first three organisations to book Foundations join as founding case-study partners: a reduced rate, in exchange for helping us measure what actually happens — and permission to tell your story (with your review) as we build a track record.

This isn't a claim that the results are already proven — it's an invitation to help us find out, together, at a price that reflects that we're just getting started.

## 6. The Distinctions That Matter

### Case-Study Rate vs. Community Tier vs. Pro Bono — Not the Same Thing

| | Community tier / pro bono | Founding case-study rate |
|---|---|---|
| Solves | Access — buyer can't afford standing rate | Evidence — MFC has no track record |
| Eligibility | Community organisations, orgs under 15 staff (means-related) | Any Foundations buyer, first 3–5 only (order-related) |
| Duration | Standing, no expiry | Capped: 3 cohorts or 4 months |
| What buyer gives | Nothing beyond the fee | Full measurement, testimonial, reference rights |
| Funded from | The access levy | Self-funding — clears cost on every sale |

These must be presented to buyers as two separate mechanisms, not one blurred "discount" story. A corporate buyer asking for community rate by pointing at the case-study offer, or vice versa, is a sign the distinction is not clear.

### Micro-Business (2–5 Staff) — Real Segment, Priced as Reach

The 2–5 staff band is a real opportunity (Hunter's judgement: "don't usually ever get the opportunity to upskill or learn") and is priced separately because it behaves differently (releasing everyone stops trading; you cannot do it).

At $700 against $639 loaded (source: the pricing research), it returns 1.10× — cost recovery with a rounding error — and is explicitly labelled reach and evidence spend funded by corporate margin, not revenue (source: the pricing research).

**What this band receives:** A one-page team note handed to the room at the end of the session (what was covered, what the room committed to, the cue), **plus, per the 2026-08-19 revision above, a coarse group-level report on whether the session landed and whether people generally reported learning something — no individual figures, no verbatims.** They also receive the Leaders' Session and full measurement collection.
