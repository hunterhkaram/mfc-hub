## 1 · The moment gallery

**Specific, ordinary moments posted around the room under five arenas — self, relationships, work,
performance, transitions.** Participants walk the room and **privately note three that connect to
them.** Thirteen minutes in the v3 Foundations budget.

> ⛔ **No disclosure. No group formation. Nobody says which arena they stood in longest.**

**Why a gallery and not a discussion — the sharpest rationale in the corpus:**

> **Asked cold to "think of a moment in your life," most people produce either something enormous or
> nothing at all.** A wall of ordinary pre-written moments makes the ordinary ones visible and gives
> permission for the small.

Choosing a life category out loud is exposing, group sizes come out uneven, and the domains overlap
anyway.

**The skill-referenced selection instruction**, spoken before the room stands up, about 45 seconds:

> *"As you read, don't just pick three you recognise. Pick them for different reasons. Find one where
> the hard part is **noticing** it's even happening — it goes past you every time. Find one where the
> hard part is **choosing** — you notice fine, you just do the other thing anyway. And find one where
> the hard part is **shifting** — you notice, you know what you'd rather do, and you can't get from
> one to the other. Three moments, three different hard parts. Still easy ones."*

**This is what gives the five skills a consumer.** Without it they are named, practised twice, and
never used again by the beat that decides what the participant actually leaves with. Foundations runs
the three-skill version; Essentials would run two (Notice and Choose). **Focus and Connect are
deliberately not named here** — Focus is trained elsewhere and Connect is practised in no product, and
naming a skill the room has never run produces a list, not a filter.

⛔ **The accessibility equivalent must be offered aloud before anyone stands up. It is a Gate 0.1
requirement, and the original pack failed it:**

> *"Note them if you want to, or just hold them in your head — either works, and nobody will look at
> your paper. You can also stay where you are and read from where you're sitting."*

**A seated route is required as well as a silent one** — "walk the room" is itself a mobility
assumption.

⛔ **Curation, not authorship. No facilitator, session or preparation step may write a new moment for
the wall** (`14-moment-examples-bank.md` standing no-invention rule). **The bank holds 18 verified
entries and none is a workplace-manager moment** — the corpus for the wall is unresolved and is a
real build dependency, not a detail.

## 2 · Application studios — Foundations only

**The room splits by kind of application, never by life domain: managing myself in a moment** versus
**responding to another person.** Twelve minutes.

**Three reasons for this split and not a domain split:**
1. **Nobody learns anything about your life from which studio you picked.**
2. It reliably produces two roughly even groups.
3. It maps a real difference — **a moment involving another person has a second party's response in
   it**, which changes what Shift and Do can be.

A domain split (sport / family / work) is exposing and produces uneven groups.

⛔ **With one facilitator, both run as structured self-guided studios — after the facilitator
demonstrates one complete worked example at the front. This is non-negotiable:** a self-guided studio
with no worked model produces vague reflection, which this service explicitly excludes.

Each guide walks the participant through their own selected moment: **where the catch is, what Shift
options exist, what the Do could be, and what changes if the first option is gone.**

**This is the only small-group device MFC has ever designed that satisfies `AG-134` (Eimear Quigley:
the workshop must be experiential, small-group practice feeding back to the whole group) without
exposing any individual.** That is why losing it mattered.
