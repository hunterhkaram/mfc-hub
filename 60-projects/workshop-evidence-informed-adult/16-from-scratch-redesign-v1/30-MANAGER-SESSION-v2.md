# The manager session, v2

## ⚠ Read this before the design

**No real manager has read or sat through any version of this session — v1 or v2.** The "manager
panel" cited throughout `03-MANAGER-SESSION-DESIGN.md` was **AI-simulated**: a reader panel run
inside the design session that produced that file. It is not manager research, not advisor
testimony and not committee evidence.

**This does not block the build.** It is the largest open risk on the product and it is closed by
one real manager reading it, not by any further internal review. It must not be hidden, and it must
not be softened into "tested with a panel."

**MFC has delivered zero cohorts.** Every beat below is reasoned, not observed.

---

## 1 · What this session is

**A planning session with an intention.** Hunter, 2026-09-09:

> *"It needs to be unique. It's going to feel like — oh, this is interesting. And we have to come in
> and there's an intention. We're coming in to plan this, we're doing this, and this is what we're
> wanting to be implemented at the end."*

Three properties follow, and they are the whole product:

1. **High-friction workplace moments, not struggle moments** (ruling 50). The participant hour's
   emotional register is wrong for this room. Nothing here is about how anyone is coping.
2. **The environment a manager creates, and what behaviours get rewarded** (ruling 25). Hunter:
   *"They're creating the environment, the setting, what's rewarded, what's not… for eight hours of
   their day, in their life. And that's why it's important."*
3. **A working block where managers build something for their own team, in the room** (ruling 26).
   This is the centre of the hour, not a closing exercise.

### The double-up question, answered

Managers attend a participant session first (`D-0123`: the seat is counted and charged; no exclusion
rule, and the session runs identically if their own reports are present). So the hour must be an
addition, not a repeat.

**It is, and the answer comes from inside the framework rather than being asserted.** ⚑ **This
paragraph is design rationale and is NOT spoken in any room, changed 2026-09-10** — beat 3 used to
say it out loud, and the Human Operating System is Essentials-and-above content that an Introduction
buyer's managers have never been taught. `K-07`'s Human Operating System has seven parts. **Six of them sit inside a person. One — Environment — is the only one that is largely not.**

⚠ **Stated precisely, because ruling 41 binds it.** `K-07` defines Environment as *"the people, places,
cues, pressures and supports around you."* It does **not** say who builds it, and v2 must not put that
sentence in `K-07`'s mouth. The 6/1 arithmetic is `K-07`'s. That a manager is one of those people, and
sets some of those places, cues and pressures, is **beat 6's own demonstration** — shown in the room,
from the room, never asserted as framework. A team can only ever work on the inside of its own moments. A manager works
on the outside of thirty other people's. That is a different job, and nobody else in the building has
it.

⛔ **Withdrawn: "same framework, different unit."** It implies a team has mental fitness, which MFC
cannot support and which invites *"so what's our team score?"*

⛔ **Never "Manager Radar" or "Team Radar"** (ruling 65). `K-07` refuses it directly — *"not one Radar
per role."* The manager's construct already exists in `K-07` and had never been used: **enabling
conditions** ⚠ (`K-07` files it as Stage-3 *Deepening* content that *"has not itself been simplified for a
first explanation"*. v2 makes it a core construct in a one-hour room; the spoken form below **is** that
simplification, written here and not yet approved) — *"what must be available, permitted, modelled or changed around the person for the next
move to be realistic."* Spoken form drops the label entirely: *"what has to be true around someone for
the next move to be realistic."*

⚠ **Three of the four "this is an addition" arguments require managers to have attended the
participant session.** Nothing in the buyer pack or the pricing model requires that attendance. Open,
carried from v1, unchanged.

## 2 · The load-bearing argument

**Not an analogy. Arithmetic, in the room.**

> *You answer the nine p.m. email in four minutes. You answer the Tuesday lunchtime one on Thursday.
> Nobody ever said a word about when to send things. And everybody now knows when to send things.*

**You are already rewarding behaviour. You just aren't choosing which.**

This replaces v1's driving / times-tables / bins set, which Hunter cut: *"I don't know what this is.
How is that carrying a whole product?"* It needs no analogy, it is in his own register, and — unlike
the cut set — **it generates the working block** rather than merely preceding it. It also puts
ruling 25's named subject, rewarding the behaviours you want to see, at the centre where it belongs.

## 3 · The spine — four movements, twelve beats

**No minutes** (rulings 1, 2). Beats widen; order never changes. Duration is a property of a beat in
a product, not of the spine.

⚠ **This is a separate spine, and ruling 3 does not reach it.** Ruling 3 fixes the **participant**
spine at four movements over **fourteen** beats (`D-0131`), and its movements are the participant
hour's own (*"Is this me?"*, *"Can it change?"*). Ruling 25 says the manager session is a separate
product and never a compression of the participant foundation, so it carries its own spine. **Twelve
beats here is not a violation of fourteen there — but ruling 3 as written is unqualified, and this
qualification is the system's reading, not Hunter's.** If he wants one spine across all four products,
this file is what changes.

### Movement A — What this hour is, and what it isn't

**Beat 1 · The frame.** This is a planning session. You will leave with something for your own team
that you intend to implement. Nothing is collected and nobody is asked to speak (ruling 8).

⚑ **WRITTEN 2026-09-10. This beat previously had no spoken words at all** — it described what the
frame was and left the facilitator to invent the first thing the room hears.

> "This is a planning hour. It isn't a workshop and it isn't a wellbeing session. By the end of it
> you'll have one thing, for your own team, that you actually intend to do next week.
>
> Nothing you come up with in here is collected. I don't see it, nobody reports on it, and there's no
> going round the table. You don't have to say anything for the whole hour if you don't want to.
>
> You're in this room because you're not just doing your own week. You're setting the conditions
> everybody else's week happens inside, and nobody else in the building has that job."

⚠ **The third paragraph is the beat's only forward-leaning claim and it is deliberately small.** It
sets up beat 3 and beat 4 without borrowing their arguments, and it says nothing about what a manager
causes.

**Beat 2 · The scope rule, said to their face.** ⚠ **Placed here deliberately, before any friction
example is named.** Compliance requirement carried from the held version: the register change from
struggle to workplace friction reduces clinical exposure and *increases* legal and industrial
exposure, because "high-friction workplace moments" sits close to the descriptive language of the
psychosocial hazards in the WHS regulations, and what a manager names in the room may concern a live
grievance. The scope rule must arrive **first**, not as a mid-hour correction.

**Carried forward in full and unweakened (ruling 46; claim-boundary load-bearing):**

> "One boundary, because otherwise this hour gets used for the wrong thing.
>
> **If the real problem is workload, or staffing, or safety, or one specific person behaving badly,
> that is not a habit, and I'm not going to let us turn it into one.** Nothing in this hour fixes it
> and pretending otherwise would be the wrong thing to do with it. Those go through whatever your
> organisation actually has for them.
>
> And to be plain about what this isn't: it's not a hazard assessment, it doesn't tell you whether
> anything here is safe or unsafe, and it doesn't discharge anything you owe anybody. There's a free
> government tool for that side of it and it's better at it than I am.
>
> And the honest version of the same boundary, because it's the one that matters most and it's the
> reason you're in this room rather than just them. **A skill doesn't replace a bad environment.**
> If the conditions are genuinely against your people, no amount of noticing a moment fixes that,
> and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. **What you can move is the part that's ordinary, and
> that's most of the week.**
>
> **The four levers are for the ordinary week. That's genuinely most of it, and it's not nothing."**

*(Delivery note carried from v1: in a community group, club or volunteer setting, cut the
government-tool sentence and keep the boundary.)*

**Two additions required by the register change, both new in v2:**

- **The named-matter exclusion, said aloud.** *"If a moment you're thinking of is about one named
  person, or it's already in a formal process, don't use it here. Pick a different one. This hour
  isn't the place for it and I'm not the person for it."*
- **The no-record rule, said aloud rather than merely being true.** *"Nothing you write goes anywhere.
  I don't see it, we don't collect it, it isn't reported to anyone, and there's no record of this
  session beyond that you attended."* It was already true under `D-0112`; a manager weighing whether
  to name something real needs to hear it, not to have it be quietly the case.

⛔ **AG-011 / AG-012: never call Stop / Shift / Do a WHS control**, here or anywhere. The negative
scope is stated in this beat, repeated at beat 11, and repeated in the thirty-second upward pitch at
beat 12. Both register entries are `pending-approval`; no grading exists and none may be asserted.

**Beat 3 · The double-up answer.**

⚑ **REWRITTEN 2026-09-10 — the six-of-seven arithmetic is cut from the spoken beat.** It rested the
single most sceptical moment in the hour on `K-07`'s Human Operating System, which is
**Essentials-and-above content**. A manager whose organisation bought the Introduction has never heard
of it, so for that buyer the beat was arithmetic on a model that does not exist. **And the same point
arrives two beats later, at level three, when the room looks at itself** (beat 5) and then at the
nine p.m. email (beat 4). **The abstraction is cut and the demonstration carries it**, which is this
whole product's own preference stated in §1: shown in the room, from the room, never asserted as
framework.

> "Some of you are sitting there thinking you've just done this. You have, and this isn't that.
>
> Everything in that session happened inside one person's own head. That's where it has to happen,
> and it's real. But not one thing in it was about the conditions those moments happen in, because
> nobody in that room could change them.
>
> You can. That's the difference, and that's the whole hour."

*Your team can do Learn It without you. They cannot do Build It without you, because Build It is the
part that's visible and slow, and you're the one who decides what slow costs around here.*

⚠ **`K-07`'s 6/1 arithmetic stays in §1 as design rationale and is not spoken in any room.** It is
why the beat is true; it is not how the beat is delivered.

Learn It / Build It / Use It is the spine of the manager's version of the argument. The five skills
are named with why they matter and are not taught here (ruling 30). *"We can't teach you skills. We
can teach you the process"* (ruling 33) applies to this room exactly as to the participant room.

### Movement B — You are already rewarding behaviour

**Beat 4 · The arithmetic.** §2 above. The nine p.m. email and the Tuesday lunchtime one. Ends on:
**you are already rewarding behaviour, you just aren't choosing which.**

**Beat 5 · The room as an environment.** Carried forward verbatim (Hunter kept this explicitly):

> "Look at this room for a second. Actually look. Chairs in rows facing me. Phones face up. That's an
> environment doing its job, and it isn't neutral. It's telling everyone in here how to behave, and
> it's working. Nobody voted for it. I set it up in four minutes."

**Beat 6 · The four levers.** Carried forward. The four cue locations read back as things the manager
controls — **people; places; objects and screens; times and transitions** — including, load-bearing:

> "**People.** For everyone who works for you, **you are on that list.** You're one of the people
> who's there when it happens. Some days you make it easier and some days you make it harder, and
> it's usually the same you."

And carried with it, because it is what stops the beat becoming guilt: **"plenty of you don't decide
any of it… that band isn't yours. That's a fact about your job, not a failing. Cross it off and use
the other three."**

⚠ **Terminology.** These are **not** "the Radar's four hiding places" and not a Radar of any kind.
They are a cue-location device living in the workshop design layer, appearing in no foundations
record. The manager's construct is `enabling conditions` (§1).

**Beat 7 · The reward boundary.** The one instruction in this product that most needs to be right.

> "You're going to reward something this week whether you mean to or not. So choose it. **Reward what
> somebody does in the shared work.** They left a gap in the agenda, they came back to a decision
> instead of firing off the reply, they said the thing later instead of in the moment. That's visible,
> it's about the work, and everyone can see the same thing you saw.
>
> **Never reward evidence that a person is practising.** Not who's using it, not who's got a list,
> not who's doing their reps. **And never reward a state.** Not who seems calmer, not who looks like
> they're handling it better. The second you do either of those, you've made it a performance, and
> the people who most need the three seconds are the ones who'll stop taking them."

⛔ **This boundary is CONSTRUCTED, NOT AUTHORISED — and must be recorded as such wherever it is used.**
A sweep of all **379** entries in `50-authority/advisor-guidance-register/REGISTER.md` — re-counted
against the live register on 2026-09-09, correcting the stale figure of 326 that the held version and
several other files still carry — found **zero** entries on reward, observation or monitoring. The two
near-misses are `AG-069` (webinar ROI) and `AG-331` (brand recognition), neither on point. There is no advisor position behind this line. It is
MFC's own reasoning, written by the system, and it is the riskiest instruction in the product because
it tells a manager what to notice about their staff.

**The one question that converts it into a HARD ruling, for Eimear Quigley (clinical psychologist,
committee):**

> *"In a workplace session we tell managers to reward what someone does in the shared work, and never
> to reward evidence that a person is practising or any change in how they seem. Is that the right
> boundary, and is there anything in it that would make a manager more likely to start reading their
> staff rather than less?"*

One question, one answer, and the line stops being constructed. Until it is asked, this beat is
delivered as designed and disclosed as unauthorised — it is not blocked, and it is not presented as
ruled.

### Movement C — The moment bank and the moves

**Beat 8 · The friction moments.** Given, never a blank page (`AG-147`). See §4 for the bank and for
what was cut out of it.

⚑ **WRITTEN 2026-09-10. This beat previously had no spoken words** — it named the requirement and
handed over a table.

> "I'm not going to ask you to think of something from nothing. That's the part that never works, and
> it's not because people can't think.
>
> Here are six that come up in nearly every team I've been in. Read them, and find the one that's
> most like your week. If none of them is yours, take the closest one and change it in your head
> until it is.
>
> One. Not a list. The whole thing after this runs on one."

⛔ **The six are read or shown, never expanded into examples of who they happen to.** The moment is a
piece of the week's structure, never a person in it.

**Beat 9 · The move menu, sorted by the four levers.** Each move is a change to a condition, never a
change to a person. Every one is something a manager does, that someone else would see.

⚑ **WRITTEN 2026-09-10. This beat previously had no spoken words** — three tables and no line to
carry them.

> "Two lists, and then I'll shut up and you'll build the thing.
>
> First, moves. Every one of these is a change to a condition, not a change to a person. That's on
> purpose and it's the rule the whole hour runs on.
>
> Second, the sentence. This is the part nobody's ever been asked to write, and it's the only part of
> your plan another human ever hears. It's about the work. Never about how somebody seems.
>
> You're taking one from each. One move, one sentence. One is the whole exercise."

**Read aloud twice, and on the leave-behind's move panel** — a spoken menu alone is a weaker prompt than a printed
one (`AG-147`), and v1 already recorded that as a known weakness.

| Lever | Moves |
|---|---|
| **Times and transitions** | Put a gap between two things that currently touch. Move the hardest conversation off the end of a Friday. Stop taking updates in the last five minutes. Give the question before the meeting rather than in it. |
| **Objects and screens** | Schedule-send anything written after hours. Say once, out loud, what your own out-of-hours message does and does not mean. Take one recurring thing off the channel that pings. |
| **Places** | Make one route to contribute that isn't speaking first in front of everyone. Find somewhere a handover can happen that isn't in a doorway with someone waiting. Make your door a real door, or stop calling it open. |
| **People** | Say out loud, once, that taking a few seconds before answering is fine here — and then be the one who does it in front of them. Stop being the first voice in the round. |

⛔ **Every move above is a condition. If a proposed move names a person, counts anybody's anything, or
depends on watching how someone seems, it is not a move — it fails the fifth test at beat 10 and it
breaches beat 7's boundary.** This applies to F6 without exception (§4).

**The five notice stems** — given, because the notice is the part nobody has ever been asked to write
and the part `AG-147` most applies to. Each is about the shared work and none is about a person's
practice or state:

1. *"That's a better way round than what I asked for."*
2. *"You came back to that instead of firing off the reply. That's the one I want."*
3. *"You left a gap in that agenda on purpose, didn't you. Keep doing that."*
4. *"You said the thing later instead of in the moment and it landed better. Noted."*
5. *"I noticed nobody had to speak first there. That was you."*

**Three worked example plans**, so nobody is composing the shape and the content at once:

| Moment | Move | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| The stand-up round-the-table where everyone hears how long you took (F1) | I stop going in the same order, and I stop being the first voice | *"I noticed nobody had to speak first there. That was you."* |
| Messages I send that land in someone's evening (F3) | Anything I write after six gets schedule-sent for the morning | *"You came back to that instead of firing off the reply. That's the one I want."* |
| The hardest conversation on a Friday afternoon (F4) | It moves to Tuesday morning, and there is a gap after it | *"You left a gap in that agenda on purpose, didn't you. Keep doing that."* |

### Movement D — Build it, in the room

**Beat 10 · The working block — the moment, the move, and the notice.** The centre of the product
(ruling 26).

Three parts:

1. **The moment** — which recurring bit of your week you're changing.
2. **The move** — what you will do differently that somebody would actually see.
3. **The notice** — **what you'll say out loud, once, when you see someone else do it.**

The third part is what Hunter said was missing everywhere, and it is **the only part of the plan
another human hears.** It is also where beat 7's boundary does its work: the notice is said about the
shared work, never about a person's practice and never about their state.

⚑ **THE SPOKEN ROUTE, WRITTEN 2026-09-10, AND IT IS SAID BEFORE ANYONE PICKS UP A PEN.** The whole
block was written, the only opt-out in it was the pair reading, and a manager who cannot write
comfortably or would rather not had no stated route through the centre of the product. Every other
MFC product carries a head-held default; this one is the product whose output is a written artefact,
which is exactly why the route has to be said out loud rather than assumed.

> "Write it or don't. If you'd rather keep it in your head, keep it in your head. It's three things,
> and it's three things either way: the moment, the move, and the sentence. Say them to yourself
> until you can say them without thinking. Nobody's checking your page and nobody's collecting it."

⚠ **Anti-ruling 115, named here rather than left unnamed.** It refuses *"butcher's paper, printed
prompts, write-on cards, per-person card sets"*, and this block runs on a per-person write-on sheet.
**This file previously argued only that `D-0112` does not reach in-session writing, which is a
different record, while its sibling file used 115 to strip a written sheet out of Foundations on the
same day.** Two products, one day, opposite conclusions on one anti-ruling. **The spoken route above
closes the accessibility gate either way** — nobody is now excluded from the centre of the product by
not writing — **but whether managers may be handed a sheet at all is a ruling, and it is Hunter's.**
Recorded as open item 10, not resolved here.

**The facilitator builds his own first, badly, and repairs it in front of them.** That is what
unblocks the page — not encouragement, and not a better prompt. It is the same principle as ruling
29d's non-negotiable worked demonstration in the application studios.

**Then the five tests.** Managers apply these to their own draft:

| Test | The question |
|---|---|
| The diary test | Is there an actual recurring slot in your week this lands in? |
| The bad-Tuesday test | Would you still do it on your worst day of the month? |
| The permission test | Do you actually control this, or does someone above you? |
| The witness test | Would anyone on your team notice you'd done it without being told? |
| The no-names-no-numbers test | Does it name a person, or count anybody's anything? If so, rewrite it. |

**Then, if they want to, a pair reads it with one job: tell them which of the five they'd fail.**

⚠ **The opt-out is written into the beat, not assumed.** Spoken: *"If you'd rather not show anyone,
don't. Run the five yourself. It works either way and nobody's checking."* Ruling 8 — *nobody speaks
unless they want to* — binds this room exactly as it binds the participant room, and a sheet a manager
has just written about their own team is the single most likely thing they would decline to show. The
tests, not facilitator attention and not the pair, are what makes this scale to twenty.

**Beat 11 · The three closes.** Carried forward. Nothing was said out loud and nothing is collected;
**it will miss, and remembering late is still a rep**; and the *don't* —

> "**Third, about your team, and it's the only thing I'll ask of you, and it's a *don't*.** When they
> come back they'll have written one sentence on their own phone. **Don't go looking for it.** If
> they bring it up, ask how it's going, that's a normal conversation. But don't ask what they wrote,
> don't ask to see it, and don't open with it. That's the one thing that would undo the whole thing.
>
> And I'll give you the reason rather than just the rule, because the rule on its own won't survive a
> week. From where you're sitting, *"how did that go?"* is a friendly question. From where they're
> sitting it's their manager asking. There's about one beat between that and *"what did you put?"*,
> and they know it before you do."

**And the safety line, carried forward verbatim. Safeguarding load-bearing (ruling 47):**

⚠ *"Panel eight" is v1's leave-behind panel number. v2 has not yet specified the leave-behind's panels, so
it is carried here exactly as spoken and **must be re-pointed at the actual referral panel before delivery**
(ruling 23: one consolidated leave-behind). Do not drop the pointer in order to fix the number — a
safeguarding line that names no route is worse than one that names the wrong panel.*

> "**And one thing that isn't true, which matters more than any of this.** If someone on your team
> goes quiet, or slow, or flat, **that is not them doing this.** Don't read anything as a technique.
> This is one sentence on a phone; it doesn't change how anybody looks. If you're worried about
> someone, then you're worried about someone, and that's the same route it's always been. **Panel eight**, and
> whatever your organisation has. **Nothing in this hour is a reason to wait and see."**

**Beat 12 · Thirty seconds upward, and the door out.** The line a manager gives their own boss, capped
so it cannot become a claim, with the non-discharge repeated.

⚑ **WRITTEN 2026-09-10, AND THIS IS THE LAST THING ANYONE HEARS.** The beat previously had no spoken
words, which meant the session's final words did not exist — **and §5 records the `AG-011` / `AG-012`
negative scope as *"stated at beat 2, beat 11 and beat 12"*, so a compliance control was recorded as
being carried by a line nobody had written.** It is written now, and it is the load-bearing sentence
in the middle paragraph.

> "Last thing, and it's for the conversation you'll have on the way out of here.
>
> Somebody above you is going to ask what this was. Here's the whole answer, and don't add to it: we
> spent an hour on the ordinary friction in our week, and I've picked one thing I'm changing and one
> thing I'll say out loud when I see somebody else do it. That's it. It's small on purpose.
>
> And be straight about what it isn't, because this is the bit that gets people into trouble. **This
> is not a safety control. It isn't a hazard assessment, it doesn't tell anyone whether anything here
> is safe or unsafe, and it doesn't discharge one thing this organisation owes anybody.** If it gets
> written up as any of those, that's wrong, and you're the person standing closest to it.
>
> [The community line goes here. See the note below: it says what exists on the day, and nothing that
> doesn't.]
>
> That's the hour. One moment, one move, one sentence."

⛔ **The negative-scope paragraph is `AG-011` / `AG-012` and is never compressed into a clause, never
moved earlier to save time, and never softened because the room is in a good mood at the end.** Both
register entries are `pending-approval`; no grading exists and none may be asserted.

⚠ **The community line is left as a bracket deliberately.** It cannot be written until it is known
whether the page resolves on the day, and writing a plausible sentence now is how a sign-up ends up
pointing at nothing. **This is a real dependency, not an omission.** The Leadership Community
sign-up goes in every service (ruling 29b) and is **not** blocked — see the reconciled note below,
which is now stated in these words in all four products.

⚠ ⚑ **RECONCILED ACROSS ALL FOUR PRODUCTS, 2026-09-10 — this beat is IN, and it is not cut.**
`CLAUDE.md` §4a applied and shown, rather than each product deciding for itself. Ruling **29b** is
Hunter, 2026-09-09, *correcting* a proposal to keep the sign-up out of the paid hour: *"I think that
should be in every single service that we do."* `D-0111` §3's build gate has **no external basis** —

ruling is the better evidence and **the internal gate is not enforced meanwhile.**

⛔ **The one real constraint is practical, not a gate.** The beat must promise nothing that does not
exist on the day: no frequency, no format, no description of what a person gets. **If a destination
resolves on the day it is named; if it does not, the beat says what is coming and how to hear about
it, and the leave-behind carries nothing.** The wording above is written to survive both cases.
