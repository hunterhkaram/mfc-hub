## 2.4 · The structure finding, and what it means

`R2` §3a, from the 8 providers with published in-house prices: **the dominant published structure is a
flat session fee for a capped group.** Where a per-head element exists it is a public-course seat
price or a **materials pass-through of $14–$50 per person.** **No Australian provider found publishes
MFC's structure — a substantial flat base plus a substantial per-head margin line.**

$95/head against that pass-through: **1.9× PTN's full-day $50, 3.2× its half-day $30, 6.8× its
presentation $14.** Small Team's $60/head above six is 2.0× the half-day figure.

**What it means, in four parts.**

1. **The number is right and the label is wrong.** $95/head is margin. MFC's marginal cost of the
   20th attendee is roughly **$7.50 of printing**, so MFC cannot answer *"what does the twentieth
   person cost you?"* honestly without conceding it. `15-THE-MONEY-CASE.md` §2 **relies** on that
   margin — it is the whole difference between $139.74 and $212.26 per engagement hour — so the
   answer is not to remove it. **It is to stop presenting it as though it scaled with cost.**
2. **It prices against MFC's own mission.** Every person a manager removes from the room saves them
   $95, in a charity whose purpose is that *more* people get the capability. **A committee will
   notice this.**
3. **It chooses the wrong competitor set.** Base+per-head *forces* the buyer to divide, and
   $5,800 ÷ 20 = **$290/head** invites comparison against the $15–$80 per-person seminar market
   rather than the $1,600–$4,600 in-house half-day market where MFC actually competes.
4. **It is procurement friction out of proportion to the value** — a variable that cannot be
   finalised until attendance is known. ⚠ **This fourth argument is `R2`'s own, and `R2` labels it
   argument, not verified finding (§3c). Do not present it as evidence.**

**§6.2 acts on this by changing the presentation, not the level — and says plainly where that is not
cost-free.**

## 3.2 · Ranked verdict

### Genuinely worth a premium — four, in order

**1 · The manager session.** $139 of cost inside a $1,445–$5,800 product. The only element where the
corpus, after an exhaustive grep, finds **no competitor selling anything of the same shape.** A
procurement person agrees for a reason that has nothing to do with MFC: **supervisor support ρ = .31
and work environment ρ = .23 are the strongest predictors a training designer actually controls**
(Blume, via `38:276`), against **d = .07 distal without it** (Vanhove). It is also the element that
touches WHS psychosocial language a risk-and-safety buyer already has budget lines for.

⛔ **The honest counterweight, which belongs here and not in a footnote: it is the element MFC can
least evidence.** `10-TEACH-CLAIM-MEASURE-CHAIN.md` row 8 — the environment limit the manager session
exists to serve — is named there as *"the single most important row in this table"* and it *"rests on
an instrument nobody has."* The manager session itself is **deliberately never measured** (`06` §3.2:
*"Collecting it would produce the version that sounds good"*). **MFC's best premium is a design
argument, not a demonstrated one, and must be sold as exactly that.**

**2 · The return session.** $162 (Essentials) to $386 (Foundations). The only competitor explicitly
recorded as lacking participant follow-up is MHFA, and the corpus's own judgement of that gap was
written by a source not trying to make MFC's argument. It satisfies `AG-156` (Rob Dooley, HARD):
*"If you do this as a one-off activity and there's no follow-up, you've missed an opportunity."* It
carries independent effect evidence (~6pp) MFC may cite as literature rather than as its own result.
**A procurement person agrees because it converts a line item into a schedule:** two dates in a diary
is a programme, one date is an event, and events are what wellbeing budgets are already embarrassed
about.

**3 · Enactment measurement — the design, not the report.** The field concedes it cannot measure
enactment; `34` §4.1's application column is a row of *No*; attempt-counting is unique in a register
of 22 claimants because **every other unit is counted by completion.** **What is worth the premium is
being measured at all, on an instrument aimed at the thing everyone else skips — not the artefact
that comes back.**

**4 · The refusal itself — the cap, the no-results honesty, and published pricing.** These cost MFC
nothing and are the cheapest credibility in the register. **Nine of ~22 claimants publish a price;
MFC's ladder does.** Gotcha4Life earns **$2,373,247 of fee-for-service (45.8% of FY24 revenue) and
publishes no workshop price at all** — *"the sharpest transparency gap in the Australian set."* And
§1's *"We have not yet delivered a measured cohort. We have no results to show you and we are not
going to invent any"* sits in a market whose published numbers are post-only self-report, with one
flagship study **post-test only, no pre-test, no randomisation, n=40,149.** A buyer who has read two
competitor impact PDFs recognises that paragraph immediately. **Free to give, impossible for a $5M
incumbent to copy without retracting its own collateral.**

### Table stakes — do not price on these

- **The workshop hour.** `AG-089` puts comparable work at $3,000–$5,000 a day; Foundations at $5,800
  for a 20-person room is **inside that, not above it**.
- **The printed leave-behind.** At $1.50 per participant reached MFC's is **a tenth to a twentieth**
  of MHFA's, TRP's and LivingWorks'. It is a safety obligation discharged (`AG-004` referral panel),
  **not a value driver** — copy that leans on "materials included" invites a comparison MFC loses.
- **A digital option.** Verified at AIS (free), Gotcha4Life, Smiling Mind, Project Rockit and TRP.
- **A group cap.** MHFA caps at 24 and licence-lapses instructors who breach it.

### What MFC over-values — five, plainly

**1 · The written report, sold as a feature to every product.** §1 tells a Small Team buyer they get
*"a written report on the same terms as everything else."* **(i)** §4.2 carries **zero report hours
for Small Team.** **(ii)** A room of 3–8 people, where fewer than five respond, gets a **delivery
confirmation only**; at 5–9 it gets tier 1 with no counts, no proportions, no ordering. **(iii)** `06`
§3.0.3 states remote Essentials cohorts will likely sit below the reporting floor and produce **no
written report at all.** Meanwhile term 7 already tells the buyer *"if you need a number for a board
paper, we cannot give you one."* **A deliverable that may not exist, may contain no numbers, and
cannot be used for the purpose a buyer would want it for is not a premium feature. It is a liability
with a delivery date.** ⚑ **The clearest overstatement found, and it is live in buyer-facing copy.**

**2 · "The environment layer is inside the product, not an extra."** True of Essentials and
Foundations. **False of Small Team Essentials and the Introduction**, neither of which has a manager
session. The sentence appears on a one-pager selling all four.

**3 · The "case-study year."** As a saleable object it does not exist: the year-long promise was
removed on 2026-09-04, the case-study **rate** is an unfilled decision blank, `D-0105`'s four

**4 · The Introduction as a paid product.** $600 against a $511.30 floor is **1.17×, $88.70 of

**5 · Small Team Essentials as a distinct rung.** Small Team and Essentials both show **150 guided

# 4 · THE ENTICEMENT QUESTION

*Owner: `head-of-marketing`, with a bounded `head-of-compliance` adjudication at §4.7. Every
buyer-facing sentence traces to a numbered line of `11-PERMITTED-CLAIMS.md` or is a commercial or
factual statement making no claim about outcomes. Where a sentence is not yet licensed it is marked
and the reason given rather than softened.*

## 4.1 · The problem, stated exactly

**What the buyer wants to hear.** `AG-084`, Rob Dooley: business buyers buy return on investment,
because *"everything is around how I can increase the profitability for the shareholders."* In NSW
the form of that is now sharper than it has ever been — `04-THE-STRATEGY.md` §1.1B records the five
triggers a commercial HR lead actually works to: **s.26A of the WHS Act 2011 (NSW) from 1 July 2026;
psychological claims at 12% of claims and 38% of NSW scheme cost; twenty new psychosocial inspectors
with on-the-spot fine powers; a bad psychosocial survey result; and the April–June FY27 budget
cycle.** The sentence that closes that buyer in one line is *"this reduces your psychosocial risk,
your claims exposure and your turnover."*

**What MFC may say.** Nothing of the kind, and the prohibition comes from four independent
directions:

- **`AG-001`** (Eimear, HARD): never make outcome claims; say the programme *targets* X and that the
  research shows Y.
- **`K-08` / `D-0104`:** *"reduces burnout / absenteeism / turnover"* is barred, and so is the
  softened form *"helps with"* — `11-PERMITTED-CLAIMS.md` §2 names it *"the assertion wearing a
  coat."* `D-0104` also closes the loophole: **no because / for-that-reason / specifically-for clause
  may tie a cited outcome back to purchase motivation, even with no causal verb present.**
- **`25-whs-psychosocial-and-mfc-fit.md` §3.1 red line 7:** MFC cannot claim its intervention reduces
  psychological injury claims or workers' compensation costs. Red line 1: the duty is non-delegable
  (WHS Act s14, s272). Red line 2: in Victoria, reg 15(4) means selling MFC as a predominant control
  would be selling the customer into non-compliance.
- **The evidence position.** Zero delivered cohorts, zero measured participants, zero case studies,
  **zero buyer interviews.**

**And the counter-pressure is on the record from the same committee.** `AG-292` (Gina): *"If people
don't know what the benefits COULD be, they simply won't buy."* `AG-293`: *"Yes. But what CAN we
claim?"* `AG-020`, `AG-025`, `AG-026`: front-load what is in it for me; the proposition today is
*"too weak, buried."*

**The size of the gap, measured.** Of the 37 permitted lines on `11-PERMITTED-CLAIMS.md`, exactly
**two** touch organisational benefit at all: `P26` (*"may support an organisation's psychosocial risk
controls"*) and `P29` (*"may support mental health and functioning"*), and `P29` is barred from
headlines. The one line built to carry a buyer-facing benefit, **`P27`'s target-and-cite formula, is
marked NOT USABLE TODAY because its cite is not held** — an adversarial pass on 2026-09-02 found the
blanks filled with *"higher engagement and lower turnover intention"* and `10-sources/` holds no
record on either. **So the seller's benefit vocabulary is one hedged sentence with a "may" in it,
against a buyer whose decision criterion is a number.**

**That gap cannot be closed with copy.** It can be narrowed three other ways, and §4.2 uses all
three: **transfer risk with terms instead of with claims; substitute what the buyer receives for what
they are promised; and choose a buyer for whom the honesty is itself the product.**

⚑ **One structural finding that should change the pricing case, and it is not a copy point.**
`04-THE-STRATEGY.md` §1.1 defines the recommended primary segment partly on this exact property:
these buyers *"accept an evidence-honest proposition where a shareholder-facing buyer will demand the
ROI claim."* **Read with `AG-084`, that is the answer to the hard problem: the gap is closable in
Segment A and structurally not closable in Segment B.** Selling a claims-constrained product to a
shareholder-facing buyer is not a marketing difficulty to be written around; it is a mismatch. That

## 4.2 · Five framings that are enticing and permitted

Written as sentences a buyer reads. No em dashes, per Hunter's standing instruction.

**1 · Risk moved onto MFC by terms rather than by claims.**

> *"We are not going to tell you what this will do for your organisation, because we have not
> measured it yet and we will not invent it. What we will do instead is put ourselves on the hook:
> the manager session, the return session and the written report are inside the price and are never
> separately quoted, and if your people tell us on the day that the session was not worth their time,
> we will not invoice you for it."*

**Why it is enticing.** An outcome claim is only ever a proxy for seller confidence, and confidence
expressed in a contract costs the seller something while confidence expressed in a sentence costs
nothing. **This is the single highest-value move available and it makes zero outcome claims.** Every
competitor can say *"reduces burnout"*; almost none will decline an invoice.

**Why it is permitted.** Clause 1 is `P19`/`P20` in substance. Clause 2 is `P32`, a factual
commercial statement. Clause 3 is a **term of trade, not a claim** — it asserts nothing about what
happens in a person or an organisation, only what MFC will invoice. `D-0104` governs outcome claims;
a billing condition is outside its subject matter.

⚑ **Not licensed yet.** No satisfaction condition of any kind exists in `07` §2, which carries only

## 4.5 · Verdict: the gap is not fully closable, and pretending otherwise is the real risk

**No. Not fully, and not by any amount of skilful writing.** Copy cannot bridge the distance between
an organisational-outcome claim and a product with no measured participants. Only evidence can, and
MFC's evidence roadmap does not reach that far. Stated precisely:

- **Closable now, and largely is.** Everything a buyer evaluates *other than* promised outcome: what
  they receive, what it costs per person, what is inside the price rather than quoted on top, what
  MFC will and will not put its name to, and what happens if it goes badly. Framings 1–5 close this,
  **and framing 1 does not exist yet.**
- **What one measured cohort closes.** After the first Foundations delivery plus its return, MFC
  holds comprehension before and after and attempted reps at three weeks (`P23`). That is roughly
  **four to five weeks after a first booking**, and it licenses statements about **comprehension and
  initial-use attempts only.**
- **What five to ten cohorts close.** A defensible pattern statement across cohorts, and the
  pre-condition `P27` actually needs: a comparable study registered in `10-sources/`, matched on
  population, dose and format. Realistically **nine to eighteen months**, and it still yields
  comprehension and attempt data, not turnover data.
- ⛔ **Not closable at any horizon MFC can reach.** *"This reduces your psychosocial risk, your
  turnover, your absenteeism."* That requires a controlled study with organisational outcome measures
  over time; `25-...` §3.3 finds the evidence does not robustly exist for the whole category, let
  alone MFC. On ~$12,000 of reserves MFC will not run it. **Treat as permanently unavailable, not as
  pending.**

**The consequence for pricing, and it is the substantive one.** The buyer who requires that sentence

## Model (d) — the fourth model: a brokered cohort block

**Four candidates considered. Three ruled out, with reasons.**

- ⛔ **Licensed / trained-facilitator diffusion.** `mfc-master-strategy-v1/15-rationale-licensed-facilitator.md`:

  funding answer — a ~$15m endowment over two decades, **median licensed instructor running 3.7

- ⛔ **Open public enrolment per seat.** Comparators exist ($385 Lifeline Tas, $1,150 AIM, $220–325

# ⚑ FOUNDER QUESTIONS — protected, and separate

*Five, each with a recommendation and a confidence score. Everything else in this paper was decided.*

**FQ1 — Will MFC put a satisfaction condition in its terms?**
> If participants tell MFC on the day that the session was not worth their time, MFC does not invoice
> for that session. **This is the strongest enticement device available to a seller who may not make
> outcome claims, and it costs nothing unless the product is bad.** `07` §2 currently has **no
> satisfaction condition of any kind** — only cancellation and reschedule at terms 8 and 9.
> **Recommendation: yes, in that narrow form.** Protected under §6a condition 5 (an irreversible
> financial commitment inside a contract). Needs `head-of-delivery` for the mechanism (who is asked,
> when, what the threshold is) and `head-of-compliance` before it is offered.
> **Confidence 7/10** — not higher because MFC has never delivered a session and has no basis for
> estimating how often it would be invoked, and at $3,900 a single invocation is material against
> ~$12,000 of reserves.

**FQ2 — Does Foundations become a banded flat fee, knowing it raises the smallest rooms by up to 9.3%?**
> Every published Australian comparator uses a flat fee for a capped group; MFC's base+per-head
> structure has no market precedent, prices against MFC's own mission (each person a manager removes
> saves them $95), and cannot survive *"what does the twentieth person cost you?"* honestly. **But
> banding at the top of each range is a real price rise — up to 9.3%, and it lands hardest on the
> smallest room, a cohort of two or three — not a presentational change, and it moves a number you
> ratified on 2026-09-04.**
> **Recommendation: yes, band it** — but note that the register records your own contrary reasoning
> (`REGISTER.md:518`) alongside the three advisors who want prices up, and this is a §6a condition 6
> question where your judgement is the deciding variable.
> **Confidence 4/10 — the weakest recommendation in this paper.**

**FQ3 — Does MFC add a fourth priced product with a rolling monthly obligation?**
> The retainer returns **$114.21 per engagement hour with no new sale** — below Foundations at every
> headcount, above Essentials and Small Team. Its argument is that it earns without a new sale, not
> that it earns most.
> `07` §4.6 retired *the bundled free promise*, not *the paid product*, and the load argument it made
> dissolves once the hours are paid. **But it would be MFC's longest-duration promise, made by one
> facilitator with no SLA (`AG-232`), no cover for illness or holiday, and no cancellation term
> written for a recurring product — and the session itself has never been designed or run.**
> **Recommendation: reinstate it, capped at eight concurrent clients, not sold until three paid
> engagements have completed their return session, and not quoted until the SLA and
> recurring-cancellation term are written.**
> **Confidence 6/10.**

**FQ4 — Does MFC set a partner/channel price so a peak body can broker cohorts?**
> Model (d) returns **$26,369 of margin on 121 delivery hours from one relationship.** **But `D-0125`
> deleted every discount concept on your own direct instruction, and a broker — particularly CCWT,
> an RTO already selling to this audience — will expect a margin share or accreditation MFC cannot
> give.** Deciding this mid-negotiation is the expensive way to decide it.
> **Recommendation: decide it before the first call, and make the concession in kind rather than in
> price** — MFC delivers a free Introduction to the peak body's own staff (loaded cost $439.56
> digital) rather than discounting a cohort. That satisfies `AG-083`.
> **Confidence 5/10** — no vault source records what any Australian peak body actually takes.

**FQ5 — Which buyer is MFC pricing for, given two records conflict?**
> `D1-who-actually-pays.md` §1 (**founder decision, 2026-08-10**) says corporates and businesses are
