# The quality checklists

**Hunter, 2026-09-09:**

> *"We've run our agents. We've reviewed it. We've analysed everything. We have ticked every single
> box of the checklist. Yes, this is now ready. I still haven't reviewed it. So that's a part of the
> thing that isn't on the checklist."*

> *"If you look at humans — what are the things that make these things great, memorable, can lead to
> the change? That's what our checklist needs to be built upon."*

**What this fixes.** MFC has 44 executable controls and none of them judges whether any piece of work
is any good. A thing can pass every one and still be a session nobody wants to sit in. **These
checklists ask the other question.**

---

## Rule zero — nothing is final until Hunter has read it

**This row appears on every session, and it is the last one.**

> ⛔ **Has Hunter read this and approved it?**

**No amount of agent review substitutes for it, and no artefact may be described as final, ready or
approved while it reads NO.** Hunter, directly: *"nothing gets finalised until that happens."*

**And one row below it will read NO for a long time, deliberately:** *has this been run with real
people?* The ledger exists to show the ceiling — **once every other box is green, the only thing left
is a real room.** No further analysis moves it.

## Checklist A · An ACTIVITY

**Anything a participant does** — silent thinking, a physical movement, a paired exchange, a written
plan, walking a room. Nine tests. Score each **PASS · PARTIAL · FAIL**, with a reason.

| # | Test | What a FAIL looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | **Barrier to entry.** Can a person do it without standing up, speaking, writing, or being seen to participate? | It requires movement, disclosure or a pen with no equivalent offered aloud. |
| **A2** | **Exposure risk.** Can this reveal something about a person they did not choose to reveal — to the facilitator, to a colleague, or to a manager in the room? | Choosing a category, a group, or a visible position tells the room something personal. |
| **A3** | **Mechanism.** Is there a real reason this produces the effect claimed, drawn from adjacent established literature and stated as a mechanism, not an outcome? | It is an activity because sessions have activities. |
| **A4** | **Engagement.** Will a sceptical adult lean in, or wait it out? | It only works for people already bought in. |
| **A5** | **Dignity.** Does this feel like being taken seriously — or like *"another bloody activity another organisation is making me do"*? | It is performative, cute, or asks adults to play. |
| **A6** | **Thursday value.** Does the participant leave with something usable in a real moment, not a nice feeling? | The value is entirely inside the room. |
| **A7** | **Failure mode.** When this goes wrong, **is there a route the facilitator can actually take** — words to say, and somewhere for the person to go if they need it? | ⛔ **A row that says the route does not exist is a FAIL, not a PASS.** A row naming an escalation MFC has not cleared is a FAIL. A row that instructs the facilitator to breach a ruling to recover is a FAIL. |
| **A8** | **One facilitator.** Can one person run it, alone, with 20 people? | It needs a second pair of hands MFC does not have. |
| **A9** | **Accessibility.** Does it work seated, silent, and without writing — and is the equivalent offered **aloud, before it starts**, not on request? ⚡ **And does the equivalent survive the whole activity**, not just its opening? | The equivalent exists on paper but nobody says it. ⛔ **Or it is offered at the door and then contradicted by a later instruction** — an alternative route that cannot complete the activity is not an alternative route. |

⛔ **A2 and A9 are gates, not scores.** An activity that fails either does not run, whatever else it
scores.

## Checklist B · A SPOKEN BEAT

**Anything the facilitator says** that carries an argument. Six tests.

| # | Test | What a FAIL looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **Cold clarity.** Would someone who has never heard any of this follow it, first time, without stopping? | It assumes a term, a model or a prior beat. |
| **B2** | **Curiosity.** Does it open a gap the next beat closes — or does it close everything and leave nothing pulling forward? | It is complete, correct, and inert. |
| **B3** | **Recognition.** Does a listener see themselves in it, or is it about people in general? | It describes a category of person rather than a moment they have had. |
| **B4** | **Claim safety.** `K-08` and `AG-001`: mechanism not outcome, *can* not *will*, no causal claim MFC cannot support. | An outcome asserted in the present indicative. |
| **B5** | **His voice.** Recognition not persuasion. Not markety. Honest without being apologetic. ⚡ **Scored on the words a facilitator actually says — the `>` quoted lines — never on stage directions, rationale or design notes.** | It sounds like a brand wrote it. ⛔ **Do not score a whole file's punctuation.** On 2026-09-10 the corpus held 417 em dashes and **only 126 were in spoken text**; the manager session, a standing FAIL three passes running, had **one**. The count rose as the documents got more honest. |
| **B6** | **Portability.** Could someone repeat the point to a colleague that night, in one sentence? | It only survives inside the full argument. |

## Checklist C · A MEASUREMENT COMPONENT

**Anything that captures data.** Four tests.

| # | Test | What a FAIL looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **Construct.** Does it capture the thing it claims to capture? | It measures satisfaction and is reported as change. |
| **C2** | **Completion.** Will people actually finish it, in the conditions it runs in? | It assumes a response rate nobody has ever observed. |
| **C3** | **Privacy.** De-identified, no small-cell re-identification, nothing attributable. | A number describes a person in a group that size. |
| **C4** | **Reportability.** Can what it produces be said out loud to a buyer within `K-08`? | It generates a figure MFC is not permitted to state. |

## Checklist D · FLOW — run across a whole session, not a part

**Added on Hunter's instruction, 2026-09-09.** His words:

> *"If this was a speech, if this was a conversation — is someone going, yeah, look at the red banana,
> oh my god bananas, oh my god monkeys? Is there too much jumping around? Is there a case of flow to
> it?"*

| # | Test | What a FAIL looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **Sequence.** Does each beat follow from the one before, or is it a list of good things in an order? | Two adjacent beats could swap with no loss. |
| **D2** | **Curse of knowledge.** Is anything explained at a level the audience has not been built up to? | A level-five explanation sits beside a level-three one. |
| **D3** | **Activity fit.** Does each activity relate to what was just taught, or is it bolted on? | The activity would work equally well anywhere in the session. |
| **D4** | **One argument.** Does the whole session make a single case, or several unrelated ones? | It is three good talks in a row. |
| **D5** | **Load.** Is the room asked to do too much, too close together? | Two demanding devices back to back with no recovery. |

## Checklist E · PROPAGATION — run across files, not inside one

⛔ **Added 2026-09-10, after the first improvement pass proved every other test blind to this.**
All twenty-four tests in A–D are **within-artefact**: they read one file and score it. The four
products share one spine, so **an edit to the shortest silently breaks the other three and the records
that govern them** — and on 2026-09-10 that is exactly what happened, within a day of the pattern
being named. **Not one test could see it.**

**Run E on every change that touches a shared beat, device, term or ruling. Five tests.**

| # | Test | What a FAIL looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **Orphaned references.** After this change, does any other file still name a device, beat or term that no longer exists? | A cut device is still referenced in a sibling product, the card set, or a governing record. |
| **E2** | **Governing records.** If the change contradicts `22-THE-PARTICIPANT-FOUNDATION.md`, `00-THE-RULINGS.md` or a `D-` record, has that been reconciled or surfaced? | The governing record and the product now disagree and nobody said so. |
| **E3** | **Sibling consistency.** Do the four products now give **opposite** answers to the same question? | Three files cut a beat as blocked; the fourth declares the gate invalid and keeps it. |
| **E4** | **Superseded material marked.** Anything this change supersedes carries `superseded_by:` and a status change **in the same change**. | A file stays on `main`, fully written, describing a version that no longer exists. |
| **E5** | **Point-in-time records dated.** An analysis or score that describes a prior version is marked as such, not left reading as current. | A flow check or assessment is quoted as the current state of a product it no longer describes. |

⛔ **E1 and E3 are gates.** A change that orphans a reference or makes two products contradict each
other is not finished, however well it scores on A–D.

⛔ **E1 and E4 run across the whole repository, or they do not run.** On 2026-09-10 E1 was scored
against a hand-picked five-file table **in the same pass that made it a gate**, and passed — while two
live orphans sat outside the list, one of them in the page Hunter reads. **A propagation test scoped
to the files you already knew about tests nothing.** Grep the repo; do not work from a list someone
compiled.

⚠ **And count what a reference DOES, not that it exists.** A file saying *"this device was cut"*
contains the device's name. On 2026-09-10 raw counts of a cut device **rose** while the defect was
being closed. **Read the line. A correction is not an orphan.**

## The anti-gaming rules — read before scoring anything

⛔ **Added 2026-09-10, after the first improvement pass cleared eight failures without changing one
word a participant would experience.**

**Rule 1 — Score the participant's experience, not the artefact.** A finding closed by **deleting the
thing** is not closed unless the participant is no better off without it. **A device moved somewhere
that does not exist yet is not fixed, it is homeless** — and the score must go DOWN, not up, because
yesterday the participant had it and today they do not.

**Rule 2 — A record of a route is not a route.** This is `A7`'s whole failure. Writing a table row
saying what a facilitator would do satisfies nothing if the thing it points at has not been built,
cleared, or written. **Ask what actually happens to the person in the room.**

**Rule 3 — A disclosure is not a fix.** Naming a problem honestly in a file is worth a great deal and
it is **not** a score. A design that says *"this fails and here is why"* still fails.

**Rule 4 — Verify the changelog, never quote it.** A fixer's account of what it changed is a claim,
not evidence. **On 2026-09-10 a changelog stated five endings had been cut to one; two had gone, and
the omitted one had been lengthened in the same pass.** That reached the founder as fact.
**Re-derive every claim from the file.**

**Rule 5 — A test that cannot be failed, or cannot be passed, is not a test.** If a criterion fails
100% of items twice running, or clears without the product changing, **the criterion is the defect.**
Say so in the score. **The checklist is under assessment too.**

## The readiness ledger — one per session

**Seven rows. This is what Hunter reads to know where a product actually stands.**

| Row | Means |
|---|---|
| **Built** | A complete design or script exists. |
| **Checked against the rulings** | Run against all 124, including the 17 anti-rulings. |
| **Adversarially attacked** | A fresh context tried to break it, and findings were applied. |
| **Gap-checked against Hunter's own words** | His captures read against the product for what is absent. |
| **Claims-checked** | `K-08`, `AG-001` and the advisor register applied to every participant-facing line. |
| **Compass approved** | The founder lens has said what Hunter would say — see below. |
| ⛔ **Hunter has read and approved** | **Nothing is final without this.** |
| **Run with real people** | **Expected to read NO. This is the ceiling of what AI review can establish.** |

## How options are presented

**Hunter's instruction: three options per activity, and no recommendation from me.**

> *"For each activity you present in Essentials and Foundations, you should give me three options…
> And I don't want to know the one you recommend."*

**Each option is scored on four columns, and the fourth is the one nobody writes down:**

| Column | The question |
|---|---|
| **Barrier** | How easy is it to take part without exposing yourself? |
| **Evidence** | What actually backs the mechanism? |
| **Engagement** | Will a sceptical adult lean in? |
| ⚡ **Resentment risk** | **How likely is a participant to think: another activity someone is making me do?** |

**Resentment risk decides whether the room leans in or waits it out, and it is the column that is
never on a design checklist.** Hunter: *"if they're not actually open and wanting to learn and
practise it, it's not applicable, and it's a waste of our time and their time."*

---

## What this is not

**These checklists do not establish that anything works.** They establish that a thing is well made,
safe, in his voice, and worth a participant's time. **Whether it produces change is a question only a
real room answers**, and no row here should ever be read as evidence that it does.
