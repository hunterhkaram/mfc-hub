# The hour, as it is said

**Register: teaching.** Second person, unhedged, analogies doing the work, lands flat.
Composed from `16-how-hunter-communicates.md` §10, not from committee-transcript sentences.

**Marking.** `[H]` = Hunter's own words, from the 2026-09-07 capture or approved script — do not
rewrite. `[H*]` = his words, grammar tidied for speech; the tidy is listed at the foot of this file.
`[D]` = drafted for him to make his own. `[BRIDGE]` = connective tissue, drafted.
`⛔` = never cut. `C1`–`C5` = the cut order, marked where the cut lands.

**Four rules that govern every line:** nothing is asked out loud about a person's own state ·
nothing is collected · **every question about the participant themselves is asked, held briefly,
and answered by him — no answers taken** · nobody writes anything.

## BEAT 6 · The four, and the five skills named

**[BRIDGE]**
> So if that's what it is — what's it actually made of?

**[H — the basis]**
> In any moment — right now, sitting here — four things are going on at once.
>
> You're **thinking**. There's something running, even if it's *how long is this*.
> You're **feeling**. Not necessarily dramatic. Bored is a feeling. Fine is a feeling.
> You're **acting**. Sitting still is an action. Checking your phone is an action.
> You're **connecting** — or not. There are people either side of you and you're doing something
> about that, including nothing.
>
> That's the whole basis. Thinking, feeling, acting, connecting.
>
> Why does it matter? Two reasons. First, when a moment goes badly it's usually because one of
> those four ran the show and the other three never got a say. Second — and this is the useful
> bit — four things means four ways in. When one is jammed, another one usually isn't. **You can't
> always change what you're feeling. You can nearly always change what you're doing.**

**[H — the five skills. Fifteen seconds. ⛔ The second sentence NEVER CUT]**
> And underneath those four there are five things we're actually training. **Notice. Shift.
> Choose. Focus. Connect.**
>
> **And here's why those five.** Every moment that goes badly, one of them was the part that
> failed. You didn't catch it — that's Notice. You caught it and couldn't shift out of it —
> that's Shift. You shifted and still did the thing you always do — that's Choose. You knew
> what mattered and couldn't stay on it — that's Focus. Or the whole thing was about somebody else
> and it went the way it always goes — that's Connect.
>
> **Five skills, because there are five ways a moment gets away from you.** I'm not going to teach
> you all five in an hour — but that's what's under the bonnet.

## BEAT 9 · Stop, Shift, Do. And the unit ⛔ **NEVER CUT**

**[BRIDGE]**
> So that's where. **Third thing — here's how you actually train it.** Three steps, and you've
> already done the first one.

**[H* — approved script]**
> **Stop.** Stop is not calming down. Stop is noticing that a moment is happening **while it's
> still happening.** That's all. You did one at minute seven. It's hard because by the time most
> of us notice, we've already sent the reply.
>
> **Shift.** Shift is changing one thing so the moment becomes workable. Not nice. Not calm.
> **Workable — and sometimes calm is the wrong thing to want anyway.**
>
> And let me be blunt about calm, because this is where a lot of this stuff loses people. **Nobody
> has ever calmed down just because someone told them to.** If your fix needs a quiet room and ten
> minutes, it will never once happen at twenty to four on a Wednesday. It isn't available.
> Pretending it is, is why this stuff gets binned.
>
> So what's available? You can shift your state, or you can shift your thinking.
>
> **A state you're stuck in — tense, flooded, checked out, foggy — isn't a fixed fact about the
> moment. It's a pattern you fell into, and patterns can be interrupted.** There are four ways in.
> **I'm going to show you all four, and I'm not going to make you do any of them.**
>
> **Body.** Change what your body is doing, or where your senses are — it can bring you down or bring
> you up. The state usually shows up there first — tight shoulders, jaw. Feet into the floor. Drop
> your shoulders. Cold water on your hands. Stand up and shake it out. Look around the room and let
> your eyes land on something. Listen for three sounds. A slower breath is one option, never the
> required one. **Changing something physical can move it, sometimes faster than trying to think
> your way out.**
>
> **Thinking.** Read it differently. Sometimes it isn't in your body, it's in how you're reading the
> moment. What would I tell a mate right now? What else could be going on?
>
> **Place.** Change what's around you. Phone on charge in another room. Step away from the smoking
> spot. *"I need ten minutes, then I'm back."*
>
> **People.** Reach someone who isn't part of it. *"Can I vent for two minutes?"* **Some states are
> hard to shift alone — not because you're doing it wrong, but because they were never meant to be
> handled alone.**
>
> And if it's more than a rough moment, see your GP, or call Lifeline on 13 11 14. In an emergency,
> call 000.
>
> **Shift your body or your thinking. Your place and your people can help you get there.**
>
> **And every one of them is answering the same question: too much, too little, or workable?**
> **Too much, shift down. Too little, shift up.** Already workable, there may be nothing here you
> need.
>
> When you're wound right up, start with Body or Place. Thinking usually works better once there's a
> bit of space. **The point is that you have one to reach for** — not that there's a right one.
>
> The test is one question: **can I do the next thing from here?** That's the bar. Not calm.
> Workable.
>
> And there's a version of that you can ask before you pick one. **We call it the State Check.**
> **Is there too much in me right now, too little, or is this workable?** That's it. Not *what* am I feeling — *how much*.
> You're not scoring it and I'm never going to ask you for your answer.
>
> **Do.** The next move, however small. Send it in an hour. Walk over instead of typing. Send the
> boring version — *got it, will look at this after three.* Or send exactly what you were going to
> send anyway, **having actually decided to.** That last one counts. This isn't about being nicer.
> It's about the moment having more than one option in it.
>
> **Not just decided. Decided toward what actually matters to you** in that bit of your life.

## BEAT 10 · Specificity of practice, and the knowing–doing gap · **the centre of the talk** · **C3**

**[BRIDGE]**
> Now — why practise it there? Why not just go and learn it somewhere quieter and bring it back?

**[H — the argument that does the work]**
> **A calf raise builds your calves. A bench press builds your chest.** That doesn't mean either
> one translates into the moment you actually need it.
>
> An athlete doesn't just get strong. They practise **the specific skill for match day.** Over and
> over. In something that looks like the real thing.
>
> So — as good as meditation is, as an isolated method — **it doesn't mean that tonight, in this
> moment, you'll catch, shift and do.** It might help you catch. It might not help you shift.
>
> **What if we practised catching, shifting and doing in the life we're already living?**

**[H — the knowing–doing gap. ⚑ C3 cuts from here to the end of this block]**
> Let me put it another way. **How many of us have heard of breath work?** Nearly everyone. We
> just did ninety seconds of it.
>
> **But doing breath work last week with a practitioner doesn't mean that in this moment, things
> are going to change.** We conceptually understand what we could do. The question is: **can we
> apply it in the moment?**
>
> Watching one more episode late at night. The scroll. The food before bed. The conversation that
> went on past when we should've stopped. And we wake up and think — **I shouldn't have done that.
> Even though I knew better.**
>
> **That isn't a missing skill. That's the gap between having a skill and applying it in a
> specific moment.**

**[H — be kind, be calm]**
> And here's what we do really well in society. **We tell people what we want them to do.** Be
> kind. Be nice. Be calm. Be this, be that.
>
> But if people don't have a basic understanding of how their own system works, it's really hard —
> because they don't know better. **And we need to know better to do better.** But that doesn't
> mean you're always going to do better.
>
> **That's the gap we're trying to solve. And that's Stop, Shift, Do.**
