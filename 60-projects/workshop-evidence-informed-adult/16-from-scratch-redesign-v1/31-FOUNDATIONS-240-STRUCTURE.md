# Foundations, as it goes

## ⚑ The one sentence this day argues — ADDED 2026-09-10

> **You leave with one ordinary moment out of your own life, and you spend the afternoon making it
> survive the version of itself that actually goes wrong.**

**Why this sentence, and what it settles.** The flow check could not write one, because the file was
carrying two arguments and subordinating neither: *you will learn what to do when this fails*, and
*you will leave with a moment*. Both are good. **The moment is the object; the failure work is what
makes the object worth having.** That is the subordination, and it is the only one that makes the
back half of the day one thing instead of two. A moment nobody has run against a bad day is a moment
that gets abandoned in week one, and a breakpoint map with no moment attached to it is a lecture.

⚑ **The alternative was live and is named rather than hidden.** The other defensible reading —
Foundations is *the product where you learn what to do when it fails*, and the moment is the worked
example — closes with the breakpoint map instead. **It was not chosen because ruling 29c makes the
moment gallery mandatory here and `[v3` §7 guardrail 5`]` says moment selection carries the stated
primary outcome.** If Hunter prefers the other reading, the change is one of order, not of content:
beat 12 becomes the close and beat 11 becomes the setup.

### What each movement does for that sentence

| Movement | What it does for the sentence | Honest reading |
|---|---|---|
| **I** — arrival, the door, the gap, the breath | Establishes there is a moment at all, and that catching one is a thing a person can do rather than a thing they know about. Activity 1 makes the room supply the evidence, the breath gives them one catch | **Serves it.** |
| **II** — naming, the category, the skills, muscle memory | Gives the catch a name and gives the room the three words it will use on its own moment for the next three hours. Notice, Choose and Shift are the filter the gallery runs on | **Serves it, with one exception.** Focus and Connect are taught at beat 6 and never used again in 240 minutes. That is two of five skills that do not serve the sentence, and the honest reading is three working skills and two decorative ones. Recorded, not fixed here. |
| **III** — the practice site, the method, the specificity argument | Establishes *why it has to be a moment of yours and not a technique in general*, which is the sentence's whole load-bearing half | **Serves it.** Beat 10 is the centre for exactly this reason. |
| **IV** — running it, grace, the ripple, the Radar | Selects the moment (gallery), works it (studios, silent shift), then runs it against failure (breakpoint map, rehearsal with options removed) and hands over the thing that survives Thursday | **Serves it, and this is where the sentence is actually delivered.** |

**What does not serve it, said plainly rather than justified backwards.**

- **Activity 4, fold your arms (beat 7).** It demonstrates that a less-practised motor pattern feels
  wrong. It says nothing about anyone's moment, and beat 7 already says the same thing in words.
  It is left in because cutting it is a design decision beyond this pass, and it is the first thing
  that should go if the day needs a minute. **The assessment's own verdict is that it fails A5 and
  should be cut.** Open item 15.
- **The kids passage and the norms question at beat 13.** They serve the ripple, which is the peak,
  not the sentence. That is legitimate: beat 13 is deliberately the one beat that widens outward.
  Named so nobody later reverse-engineers a moment-related justification for them.

2. **Application studios.** Foundations only. Ruling 29d. The only small-group device MFC has ever
   designed that satisfies `AG-134` without exposing anyone.
3. **Four separate pieces of practice**, against Essentials' one and the Hour's none.
4. **Three spaced unaided retrievals**, recovered from the v3 pack, which is the strongest single

5. **The two arguments only a half day can carry**: the breakpoint map, and the case that separates
   a Do from its outcome so that private work does not turn into self-blame.

## The four rules that govern every beat, unchanged at 240

Nothing is asked out loud about a person's own state. Nothing is collected. **Nobody writes
anything.** Nobody speaks unless they want to. **Cap 20 with one facilitator**, 30 only with a
co-facilitator (`AG-131`, `AG-160`, ruling 8).

**⛔ The room cannot perform a Do, at any depth, ever** (ruling 6, `AG-010` HARD). Everything called
practice below works a moment the participant **already lived**, or is a Stop or a Shift with nothing
staged and nothing offered by anyone in the room. That rule binds the 240 exactly as hard as it binds
the 58, and it is the reason v3's ten-rep architecture is not carried whole.

**Hands up is allowed and wanted for recognition** (ruling 17): have you heard of this, have you been
in a situation like this. Never for a person's own state.

**The four routes are offered aloud, not merely permitted, at every block that asks for anything**
(`[v3` §6`]`): join in, think privately, use an invented example, observe, or say nothing at all.
In a half day this has to be said in the first two minutes and then honoured every single time.

# MOVEMENT I — arrival, the door, the gap, the breath

## BEAT 1 · The human, and the promise

**What changes in the room.** People stop calculating whether they will be made to speak, and start
listening. In a half day this has to happen faster and harder than anywhere else, because four hours
of unrelieved exposure anxiety is a different animal from sixty minutes of it.

**CARRIED in full** from the Hour, plus Essentials' workplace clause and its manager sentence.

**What must be said.**

`[E]` The exposure answer, unchanged:
> Nothing you think about in here is going to be said out loud, to me or to anyone in this room.
> There is no going round the table. There is no pairing up. Nobody is reporting back on anything.

`[v3` §2, permission block`]` The half-day addition, and it is the one thing beat 1 gains:
> You can join in, think privately, use an invented example, observe, or say nothing at all. Nothing
> is collected. Nobody is asked to share anything personal, at any point today.

`[v3` §2, shape of the day`]` And the shape, which only exists because the day is long enough to have
one:
> You'll do things today before I explain them. That's deliberate. And the parts that touch your own
> life come late, and stay private.

**⛔ No personal check-in round, ever.** `[v3` §2`]`: a half day creates an expectation of disclosure
that must be defeated in the first two minutes, not managed later.

**Foundations adds over Essentials:** the four routes and the shape of the day, said once, at the
front, because a long room needs to know when its own life gets involved and that it is late.

## BEAT 2 · The silent poll, the gap, and the car that never learned to brake

**What changes in the room.** "I already know this" becomes "I have never once practised this," and
then becomes "and that is not my fault."

**CARRIED in full** from the Hour: the poll, the hook, the CPR comparison, the braking analogy, the
red pen, the roads you stop taking. **Plus Essentials' two additions carried whole**: the picture in

**What must be said.**

`[E]` The structural version of the gap, which is what stops the room hearing all this as a personal

> Nobody would run physical health that way. We do not tell people to wait until their back goes and

`[H]` And ruling 52, which is the register of the whole beat:
> It's not on you. It has always happened. You have not learned these things, and that's a problem,
> not a fault.

**Foundations widens two things and adds one.**

`[F` card 13 `d3]` **The reactive-only pattern, named.** Mental health days, EAPs, a number to call,
a poster: things brought in to manage a problem after it exists. Essentials says we wait. Foundations
says what we do instead of building, and names it.

`[F` card 8b `d3]` **The watching passage gets its second half:** what a person can choose to pass on
differently. Essentials stops at "nobody asked you which was which." Foundations goes one step
further, and it is the step that connects beat 2 to beat 13's ripple.

`[v3` beat 1`]` **Why the gap stayed open.** No school subject, no recognisable venue, no shared
vocabulary, no visible unit of practice. ⛔ **That is a description of a gap, not a claim that other
approaches failed and not a claim that MFC has solved it. Do not disparage any other practice or
profession** (ruling 38, ruling 39, `K-08`).

### ⚡ ACTIVITY 1 · The technique that did not work
`[v3` beat 1, carried whole`]` **Foundations only.** The first thing anyone in the room *does*, and it
happens before any content, which is `AG-144` answered directly. ⚑ **The door has already been said**
(beat 3, delivered above): this activity is the reason it moved, because it asks a person to bring
back something that failed them, and that is an experience, not a preamble.

**What happens.** He asks the room to bring to mind one thing they were once given that was supposed
to help and did not.

**What participants physically do.** Nothing visible. They sit and think for about twenty seconds.
Nothing is said, nothing is written, no hands.

**What the facilitator does.** Says the prompt, says the boundary in the same breath, waits, does not
scan the room, and closes it himself:
> Think of something you were once given that was supposed to help and didn't. Don't tell me what it
> was.

then, after the silence:
> For a lot of people the technique wasn't the problem. Nobody gave them the process it was supposed
> to sit inside.

**Accessibility equivalent.** None needed beyond the standing routes: nothing is written, nothing is
said, nobody moves. If nothing comes to mind, that is a complete answer and he says so before the
silence starts, not after.

**Foundations adds over Essentials:** the room supplies its own evidence for the gap before he argues
it, instead of being told about it.

## BEAT 4 · The breath. Experience, with no name on it ⛔ **NEVER CUT**

**What changes in the room.** The room does a Stop before the word Stop exists. Experience first,
name second (ruling 9).

**CARRIED in full** from the Hour, plus Essentials' elicit-before-explain in front of it and its
payoff after it.

**What must be said.** `[E]` The elicitation, held in the head, nothing written:
> Nothing gets written down and nothing gets said. Just hold them in your head.

`[H]` And the debrief line, which is the single most important sentence in the beat and is spoken
exactly, every time:
> The rep was not maintaining perfect attention. The rep happened when you caught the drift and came
> back.

### ⚡ ACTIVITY 2 · The breath, ninety seconds
`[v3` R1, and Hunter's own worked example`]` Present in all three products. Foundations runs it at
ninety seconds, as v3 did, not sixty.

**What happens.** Ninety seconds of silence, with no name on it. The facilitator says nothing.

**What participants physically do.** Sit. Bring attention to the breath. When they notice attention
has wandered, bring it back. Nothing written, nothing said.

**What the facilitator does.** Sets it up, does it himself, and ⛔ **lets the silence run.** Recovery
row 3: do not narrate it, do not shorten it, do not fill it. If someone laughs or speaks, continue
without comment. Then the debrief line above, unchanged.

**Accessibility equivalent.** Eyes open or closed, both said aloud. Breath is one option, not a
requirement (`K-08` rule 6: do not force calming, breathwork, eye closure, disclosure, touch, partner
work or public sharing). A participant who prefers to rest attention on a sound in the room instead
is doing the same rep and is told so before it starts.

**Foundations adds over Essentials:** thirty more seconds, which is not a detail. The catch only
happens after attention has actually left, and at sixty seconds a portion of the room never gets
there.

### ⚡ ACTIVITY 3 · The body catch
`[v3` R2, carried with its Do removed`]` Foundations and Essentials.

**What happens.** A Stop performed on the body, before Shift has ever been defined, followed by an
optional Shift with an explicit permission not to.

**What participants physically do.** Notice where their shoulders are, their jaw, whether they are
leaning forward or back. Ten seconds. Then, if they want to, change one thing. Or not.

**What the facilitator does.** Says it, waits, and says the "or don't" without irony:
> Without moving yet, notice where your shoulders are. Your jaw. Whether you're leaning forward or
> back. Don't fix anything. Just find it. ... Now, if you want to, change one thing. Or don't,
> that's a real option.

⛔ **v3 added a required Do here on 2026-08-31** (*"decide, on purpose, what you're doing next"*).
**It is not carried.** Ruling 6 bars a staged Do at any depth, and v3's own note concedes that the
Do was facilitator-directed and that R2's "occurs without manufacture" rating was already marginal.
**The "or don't" is what survives, and it is the load-bearing half:** it stops Shift being heard as
an instruction to calm down, delivered physically before Shift is ever defined.

**Accessibility equivalent.** A participant who does not want to attend to their body attends to the
sound in the room instead, and is told that before it starts. Nobody is watched, nothing is checked.

**Foundations adds over Essentials:** nothing in the activity itself. The addition is the roles pass
on the five skills, above.

### ⚡ ACTIVITY 4 · Fold your arms
`[v3` beat 4, moved here`]` Fifteen seconds plus one framing sentence. Present in Foundations and
Essentials; here it earns its place at beat 7 rather than beat 10 because this is where repetition is
the subject.

**What happens.** Everyone folds their arms. Then folds them the other way.

**What participants physically do.** Fold, unfold, refold the wrong way. It feels wrong. That is the
whole demonstration.

**What the facilitator does.** Demonstrates first, then:
> That's harder, and it feels wrong, and it isn't wrong. It's just less practised. You've done the
> first one tens of thousands of times. The difference between them is repetition.

⛔ **A demonstration, not a rep. Never counted as one anywhere.** ⛔ **If it grows into a discussion
about habits, it has failed** (`[v3` beat 4`]`).

**Accessibility equivalent.** Participants may do it off camera, at their desk, or not at all, and
are told so. A participant with limited arm movement does the same demonstration with which hand
they would pick up a cup. Nobody is looked at.

`[F` card 17b `d3]` **And Foundations runs Learn It, Build It, Use It across all five skills rather
than one**, plus the honest reason Build It is where almost everything fails:
> A session tells you the thing, you agree with the thing, and nobody ever does the clunky middle

⚑ Nothing is asked out loud. Nobody places themselves publicly. This is the private twin of the four
positions at beat 5 and the same rule applies.

**Foundations adds over Essentials:** the stages run across all five skills instead of one, and the

# MOVEMENT III — the practice site, the method, the specificity argument

**Foundations widens three things.**

### ⚡ ACTIVITY 5 · Where the pressure is in your week
`[F` card 31 `d3]` **Foundations only.** Private, silent, nothing written.

**What happens.** Immediately after the drawbridge, the room finds its own high-pressure moments so
that the low-friction rule has something concrete to be a rule about.

**What participants physically do.** Sit and think for about twenty seconds. Nothing said, nothing
written, no hands.

**What the facilitator does.** Gives the search a boundary before the silence, says what to do if
