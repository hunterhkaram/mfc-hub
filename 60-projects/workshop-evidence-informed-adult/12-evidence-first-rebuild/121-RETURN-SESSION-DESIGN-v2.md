# The return session, v2

## 1. Why we come back at all

**A skill that is learned once and never revisited is expected to decay.** That
is the reason this session exists. **How much it decays, and whether one contact
inside the window is enough, is not established** (see section 6). It is not a
second lesson, and it is not a drill.

**It is a check-in.** People come back, see what they and the room actually
did, get an answer to the thing they most wanted to know, and leave with the
practice alive rather than filed.

**Founder framing, 2026-08-30, verbatim:** *"we're not trying to capture. Is it
a bad workplace? Tell us, be honest. It's like, hey, what are the things that
you wanna work on as a group... we wanna help people. This isn't just about
making the most profitable product."*

## 2. The shape: same opening for both products

**The first twenty minutes of the Essentials and Foundations returns are
identical.** One design, delivered twice.

**Who is in the room for it.** Participants only. **No manager, leader or host
is present for the opening twenty minutes of either return, including the
Essentials return, which is online.** The host is admitted at the point the
design says so and not before. If a leader will not leave or cannot be excluded
from the call, **the room's own picture is not shown** and the session moves
straight to practice. Do not explain why in front of them.

### 0 to 3 minutes: what today is

Say what this is: a check-in on what they have actually done since, not a test
and not a new lesson.

### 3 to 8 minutes: here is what you did

Show the room what happened since session one. Plainly and positively: the
kinds of moment that came up, that some people caught them at the time, that
some people tried something.

**The rule, and it replaces the retired response floor. Show what happened.
Never how many.**

- *"Handovers came up, and a couple of people caught it at the time"* — **yes**.
- *"Two of you tried it"* — **no**. **No counts, no fractions, no percentages,
  no "most of you", at any group size.**

**Why this is a ban on numbers rather than a minimum group size.** A count is
arithmetic, and in a small room arithmetic identifies people whatever words
surround it. In a group of three, *"two of you tried it"* names the third by
subtraction, and no phrasing prevents it. Removing the denominator removes the
exposure, and it does so identically at every group size, so no room is ever
told it is too small for something.

**Also never:** attribute a moment, quote anyone, or single out one unattributed
act for praise in a small room, because the room will assign it to a person and
mark by contrast whoever it decides did not do it.

### 8 to 14 minutes: warm reps

Three short scenarios, answered as Stop, Shift, Do.

**Nobody is required to speak.** Offer, out loud and before starting, that
people may answer aloud, answer quietly with the person next to them, or think
it through alone — then take answers only from whoever offers. **Below six
people, default to quiet pairs or private thinking.** `K-08` rule 6 forbids
forcing public sharing and rule 4 requires a built-in opt-out; this beat is not
an exception to either.

### 14 to 20 minutes: the thing you asked about

At the end of session one, or by a short form about a week before this session,
everyone is asked one question: **what is one more thing you would like to
learn, or one thing you are finding hard?**

**Whatever comes up most, we cover here.** Not an algorithm, not a
classification. They tell us, and we bring it.

**What the form must carry, in the participant's sight, before they answer:**

> This comes to us, not to your workplace. **Your organisation gets nothing
> about what any of you answered** — no quotes, no individuals, no breakdown by
> team, shift, site or role. Only an MFC facilitator reads it, and it is deleted
> once this session has run. **It is not a support channel and it is not
> monitored day to day.** If something needs help now, [referral line].

**Unresolved conflict, stated rather than reconciled (`CLAUDE.md` §4).** `110`
states as a standing rule that the between-session capture **never reaches the
commissioning organisation at any group size**. It is not settled whether this
form is that same instrument or a second one, nor whether anything derived from
it may go to the organisation at all. **Until that is resolved, nothing from
this form goes to the organisation**, and themes from it are not part of the
written report.

**Why "themes to the manager" is not by itself a privacy control.** In a team of
four, a theme is often the same content with the pronoun removed, and the
manager can usually work out who. `110`'s themes-not-participant-data rule exists
to stop small groups being treated worse; it is not a finding that themes are
safe at small numbers.

## 3. Then the two products diverge

### Essentials, the remaining 40 minutes

**More understanding, and more practice.** Go deeper on whatever they asked
for, then run reps on it. Two or three rounds, in pairs, on supplied scenarios,
with quiet or solo options offered aloud so that taking one is invisible.

**Essentials ends there**, with people having practised the thing they said they
wanted to work on. **No claim is made that they are now better at it.**

### Foundations, the remaining 55 minutes

**Everything above, plus the collective piece: how this team does it
together.**

**`[GATE]` This beat does not run unless three things are true.** The leaders
are out of the room and stay out. **`D-0110`'s per-engagement approval for the
team-nomination item has been obtained for this engagement.** And the candidate
material was collected and safely aggregated beforehand. **If any is not true,
substitute practice, and do not say why in the room.**

**Choosing what the team works on, 20 minutes.** Put four or five candidate
moments in front of the room, drawn from what people have already said. Talk
about it. Agree two, each with one observable move.

**The costless exit, said out loud before the discussion starts:**

> *"If any of these doesn't sit right with you, tell me at the break or on the
> way out. I'll take it off, and I won't say who or why."*

**Removal is unconditional and unattributed, including a candidate MFC wrote.**
This replaces the retired paper ballot and does the one thing the ballot was
actually for: letting a person remove something without arguing for it in front
of colleagues. **It matters most in small teams**, where spoken dissent is fully
attributable and permanently remembered, and where the alternative is silence
followed by a commitment several people privately object to. **No shared moment
is better than a false one.**

**The scope rule.** If something is really about workload, safety, staffing, or
how one person behaves, **it is not a shared moment.** Say so plainly in the
room: *"that one isn't ours to fix with a habit"*, and put it in the
enabling-conditions column as a category.

**How that column is written, because leaders see it.** Categories in general
terms, no names, and **nothing so specific that one person's role, shift or site
identifies them.** "Handover time" is a category; "time to hand over properly on
night shift" identifies whoever works nights.

**`[NEVER]`** describe or record the enabling-conditions column, the team Radar,
or anything else in this session as a psychosocial hazard control, a hazard
identification, or anything an employer may rely on to discharge a duty.

**Placing them, 15 minutes.** Each of the two moments gets a named routine that
already happens, a named person who starts it, a ten-second description, and a
review date agreed in the room. **A team that agrees two moves and does not
place them in something that already exists has produced a decision that
evaporates.**

**The leaders, last 20 minutes.** They join at the end, having not been in the
room before it. They receive the enabling-conditions column, and each answers
one question out loud, in one sentence:

> **"Which one of these can you make true, and by when?"**

**Ask for the commitment and the date, not a verdict.** *"Yes, we support
that"* is free.

**Before they come in:** anything an individual wrote is off the walls and off
the tables. Categories only.

## 4. What this design deletes, and why

| Removed | Why |
|---|---|
| TEACH / PRACTICE / IMPLEMENT classification | We ask people what they want instead of sorting them |
| Six modules and five allocation rules | A solo facilitator who has never run this cannot carry a decision tree live |
| The eight-response floor on the team beat | Built for a disclosure question. The question is "what shall we work on together" |
| Private paper slips, the veto box, the disposal protocol | Ceremony that signalled danger the question does not carry. **The veto itself is retained** as a spoken, costless exit |
| Candidate moments composed offline at day 19 to 20 as a hard gate | Candidates now come from what people have already told us, and the room talks about them |

**Deliberately NOT deleted, and reinstated here after the 2026-08-30
safeguarding review:** small-number protection when showing the room its own
data, now expressed as a ban on counts rather than a group-size floor; the
private route to remove a candidate; the leader-free requirement for the team
beat; and the opt-out on speaking aloud.

**Kept from v1:** the reason the return exists; the leader commitment with a
date; the scope rule; aggregate-only reporting; and the rule that nothing
individual is visible when a leader enters.

## 5. Length and price consequence

Extending the Essentials return from thirty to sixty minutes adds about
**0.75 of a facilitator hour** (half an hour of delivery, a quarter for
preparing what they asked for).

**The baseline, stated so the arithmetic can be followed.** The Product and
Pricing Book costs Essentials at **8.25 hours**. `D-0113` adds **0.25** for the
manager session moving from forty-five to sixty minutes, giving **8.5**. This
change adds a further **0.75**, giving **9.25 hours**, direct cost $671.43 and
loaded $940.00 against the book's 8.25-hour figures of $605 and $847.

At current prices the middle band's retained margin falls from 61% to 57%.
**Restoring it would take about $100 across the bands, to $1,350 / $1,650 /
$1,900** (modelled 2026-08-30 from the Product and Pricing Book's own rate and
multiplier).

**Not applied.** The committee is being asked on 31 August to adopt the current
prices, and this change comes back to them as a product improvement once it is
built. `D-0113` governs what the price includes; nothing here changes it.

**One effect worth seeing.** Essentials goes from 2.0 to 2.5 hours of
participant contact, so the gap to Foundations closes from 2.6x to 2.1x.
**Foundations then earns its price on the depth of its teaching, the on-site
return and the written report.** `D-0110` forbids positioning the team-shared
mechanism as an active differentiator while its approval gate is open, so **the
collective piece is not the commercial argument.**

## 6. What is still unknown, and what blocks delivery

**Nothing here has been run or rehearsed.** No return session of any design
ever has.

Specifically unevidenced, and it must be said that way: whether one contact
inside the decay window is enough or two would be better; whether peer
correction against a short rubric is any good; and whether a facilitated team
agreement survives three weeks. These are reasoned judgements placed where the
transfer literature points, not findings.

**Blockers, in full rather than by implication:**

1. **The escalation and distress-response procedures do not exist in cleared
   form.** No facilitator, including Hunter, can be cleared to deliver until
   they do. This covers written disclosure arriving on the pre-session form as
   much as disclosure in the room.
2. **`D-0016`'s six safeguarding matters remain unresolved**, and `D-0099`
   changed how that gate is framed without resolving any of them. Escalation
   wording is one of the six, not the whole set.
3. **`D-0110`'s per-engagement approval gate on the team-nomination mechanism is
   open**, so the collective piece is designed but not cleared to run.
4. **This document has had internal AI review only.** The clinical governance,
   legal and privacy, and insurance gates are all open. **Nothing in this file
   is clearance.**
