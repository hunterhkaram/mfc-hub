*What MFC sells, what each thing is, what it costs us to run once, what we charge, and what the

## A note on "manager" throughout this book (founder direction, 2026-08-15)

**Every product below has a beat where the session expands from the individual out to the people
around them** — the week −1 leader session in Foundations, the 45-minute leader briefing in
Essentials, and the last twenty minutes of session 2. **That beat runs in every context MFC delivers
in. Only the word for who that person is changes:** a manager in a workplace, a nurse unit manager on
a ward, a shift or team leader, a coordinator, a community or club leader.

This book uses **"the leader"** for that role and prices it as one component. **Corporate and
workplace buyers are the primary near-term market — that is where the funding is, and the pricing
below is built for them — but the product is not built on the assumption that a workplace is the only
room it can run in.** "Employer" appears only where the point is genuinely employment-specific: paid
time, anti-detriment, WHS duties. Full statement of the correction: `120` (RETIRED, see `121`) §0.2 and
`90-meta/agent-context-v1/15-founder-voice-mission-over-whs-portability.md` §5.

**"Charity keeps" means exactly what Hunter asked for:** price minus the facilitator's pay for every

committee sees, which also carries a 1.4× overhead allocation — both numbers appear in every table
below, and the difference between them is explained in *The two ways to read these numbers*.

## The two ways to read these numbers — read this before the tables

There are two honest answers to "what does MFC make on this," and confusing them is how the last
pricing round produced a $139 discrepancy that survived four documents (see *Open items*).

- **Charity net (cash).** Price − facilitator hours × rate − direct cash costs. This is the number
  Hunter asked for. It is real money in the account after the person who ran the session is paid.
- **Loaded margin (governance).** The same, but with every direct cost multiplied by 1.4 first, to
  allocate the overhead MFC really carries but doesn't invoice — governance, insurance, product
  development, unfunded founder time. This is the number that belongs in a committee paper, and it
  is the one that enforces the standing rule that **nothing is sold below loaded cost.**

The cash number is always larger. Neither is wrong. Quoting the cash number to the committee, or the
loaded number to Hunter, is.

**MFC is not GST-registered, so every MFC price in this book is a final price.** Every comparator
quoted "+ GST" is 10% higher again to a buyer who cannot claim the credit.

# The products

## 1. Introduction — 60 minutes

### What it is, minute by minute

One hour, one room, one facilitator. The mental fitness definition, the Stop–Shift–Do loop taught as
traffic lights, the four shift routes shown so "shift" has somewhere to go, and one guided rep on a
moment from the participant's own week. **It ends with the Reference Card** — the same printed
wallet-sized fold card every MFC participant receives, at every product tier, carrying Stop–Shift–Do,
the four routes and the areas they apply to. There is no return, no report, and no follow-up.

**Measurement is deliberately light** — one matched item, one harm item, the referral line, and
nothing else (`FINAL-02` §3.7). No linkage code, no follow-up survey.

### Who it's for

An audience, not a client. Chamber slots, conference sessions, a team meeting, a community group, a
bouldering gym. Adult community and workplace audiences only — **schools and youth are excluded**
until `C-16` closes and a safeguarding lead is named.

### Why this design

An hour is what you give an audience; ninety minutes is what someone buys (`D-0098`). The honest
reason the Introduction stays an hour is that the full measurement instrument protects ten in-session
minutes, and putting that into a 60-minute kit leaves fifty minutes of teaching in a container built
for sixty — content cut to make room for data collection. **What this product is designed to do is
create reach and a conversation. It is not designed to produce durable change and nothing here claims
it does** — a single session with no contact afterwards sits exactly inside the pattern MFC's own
research corpus describes, where in-room gains are common and four-week gains are not. This is the
one product in the lineup that is honest about being a doorway.

**It is also the only product built and tested today.** Everything else needs a build pass.

### Per-run economics

| | Booked | Outreach |
|---|---|---|
| Price | **$600** | **$0** (capped 20/yr) |
| Facilitator hours (prep 1.0 · travel 1.0 · delivery 1.0 · light-form admin 0.5) | 3.5 hrs | 3.5 hrs |
| Facilitator pay @ $66.10 | $231.35 | $231.35 |
| Direct costs (materials) | $40 | $40 |
| **Direct cost total** | **$271.35** | **$271.35** |

| Loaded cost (×1.4) | $380 | $380 |
| Loaded margin | +$220 (1.58×) | −$380 |

**Digital variant:** drops the 1.0 travel hour, materials to $10. 2.5 hrs = $165.25 + $10 = **$175.25
direct**, **$245 loaded**. At **$450**, the charity keeps **$274.75 (61%)**, loaded margin 1.84×.

### What the buyer gets

The hour, **the Reference Card for everyone in the room**, and the referral line. Nothing is reported back — and
saying so plainly at the point of sale is what stops a booked Introduction being mistaken for the
paid product.

## 2. Essentials — 90 minutes, a return check-in of up to an hour about three weeks later, and a short written report

### What it is, session by session

**Session 1 — 90 minutes, on site.** Everything in the Introduction, plus the part that makes it a
product rather than a demonstration: all three steps run on the participant's **own** chosen moment
rather than a generic one, the five public skills, and the ten protected minutes of in-session
measurement. It ends with the complete usable object — one rehearsed rep and one written if-then
sentence on their practice sheet, **and the wallet Reference Card issued at the close**.

**The leader briefing — 45 minutes, included, never sold separately.** Held before the session where
scheduling allows. **Whoever leads or cares for the people in the room attends** — the line managers in
a workplace, the nurse unit manager on a ward, the coordinators or committee in a community group. Charging for it separately invites the buyer to decline the mechanism the product
depends on.

**Session 2 — the return check-in, up to 60 minutes, remote, at about week 3.** A check-in, and **what it
contains is decided by what participants said they wanted.** It opens with the room's own picture from
session 1, shown as aggregate counts with no names, then covers whatever people said they most wanted
to learn or were finding hard, then practises it. Every participant runs the complete sequence several
times and is corrected by a partner against the three-line rubric. It closes with the day-21
measurement. `121` §2-3.

> costed model in this book carries the 8.25-hour Essentials; `D-0113` adds 0.25 for the longer
> manager session, giving 8.5. Extending the return adds a further 0.75

> to hold margin. **That reprice is modelled and deliberately not applied**, because the committee is
> asked on 31 August to adopt the current prices.

**The report — short, written, and deliberately lighter than the Foundations one (added 2026-08-16 by
founder decision).** After the return session the organisation receives a short written summary: what
was covered, what the team worked on, the themes the forms surfaced across the group, and what the
organisation could do next to keep the practice alive. **It is one to two pages, not a findings
document.** Foundations carries four hours of report work — cohort form review 1.5, findings write-up
1.5, post-findings partner review 1.0 — and produces an interpreted findings report on that cohort's
own measurement, followed by a conversation about it. **Essentials carries one hour and produces
neither.** Two limits hold and must be stated wherever this is sold: **it reports themes, not
participant data — at every group size, not as a workaround for being under a headcount** (corrected
2026-08-19, `FINAL-02` §2); and **the short voluntary capture between sessions
still never reaches the commissioning organisation** and is still deleted once the session has run.
The report is written from what the facilitator saw and from group-level themes, and it is not a
route around either rule. **Do not describe it as a report on the team's results.**

### Who it's for

A single organisation's team, 2–15 people. Micro-businesses (2–5) are a real and deliberately served
segment — Hunter's own read is that they *"don't usually ever get the opportunity to upskill or
learn"* — and they are priced as reach, not as revenue.

### Why this design

The return is the whole reason this is not just a shorter Foundations. Every durable number MFC has
is about the interval, not the room: intention converts to behaviour at roughly half its size (Webb &
Sheeran 2006), and workplace resilience effects fall from d = 0.26 proximally to d = 0.07 distally
(Vanhove et al. 2016). A return puts a facilitator inside that interval, and it is the point where
every participant runs the sequence out loud and gets corrected — the feedback component of
behavioural skills training, which a single block strips out.

**Two things about the return that must both be said.** Everyone practises out loud and gets
corrected — but the correction comes from the person they are paired with and from the facilitator
moving between pairs, **not from a one-to-one coaching session, and MFC does not claim it is the same
thing.**

**Honest status:** that is a mechanism argument, not a measured one. Nothing tests a remote group return session. The return is proposed because it is cheap, safe, placed where the decay curves say
the loss happens, and because it closes a component the literature says is required — **not because
anything shows it works.**

**Why Essentials' return stays remote** while Foundations' moved on site (`120` (RETIRED, see `121`) §8.1).
Two alternatives were computed and rejected. **In person** adds ~1.5–2.0 facilitator hours; at the 2–5
band that pushes loaded cost from **$847 to about $1,009** against the $1,250 price — **about 1.24×
loaded, which is thinner than any band in this book** — and raising that band further prices out
exactly the micro-business segment Hunter deliberately serves. *(At the prices in force when this was
first computed, the same alternative came out at 1.07× loaded and broke MFC's standing floor outright.
The rejection now rests on the margin being too thin for a product with zero delivered cohorts rather
than on the floor, which is a weaker reason and is stated as one.)* **Dropping the return** leaves
Essentials with no contact inside the decay window
at all and nowhere to administer the day-21 measurement. **Keeping it remote costs +0.25 hours and holds every band.**

### Why this design

searched specifically for a dose–response curve between ten minutes and half a day at a single
occasion and found nothing. What it did find is that everything about durability is about the
interval: decay starts immediately and is faster for cognitive, accuracy-based skills than physical
ones (Arthur et al. 1998); in the adult single-session megastudy, 11 of 12 interventions raised
in-room agency and 10 of 12 produced no benefit at four weeks; and supervisor support and work
environment are among the strongest transfer predictors (Blume et al. 2010).

**So the half-day stays intact and the new minutes go into the interval.** Three specific reasons this
shape rather than three or four separate teaching hours:

1. **The participant leaves day zero with the whole thing.** Under a split course, the Reference Card
   and the three moments do not exist until week three — the two weeks of steepest decay spent holding a
   half-taught skill and no plan.
2. **It fails gracefully.** A participant who misses the return has the whole skill and missed a
   reinforcement. A participant who misses session two of three has a broken skill. Same absence,
   very different damage — and this is the strongest argument in the design because it does not depend
   on any effect size being right. **Stated honestly against the cut: with one return rather than two,
   a person who misses it now receives no facilitated contact inside the decay window at all. Graceful
   failure still holds for the skill; it no longer holds for the reinforcement.**
3. **The spacing argument does not apply and MFC must not use it.** Distributed practice beats massed
   practice on average, but the advantage correlates *negatively* with task complexity and disappears
   for highly complex skills (Donovan & Radosevich 1999) — which is exactly the cell a self-regulatory
   loop sits in. The honest justification is the transfer evidence and the missing feedback component,
   not the spacing effect.

**What is demonstrated versus designed for, stated plainly.** Demonstrated: that single-session
formats reliably produce in-room gains and unreliably produce four-week gains; that decay is fast; that
the environment around the person is a strong predictor of transfer. Designed for: that one facilitated
return and a leader layer close enough of that gap to matter. **Nobody has tested this shape, and
nothing evidences that one contact inside the window is enough or that two would have been better.**
Zero cohorts have run.

2. **The $1,228 / $1,367 seam — reconciled, and neither number survives.** `D-0096` §1 states a
   Foundations loaded cost of **~$1,228**; `FINAL-02` and `D2` state **~$1,367**. They are not in
   conflict: $1,228 is the half-day alone ($877.10 direct × 1.4), and $1,367 is the half-day **plus the
   bundled Leaders' Session** ($877.10 + $99.15 = $976.25 × 1.4 = $1,366.75). Since the Leaders'
   Session is included in every engagement and never sold separately, **$1,367 was the correct figure
   for the product as sold** and $1,228 was a figure for a component. **`D-0096` §1 should be read as
   costing the half-day, not the offering.** Both are now superseded regardless: with the single day-21
   return, Foundations costs **$1,174.55 direct and $1,644 loaded**, and that is the number this book
   uses throughout. *The root cause is worth naming: a component cost and a product cost were given
   the same label in two documents, and nothing forced a reconciliation because both were arithmetically
   correct.*

3. **Six and a half of the fifteen and a half Foundations facilitator hours are estimates, not measured
   time.** The form review (1.5), the findings write-up (1.5), the return preparation (0.75) and
   session 2 (2.75) have never been performed. If the report work takes twice as long as estimated, direct cost rises by about $200 and
   the charity keeps about $200 less per run — it does not change the price, but it changes the
   honesty of the margin.

4. **The safeguarding position, stated accurately rather than by habit.** `D-0016` is **closed and
   superseded** by `D-0099` (Hunter, direct instruction, 2026-08-10) — it is no longer a gate on
   pricing, committee decisions or the build order, and nothing in this book is held up by it. But
   `D-0099` is explicit that closing the gate did not resolve the six matters underneath it:
   distress-response and referral-pathway design still require qualified clinical-governance input;
   legal exposure still requires qualified legal input; insurer confirmation of cover has not been
   obtained. **None of that blocks quoting these prices. It does bear on delivering to a real
   participant**, and the one-hour live digital session (item 5) remains the most exposed
   configuration in the ladder.

4a. **The community and universal-access line is not funded and not designed.** MFC's own
   market-to-product reconciliation records the honest verdict: the universality claim is true of the
   *teaching* and not yet true of the *product* — the community variant's price is zero or donation,
   funded by corporate margin that has no source until corporate sales exist. Say that plainly rather
   than promise an access programme MFC cannot yet fund. The access levy inside the Foundations base
   is a commitment that a fixed share of every paid engagement accrues to a fund, **not** a promise of
   a volume of free delivery.

5. **Two digital products cannot be sold.** Foundations virtual is withdrawn from buyer-facing material
   until the online variant is designed. The one-hour live digital session is the most
   safeguarding-exposed configuration in the whole ladder and its preconditions are open. Their prices
   are shown here so the model is complete, not so they can be quoted.

6. **The Essentials 2–5 band is the thinnest in the book at 1.48× loaded**, after the 2026-08-16
   report addition and reprice took it from 1.26×. It is deliberately reach spend. If it
   turns out to consume founder hours that Foundations needed, it should be cut rather than repriced —
   a micro-business cannot absorb another increase.

6a. **The Essentials report hour is an estimate, and the boundary it sits on is a judgement, not a
   rule anyone has cleared.** One hour to write a short report has never been performed and could
   easily be 1.5. More importantly, the report is written from group-level themes while the
   between-session capture is recorded as never reaching the commissioning organisation. **Themes
   drawn from that capture are not the capture, but that is MFC's own reading of its own rule, not a
   privacy determination**, and it should be put to whoever does the privacy read along with the rest
   of the reporting floor. Until then, keep the report at what the facilitator observed and at
   group-level themes, and never at anything a participant could be identified from.

7. **This book has not been reviewed by anyone qualified.** No legal, WHS, clinical, privacy, insurer
   or research-ethics review has been obtained. Two sceptical re-reads were run and are recorded below,
   but they were run by the same system that wrote it.

8. **What cohort one settles almost for free.** Whether people come back to session 2 at all — if fewer
   than half a cohort attend a rostered 75-minute on-site session three weeks after the half-day, while
   still employed by the buyer who paid, then the return thesis is in trouble and the price built on it
   is too. **This is now the design's only attendance test rather than the first of two, which makes
   cohort one's number both cleaner and more consequential.** And whether the day-21 barriers are capability-side (*couldn't remember it*) or opportunity-side
   (*no time, no permission, it would look weak here*). The second vindicates this design. The first
   says the minutes belong back in the room.

9. **Foundations' facilitator capacity is now genuinely open, and the previous answer is void.** With
   both facilitated events on site, one facilitator running Foundations spends two separate site visits
   per engagement rather than one visit plus a remote call. **The old reassurance — that the remote
   return kept the shape affordable — no longer applies.** Nothing in this book models how many
   Foundations engagements one person can run in a month, and the honest position is that it is fewer
   than before. **This does not change any price. It changes how many times a price can be charged**,
   and it should be sized before MFC commits to a sales target.

## The two sceptical re-reads, and what they changed

**Read one — as the buyer's CFO: "what am I actually paying, per person?"**

The CFO does not see a programme; they see a line item next to an EAP invoice. At $5,325 for fifteen
people, MFC costs $355 per head once, against an EAP at $30–80 per head for the whole company for a
year. That comparison is unflattering on its face and it is the first thing a finance function will
run.

*Three things changed.* A **per-person row was added to every economics table**, because a price a
buyer cannot convert to a per-head figure is a price they will convert unfavourably in their own head.
The **value comparison was rewritten to state plainly that these products do different jobs** rather
than implying MFC is a better-value EAP — an EAP is reactive access for whoever uses it, and MFC is
training for everyone in the room; comparing them on unit price alone is a comparison MFC would lose
on volume and win on nothing honest. And **every trace of an ROI argument was removed**, including any
temptation to reach for Black Dog's 10:1 figure, which MFC's own research flags as unverified and
possibly derived from a different study. The honest CFO answer is now written into the document: MFC
cannot yet say what the return is, and the measurement is how it intends to find out.

The CFO's second question — *what if half my staff skip the day-21 return* — produced the fourth
change: **all four dates fixed at contracting** is now stated as part of what the buyer gets, not left
implicit in the design document.

**Read two — as Rob: "why isn't this $5,000?"**

This read did the most damage to the draft, in a useful direction. The first version of this book
inherited $2,500 + $95 from `D-0096` and treated Rob's number as an opinion to be tested against the
market. That was the wrong move, and Rob's question exposed why: **`D-0096` priced a half-day workshop,
and the product is no longer a half-day workshop.** Once the leader session, both returns, the
measurement and the report are costed as one purchase, the price has to be re-derived, and when it is
re-derived it lands at $5,325–5,800 at the 2026-08-16 prices — past Rob's number rather than near it,
from the cost and positioning side rather than from his assertion.

*Two things changed.* **Foundations was rebased from $2,500 to $3,500 + $95/person**, at the bottom of
the band MFC's own format analysis had already flagged as indicative, and was raised again to **$3,900
+ $95/person** by founder decision on 2026-08-16. And **Essentials was repriced**,
because the same re-derivation showed the added return session pushed its loaded cost from $639 to $755
— which put the existing $700 entry band below loaded cost and silently broke MFC's own standing rule.
That error would have shipped. **Essentials moved again later the same day**, when the founder decided
it should carry a short written report: loaded cost $755 → $847 and the bands to
**$1,250 / $1,550 / $1,800**.

*One thing this read did not change, deliberately.* Rob's $5,000 is **not** adopted as a minimum. Doing
so would delete the Introduction and price a five-person business out of Essentials entirely, which
costs MFC the funnel and contradicts universality. The recommendation splits his claim rather than
accepting or rejecting it: right about where the flagship lands, wrong as a floor — and that split is
stated in the market section rather than buried here.
