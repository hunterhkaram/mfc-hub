# MFC Introduction — 60-minute facilitator packet v1

**Status:** internal product candidate; standard service, no materials and no routine participant data  
**Audience:** broad adult public audience; adapt examples, not the core mechanism  
**Product job:** a human first encounter that makes MFC’s category and simple process recognisable, memorable and worth voluntarily trying later.

**Required companion:** use `MFC-PACKET-OPERATIONS-SPEC-v1.md` for the 57–60 hidden-visual retrieval protocol, accessibility/host preparation, and the Introduction scenario switch rule. “No materials” means no required participant worksheet, writing, device or routine data—not no optional accessibility aid.

## Non-negotiables

- No forms, worksheets, QR codes, mandatory phone tasks, personal cards, attendee identifiers or expected follow-up.
- People may pass, observe or use a fictional example. Do not invite public disclosure.
- A guided activity is called **guided rehearsal**. A **Mental Rep** occurs only in a naturally occurring real-world moment and is not claimed here.
- Do not diagnose, promise wellbeing change, suggest another practice is inadequate, or frame a constraint as someone’s failure.

## Materials and room set-up

Facilitator, a visible three-word slide or board (`Stop – Shift – Do`), timer and optional accessible verbal description of the slide. The product must work if the visual aid fails.

## 0:00–0:05 — welcome and permission

**Say:**

> Thanks for being here. This is a conversation about the ordinary moments where life asks something of us—at home, at work, in our heads, or with other people. You never need to share a personal story here. You can join in, think quietly, use an invented example, or simply listen.

> This is not therapy, diagnosis, or a claim that every difficult situation can be solved by an individual response. Some situations need support, time, safety, or change around us. Today is about having one small process available in moments where it is useful.

**Facilitator check:** pace slowly; do not ask “who is struggling?”

## 0:05–0:12 — recognise the kind of moment

**Say:**

> Think of a small, recognisable moment: you see a message and feel a reaction before you reply; you notice you have been scrolling longer than you meant to; someone says something in a conversation and you can feel yourself closing off. Nothing dramatic needs to be happening for a moment to matter.

> You do not need to tell anyone which example fits. Just notice whether you recognise the kind of moment.

**Optional room interaction:** “A nod or a quiet hand-up if this kind of thing sounds familiar.” Do not count or record responses.

**Recovery route:** if the room is silent, say: “You do not need to respond—quiet recognition is enough.” Continue.

## 0:12–0:20 — reframe without blame

**Say:**

> We are often expected to handle these moments as though we should already know how. But people learn ways of responding through guidance, observation, experience and practice—and what is available to us changes with stress, safety, time, relationships and the situation itself.

> Mental health is about how we are doing. Mental fitness is about what we are able to do in life’s everyday moments. They connect, but they are not the same thing. Needing more options or practice is not evidence that someone is broken.

**Do not say:** “Nobody taught us this,” “everyone can learn this,” or anything that implies equal control/capacity.

## 0:20–0:31 — teach Stop–Shift–Do

Display or say the three words one at a time.

**Say:**

> MFC uses a simple process: **Stop – Shift – Do**.

> **Stop** is the chance to notice that a moment is happening. It might be before, during, or after it. It does not mean freezing, being calm, or getting it right.

> **Shift** is anything that gives you a little more space or perspective when one is available: a breath, a pause, a different question, stepping away, talking to someone, or deciding this is not a moment to handle alone. It is an option, not a rule.

> **Do** is choosing a next move with intention—something that fits what matters in that moment. It may be speaking, waiting, asking for help, leaving, coming back later, or doing nothing for now.

> The result does not decide whether the attempt counted. You can make an intentional next move and still have a difficult outcome.

**Worked fictional example:**

> You receive a message that lands badly and your first impulse is to reply immediately. Stop: you notice the pull to react. Shift: perhaps you put the phone down, read it again later, or ask what you need before replying. Do: you choose whether to respond now, wait, ask a question, or speak to someone. The best move depends on the situation; the point is having a moment to choose.

## 0:31–0:41 — collective retrieval and discrimination

**Scenario:** “You are about to walk into a conversation you are already dreading. You notice your body tightening and your mind rushing ahead.”

Ask, one at a time:

1. “What is the first word in the process?” Pause five seconds. Confirm: “Stop—notice the moment.”
2. “What might Shift mean here?” Accept several answers; add: “It could also be recognising that you need support or that now is not the right time.”
3. “What could Do look like?” Accept multiple safe, context-sensitive examples.

**Correct gently:** if someone says “calm down” as the only answer, say: “Calm might be welcome, but it is not the requirement. The process is about having an intentional next move available.”

## 0:41–0:52 — shared guided rehearsal

**Set-up:**

> We will do one rehearsal together. It is not a test and you do not need to use a personal example. Think privately, use this situation, or simply listen.

**Scenario:** use S5 Stage 1 then Stage 2 from the Operations specification. If that public-moment scenario could resemble a recent threat, violence or culturally loaded incident, switch to **S1 Message**, keep it fictional, and use the same rehearsal sequence.

Guide slowly:

1. “Where might you notice the moment? That is Stop.” Pause.
2. “What are a few different ways someone might create a little space, if it is safe and available? That is Shift.” Invite two or three possibilities, including “move away” or “do nothing for now.”
3. “What could an intentional next move be? That is Do.” Invite alternatives; affirm that safety, leaving, asking for support or not engaging can all be appropriate.

**Close the rehearsal:**

> That was a guided rehearsal in a shared scenario. It is not a Mental Rep. A Mental Rep is when a real moment occurs in your life and you catch it and choose what you do next with intention.

## 0:52–0:57 — future bridge with no obligation

**Say:**

> If a moment that matters shows up after today, you might notice it and see whether Stop, Shift or Do gives you one more option. If no moment shows up, if the process does not come to mind, or if the situation calls for support rather than action, that is information—not a failure.

> You do not need to make a promise, choose a target or report anything back. The point is simply that this language is now available if it is useful.

## 0:57–1:00 — unaided recap and close

Ask: “Without looking, what are the three words?” Allow the room to answer collectively.

**Say:**

> Stop—notice the moment. Shift—create some space or perspective if it is available. Do—choose an intentional next move. Mental fitness is not about controlling every outcome. It is about having more chance to notice and choose in the everyday moments that matter.

> Thank you for thinking with us.

## Facilitator recovery rules

| If this happens | Do this |
|---|---|
| A participant discloses distress or an unsafe situation | Thank them briefly; do not invite detail or group advice. Acknowledge that safety/support may be the next appropriate step; follow the host’s approved support process if one exists. |
| Someone challenges the distinction from mindfulness/therapy | Agree that many practices can support in-the-moment awareness; state that this session offers one simple language/process and is not competing with care or other practices. |
| Discussion runs long | Protect the rehearsal and close. Shorten examples before removing the core retrieval/rehearsal beats. |
| The visual fails | Teach verbally; ask for the three words again at close. |
