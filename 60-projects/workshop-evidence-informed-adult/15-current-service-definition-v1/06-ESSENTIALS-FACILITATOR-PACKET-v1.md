# MFC Essentials — 90-minute facilitator packet v1

**Status:** internal product candidate; team-context learning service  
**Core boundary:** no participant data, return contact or manager component is required for this 90-minute product. Those are separate optional modules.  
**Product job:** contextual, private, practical learning—not a team-culture intervention.

**Required companion:** use `MFC-PACKET-OPERATIONS-SPEC-v1.md` for host/access preparation, S1–S6 scenario selection and the 86–90 verbal-or-hidden-visual retrieval protocol. Its S-ID scenario text is the sole canonical scenario source.

## Before the session — tailoring record

Record only: setting/sector, common non-sensitive work rhythms, accessibility and language needs, words/examples to avoid, power/seniority mix, and two fictional scenarios relevant to the setting. Do not ask a host to identify individual problems or collect health information.

## Facilitator rules

People may pass, observe, choose a hypothetical scenario, or keep reflection private. No one shares a personal situation with a manager or colleague by design. A classroom attempt is **guided rehearsal**, not a Mental Rep. In unsafe, discriminatory, coercive or clinical situations, support, protection, delay or leaving may be the appropriate next move.

## Run sheet and exact core language

| Time | Facilitator move | Participant route | Non-negotiable correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | Welcome: “We are working with ordinary moments in a team context. No disclosure is required. This is not therapy, performance management, or a promise that individuals can solve workplace conditions alone.” | listen/pass | No baseline form in the core session. |
| 8–18 | Use two tailored but fictional examples. Ask: “What makes this a moment rather than a personality, diagnosis or performance label?” Then explain that access varies with capacity, safety, workload, relationship and environment. | quiet reflection or whole-room responses | Never convert a contextual problem into “mindset.” |
| 18–31 | Teach Stop–Shift–Do using a neutral example: an unexpected change to a priority creates a quick reaction. **Stop:** notice the moment. **Shift:** create space/perspective if available (pause, question, seek support, defer). **Do:** choose a next move aligned with what matters. “The outcome does not decide whether an intentional attempt counted.” | listen, then recall each word | Shift is an option, not a demand to calm down. |
| 31–42 | Shared discrimination case: “A deadline moves; someone says, ‘Just deal with it.’” Ask: “What is the moment? What might be a next move? What condition/support is separate from either?” | fictional whole-group analysis | Correctly separate moment, action and context. |
| 42–56 | Paired/small-group fictional rehearsal using the recorded **selected S-ID 1**, with Stage 1 then Stage 2 reveal where the Operations specification supplies one. Ask each group to name more than one safe Stop/Shift/Do route. | join, observe or work silently | No personal storytelling; no best answer. |
| 56–68 | Private translation: “You may privately think of a future, recent, or invented moment. What might help you notice it? What is one possible next move? What is an alternative if that move is unavailable?” | private/paperless thought only | No collection or invitation to share. A plan is not a rep. |
| 68–78 | Conditions rehearsal using the recorded **selected S-ID 2**. Ask: “What changes in the conditions? What routes remain possible? When might support, delay or non-action be appropriate?” | shared or paired fictional analysis | Do not imply courage is the only answer. |
| 78–86 | Future bridge: “A moment may appear; it may not. You may remember this process or not. Those are not moral scores. If it is useful, you have one more language for noticing and choosing. If a situation needs support or change around you, that matters too.” | listen/private thought | No pledge, commitment card or manager-visible artefact. |
| 86–90 | Ask unaided: “What are the three words?” Close: “A guided rehearsal is something we did here. A Mental Rep is a real-world moment where someone catches the moment and intentionally chooses what they do next.” | collective recall | Keep the distinction exact. |

## Core scenario source

Select exactly two canonical S-IDs under the companion operations specification. Record selection, avoided scenario/reason and fallback before the session. Do not attach a scenario to an assumed identity, hierarchy or diagnosis.

## Facilitator recovery routes

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| A manager asks participants to share their situations | “We keep this private by design. Let’s work with the fictional case instead.” |
| A participant says the process ignores systemic problems | Agree that individual choices do not replace fair conditions, safety or support; return to the distinction between an available next move and a responsibility to fix the system. |
| Pair work feels unsafe or unwanted | Switch immediately to silent individual or whole-group fictional analysis. |
| Timing slips | Keep 18–31, one fictional rehearsal and 86–90. Shorten the number of examples, not privacy/scope language. |
