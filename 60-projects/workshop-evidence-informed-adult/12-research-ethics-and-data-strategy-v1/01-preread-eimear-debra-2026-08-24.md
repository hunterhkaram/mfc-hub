# Measurement, evidence and the research pathway

**Pre-read for Eimear Quigley and Debra Fidler. Meeting Monday 24 August 2026, 6.00 to 7.00pm.**

# Executive summary

Eimear, Debra, thank you both for making time. **This paper is not asking you to review anything
yet.** It is here so you arrive knowing what I intend to walk you through, and so you can pull apart
the parts that do not hold. It is built to be skimmed. **If you read one section, read section 3.**

**Three things frame everything below.** We have no data: measurement is designed and has never been
run, and no cohort has been delivered. We have no university relationship, and nobody has been
contacted, so when I have spoken about a research partnership I have been describing an intention
rather than a conversation in progress. And we are not waiting for either before we start. The plan
is to finalise the forms, close the safety, privacy and clinical gates, and only then deliver and
measure from the first cohort, building the record while the university pathway develops alongside
it.

**What Monday is for.** I will walk through the measurement design, the two candidate research
instruments, the safety matters in section 4, and the research pathway. Several of those carry real
decisions I would rather test with you in the room than settle alone, and I will bring them to the
conversation rather than set them out here as homework.

**Nothing in this paper is a finding.** Every claim about what MFC will learn is a plan.

# 1. The three services, and what we measure

| | Introduction | Essentials | Foundations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | 60 min, no return | 90 min plus a 30-min return at about week 3 | Leader session, half-day, and a 75-min on-site return at day 21 |
| **Taught** | Mental fitness defined; Stop–Shift–Do; the four shift routes; one guided rep on a moment from the room | The same, run on the participant's **own** moment; the five public skills; ends with a rehearsed rep and a written plan | The loop in depth, three of the participant's own moments, and a return session shaped by what the team reports back |
| **Not claimed** | A doorway, not an intervention | Ninety minutes alone does not build a durable skill. The return exists for that reason | A designed shape rather than a demonstrated one. No cohort has run |

**The same core questions run for all three services**, so every session feeds one growing picture
rather than three incompatible ones. Results are analysed **per service and never pooled**, because
a 60-minute session and a three-week programme are different interventions. The Introduction has no
return, so it carries baseline and post-session only.

**What the core asks:** whether people understood it, whether they can recall Stop–Shift–Do
unprompted weeks later, whether they recognised a real moment in their own life, tried the rep, and
did it more than once, and what helped or got in the way. **It is about whether the skill gets used
rather than how people are feeling.**

**Linkage** is a random system-generated ID with the contact key stored separately and
access-restricted. That makes the data **pseudonymous rather than anonymous**, and I would rather
describe it to participants accurately than call it anonymous because it sounds better.

# 2. Why the environment is central

**We are not claiming a one-off session produces change.** That is the claim this design exists to
avoid. What we propose is the sessions together with what gets implemented into the environment
afterwards: the team's shared language, the manager's conversations, whether Radar shows up in a
real meeting.

That is why the day-21 return does two jobs. It is a check-in with individuals, and it is a read on
the collective. The environment is where a skill survives or dies.

**Beyond that:** every partner organisation gets one further check-in at the end of the first
quarter, with individuals completing a personal form at about three months. A small number of
deliberately selected **case-study organisations** continue quarterly for a year. Selection would be
led by manager engagement at the day-21 return, since transfer climate and social support show
consistent but *modest* associations with whether training gets used (r = .27 and r = .21; Blume et
al. 2010, correlational, within workplace samples). **One limit stated precisely:** because we stay
in contact with case-study organisations across the year, this cannot show anything survives
independently of us. It is ongoing signal, not proof of durability.

# 3. Research measures, and why WHO-5 came out of the core

**So it is not in question: we know the forms need a validated instrument.** Our own questions alone
are not enough to stand on. The question is which instrument, and where in the timeline it sits.

| | **TYDQ-15**, Things You Do Questionnaire | **WHO-5**, Wellbeing Index |
|---|---|---|
| **Measures** | Whether someone is *doing* the actions associated with good mental health | How someone has been *feeling* over the past two weeks |
| **Validation** | 15 items, α > 0.90, on 6,070 community adults and 14,878 users of a digital psychology service (Titov et al., Macquarie) | Short, credible, internationally used, two-week recall |
| **Why it fits** | Built deliberately **not tied to any single therapeutic approach**, on general community adults | Our theory of change plausibly ends in wellbeing |
| **Falls short** | Does not ask whether someone caught a moment as it happened, or retrieved a process under load | A timing and governance problem, not a quality problem |

We also examined the **ANU skill-enactment scale** (Jackson et al., *JMIR Mental Health*, 2024): 13
items, α = 0.84, on 487 university students. Close to our question, but bespoke to that programme,
with items on sleep hygiene and social media that are their curriculum rather than ours. Their own
paper says they built it because nothing standardised fit, and points readers to TYDQ.

**Three reasons WHO-5 left the three-week core**, none of them about instrument quality.

1. **The timing does not work at three weeks.** If wellbeing moves in a small, uncontrolled,
   self-selected group, we cannot attribute it to the workshop. If it does not move, that reads as
   failure when it should not. A positive result would also be the most tempting thing to over-claim.
2. **The licence.** WHO-5 carries a CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO licence, NC being NonCommercial. Foundations
   is sold to employers. Whether that constitutes commercial use needs a proper determination.
3. **It is screening-adjacent.** WHO's scoring guidance treats low scores as a depression-screening
   signal. Collecting it means holding something specific about a specific person, and we have
   neither a named clinical owner nor an approved response pathway.

**Where I have landed.** **TYDQ-15 as the validated anchor, at baseline and three months only**, not
post-session and not in the Introduction: it measures a broad repertoire that cannot move in sixty
minutes, and running it there would manufacture a documented non-result on a validated instrument.
Our own core items stay identical across all three services. **WHO-5 returns on the case-study arc at
three and twelve months, and only once the response pathway exists.** This does not fix attribution,
and I want to be exact: a twelve-month score across a handful of self-selected organisations, with us
in contact throughout, is *more* confounded than a three-week one, not less. It would be descriptive
context and hypothesis generation, never an effect and never attributed to us.

**Worth flagging, since it changes what you last saw.** The version of the forms you have seen
carried WHO-5 at pre-session and again at three weeks. That is what has moved, and it is the part of
the design I hold least firmly.

**What would actually get us closer to attribution.** A **staggered start** inside one organisation:
team A now, team B in three months. Nobody is denied anything, it costs nothing, and team B is a
genuine comparison group while they wait. Cheaper still, **benchmark our TYDQ scores against Titov's
published community norms** from 6,070 adults, so a reference distribution does some of the work a
control group would.

**Two things to be straight about.** A modified validated scale is no longer validated, so we either
use one intact or label ours clearly as MFC's own. And we have not confirmed TYDQ's licensing terms.

**One thing I would rather you hear from me than find.** This instrument work was done properly in
early August, and **no validated instrument ever went into the forms.** To be fair to the decision
that removed WHO-5, it was deliberate and reasoned, and it kept WHO-5 available as a future module.
The gap is not that WHO-5 came out. It is that **nothing went in to replace it.**

# 4. Two safety matters I had wrong

**Disclosure risk is not specific to WHO-5.** Our own core items ask what got in the way and for a
real moment from someone's life, in free text, from people who chose to attend a mental-fitness
session, linked to a code whose key we hold. That will surface difficult material with or without
WHO-5. **The clinical-response gate therefore applies to the whole core**, and nothing is fielded
before a named clinical owner and a written response pathway exist. That is a change in my thinking
from a few days ago.

**The employer context creates risks I had not named.** Foundations is bought by an employer, the
participants are their staff, and the organisation receives a report. Consent inside an employment
relationship is not freely given by default, so opting out must be genuinely possible and invisible
to a manager. Aggregate reporting can still re-identify people in a small team, so there needs to be
a minimum group size below which we report nothing. And employees may under-report distress precisely
because their employer commissioned the session, which makes low scores both less reliable and more
concerning. Where those lines sit is not settled.

# 5. The research pathway

**The gap we are trying to occupy** is not that skill use over time in ordinary non-clinical settings
is under-discussed. It is that **it is not measured.** Measurement here either sits inside clinical
or academic intervention research, or stops at a post-session survey capturing whether people enjoyed
it. Whether someone was still doing the thing three months later, in a workplace that either
supported it or did not, is data that mostly does not exist.

**Ethics approval.** We are not pursuing HREC approval yet. Anything captured before a formal ethics
relationship exists **will not count toward a publishable study**, and cannot be retrofitted. It
remains good for our own findings and for a **future Rapid Research Review** building on Debra's. A
future-research consent clause costs nothing now and keeps the door open.

**Universities.** Of 31 Australian universities checked directly, 13 will not accept an application
led by an unaffiliated external organisation. The genuinely open pathways, Adelaide's being the best
at no fee, still require a collaborating academic attached. **The real work is finding one researcher
rather than emailing ethics offices.** And if a researcher asked today what we have, the answer is
nothing yet, which is the argument for starting.

The obvious people are the authors of the two instruments closest to our question: the ANU group and
Titov's group at Macquarie. Not for permission, but to ask whether they would review what we build or
co-develop the part their instruments do not reach. The ANU centre already partners with a community
organisation, Menslink, to evaluate their programme, so there is precedent. **What that unlocks
beyond goodwill:** a named academic as investigator is what makes a university's ethics committee
available at all. **The collaborator is the ethics pathway.**

# 6. What I will send in the next fortnight

The **final workshop outlines** for all three services, the **learning outcomes** for each, and the
**measurement forms themselves** with the exact questions at pre, post, three to four weeks and three
months. That last one is what I most want marked up.

# 7. Honest limitations

No data exists, so everything here is a plan rather than a finding. Every institutional fact is desk
research, nobody at any university has been contacted, and Adelaide's no-fee pathway has not been
confirmed by emailing them. The primary NHMRC guidance document could not be retrieved, so our ethics
reading rests on secondary sources. MFC has **no evidence that Foundations specifically works**: what
we have is an evidence-informed design and Debra's rapid review establishing these skills are
trainable in general, which is a different claim. The quarterly check-in form does not exist yet and
no organisation has been approached as a case study. Our read of the measurement gap is a bounded
search rather than a systematic review.

Three further points. **Our forms currently contain no validated instrument at all.** **Nothing in
this design controls for anything**, and the staggered start is the only idea here that would change
that, untested. And **the clinical-response gate covers every form we field**, not only a wellbeing
one, which I had not properly accounted for until this week.
