## The short version

**Corrected recommendation, 2026-08-25.** One sequence, not three tracks:

1. **Find a researcher whose existing interest is skill enactment or skill acquisition.** This is the
   precondition for everything else, and it is the only step MFC can act on unilaterally today.
2. **Approach them on their research interest, not with MFC's questions.** What MFC offers is
   participants and a running intervention, not funding. Debra Fidler, 2026-08-24: *"You give them
   participants. That's why the research is getting participants."*
3. **Their institution runs the ethics application.** MFC supports it; MFC does not lead it.
4. **Hold the paid non-university HREC route as a bounded fallback**, understanding it buys approval
   without a partner.

### How to find them, per Eimear and Debra, 2026-08-24

- Go to a university's **School of Psychology staff profiles** and read research areas and interests.
  Eimear: *"I would go to ACU School of Psychology and then go into the Staff Profiles."*
- If interests are not listed, **read their publications**. Debra: *"they'll have all their citations,
  all their articles."*
- Or ask the question directly of a search engine or AI: *"who at \[university] is leading
  \[topic] research?"*
- **The research coordinator route.** Eimear: *"you find the research coordinator in the school of
  psychology where their job is to oversee all of the research."* They know which supervisors have
  honours, masters and PhD students needing real-world projects.
- **Do not filter to designated researchers only.** Debra: *"all academics have a research
  component."*
- A supervisor who likes the question *"will go in and bat for you"* (Debra), because a novel applied
  project they can publish is a win for them too.

### One hard constraint on outreach

Eimear, 2026-08-24: *"academics are very wary of AI... they are probably one of the best people to
see an email and go, that's AI, I'm not reading that... We want it to be really connected with you
and your passion and what you're about, rather than the AI version of you."*

**Researcher outreach emails must be written by Hunter, in his own voice.** Eimear and Debra both
offered to review before anything is sent. This is a binding constraint on how MFC and this system
prepare that outreach, not a stylistic preference.

## What HREC is, and why it matters here

A Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) reviews and approves research involving human participants
before it starts, under the NHMRC's *National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research*
(updated 2025). It exists to check what a well-meaning organisation can miss from the inside: whether
consent is genuinely informed, whether a vulnerable participant could be harmed, whether data will be
handled honestly, whether the study can answer the question it claims to.

**Why MFC can't skip it and still call this "research."** Ethics approval is expected prospectively,
not retrospectively — university HRECs will not bless a dataset already collected. If MFC gathers
WHO-5 responses now without approval, that dataset is at real risk of being unusable later for a
university partnership, conference presentation, or journal submission. The workshop itself doesn't
need to stop; the data collected before approval likely won't count toward the research goal.

**The 2025 update, in plain terms:** review is now a risk continuum rather than one-size-fits-all — a
genuinely low-risk study (a short, validated, non-clinical survey is the kind of thing this was built
for) can go through a faster delegated/expedited review instead of a full committee sitting, typically
weeks rather than months.

## Finding an academic partner — the fastest real path

Almost every closed door above opens the same way: a university-affiliated academic as Chief
Investigator, with MFC as the external partner rather than the applicant.

**A real, currently-operating scheme:** University of Melbourne's **Community Fellows Program**
matches a community-organisation employee with an assigned academic mentor to design and run a
research project. Pays the organisation $7,000 to cover the Fellow's time, plus a separate small
research budget. Victoria-based only — requires ability to attend Parkville campus for a three-day
orientation and periodic events. Confirmed via multiple independent listings; the program's own pages
were not directly fetchable this session.

**Where to actually look:**
- University community-engagement offices — UNSW Knowledge Exchange, Griffith Sciences Partnerships
  Office, and University of Sydney's Director of Engaged Research Development all exist specifically
  to connect external organisations with researchers — a warmer entry point than a cold central-ethics
  email.
- Named academic staff directories — psychology and public-health departments publish searchable staff
  pages with stated research interests; UNSW, Melbourne, Adelaide, ANU, Flinders and ACU all confirmed
  live and browsable.
- Authors of relevant published research — finding a recent Australian paper on workplace resilience
  or wellbeing training and cold-emailing its authors is a real, workable tactic; their affiliation and
  contact details are public by design.

Honest negative finding: no Australia-wide platform matching charities directly with academic research
partners was found to currently exist. Melbourne's Community Fellows Program is the strongest concrete
example located, and it's geographically limited.

## Could MFC skip HREC review entirely?

Likely answer: no, not full exemption — but probably not full committee review either.

Exemption (National Statement §5.1.15–5.1.18) requires either fully de-identified data before a
researcher ever sees it, or a method that's both anonymous *and* "highly unlikely to cause distress."
WHO-5 responses tied to identifiable workshop participants, collected with consent, don't fit the
de-identified path — and personal information used with consent specifically cannot be exempted under
this framework (§5.1.16, corrected from an initial mis-citation of §5.1.17). Separately, WHO-5's own
scoring guidance flags low scores as a depression-screening signal — a genuine, documented feature of
the instrument that sits uneasily against "unlikely to cause distress," especially administered to
people self-selecting into a resilience workshop.

Most defensible working assumption: MFC's pilot most likely sits in the lower-risk/delegated-review
pathway — faster than full committee review, but still a real review, not a skip. This is a working
hypothesis for planning, not a substitute for whichever HREC actually reviews it making the call.

## The bigger picture — data handling beyond this one pilot

Not just a workshop-forms problem. As MFC grows, donor data, volunteer data, and future programme data
all need the same basic discipline. Proportionate for a charity on MFC's budget — not enterprise
governance.

**What MFC should NOT build:** a dedicated Data Protection Officer, a formal enterprise governance
framework, or ISO 27001 certification — genuine over-engineering at this scale and budget.

**What MFC should build, sequenced:**
1. **Now** — one plain-language data policy covering donors, volunteers, and participants together.
2. **Now** — a consent form and collection notice; express, standalone consent for health-adjacent data.
3. **Now** — basic technical controls: access-controlled storage, named staff only, verified encryption.
4. **Now** — a one-page breach response plan; required regardless of MFC's size once health data is involved.
5. **As partnership firms up** — a data-sharing agreement with whichever university/academic partner becomes real.
6. **Only if MFC scales materially** — a formal governance framework; multiple programmes, paid staff beyond the founder, or an institutional funder requiring it, not before.
