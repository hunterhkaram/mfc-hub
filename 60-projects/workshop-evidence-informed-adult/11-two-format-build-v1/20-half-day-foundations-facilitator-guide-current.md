# Half-Day Foundations — current facilitator guide

> [!DANGER] Not cleared for participant delivery
> This is the current education-led teaching candidate. It is not permission to run a session.
> Do not use it until the cohort has an approved participant/referral card, approved and frozen
> measurement forms, privacy/legal and safeguarding procedures, insurer/host clearance, accessible
> materials, a resolved butcher-paper route and a successful timed room rehearsal.

## 1. What this guide controls

This is the delivery instrument for the current Half-Day Foundations arc in
`workshop-package.html`: 180 instructional minutes, two 15-minute breaks, then an optional
approximately four-minute immediate form after the teaching close. It replaces the sequence and
timings in `10-facilitator-guide-3hour.md`, which remains historical evidence of the rejected 44:56
practice-heavy arc.

The communication job is: **by the end, adults should understand that mental fitness is a
developable capacity for everyday life, recall Stop–Shift–Do, complete at least one small Mental
Rep, and leave with one personally meaningful moment they can notice again.**

The facilitator teaches the meaning. The slides hold the room's shared attention. The participant
materials support recall and private application. None substitutes for the others.

## 2. Authority and non-negotiables

The following wording is exact where shown:

- “Mental fitness is how well we navigate life's everyday moments.”
- “Mental health is how we are doing. Mental fitness is what we are able to do.”
- the five full public skill sentences;
- Stop — catch the moment; Shift — create enough space where useful and available; Do — make the
  next move with intention;
- “The Passenger is not bad.”
- “Is my state too much, too little, or workable for this moment?”
- the full Mental Rep definition;
- “Think about the person you are trying to become in this season of life.”

Do not:

- call the product the “3-hour workshop”; three hours describes approximate teaching time only;
- teach the seven deeper skills as participant-facing content;
- present the Human Operating System as neuroscience, diagnosis or a map of the brain;
- turn the State Check into a fourth step, traffic light, mood scale or public disclosure;
- say amber means calming down or green means the right answer;
- imply a rep worked only when the outcome was good;
- ask participants to disclose personal material;
- suggest unsafe, structural or workload problems are solved by individual skill;
- claim this workshop causes wellbeing improvement, prevents illness or produces habit formation.

## 3. Delivery roles and room conditions

Minimum operational roles once cleared:

1. **Lead facilitator** — teaching, timing, room observation and opt-out protection.
2. **Host contact** — venue, accessibility adjustments and agreed response procedure.
3. **Support/escalation contact** — the qualified procedure must state who this is and what they do.
4. **Second facilitator or tested film route** — required for the contrast activity.

Room requirements:

- 12–20 participants; any other range requires a fresh facilitation/load review;
- accessible entry, seating, toilet and break route;
- wall or alternative display space approved by the venue;
- screen readable from the furthest seat, with a tested dark/light fallback;
- seating that permits individual work without facilitators reading over private worksheets;
- printed large-type alternatives available without a participant having to justify the request;
- paper alternative for every digital form;
- visible clock for the facilitator, not a public countdown.

cognitive load.

## 6. Facilitation conventions

- `[SAY — EXACT]` preserves locked wording.
- `[SAY]` is a safe working script; natural delivery is allowed if meaning and boundaries remain.
- `[DO]` is an operational instruction.
- `[CHECK]` is evidence the facilitator must observe; it is not a participant test.
- `[CUT]` states what may be shortened if the room runs late.
- `[NEVER CUT]` protects the learning or safety function.
- `[GATE]` means the session may not proceed without the named condition.

Use we/us for shared human explanation. Use you for direct exercises. Avoid “the system” when a
normal verb will do the work.

**Job:** make low-friction practice legitimate rather than lesser.

[SAY]

“You would not walk into a gym and start with the heaviest thing in the room. You would train with

## 2:40–2:46 — Four kinds of shift

**Job:** widen the menu without turning it into a prescription list.

[DO] Issue the shift-toolkit sheet.

[SAY]

“A shift can work through four places: body, attention, perspective or connection. The same tool can

- attention — widen to the room or narrow to one next task;

[SAY]

“Connection is only where it is safe and appropriate, and only with people you would choose. Some

## 2:52–3:04 — The same moment twice — the engineered peak

**Job:** let participants see the process without promising a better outcome. **Named 2026-08-19,
Hunter, direct decision, as this session's deliberate peak moment** — per the peak-end research this
redesign is built on (Kahneman, Fredrickson & Schreiber), people remember an experience by its most
intense moment and its ending, not the average, and this beat already has the dramatic shape to carry
that weight — a real scene, a real contrast, played straight. It just needed to be treated as the
peak on purpose rather than one beat among many.

# 7. If time slips

Never recover time by removing consent, privacy, breaks, the gap/feelings foundation, the three
Stop–Shift–Do corrections, a real rep, private Radar safeguards or the close.

Recover in this order:

1. shorten facilitator examples within the five skills;
2. shorten HOS elaboration while preserving three parts and its boundary;
3. use fewer optional retrieval repetitions while preserving both directions and three corrections;
4. shorten contrast debrief examples, not the two versions;
5. reduce optional pair discussion;
6. protect the full six-minute Radar window and keep the approved instruction of exactly three
   moments; invite participants to leave a line blank rather than force it. Do not silently turn a
   one-moment start into the approved three-moment Radar or change the product because time slipped.

Do not run past the room's agreed finish without asking the host and participants. Form 2 sits after
the teaching and may be completed through the approved later route if the cohort protocol permits.

# 8. Incident, disclosure and distress boundary

This guide deliberately does not invent a response procedure. The approved qualified-human
procedure must state:

- what the facilitator says and does if someone is distressed;
- when and how the support contact is involved;
- emergency and mandatory-reporting thresholds;
- host responsibilities;
- documentation and privacy rules;
- the limits of confidentiality;
- post-session follow-up responsibility.

General facilitation rule: do not invite details, diagnose, counsel, promise confidentiality beyond
the approved policy, or improvise a referral. Preserve dignity, choice and the person's immediate
safety while following the approved procedure.
