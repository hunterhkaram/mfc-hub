# Material 2 — Workshop map

> ### Half-Day Foundations
>
> Three one-hour learning blocks, two 15-minute breaks, then an optional short form after the
> teaching close.
>
> **First — why none of us were ever taught this**
>
> We will start with the room's own words, make the mental-health/mental-fitness distinction, and
> explore thoughts, feelings and what can be practised.
>
> *Break — 15 minutes.*
>
> **Second — the tool and what shapes access**
>
> Five skills. Stop–Shift–Do. A simple three-part model. A private State Check. Then one small Mental
> Rep.
>
> *Break — 15 minutes.*
>
> **Third — use it and carry it into life**
>
> Try two kinds of shift, watch the same moment twice, choose exactly three personally meaningful
> moments for your private Radar, and take one optional reminder for one priority moment with you.
> Three is MFC's pilot structure, not a scientifically optimal count. Leave a line blank rather
> than forcing an answer; nobody checks whether you filled the page.
>
> **How today works**
>
> - You never have to share anything personal.
> - Your private writing stays with you.
> - You can sit out, step out or ask for another way to take part.
> - “One” is a complete outcome. Nobody is checking whether you fill a page.
> - Toilets, water, access and support: [APPROVED VENUE DETAILS].
>
> **The meaning we use today**
>
> Mental fitness is how well we navigate life's everyday moments.
>
> Mental health is how we are doing. Mental fitness is what we are able to do.
>
> A mental rep is catching a moment and choosing your next move, on purpose, toward what matters. You may catch the
> moment before it happens, while it is happening, or afterwards. The outcome does not determine
> whether it counts.

# Material 3 — The five skills

> ### The five skills
>
> **Notice** — notice what is happening inside you, around you, and between you and others.
>
> **Shift** — create enough space to become workable — through your body, or through your thinking.
>
> **Choose** — identify what matters and follow through.
>
> **Focus** — put your attention where it matters and return it when it drifts.
>
> **Connect** — connect better with yourself and others.
>
> **Workable** means enough energy, steadiness and clarity for what this moment needs. It does not
> mean calm.
>
> Most moments do not need all five. Naming the skills gives us something we can practise.

The five public sentences are locked. The seven deeper skills do not appear on this card.

# Material 4 — Stop–Shift–Do

## Front

> ### Stop · Shift · Do
>
> **STOP** — catch the moment.
>
> **SHIFT** — create enough space where useful and available.
>
> **DO** — make the next move with intention.

Production must use word + distinct shape + colour + fixed position. Red requires a noticing or
catching glyph—not a stop palm. Amber uses a change-direction glyph. Green uses a next-move glyph.

## Back

> ### Three things the colours do not mean
>
> **Red is not freezing.**
>
> Stop means catching the moment. It does not mean halt or wait for permission.
>
> **Amber is not calming down.**
>
> Shift means creating enough space to become workable. Sometimes that is settling; sometimes it
> is becoming sharper or more connected. Some moments need no obvious Shift.
>
> **Green is not the right answer.**
>
> Do is the next move made with intention. The outcome does not determine whether the rep counts.

# Material 5 — Human Operating System

> ### A simple model for one useful question
>
> This is a teaching model—not a map of the brain, not a diagnosis and not something you need to
> memorise.
>
> **Driver**
>
> What you can reach right now: noticing, paying attention, pausing, choosing and making your next
> move with intention.
>
> **Passenger**
>
> Thoughts, feelings, urges, what you want and need, memories, and how you read a situation.
>
> **The Passenger is not bad.**
>
> **Engine**
>
> Your body: energy, tiredness, tension, breathing, hunger, sleep and recovery.
>
> ---
>
> ### The point
>
> **What is shaping what is available to me right now?**
>
> What is making a useful response easier or harder to reach?
>
> Context, support and what the situation asks of us matter too. This model explains access; it
> does not assign blame.

This current workshop material intentionally teaches three parts only. The six-part sheet in the
superseded pack must not be issued with this guide.

# Material 6 — State Check

> ### State Check
>
> ## Is my state too much, too little, or workable for this moment?
>
> **Too much** — downshift towards workable.
>
> **Workable** — probably no shift needed. **Workable does not mean calm.**
>
> **Too little** — upshift towards workable.
>
> “For this moment” is the point. The answer may change across a day. It is not a score, mood label
> or judgement about you.
>
> Nobody asks you for the answer. It is yours.

No colours, scale, traffic-light layout or fourth-step framing.

# Material 7 — Shift toolkit

> ### Four places a shift can work through
>
> A shift creates enough space to become workable. The same tool can bring us down or bring us up.
>
> **Body**
>
> Stand. Release your shoulders. Walk. Eat something. Step outside. Try a slower out-breath if it
> suits you.
>
> **Attention**
>
> Name what you can see. Feel your feet on the floor. Widen to the whole room. Narrow to one next
> task. Put the phone elsewhere.
>
> **Perspective**
>
> Is this a fact or a story? What else could be true? What would I say to someone I care about in
> this spot? What matters here?
>
> **Connection**
>
> Ask for two minutes. Text someone. Sit near a person you choose. Seek appropriate support.
>
> Connection is only where it is safe and appropriate. Some situations need a boundary, support,
> system change or something outside this workshop—not a better individual response.
>
> ---
>
> ### When we practise
>
> You can watch instead. You do not have to close your eyes, control your breathing, stand, touch
> anyone, work with a partner or explain what happens.
>
> If a strategy does not suit you, that is information about the strategy—not a failure in you.
