# Unit economics

## How a cost is actually built

> **(hours worked × $66.10) + materials, all of it multiplied by 1.4**

**Three things in that formula, and each is a decision somebody made.**

**$66.10 an hour** is what the facilitator is paid for every hour actually worked on an engagement:
travel, setup, pack-down, scoring, not just time in the room. It sits just below the 90th percentile
of the national training-facilitator band. **One adviser's test was minimum wage, then double, then
double again. It clears double and not quadruple**, and that gap is recorded rather than closed.

**Materials** are the cash cost of what gets printed and used. **Costed by the founder on 18
September, from what the sessions actually need**, replacing figures that had been inherited rather
than derived.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **The half day** | **$50 a session** | About $25 of material used in the room, plus the cards. **Participants do not take a pack away**, so it barely moves with headcount |
| **The Hour** | **$25 a session**, bulk printed | An eight-sided sheet folded into a card. **This one does go home with people**, so it is the figure that would move if a room got large |

**This is a founder estimate rather than a quote**, and it is the first time these numbers have come
from knowing what the session needs rather than from a previous document.

**The 1.4** allocates the overhead MFC carries and never invoices: governance, insurance, product
development, unfunded founder time. **Without it the price only covers the day and none of the
organisation behind it.**

**Two numbers come out of every engagement, and confusing them is how a previous round produced an
error that survived four documents.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cash kept** | Price, minus the hours paid, minus materials. Real money in the account |
| **Loaded cost** | The same with the 1.4 applied. **This is the floor, and nothing is sold below it** |

## What can come off, and what cannot

**This is the part that has to be settled before anything is quoted, and two of the three answers are
already in MFC's own records.**

### Two rules, both already settled

**The report is a half-day product only.** The Hour does not produce one.

**And a client may decline a report; MFC may not withhold one.** The reporting decision of 9 September
says every cohort buying a measured product receives a written report. **That was about not
suppressing reports for small cohorts, not about a client declining one.**

### Measurement is not a module

**Settled 18 September.** MFC delivers education and training **with measurement**, so an engagement
sold without it is not the thing MFC says it is. **The half day is not offered without it.** The Hour
is the only exception, because it is a talk rather than an intervention.

**This was the hardest question in the model and it is worth recording why it was hard.** The
measurement block is **8.17 hours on site or 6.75 online, 45% and 37% of every hour in the
engagement**, at a loaded cost of **$756 or $625**. **The thing MFC needs most is the most expensive
thing to give away, which would have made it the most attractive thing for a buyer to cut.** Taking it
off the table is what resolves that, and pricing could not have.

### The leader session is the one genuinely removable part

**1.5 hours, $139 loaded. The cheapest block by a distance.** Removing it saves an organisation
almost nothing and costs MFC almost nothing, which makes it the right thing to concede first and the
easiest thing to argue for keeping.

## The floor

**A formula, not a number, because the package varies.**

> **(hours in that configuration × $66.10 + materials) × 1.4**

**And a harder line underneath it.** Below **pay plus materials with no overhead** the facilitator is
working unpaid. For a full half day that is **$1,262**. **That is the absolute limit, and it is not a
price, it is the point where a concession stops being a concession.**

**Two things move the floor and neither is settled.**

**Travel is an assumption, not a term.** One hour each way is built into every on-site figure. **A
regional engagement at two hours each way adds $278 loaded**, and the vault records no travel radius
anywhere. **A product priced at a thin multiple goes underwater on a single regional trip.**

**The concession lane is not set.** A previous attempt breached the loaded floor on a regional
engagement and inverted the ladder, and the record says plainly that it is being rebuilt. **Its
design principle is already ruled: the facilitator's hours and travel stay whole, and MFC's margin is
the only thing that reduces.**

## Settled, 18 September: measurement is not removable

**Founder decision.** MFC delivers education and training **with measurement**. An engagement sold
without it is not the thing MFC says it is, so it is not offered without it.

**The one exception is the Hour.** It is a talk, not an intervention, and its measurement can be cut
on request.

**What follows from that, and it is the important part.**

| Product | Required | Can come off |
|---|---|---|
| **The half day** | The core, and **the return session** | **The manager session** and **the report** |
| **The Hour** | The core | Measurement, on request |

**If the manager session comes off, the managers attend the half day itself.** That is a condition of
removing it, not a concession, and it is said plainly: **it is less likely to hold without their own
session**, because the environment is the thing they control.

**The report is 2.5 hours removable, not 4.** The cohort form review has to happen either way, because
that is where MFC's own evidence comes from. What comes off is the write-up and the conversation.

**Reports are a half-day thing only.** The Hour does not produce one and is not sold as though it
might.

### The configurations

| | Floor | Price at ten | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|

| **No manager session** | $1,628 | $4,550 | 2.79× |

**One wording clash to record before somebody reads it the other way.** The reporting decision of

about not suppressing reports for small cohorts, not about a client declining one.** A client may
decline; MFC may not withhold.

## The quarterly check-in, and who MFC goes deep with

**Offered, never sold.** It sits outside contracted scope and outside the floor, which is consistent
with the ruling already on the record that check-ins are offered rather than sold.

**And MFC chooses.** Not every organisation gets ongoing tracking. The criterion is whether the place
is actually doing it: **are the managers behind it, is the practice visible, is there anything worth
following.** Some organisations will agree to everything and nothing will happen, and tracking those
produces a measurement of the absence of conditions rather than of the programme.

**Where an organisation wants it and is struggling, MFC goes back anyway.** That is the case-study
relationship, and it is where discounts and a mutual arrangement belong, because the organisation
knows what it is trading.

### Why this is stronger than it looks, and the one trap in it

**Everyone already gives MFC day-21 data, because the return session is required.** So the selection
is not about who gets measured. **It is about who gets measured twice.**

**That produces the comparison nobody else in this market has.** Day 21 across every cohort, and a
quarterly line only through the organisations whose managers backed it. **The difference between those
two is the evidence for the conditions layer**, which is the half of MFC's position that does not yet
exist.

**The trap is selection bias, and MFC has already diagnosed it in a competitor.** The market work
found that a schools programme's six-year results come from self-selected survivors, and that long
exposure and survivorship are not separable. **The same applies here the moment MFC publishes from the
engaged subset alone.**

**So the rule that has to travel with this:** record why each organisation was or was not selected, in
writing, at the time. **Manager support is not a filter applied quietly before the analysis. It is a
variable, and it is the one MFC most wants to be able to report on.**

**So scope is almost no longer the lever.** Under the earlier model an organisation could strip its
way down to a cheaper engagement. Now they can remove one cheap component or move to a different
product. **Price flexibility has to come from the concession lane, not from taking the product
apart** — which makes building that lane properly the next real piece of work.

## The half day, per person

**$3,900 plus $95 a person, capped at twenty.**

**The floor is $1,767 at two people and at twenty**, because the half day's materials are used in the
room rather than handed out.

| People | Price | **Per head** | Multiple | MFC keeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | $4,090 | **$2,045** | 2.31× | $2,323 |
| **4** | $4,280 | **$1,070** | 2.42× | $2,513 |
| **6** | $4,470 | **$745** | 2.53× | $2,703 |
| **8** | $4,660 | **$582** | 2.64× | $2,893 |
| **10** | $4,850 | **$485** | **2.74×** | **$3,083** |
| **12** | $5,040 | **$420** | 2.85× | $3,273 |
| **15** | $5,325 | **$355** | 3.01× | $3,558 |
| **20** | $5,800 | **$290** | 3.28× | $4,033 |

**Every configuration clears its floor comfortably**, thinnest at two people and now at 2.31× rather
than 2.14×. **The published multiples were built on the $150 figure and are all conservative by about
0.2×.**

### The rule that comes with this table

> **Sell it on the engagement. Never on the per-head number.**

**A two-person room is $2,045 a head and a full room is $290.** Nothing about the delivery differs
enough to justify that spread to a buyer who does the division, and **the cost barely moves with
headcount: two people cost almost exactly what twenty cost to deliver.** The per-head figure is an
artefact of a base-plus-person structure, not a statement about value.

**If a buyer raises it, the honest answer is that the cost is the day, not the head count**, and that
a small team is buying the same four hours a large one is.

### The marginal person is where the money is

| | |
|---|---|
| **One extra person adds** | **$95** |
| **And costs** | **Almost nothing.** No extra facilitator hours, and the half day's materials stay in the room |
| **Net contribution** | **About $95 a person, effectively all of it** |

**This is the single most useful number in the model.** Filling a room from eight to sixteen adds
**$760** for the same day's work. **Every conversation about headcount is worth more than every
conversation about price**, and MFC has been having the second one.

**And it now holds for a real reason rather than a modelling gap.** The half day's materials are used
during the session rather than handed out, so a twentieth person genuinely adds close to nothing.
**The earlier version of this document asserted the same conclusion from a flat figure nobody had
checked, which happened to be right for the wrong reason.**

**One place it would move.** The Hour's card does go home with people, so at a large room that figure
scales. At the current cap of twenty it is immaterial; if the Hour is ever run for a bigger audience,
it needs revisiting.

## The Hour

**A flat $600 stops working as soon as the room grows**, because the card goes home with people at
about $1.25 a head. At a hundred people the floor is $630 and $600 is **0.95×, below cost**.

### Banded, 18 September

| | Price | At the top of the band |
|---|---|---|
| **Up to 20** | **$600** | 1.22× in person, 1.36× digital |
| **21 to 50** | **$800** | 1.47× |
| **Over 50** | **$1,000** | 1.59× at 100, 1.39× at 150, 1.24× at 200 |

**$1,000 is a ceiling, not a band.** It holds above floor to about 312 people, so no realistic room
breaks it, and it sits just under the nearest published comparator at $1,199 for a session of the same
length.

**The real argument for a ceiling is not the arithmetic.** At $1,000 the Hour keeps **$370**. The half
day at ten people keeps **$3,083**. **That gap is what stops the Hour becoming the business.** If it
could earn $1,500 there would be a standing temptation to chase large rooms instead of doing the work
that produces evidence. **The ceiling keeps it a door.**

**The break comes at twenty rather than fifty**, which is the number MFC already uses everywhere else.
At a flat $600 a fifty-person room returns 1.11×, thinner than a twenty-person room — bigger room,
worse margin, which is the wrong shape.

### Two things this does not fix

**Twenty is an absolute cap on every product**, set with the committee and a clinical adviser and
recorded as not negotiable at any price. **Bands above twenty break it.** The cap's reasoning is a room
where people work on their own real situations with one facilitator; **a talk is a different shape, and
whether the cap applies to it is a clinical judgement rather than a pricing one.** The buyer pack
already carries this as an unresolved objection: a conference cannot buy the Hour under the stated cap.

**And an in-person Hour outside the assumed travel range is already underwater.** Twenty people, one
regional trip: floor **$768** against $600. **0.78×.** The fix is an explicit travel term rather than a
higher base, which is MFC's own established reasoning for the identical case.

**With measurement removed on request** the floor falls to about **$393** and the multiple rises to
1.53×. **So dropping it costs the buyer nothing and improves MFC's margin**, which is exactly the
wrong incentive and worth watching. **The reason to keep it is that comprehension before and after is
the only thing the Hour can honestly report.**
