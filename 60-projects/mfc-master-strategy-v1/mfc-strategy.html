The mission

Help people understand and strengthen their mental fitness through education and lifelong practice. The world we're aiming at: mental fitness as normal a part of life as physical fitness already is.

The gap nobody else is closing

Mental health support mostly sits at two ends: crisis care, and general wellness content. Almost nothing teaches the operational skill for the ordinary moment someone is already in. People usually know, in the abstract, what they should do under pressure. Knowing isn't the problem — not having a practised process to actually retrieve in the moment is.

Stop–Shift–Do: notice the moment, shift what's needed, take the next intentional action. Practised enough times, it becomes a mental rep.

We didn't invent the underlying pieces from nothing — they draw on established therapeutic and behavioural traditions. What's ours is the specific combination: one loop, one cue, one countable unit of practice, taught as a skill rather than delivered as therapy.

Where the field has failed, and where we're different

Almost every programme before us has quietly found the same thing: teaching content once, without support afterward, doesn't produce lasting change. Our answer isn't to teach harder. It's to build the environment around the practice. See Pillar 2 for exactly how.

How we fund it

Organisations pay for the workshop and the ongoing engagement. That funds the organisation and, over time, access for people who can't pay. We have zero paying customers as of today — every figure is a tested hypothesis, not a guarantee. See Products and Pillar 4 for the full picture.

How this document changes

When we learn something — new evidence, a real result, a conversation that changes our read — we come back and ask whether it still holds, and change it here, plainly. What doesn't change without a real reason: the mission, and the honesty about what we can't yet prove.

Message Design & Emotional Resonance — mini-strategy

Independently fact-checked, 19 Aug 2026, 2 rounds - 7 findings applied total

Why we chose this →

1. The goal

Design MFC's messaging so it evokes genuine curiosity and emotional connection across different audience segments, without triggering avoidance or reactance in any of them.

2. Why

MFC's own commissioned research already argues against the two things Hunter asked about, though as reasoned positions rather than completed tests: the category-entry research argues against leading with the category definition at MFC's scale (tagged as inference, not yet tested on MFC's own audience); the Australian-audience research named the toxic-positivity/individual-blame trap the fitness metaphor carries as MFC's single highest-probability way of pushing people away; the social-norms research found that kind of messaging risks licensing MFC's actual audience - people who've self-selected as already caring - to do less.

3. The plan
  1. Stop opening any buyer-facing or public copy with the category definition - lead with one concrete, specific trigger or moment instead (the category-entry research's own standing, previously unapplied recommendation - reasoned, not yet tested on MFC's own audience)
  2. Audit current messaging for the toxic-positivity/individual-blame framing the Australian-audience research names as MFC's highest-probability failure mode - check every use of the fitness metaphor for whether it implies unfitness is a personal failing
  3. Do not build a social-norms-correction message ("most people struggle more than they admit") as a standalone tactic - the social-norms research shows this is one of the worst-performing techniques in the field, with a real backfire risk on a self-selected, already-caring audience
  4. Use the Australian-specific permission structures already evidenced: self-deprecation as entry token, humour as a documented, under-used asset
  5. Check message design against the delivery format it will run in, not as a substitute for it - the campaign-comparison research (parkrun, Movember, time-boxed abstinence months, not R U OK? which it classifies separately as a partial-lever case) found that low-budget behaviour change came from structural properties, not message content alone
  6. This research is now screened into the source library and reconciled into MFC's committee-facing marketing strategy, so it's a standing reference, not a one-off citation
4. How we'll know it worked

Real engagement signals - curiosity-driven sign-ups, session attendance, unprompted shares - increase, and defensive or negative reactions decrease, compared against current messaging.

5. What could go wrong

This research is real and source-cited but still marked raw and unscreened - not yet independently reviewed. No current MFC messaging has been checked against any of this yet. The campaign-comparison research carries its own self-critique too: "concrete beats diffuse" could itself be a measurement artefact, not a settled law.

Practice Sustainment Model — mini-strategy

Independently fact-checked, 18 Aug 2026 - 3 findings applied

Why we chose this → Full specification →

1. The goal

Make MFC's teaching actually stick past the workshop, by building a weekly practice into the team's real week rather than relying on people to remember content from one afternoon.

2. Why

MFC's own inference (not a directly measured field statistic) is that 10-25% would still be practising at 3 months and 2-10% at 12 without a change to the design. The individual-content model lacks demonstrated durable effect on its own; the practice-environment layer is where the transfer literature points. This is MFC's answer to that gap.

3. The plan
  1. Setup conversation with the manager: identify the team's existing recurring meeting, name who owns the slot
  2. Weekly 3-minute slot runs on a fixed script and a rotating prompt bank - never a new meeting
  3. A visible shared team artefact (a Radar card or similar) is required - the specification leaves the exact format flexible, added 18 Aug
  4. Day-21 return session, in person, reinforces the method once
  5. Manager attends the monthly peer forum, brings back one thing each time
  6. Quarterly check-in call with MFC; annual report-back to the client, doubling as the case-study evidence
4. How we'll know it worked

The slot's own ran/skipped count - a real, non-self-report number, not a survey. Target: still running at the quarterly check-in. Monthly forms track recall and reported use alongside it.

5. What could go wrong

Never run with a real team. No psychologist co-host confirmed for the forum side. No facilitator exists but Hunter, so this can't scale past a handful of clients yet. Genuinely untested whether managers experience the weekly slot as meaningful or as one more resented meeting.

Funding & Revenue Diversification — mini-strategy

Independently fact-checked, 19 Aug 2026

Why we chose this → Pricing decision →

1. The goal

Build a mixed-income model so MFC does not depend on any single revenue source - direct-payment delivery, funded/community access, and eventually grants or philanthropy.

2. Why

MFC's own research is explicit: in this sector the product doesn't kill the organisation, the funding model does. Relying on one income stream is the pattern behind most failures studied.

3. The plan
  1. Direct-payment delivery (Service Portfolio & Pricing Tiers, priced per the pricing decision) is the near-term primary revenue lane
  2. Community/funded-access delivery (Target Market) is the secondary lane, paid by a sponsor or council rather than the beneficiary directly
  3. Grants and philanthropy remain a real but not load-bearing third lane - MFC has zero grant income currently and this must not be treated as available runway
  4. Reconcile the Grand Strategy's access-levy model against the current $3,900 base before quoting it to anyone (flagged separately, unresolved)
  5. Do not add a fourth revenue stream (merchandise, licensing) until the first two are proven - see Growth Pathway
4. How we'll know it worked

MFC has at least one real client in both the direct-payment and funded-access lanes within the same period, rather than all revenue coming from a single lane.

5. What could go wrong

MFC has zero customers in any lane currently. The access-levy reconciliation gap could mean the funded-access lane's actual mechanics aren't real yet, only assumed.
