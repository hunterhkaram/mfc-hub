# Why the new model is better, and where it is worse

## First, what actually changed

| | **Before** | **Now** |
|---|---|---|
| **Products** | Three, advertised as a ladder | **Two**, for different rooms |
| **What you lead with** | Whatever the buyer picks | **The half day, every time** |
| **The Hour** | $600 flat, a taster | **$600 / $800 / $1,000 by room size.** A real product |
| **Essentials** | $1,550 + $60 a person | **Gone as a product.** Reachable as a configuration |
| **Measurement** | Bundled, and detachable in a negotiation | **Required. It is what MFC is** |
| **What comes off** | Whatever got discounted | **The manager session and the report**, with conditions |
| **Discounting** | A number moving for no stated reason | **Scope comes off, or the concession lane** |
| **Materials** | $40 / $60 / $150, inherited | **$25 and $50**, costed from what a session needs |
| **Half-day floor** | $1,907 | **$1,767** |
| **Reports** | Every measured product | **Half day only** |
| **Quarterly check-ins** | Offered | **Offered, and MFC chooses who** |

---

## The number that settles it

**Three Essentials and one half day return about the same margin, and the half day gets there in half
the time and a third of the selling.**

| | Margin | **Hours** | **Sales to close** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three Essentials** | ~$3,000 to $3,175 | **34.3** | **Three** |
| **One half day** | **$3,083** | **18.3** | **One** |

**The hours and the sales count are the argument, and they hold under every assumption.** Essentials
returns **$88 to $93 an hour** depending on how its materials are costed. The half day returns
**$168**, because the cost of an engagement is the day rather than the depth.

**The margin comparison itself is close enough to be fragile, and it is worth saying so.** Essentials'
floor still carries an inherited $60 materials figure that has never been checked the way the half
day's was on 18 September. **Restated at $25, which is the cost shape of a ninety-minute session with
a takeaway card, three Essentials return $3,175 and beat the half day on margin alone.** The case for
the half day does not rest on that $55 gap, and it should not be argued on it.

**What does hold at every materials figure tested:** half the hours, a third of the sales, and nearly
twice the return per hour. **And the break-even follows from those**, not from the margin: moving from
three products to two has to convert about one of three Essentials buyers to leave MFC where it was.

## The year, modelled both ways

**Same plan, eight engagements.**

| | Revenue | Margin | Founder hours | Margin per hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Old model** | $13,700 | $6,752 | 71.6 | $94 |
| **New, if the bet holds** | **$21,800** | **$12,973** | 92.4 | **$140** |
| **New, if it fails entirely** | $7,250 | $3,725 | 37.3 | $100 |

**Read the bottom row honestly.** If every organisation that would have bought Essentials simply walks
away, revenue falls by nearly half. **That is the real downside and it is not small.**

**But look at the last column.** Margin per hour is **better in the good case and slightly better in
the bad one**, because a failed bet also means MFC did not spend the hours. **The model is more
efficient at every outcome. What varies is scale, not productivity.**

## Why it works, in commercial terms

**It stops MFC competing with itself.** Three products at different depths meant the cheap one ate the
good one. A buyer comparing $2,150 against $4,850 for something described as the same teaching picks
the cheaper one, and does so rationally. **Two products for different rooms do not compete.**

**It anchors high, and anchors only move down.** The first number said is the one everything else is
judged against. Leading at $4,850 and landing at $3,850 reads as a concession earned. **Leading at
$2,150 means $4,850 is never seen in that organisation again.**

**It protects the asset the whole strategy rests on.** Measurement is now constitutional rather than
bundled. Under the old model it was the most detachable thing in a price conversation. **Nobody
negotiates away the thing that is not on the table.**

**It makes every concession legible.** A price that falls for no reason teaches a buyer the first
number was invented, and every quote after that is a starting position. **A price that falls because
scope came off leaves the first number intact.**

**And it prices the Hour like a product instead of a favour.** Flat $600 went below cost at a hundred
people. Banded, it earns $22 an hour at twenty and $75 at a hundred, **and the $1,000 ceiling is what stops it becoming the business**: at its best it returns about half
a half day's return per hour, and an eighth of its margin per engagement.

---

## Where it is worse, stated plainly

**Fewer engagements means less learning.** Eight becomes perhaps five. For a pre-launch organisation
whose real shortage is evidence rather than revenue, **fewer rooms is a real cost** and it does not
show up in any of the tables above.

**Every sale matters more.** A lost half day is $4,850, not $2,150. With three or four sales carrying
the year, one falling through is a quarter of it.

**And a five-thousand-dollar decision takes longer than a two-thousand-dollar one.** More approval,
more people, a longer cycle. **The first quarter's plan assumes five quotes issued in ninety days**,
and that assumption got harder today, not easier.

**The concession lane is now load-bearing and does not exist.** Measurement cannot come off and the
two removable components total about a thousand dollars. **So when an organisation says the price does
not work, there is almost nothing left to give except the lane**, which the record says is being
rebuilt after the last version breached the floor.

**And the hours go up if it works.** 92 against 72, in a first quarter already asking for more founder
hours than anybody has established exist.

---

## What would tell us we were wrong

**Written down now, so a hard number reads as information rather than as failure.**

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Objections are about time, not money** | The model is right and the offer needs a shorter shape, not a lower price |
| **Objections are consistently about money** | The segment cannot carry $5,000 and the concession lane has to do real work |
| **Quotes issued but nothing accepted by six months** | The anchor is wrong, and Rob and Gina see it before a sixth quote goes out |
| **Half days sold but rooms are small** | The per-person element is where the money is, and headcount is the conversation MFC is not having |

**The cheapest test is the first ten conversations**, and the plan already schedules them.
