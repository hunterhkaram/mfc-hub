# KN-0006 — Current working direction

## Read this first

**This is the latest working direction as at 24 August 2026.** It is the source Claude should use
to understand the current logic before proposing forms, workshop measurement or researcher
outreach. It does **not** replace [[30-decisions/D-0100-first-pilot-measurement-core]], does not
make a form field-ready, and does not authorise a wellbeing, clinical, ethics or causal claim.

The central idea is simple: MFC is no longer treating the offer as one workshop that somehow causes
change. It is a **three-part intervention model** with separate roles, and the measures must keep
those roles separate.

## The three parts of the intervention

| Part | Job in the model | What it is not | What we need to learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Stop–Shift–Do teaching** | Give a person a simple, recallable process they can retrieve in an everyday moment. | Proof of competence, behaviour change or wellbeing change merely because it was explained. | Can they understand it, recall it without a prompt, and apply it once to a relevant moment? |
| **2. Radar and Mental Reps practice design** | Help a person connect the process to personally important, recognisable moments, choose a next move and practise the loop. | A habit tracker, a guarantee of repetition, or a requirement to habit-stack. | Did a real moment arise, did they notice it, recall the process and attempt a Mental Rep? What made that easier or harder? |
| **3. Environment and implementation conditions** | Give the team or organisation conditions that make use more possible: manager support, shared language, time, permission, resources and normal work rhythms. | A claim that MFC controls the workplace environment, removes psychosocial risk, or makes individual practice public. | Did the setting create opportunities and support for using what was taught? |

These are connected, but not interchangeable. Someone can remember Stop–Shift–Do without using it.
Someone can want to practise but have little opportunity in their environment. An organisation can
create better conditions without proving that every person used the process. Keeping the parts apart
is what lets MFC learn instead of telling itself one vague success story.

## The minimum practice package

The current product hypothesis is that Stop–Shift–Do should not be left as conceptual information.
The minimum package to test is:

1. teach the three-step process;
2. complete one guided Mental Rep during the session;
3. connect it to one personally relevant, recognisable moment; and
4. offer one optional recall or visibility support for the whole Radar.

The support is optional. A routine anchor can help some people, but it must not become a mandatory
habit-stacking rule, a streak or a test of personal commitment. Structural, relational, safety and
capacity constraints remain real explanations for non-use.

## Measurement: one layered design, not one giant form

### MFC's own questions: the mechanism layer

MFC questions are needed because no existing instrument asks the exact MFC questions. The core is
about participant-reported understanding, unaided Stop–Shift–Do recall, recognisable moments,
opportunity, noticing, attempted use, repetition, conditional helpfulness, barriers and supports.
Those questions should be explicitly labelled **MFC-designed**. They are not a validated scale.

They are also the way to distinguish the three intervention parts:

| Timing / question family | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Baseline and immediate post | Understanding, unaided recall and one guided application of Stop–Shift–Do. |
| Day-21 / week-3 follow-up for offers with a return | Whether a real moment arose; whether the person noticed it, recalled Stop–Shift–Do and attempted a Mental Rep; barriers and supports. |
| Three-month follow-up for selected case-study or research cohorts | A later descriptive read on reported use and conditions. It is not proof of durability or effect. |

### IMTTAQ: the validated transfer-and-opportunity comparator

**Current leading proposal:** use the intact six-item IMTTAQ module alongside, not instead of, MFC
questions: the three-item **Training Transfer** subscale plus the three-item **Opportunities to
Integrate** subscale.

Why it is a strong fit:

- it gives MFC a validated workplace-training comparator for reported transfer and environmental
  opportunity;
- it makes the workplace-condition part of the model visible rather than assuming it; and
- it leaves MFC's own questions free to capture the specific retrieval-and-practice mechanism the
  IMTTAQ does not ask about.

What it cannot do: prove skill acquisition, observed behaviour, wellbeing improvement, or causation.
The items are workplace-worded, so they are best suited to workplace cohorts and should not be
silently rewritten for family, school or community settings. A rewritten version becomes an
MFC-adapted item set, not a validated IMTTAQ subscale.

**Proposed timing, pending Eimear/Debra wording review and implementation decision:** day-21/week-3
for Essentials and Foundations, where the participant has had a real chance to use the learning;
repeat at three months for selected case-study/research cohorts. Do not use this proposal to state
that baseline or immediate-post IMTTAQ transfer scores are meaningful: the transfer and opportunity
items assume a post-training chance to use the learning.

**Original 2026-08-24 text, retained for traceability, no longer current:** The 24 August discussion
made a credible case for a wellbeing measure alongside transfer/enactment in a research or
case-study arc. If it is later approved, WHO-5 could provide descriptive wellbeing context and

question, licence confirmation, analysis plan, clinical-response pathway, privacy review and
appropriate ethics determination. This is an open decision/gate, not a quiet reversal.

### TYDQ-15 / Things You Do: useful, but not the current priority

TYDQ-15 is a validated broad measure of everyday actions associated with mental health across life.
It is useful for a different question: whether people report doing a wider repertoire of healthy
actions. It does **not** specifically measure retrieving and applying Stop–Shift–Do in a moment, or
whether a work environment enabled transfer.

For the current MFC question, adding all 15 items would increase burden without replacing the
six-item IMTTAQ plus MFC mechanism questions. It therefore remains a reference and a possible
future module, rather than the leading routine-form proposal.

## Research and partnership implication

The most useful research question is not “does one workshop work?” It is whether a brief
non-clinical programme can support reported retrieval and application in real moments, and how
reported use relates to opportunity and support in the participant's environment.

That makes the relevant researcher profiles:

- **skill enactment / implementation** researchers, including the ANU UVC-Lite authors;
- **training-transfer and workplace-learning** researchers, who can advise on use of IMTTAQ; and
- researchers with the capability to help MFC build a proportionate, ethical study rather than lend
  a name to an untested product.

No researcher or university has agreed to work with MFC. A university HREC pathway exists only if a
university researcher becomes an investigator; MFC cannot independently apply through a university
HREC. Internal service evaluation and publishable research remain different paths, and HREC cannot
be obtained retrospectively for already-collected research data.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Describe data as **participant-reported** unless it is independently observed.
- Do not call MFC's own questions validated, or describe an adapted subscale as validated IMTTAQ.
- Do not claim skill acquisition, competence, habit formation, environmental change, wellbeing
  improvement or causation from this design.
- Linked responses are pseudonymous, not anonymous, if MFC holds a contact-to-ID key.
- Participation and opting out must be genuinely voluntary and invisible to managers; small-group
  reporting, privacy, clinical-response and safeguarding rules remain open gates.
- No form is field-ready until D-0100's gates, including adviser wording review and two rounds of
  participant comprehension/usability testing, are completed.
