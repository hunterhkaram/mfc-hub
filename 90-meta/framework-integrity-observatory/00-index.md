# MFC Framework Integrity and Evidence Observatory — Hunter Review

**Start here. You should not need to open any other file in this package unless you want more depth.**

## What the framework currently is

MFC's mental fitness framework has three layers: a **category claim** (mental fitness as a developable capacity to navigate everyday moments, distinct from but complementary to mental health), an **explanatory architecture** (a teaching metaphor — the Human Operating System — plus five public skills simplifying seven deeper constructs, plus a simple in-the-moment method, Stop-Shift-Do), and a **practice mechanism** (State Check, Mental Reps, Radar). See [[90-meta/framework-integrity-observatory/02-canonical-framework-map|02-canonical-framework-map.md]] for the full picture and diagram.

**The single most important fact in this whole review:** none of it is committee-approved yet. Every kernel record is `status: provisional`. This review does not change that — it maps what exists and rates how well-supported each part is.

## How the main parts connect

Category → explanatory architecture → practice mechanism, roughly top-down, with the five public skills simplifying the seven deeper ones and Stop-Shift-Do drawing loosely (not rigidly) on both. Full diagram: [[90-meta/framework-integrity-observatory/02-canonical-framework-map|02-canonical-framework-map.md]].

## What is strongly supported

- **Radar** (the mechanism of pre-selecting a few recurring moments to notice/practise) — this is MFC's best-evidenced component, resting on decades of well-replicated implementation-intentions research (d=.27–.66).
- The individual **seven deeper constructs** each draw on genuinely evidenced adjacent psychological literatures (self-regulation, attention, cognitive flexibility, values-based action, self-compassion, relational skill) — though none has been tested as MFC's own integrated system.

## What is moderately or indirectly supported

- General trainability of psychological skills in non-clinical adults (moderate support for the components, limited support specifically for MFC's brief one-off delivery format).
- Mental Reps as a practice unit (adjacent literatures — single-session interventions, deliberate practice — are consistent with the idea; the specific "one outcome-independent rep" mechanism itself is untested).

## What is an MFC construct (not itself a scientific finding)

The public definition, the short distinction, the five-skill labels, the Stop-Shift-Do method, State Check, and the Human Operating System are all MFC's own synthesis and communication choices — legitimate product design, not claims requiring external validation in themselves, provided they are never presented as independently validated.

## What remains provisional / genuinely open

Whether "mental fitness" is a genuinely distinct scientific category, or a well-branded synthesis of existing constructs — **no construct-validation literature was found either way**, confirmed by a live proof-run search on 30 July 2026 finding no new hits. This is this review's single most consequential open question (see `10-...md`, Decision D-1).

## Strongest criticisms (and MFC's current responses)

1. **The Human Operating System risks reading as pseudo-neuroscience**, even with its disclaimer — external evidence shows disclaimers reduce but don't eliminate lay literalisation of brain metaphors. MFC's current disclaimer is necessary but not, on its own, fully sufficient.
2. **"Mental fitness" as a framework risks individualising problems that are actually social/structural** — a mature, credible critique lineage exists. MFC's "Environment" component addresses this in principle; whether it's delivered with equal weight in practice is unverified.
3. **No causal wellbeing claim can currently be supported** — MFC's own measurement (uncontrolled pre/post) cannot establish this, and MFC's own claim boundaries already correctly prohibit making such a claim. This finding *confirms* an existing correct boundary; it does not discover a live violation.
