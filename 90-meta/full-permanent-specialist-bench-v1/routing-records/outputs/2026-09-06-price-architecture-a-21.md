## Position

**The cost seam is closed:** `D-0113` (16.0h/8.5h) and `110-PRODUCT-AND-PRICING-BOOK` (15.75h/8.25h) are superseded on cost basis by `07-BUYER-PACK` §4.2 (18.34h/10.42h → $1,907.18/$1,048.26), which carries two structural under-costs neither earlier basis had: setup/pack-down 0.42h and booking/contract/invoicing 1.00–1.25h. Neither is marked `superseded_by:` — a live propagation gap.

**Five axes tested.** Headcount: KEEP, and it already does what Hunter asked — from 8 to 20 the buyer's Foundations cost per head falls 50.2% while MFC's return per hour rises 41.4%, simultaneously, with no discount. Organisation type: a published two-tier is precedented (Evolve WA) but DESIGN AND HOLD, because it would discount MFC's own recommended primary segment and load margin onto the segment MFC cannot pitch; a funded-access lane with a checkable eligibility test runs instead. Depth: KEEP — but **the claim that the next rung is better value per head is FALSE** (Small Team @8 = $139.38/head against Essentials @8 = $193.75), so the ladder must be sold on inclusions. Commitment: multi-cohort KEEP, annual retainer RULE OUT on capacity ($114.21/hour, one facilitator, no SLA). Payment shape: KEEP within three bounds.

**The crux is answered.** A repeat cohort releases 4.25–6.50 hours and $498–$707 of loaded cost, so both sides can gain at once.

## Strongest objection

**That the recommendation I originally attached to the crux was already decided against, and I did not cite the record.** It was, and I did not. `D-0122` §4 (2026-09-03) records Hunter rejecting a repeat discount with the repeat fractions in front of him — *"solve for the discount that holds per head at the benchmark and it comes out at zero on both products"* — and I attributed his words to `D-0125`, dated the decision eleven days old when it was three, and claimed he ruled without arithmetic that the record contains by name. **The objection is upheld in full and the recommendation is withdrawn.**

Second objection, from the builder rather than the skeptic, and it is worse: the three-cohorts-of-thirteen structure I proposed **breaches `D-0122` rule 2 against the buyer**. Essentials band pricing is not linear, so three rooms of thirteen charge $138.46/head where the decided rule (20+19) requires $105.13 — a 32% overcharge presented as a recommendation.

## Evidence strength and limitations

Strong on arithmetic: every figure re-derived from `07` §4.2's hour counts rather than copied, and independently reproduced by A-24 to the cent. Weak on inputs. **Zero delivered cohorts, zero quotes ever issued, zero buyer interviews.** Hunter's available hours, the conversion rate, MFC's fixed costs and any account-management line are `NO VAULT SOURCE`. The review-clause numbers (5 quotes, 3 cohorts, +$500 base) are chosen, not derived. `R2` is stale on MFC's own price and unmarked. GST is contradicted inside MFC's own documents and moves every figure by up to 10%.

## Alternatives considered

A published commercial tier now — rejected for four stated reasons, held behind Trigger D. An annual retainer — rejected on cover, not margin. A volume discount across bookings — already rejected by Hunter and by `07` §4.7 on correct reasoning. Raising Essentials materially (Route 1) — rejected now: even +38% on the entry band still needs 29.6 sales against a 23-sale ceiling, and the market median it would rest on comes from a sample where 18 of ~26 providers publish nothing. A flat price list with no structure — costed by A-24 and rejected: it needs 54–91 sales events and reaches minimum viable at no price.

## Recommendation

Adopt the architecture at §3.1 with **no discount anywhere**, following `D-0122` §4 as decided: groups filled to twenty in sequence, each at its own band. Take Route 2's 1.25 removable hours. Hold Route 1 behind Triggers A and B. Run the funded-access lane rather than a published tier. Adopt the review clause at §6 including its log schema, Trigger D (tier release), Trigger E (travel-radius breach), Trigger F (unconverted sequences) and the owner fallback. **Ask Hunter one question only: does `D-0122` rule 6's two-group cap permit a third group as a separate sequential engagement.**

## What would change this view

The first three quotes. A buyer who negotiates on structure rather than level would settle the banding question the market rather than by argument. A first repeat sale would test the 64.6% amortisation, the account-management gap and whether a purpose-led SME can fill three rooms at all. Three consecutive losses with price as the recorded reason would fire Trigger C. **None of it is knowable without a quote log, which does not exist and is the cheapest item in the whole corpus.**
