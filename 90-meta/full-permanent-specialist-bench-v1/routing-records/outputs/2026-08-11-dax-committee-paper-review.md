**Overall read:** the core pricing ladder itself is commercially sound — margins are real (1.1×–3.2×), nothing sells below loaded cost, and the market-comparator positioning in Section 3 is defensible. The structural weakness isn't the price list; it's that the paper presents the founding-rate mechanism and the sustainability target with more certainty than the working files behind them actually support.

**1. Founding-rate mechanism presented as settled when its own source flags it as an open recommendation resting on an unverified assumption.**
`D5-founding-case-study-pricing.md`'s status line reads: *"recommendation — awaiting founder decision on cohort count and trigger; the cohort-count reasoning rests on an unverified response-rate assumption, corrected and flagged in §1 after independent review."* D5 §1 states plainly that the 50% matched-response figure used to justify "3 cohorts clears the N≥20 reporting floor" is *"a working assumption, not a D3 finding"* — D3 has no verified Foundations-specific response rate at all. The final paper's Section 3 and ask #1 ("ratify... including the founding-rate mechanism") present the 3-cohort, 4-month-or-3-delivered structure as a fully reasoned mechanism ready for approval, with no trace of that uncertainty. A committee ratifying this on the paper's own terms would be approving a number whose load-bearing assumption is explicitly unverified in the source it was drawn from.
**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER** — this is a representational accuracy gap against the paper's own source material, not a judgment call.

**2. The against-case for the founding discount (the visible pricing seam) is in the source but not surfaced to the committee.**
D5 §7's own strongest "against" point: *"cohort 3 pays ~26–29% less than cohort 4 for what is, from the buyer's side, an identical product... a procurement-literate buyer or a committee member who notices the gap will ask why."* This is exactly the kind of risk a board should see before ratifying a pricing mechanism, and D5 itself judged it real enough to argue against explicitly (before concluding "for" wins). The paper's Section 3 and ask #1 give the committee only the polished mechanism, not this trade-off.
**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER** — omitting a risk the source document itself flagged as the strongest counter-argument is a completeness gap, not a discretionary editorial choice.

**3. "20 sessions a year" sustainability claim is asserted, not shown.**
Section 3 states the 20-session target "generates the contribution needed to sustain MFC's operations" and calls it "realistic, not aspirational," but gives no dollar figure, no derivation, and no visible stress test against Section 4's own admission that most of the six demand routes are still nascent (route 1: "we have not yet designed what's in it for them"; route 3: "we do not have a relationship with this insurer yet"; the digital session is "in build"). For a paper whose central ask is ratifying commercial viability, the sustainability claim should show its arithmetic and reconcile it with the demand-side reality described two sections later.
**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER** — not because 20 is wrong, but because an unsupported "realistic" claim sitting next to a demand pipeline that's mostly not yet built is exactly the kind of gap a financially literate committee member will catch immediately, and it's better caught here than in the room.

**4. Cover-page "$9,500 near-term grant funding" bundles an unconfirmed grant into a firm headline stat.**
Section 5's own table shows the City of Parramatta $3,000 as "Expected 1 September, pending confirmation with council" — not yet a live, open grant. The cover stat presents $6,500 + $3,000 as a settled $9,500 figure without that caveat, even though the body text later discloses it correctly.
**NOTED_NOT_BLOCKING** — minor precision issue; the caveat does exist in the body, just not on the cover.

**Not flagged (outside lens or genuinely fine):** the underlying price points, margin structure, and market-comparator reasoning in Section 3 check out against D2's worked figures — no arithmetic errors found. The founder-decision items in D5 §8 (whether "first couple" means a hard cap of 2, whether to extend the trigger window) are correctly left as Hunter's call and don't need to be resolved in this paper — but the paper should have said "recommendation, pending founder confirmation" rather than presenting the mechanism as ready to ratify outright.
