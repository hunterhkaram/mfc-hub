# A-19 research — what MFC may say about WHS in NSW

## Bounded question

What does NSW WHS law and the SafeWork NSW psychosocial Code actually require of an employer, where
does training sit, what can MFC honestly help with, and what does the evidence support?

## Position

**Two facts change MFC's footing, and both postdate the vault's current framing.**

1. **The Work Health and Safety Regulation 2017 (NSW) is repealed.** It was replaced by the **Work
   Health and Safety Regulation 2025 (NSW)**, commenced **22 August 2025**. The psychosocial
   provisions carried over as ss 55A-55D, with one material change: **NSW now applies the s 36
   hierarchy of control measures to psychosocial risk**, which the 2022 model regulations
   deliberately excluded. Any MFC document citing the 2017 Regulation cites a repealed instrument.
2. **Since 1 July 2026, s 26A of the WHS Act 2011 (NSW) makes approved Codes of Practice enforceable
   minimum performance standards.** The Code that names reliance on administrative controls as a
   common failure is no longer persuasive guidance. It is the benchmark, and the regulator does not
   need to prove harm occurred.

**Training is an administrative control, in the lowest-reliability tier**, and the Code says so in
terms: *"Relying on administrative controls should never be the main risk management approach"*
(s 3.4, p.21). The Code separately names, as a common failure, *"not following the hierarchy of
control principles and relying on mainly administrative controls"* (s 3.5, p.23).

**The Code's other named failure bears on MFC's product shape directly:** focusing on poor workplace
behaviours by an individual without controlling the hazards giving rise to them. MFC's conditions
layer is what keeps the product out of that limb, which makes the layer load-bearing to the pitch
rather than an optional extra.

**New finding, not previously recorded.** The Code (s 3.4, p.22) specifies what a PCBU's training
must cover: the nature of the work, **the organisation's own psychosocial hazards**, **its own
control measures**, and **how workers report and respond**. It also contemplates delivery by a
*competent person*. MFC's Foundations workshop teaches none of those four things. **MFC is therefore
not a substitute for the Code-required training a PCBU separately owes**, and saying otherwise would
be false. MFC should say this out loud before a buyer asks.

## Evidence strength and limitations

**There is no published evidence that a skills-training intervention of MFC's shape shifts a
measured psychosocial risk indicator.** Not weak evidence. Absent.

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Sakuraya et al. 2026, *J Occup Health* 68(1):uiag024 | Systematic review and meta-analysis, 14 cluster-RCTs, 8 meta-analysed | **Null.** Mental health conditions SMD -0.04 [-0.10, 0.03]; positive mental health -0.004 [-0.077, 0.068]; work performance 0.01 [-0.10, 0.13]. High risk of bias |
| Milligan-Saville et al. 2017, *Lancet Psychiatry* | Cluster RCT, Fire and Rescue NSW, 4-hour manager training | Significant reduction in work-related sickness absence. Manager training, one high-trust organisation |
| Gullestrup et al. 2023, *Health Promotion International* 38(4):daad082 | Systematic review, 12 studies | Increased help-offering intentions; authors state evidence is limited for causal inference, with no experimental studies |
| Fleming 2024, *Industrial Relations Journal* 55(2) | **Cross-sectional**, 46,336 workers, 233 organisations | Participants in individual-level interventions appeared no better off. **Cannot establish causation** |
| Int Arch Occup Environ Health 2024, 10.1007/s00420-024-02065-z | Systematic review, 22 studies, healthcare | **Strong** evidence for job and task modifications; moderate for scheduling and physical environment; **insufficient** for all other types |

**The pattern points away from training.** The intervention class with the strongest evidence is job
and task modification, which MFC explicitly cannot do. The class closest to MFC's shape has a null
meta-analysis. MFC holds zero cohorts and zero outcome data of its own.

Limits, stated plainly: `legislation.nsw.gov.au` and AustLII both returned **HTTP 403**, so
**regs 55A-55D and s 36 are not quoted verbatim** anywhere here; the numbering and the s 36
application are confirmed by three converging sources but a lawyer should confirm the operative
words before this reaches a buyer. ISO 45003 clauses 6, 7.2, 7.3 and 8 are paywalled and were not
read. Four of the five studies are vault-recorded from earlier passes and were not re-read today;
the fifth was read at abstract only.

**Excluded and recorded so a later session does not repeat it:** vendor marketing asserting
"mindfulness reduces stress by 38 per cent", "reduce absenteeism by 33 per cent" and "depression
rates by up to 79 per cent". None carries a traceable source. These are exactly what `AG-200` warns
about.

## Alternatives considered

Arguing the workshop is a higher-order control because the conditions layer touches systems of work
is defensible as an argument but is not an established position, and no Australian instrument
classifies group behavioural practice as higher-order. It may be used only labelled as MFC's own
argument, never as a settled classification.

## Recommendation

**Nine sentences MFC can defensibly say**, each sourced:

1. Not clinical care, not treatment, not therapy, not a substitute for legal or WHS advice, and not
   a standalone psychosocial risk management system. Nothing in it discharges any duty your
   organisation has. *(Code s 2.1 p.12: a PCBU cannot transfer or delegate any of its duties.)*
2. A measurable training intervention that may support an organisation's psychosocial risk controls.
   *(`AG-011`, the Chair's own wording. Use the register's words exactly.)*
3. The Code puts most of the effort on work design and systems of work. We are not that, and we are
   not a substitute for it. *(Code s 3.4.)*
4. Information, training, instruction and supervision is one of the matters a PCBU must have regard
   to in determining control measures. *(Reg 55D. Descriptive of the law, no claim about MFC.)*
5. **The Code says relying on administrative controls should never be the main risk management
   approach. Training is an administrative control. That includes ours.** *(Code s 3.4 p.21,
   verbatim. The strongest sentence in the set, because no competitor will say it.)*
6. Your Code-required training, covering your hazards, your control measures and your reporting
   pathway, is a separate obligation we do not meet. *(Code s 3.4 p.22. New; MFC has not been saying
   this and should be.)*
7. Sessions can feed your consultation with workers. They cannot be your consultation.
   *(WHS Act ss 47-49; Code s 2.1 p.11.)*
8. ISO 45003 names worker competence to recognise psychosocial hazards as something an organisation
   should consider. It is guidance, not a certifiable standard, and nobody is certified to it.
   *(ISO 45003:2021 cl. 4.1(f), 5.1(h), scope.)*
9. We have not yet delivered a measured cohort. We have no results to show you and we are not going
   to invent any. *(True, and the sentence that makes the other eight credible.)*

**Must not say:** Stop/Shift/Do is a psychosocial control (`AG-011`, and no evidence exists);
meets, satisfies, discharges or covers your psychosocial duty (non-delegable, ss 14, 272);
WHS compliant or compliance solution; counts as your Code-required training (false, per s 3.4 p.22);
counts as consultation; ISO 45003 certified (not certifiable); the Radar assesses your psychosocial
risk (it is a free self-assessment, not a validated instrument); reduces claims, absenteeism or
workers' compensation costs; "research shows this kind of training supports psychosocial risk
management" (**the most relevant meta-analysis is null, so this inverts the evidence**); any
statement of what a specific employer is legally required to do; anything citing the repealed 2017
Regulation.

## Strongest objection

That leading with "training is a low-order control, including ours" weakens the sale. The answer is
that s 26A made the Code a benchmark on 1 July 2026, so a buyer's safety adviser will apply that
test whether or not MFC raises it. Saying it first is the credibility position, and it is
`AG-292`-compatible because it clears the ground for what MFC *can* honestly offer.

## What would change this view

A revision of the SafeWork NSW Code, a published controlled trial of an intervention of MFC's shape
with a psychosocial outcome, or MFC's own measured cohorts once they exist.

## Adjacent specialist or human boundary

This is research, not compliance clearance; `head-of-compliance` owns what enters the permitted-claims
register. Characterising any specific employer's duty is legal advice and outside MFC's lane.

**Unresolved and needing qualified human input, not another research pass:** MFC delivering inside a
client's workplace **is itself a PCBU** with its own s 19(2) duty, and s 46 requires duty holders to
consult, cooperate and coordinate. What MFC must do if a participant discloses bullying or a
safety-critical matter mid-session, what it must pass to the host, and how that interacts with the
confidentiality MFC promises participants, is not resolved. The return-session report makes it live.

## Proposed learning

Two items for `head-of-compliance`, candidate updates rather than settled:

1. **A wording discrepancy.** The brief paraphrased the Chair as "psychosocial risk **management**";
   `AG-011` records "psychosocial risk **controls**", and `11-PERMITTED-CLAIMS.md` P26 uses the
   register's version. These are not the same claim. **The register wording governs**, and a
   paraphrase must not silently become the permitted form.
2. **A gap in the permitted-claims page.** Nothing covers the Code's four-element training-content
   requirement. A buyer will ask whether the workshop counts toward their training obligation, and
   with no permitted sentence a seller in the room will improvise one. Sentence 6 is the shape.
