# A-15 - getting an email address in the room without breaking anonymity

## Bounded question
How does MFC obtain an email address for each participant, in the room or around it, without
wrecking the anonymity the rest of the measurement design rests on?

## Position
**MFC collects the address itself, digitally, in the room, as a separate one-field form; and the
client sends a content-free nudge under a contract clause.** One channel MFC controls, one that
costs nothing and needs no address to change hands.

The capture rides on a moment that already exists - the close of the session, while Form 3 is being
completed on phones. One field, one tick, its own short link or a second QR on the same slide. No
code field, no name. The cohort is the hidden parameter, exactly as on the six forms.

⚑ **The separation is structural, not procedural.** The address and the response never arrive
together and there is no key connecting them, so the never-merged rule holds by construction rather
than by anyone remembering it.

## Professional principles applied
A control a facilitator cannot perform in a live room is not a control. The decline path has to be
safe before anyone exercises it, or it is not really optional.

## Evidence strength and limitations
**MFC has delivered zero cohorts.** None of this has been said in a real room, and the completion
rate it produces is unknown. `AG-176`'s ~15% is the number to beat and the first cohort is the only
thing that will say whether thirty seconds of script beats it.

## Alternatives considered

**The client organisation sends the reminder - taken seriously, rejected on three specific
grounds.** It is genuinely attractive: they hold the addresses, they want completion because
completion produces their report, and it removes the only identifiable data MFC holds.

1. **It breaks the withdrawal control.** §2.4 makes withdrawal verifiable by requiring the request
   to come from an address on the cohort list. With no list, the only verifier is the employer, so a
   participant wanting an anonymous survey removed must ask their manager to vouch for them.
2. ⛔ **The client would know who completed and who did not.** In a room of eight, a manager chasing
   non-completers is a coercion problem MFC cannot see and cannot stop. The whole point of *"never a
   breakdown by team, shift, site or role"* is that a small-team result is not anonymous, and a
   client-run chase makes participation itself visible.
3. **Timing.** One internal comms contact on leave in week three costs the cohort's primary change
   score.

**Paper sheet passed around the room - rejected.** It exposes both the addresses and the abstentions
to colleagues, and a manager at the table changes what people write. A few blank cards for anyone
without a phone covers the same need without the exposure.

## Recommendation
Build the one-field form. Put the thirty-second script on the run sheet. Add the content-free
client nudge as a contract duty:

> *The organisation will circulate one reminder to session attendees stating that the follow-up form
> has been sent. The organisation will not be told which individuals have or have not responded, and
> will not ask.*

The second sentence is what stops it becoming a chase.

## Strongest objection
That MFC is choosing to hold identifiable data it could avoid holding entirely. **True, and named
rather than hidden.** The answer is that the alternative hands the chase to the participant's
employer, which is worse for the participant on the exact dimension the design exists to protect.

## What would change this view
A first cohort where the sign-up rate is so low the list is not worth holding. A client refusing the
no-chase clause, which would say something about that client.

## Adjacent specialist or human boundary
⚠ **The facilitator script has not been past `lens-eimear-quigley`**, and what is said aloud in a
room is her territory. Before it reaches a run sheet, it should be. The contract clause is a draft
for Bird & Bird, not a finished term.

## Proposed learning
Candidate: the cheapest-looking option here removed MFC's data-holding entirely and was still wrong,
because it moved a coercion risk onto the participant instead. **Cost to MFC and risk to the
participant are different axes and the cheap option was only cheap on one of them.**
