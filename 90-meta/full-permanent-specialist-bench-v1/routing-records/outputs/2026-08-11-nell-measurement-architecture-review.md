## Finding 1 — The day-90 wave creates a safeguarding exposure nobody has actually assessed

**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER.**

Trace the concrete sequence a real disclosure at T4 would follow, as the documents currently
specify it:

1. A participant completes Form 4 alone, at home or wherever, on day 90–104, with no facilitator
   present and no session context fresh in mind (`B5c` §2, row T4).
2. `H-01` is asked, unconditional, never routed away (`B5c` §3.4 row 8; `B5` §4 row `H-01`). Say
   the participant writes something real — not "the form was long," but a genuine disclosure:
   distress, an unsafe situation, or worse.
3. **Who receives it, and how fast?** `B5` §10 blocker #5 — "written free-text screening rule for
   `A-06`, `F-07`, `H-01` and `P-05`" — is listed as **"Not drafted."** There is currently no
   answer to who reads a `H-01` "Yes" at T4, on what cadence, or what they are authorised to do
   next. This is not a detail left for later; it is the entire mechanism the question exists to
   trigger, and it does not exist yet.
4. **Can MFC even reach the person back?** `B5c` §7 states plainly: the opt-in slip that would let
   MFC contact a participant directly "is specified as deleted at day 28 when Form 3 closes —
   which leaves no MFC-held channel for a day-90 invitation at all." Read together with point 3,
   the honest answer to "what happens when someone discloses at day 90" is: **the disclosure may
   arrive through a host-circulated generic link that MFC cannot trace back to a person, MFC has
   no defined process for who reads it or how quickly, and even if MFC wanted to follow up
   directly, it may hold no channel to do so.** The only thing definitely reaching that participant
   is whatever standing referral line is printed on the form itself (`B5` §3: Lifeline, 13 11 14) —
   a generic national service, not an MFC-initiated response.
5. `B5` §10 blocker #4 already names this precisely: **"Clinical governance on the distress
   pathway for a form completed alone, weeks later — `H-01` makes this more urgent, not less,
   because it invites disclosure."** Status: **Open.** `B5c` adds a *second*, later, alone-completed
   wave on top of a distress pathway that was already flagged as unresolved for the *first* alone
   wave (T3, day 21–28) — and does not re-surface blocker #4's urgency in its own text. It discusses
   the channel problem (§7) but not the fact that it has just made the exact gap blocker #4 names
   worse by tripling the elapsed time.

**Say plainly: this is not acceptable to field as currently specified, and it cannot be made
acceptable by this system alone.** A standing crisis-line reference on a form is a floor, not a
distress-response pathway — `D-0016` item 1 requires qualified clinical-governance input on
distress-response content and referral-pathway design specifically because a printed phone number
is not, on its own, an adequate answer to "someone just told us something is wrong and we have no
reliable way to reach them or know anyone read it." **I am not the clinical sign-off and do not
attempt to design the pathway here** — that is exactly `D-0016` item 1's territory, and item 4
("host responsibilities," informed by 1–4) bears on the channel question too. What I can say from a
programs/safeguarding governance lens: **the T4 wave should not be built, let alone fielded, until
blocker #4 and the free-text screening rule (blocker #5) are resolved with named ownership,
turnaround time, and an actual escalation path — and until the channel gap in `B5c` §7 is closed
so a genuine day-90 disclosure has somewhere real to go.** This is squarely inside `D-0016`'s hard
gate, already acknowledged as open by all three documents, and I am reinforcing rather than
introducing a new requirement.

## Finding 2 — `H-01`'s "today" wording is frozen without being checked against the timepoint it now serves

**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER** (fixable at zero cost, before the one-way door closes — see below).

`B5c` §4.5 explicitly checked whether `A-01`'s wording survives all four timepoints and found the
two-week window makes it "timepoint-agnostic... The matched wording survives the addition of a
fourth timepoint at zero cost." **No equivalent check appears anywhere for `H-01`.** `H-01`'s
literal wording (`B5` §4, §5) is *"Has anything about today been difficult or unhelpful for
you?"* — and `B5c` §3.4 places it at T4, day 90, marked **"frozen, unconditional"** in the exact
same table row as the safety property "never routed away."

At day 90, "today" refers to nothing MFC controls. A respondent completing this alone, three
months after a single session, reading "has anything about *today* been difficult" has to guess
what "today" means — the session day (long past), the act of filling in this form, or their actual
day generally, which has nothing to do with MFC. Three concrete failure modes follow from that
ambiguity:

- **A genuine MFC-relevant disclosure could be suppressed** because the respondent reasonably reads
  "today" as referring to the session and concludes the question doesn't apply three months later.
- **A genuine but MFC-unrelated disclosure could be captured** (something difficult about the
  respondent's actual day, unconnected to MFC) and then sit inside a screening pipeline that isn't
  built (Finding 1) with no framing to distinguish it from something MFC caused or should act on.
- **It reads as careless** to a respondent who is already three months removed and being asked to
  give MFC more of their time — a small but real trust cost on the one item that exists purely to
  protect them.

This is a real defect in a safeguarding-critical item, not a stylistic nit. It happened because the
Tier A freeze (`B5c` §4.1) locked `H-01`'s stem and format alongside `A-01`'s, without giving it the
same per-timepoint scrutiny `A-01` received. `B5c` §4.7 states the free-change window "closes the
moment the first participant completes Form 1" — meaning this is fixable **now, at zero cost**, and
becomes unfixable (only restartable) the moment fielding begins. **I am not proposing exact
replacement wording here** — that is Eimear Quigley's and Debra Fidler's call under `B5` blocker
#7, and a safeguarding item's precise phrasing is exactly the kind of content `D-0016` item 1
reserves to qualified clinical input, not to a committee-persona review. What I am flagging with
confidence: the current stem does not survive the timepoint expansion as specified, and it must be
tested and very likely revised (something timepoint-neutral, e.g. asking about "anything right now"
or "anything about the program or this process," rather than "today") before any participant sees a
T4 form.

## Finding 3 — Item-level read of `B5b`

**Overall: the drafting discipline is genuinely good** — the shared rules stated at the top (no
shaming of non-practice, no completion framing, balanced distractors, escape options) are followed
consistently, and the author flags their own ceiling-risk and bias concerns more thoroughly than I
would ordinarily expect at this stage. Specific items, in the order the task asked for them:

**`SJT-03` (avoidance/dread/procrastination) — GENUINE_FOUNDER_DECISION / flag for cognitive
testing, not a blocking defect.** The stem ("a familiar wave of dread and the urge to check your
phone instead," "a task you've been putting off for a few days") describes ordinary procrastination
in the framing intended, but the same phenomenology — dread, avoidance, phone-checking as escape —
is also how a person currently experiencing depression or burnout might describe their actual
functioning. The item's correct answer (C) and its distractors are all compassionate and none blame
the respondent, which is the right design choice and satisfies `K-08` rule 3 ("do not imply
struggle... [is a] personal failure"). But the document's own cognitive-testing plan doesn't ask the
one question I would add: **whether a respondent who is currently, genuinely struggling with
low mood finds this item's premise (that this is "a familiar wave") uncomfortably close to home in a
way that affects their willingness to continue the form.** This is not a reason to cut the item —
the construct is legitimate and well-handled — but it should be an explicit probe in blocker #6's
cognitive testing, alongside the wording checks already listed.

**`B-06` (the interrupting vignette) — NOTED_NOT_BLOCKING.** The 2026-08-11 resolution to an
unnamed third person, rather than a named actor or first-person "you," is the right call and for
the right reason (avoiding both demographic cueing in a vignette where the subject fails, and
self-serving attribution bias). None of the four options blame the subject in a way that would read
as shaming if a respondent saw themselves in it. This is a well-designed item.

**`A-05` (barrier list) — see Finding 4 below; the barrier framing itself is sound** (no "I didn't
try" language, all options describe something happening to or around the respondent, matching
`K-07`'s locked non-completion rule), but the free-text handling around it is where the real risk
sits.

**`P-03` ("workable") — NOTED_NOT_BLOCKING.** Correctly resists the "calm is the goal" conflation
`K-08` rule 9 exists to prevent, and none of the distractors pathologise discomfort. Good item.

**`DUR-01` — NOTED_NOT_BLOCKING, with one presentation caveat.** The five options are genuinely
neutral ("I stopped for a while, then came back to it," "I stopped and haven't come back to it," "I
never really got started with it") — none use failure language, and the item's own note that it is
"never scored, and no option is a failure" is accurate to how it's built, not just asserted.
**The caveat is about reporting, not construction:** "never scored" needs to survive into whatever
table or chart eventually shows this distribution to Hunter or the committee. A bar chart with
"stopped and haven't come back" sitting visually below "kept using it steadily" will read as a
ranking to anyone looking at it quickly, however carefully the underlying analysis avoids scoring
it. This is a presentation-design note for whoever builds the eventual report, not a defect in the
item.

## Finding 4 — Vulnerable-participant and accessibility gaps not addressed anywhere in these three documents

**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER**, specifically on the free-text screening scope point below;
**GENUINE_FOUNDER_DECISION / NOTED_NOT_BLOCKING** on the broader accessibility points, which are
real but are properly resolved through cognitive testing (blocker #6) rather than by me now.

1. **A real, concrete gap between what `B5b` claims and what `B5` actually specifies.** `B5b` states
   twice that `A-05`'s and `F-09`'s "Other (please say)" free-text options are "screened under the
   same rule as `A-06`/`F-07`/`H-01`/`P-05`." But `B5` §10 blocker #5's own stated scope is
   **"`A-06`, `F-07`, `H-01` and `P-05`"** — it does not name `A-05` or `F-09`. `A-05` is precisely
   the item with the option *"Someone else's reaction or behaviour got in the way"* — the exact
   free-text field a participant disclosing bullying or an unsafe manager would most plausibly use.
   If blocker #5's screening rule, once actually drafted, does not explicitly include `A-05` and
   `F-09` in its scope, a bullying or unsafe-workplace disclosure typed into "Other" on a barriers
   checklist could sit **completely unscreened** — not slow, not delayed, but never read by anyone
   with authority to act, because the governed process that would catch it was never built to cover
   that field. This needs to be corrected at the source: whoever finally drafts blocker #5 must be
   told explicitly that its scope is every free-text field in the instrument, not the four named in
   `B5`'s current list, and `B5b`'s assumption should be verified against the actual drafted rule
   rather than asserted.

2. **Neurodivergence is not addressed anywhere in the item bank.** The `SJT` vignettes and `B-06`
   describe internal signals in fairly specific interoceptive language ("chest tight," "a familiar
   wave of dread"). Some neurodivergent respondents experience or describe internal signals
   differently (reduced interoceptive awareness, alexithymia, different framing of overwhelm), and
   none of the fifteen items or their rationale sections mention this. `K-08` rule 12 requires
   "sensory considerations, inclusive examples" as part of accessible delivery — this instrument
   doesn't visibly meet that bar yet. I am not asking for a redesign; I am asking that blocker #6's
   cognitive-testing sample (5–8 participants) be deliberately checked for whether it includes
   neurodivergent, low-literacy, or EAL participants, and that the think-aloud probes explicitly ask
   whether the vignette language matches how they'd actually describe their own experience — not
   only whether the wording is comprehensible in the abstract.

3. **Low literacy / EAL.** `B5b`'s stated drafting rule ("plain Australian English at roughly Year 8
   reading level") is a reasonable target but is, by the document's own admission, untested — no
   psychometrics exist yet (`B5b`'s closing caveat). This is the correct honesty and doesn't need a
   separate fix beyond what blocker #6 already covers, but I'd want it named explicitly as a required
   probe, not folded silently into general cognitive testing.

4. **A currently-distressed respondent completing the form generally.** None of the fifteen items, and
   nothing in `B5c`, addresses what happens if a respondent arrives at the form already in an unsafe
   or acutely distressed state unrelated to a specific `H-01` disclosure — e.g., someone who
   struggles through `SJT-03` because it mirrors a real depressive episode, without ever ticking "Yes"
   on `H-01`. The standing referral line at the head of every form (`B5` §3) is the only safety net
   for this case, and it is passive — it requires the respondent to act on it unprompted. This is
   inherent to any self-administered instrument and I don't think it's fixable by rewording; I note it
   so the committee understands the referral line is carrying more weight than a single printed
   sentence usually can.

## Finding 5 — The `R9` §4 HREC recommendation, read as a safeguarding and participant-trust question, not a financial one

**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER**, on the specific point below; the underlying legal question is correctly
left to a lawyer and I am not attempting to answer it.

`R9` §4 recommends: don't pay for Bellberry review now; use internal-service-evaluation consent
language for the first cohort(s); **but do not destroy raw data on the stated retention schedule,**
so a future re-consent-and-publish path stays open if MFC later finds a willing academic partner.
`R9` is honest that this rests on an unanswered legal question (does extended retention itself
trigger different privacy obligations) and states plainly it is not resolving that gate.

Setting the legal question aside, and reading this purely from what a participant would reasonably
understand and expect: **I would not sign off on this as currently framed.** A participant who
completes these forms under an "internal service evaluation, data destroyed on schedule" consent
has been told, in effect, that their data — including a `H-01` disclosure, a `A-06` narrative about
a real moment in their life, or free text about a difficult workplace situation — will be used
internally and then destroyed on a stated timeline. Quietly retaining that data past the stated
schedule, on the possibility of a future request to reuse it for research they were never told about
at the time, is a departure from what was actually promised, even if done with good intentions and
even if the eventual re-consent ask is genuine and respectful when it happens. **The problem is the
gap between what the consent says now and what MFC is actually doing** — not the idea of holding
optionality open per se.

This is not, actually, purely a legal question, and it doesn't need to wait for the lawyer's answer
on Privacy Act obligations. **The fix available right now, independent of that legal read, is
disclosure:** if MFC wants to preserve the option of a future re-consent request, the consent
language itself should say so at the point of collection — something to the effect that data may be
held longer than the standard internal-evaluation period in case MFC later seeks permission to use
it for a published case study, and that the participant can decline that specific possibility while
still taking part. That is a design change available today, at the same document layer `B5` and
`B5c` already operate in, and it converts a quiet retention decision into an honest one. **I would
not approve the current framing going to a participant**, and I'd want this raised to Hunter as a
concrete choice: change the consent wording now to disclose the possibility, or destroy data on the
stated schedule as originally promised and accept that the future re-consent option closes. Holding
data longer than promised, unstated, is the one option I don't think should be on the table,
independent of what the lawyer eventually says about Privacy Act exposure.

## Finding 6 — Whether `D-0016` is actually respected, checked for quiet assumptions

**MUST_FIX_BEFORE_HUNTER**, one specific gap; otherwise the documents are honest about the gate.

All three documents state clearly and repeatedly that nothing is participant-facing, built, or sent
until `D-0016`'s six matters close, and `B5c` §7 and `R9` both name open items explicitly rather
than glossing them. That discipline is genuinely present and I want to say so plainly rather than
only listing problems.

**One place quietly assumes more than it should.** `B5c` §4.6 names exactly three things that would
legitimately force a change to a frozen Tier A item: (1) cognitive testing finds the scale doesn't
work, (2) the baseline ceilings, (3) a human research ethics committee requires a change as a
condition of approval. **`D-0016` item 1 — qualified clinical-governance review of distress-response
content — is not on that list, despite `H-01` and the referral line being Tier A, frozen items, and
despite item 1 being the actual, named, still-open authority over exactly that content.** As
written, the three-tier lock (§4.4: "a Tier A item may be changed only by a Hunter decision record
that states... the evidence base restarts from zero") reads as though `H-01`'s wording is a
committed, load-bearing measurement decision on the same footing as `A-01`'s — when in fact it is
safeguarding content that a qualified clinical reviewer, under `D-0016` item 1, has not yet seen or
approved at all. Freezing it now, before that review happens, risks the freeze itself becoming
pressure not to change it later ("it's locked, changing it restarts the evidence base") exactly at
the moment `D-0016`'s actual gate is supposed to have the final say on its content. This should be
corrected explicitly: **`D-0016` items 1 and 4 clearing (or requiring a change) should be added as a
fourth named trigger in §4.6, standing alongside HREC, so that clinical governance's authority over
`H-01` and the referral line is never read as subordinate to the measurement-durability lock that
governs everything else in Tier A.**

Everything else checked clean: `B5-forms-build-ready.md` §10 blocker #1 is stated as the controlling
gate in all three documents; `R9` correctly treats the HREC fork as outside its own authority and
routes it to Hunter under `CLAUDE.md` §6a condition 3/6; `B5c` §5's draft decision record explicitly
states `D-0016`'s six matters are "untouched and remain a hard gate."
