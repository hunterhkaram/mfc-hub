# A-17 — is the market research accurately represented in the strategy?

## Bounded question

Is every market claim in sections 1, 2 and Appendix A supported, at that scope and that strength, by
MFC's own census?

## Position

**Three blocking inaccuracies, two worth fixing, one minor. All applied.** Six prior passes read the
paper against itself and found it consistent. It was consistent, and wrong, because nothing had read
it against the research it summarises.

## Sources reviewed

- `60-projects/committee-pack-oct-2026/THE-CONSOLIDATED-STRATEGY.md`, sections 1, 2, Appendix A
- `60-projects/committee-pack-oct-2026/market-assessment-full.md`, coded 14 September 2026
- `60-projects/market-and-competitor-intelligence-v1/12-stop-shift-do-prior-art.md`
- `60-projects/market-and-competitor-intelligence-v1/00-index.md` and the checker-findings files

The check corrected the governing source along the way: the live census is the market assessment in
the committee-pack folder. Several files in the older intelligence project are marked superseded and
carry explicit warnings not to take counts from them.

## Professional principles applied

A claim is reported at the scope its evidence carries, not the scope that reads best. A desk census
supports statements about what it registered and read, and nothing wider. Where a paper and its
source disagree, the source governs.

## Evidence strength and limitations

Every blocking finding was re-verified line by line against the market assessment by the session
before any edit was made. The census is a desk study across two months and cannot support claims
about programmes it did not register; nineteen of the 34 organisations were not read in comparator
depth.

## Alternatives considered

Leaving the audience figures as published, on the grounds that they appear in the source. Rejected:
they appear as the verdict on one test clause, not as the audience composition, and the paper
presented them as the latter. Narrowing the differentiation claim to avoid the treatment-service
problem was also considered and rejected, because acknowledging the inclusion is a stronger argument
than avoiding it.

## Recommendation

**Blocking 1 — the audience figures were the wrong metric.** The paper's *"23 scoped"* and *"10 of
the 58 open to any adult"* are the pass/fail verdict on one clause (*"On a universal audience, 10 of
58 meet it, 23 fail it, two partly fail, and 23 are unestablished"*). The audience composition is
**42 scoped, six open, ten unestablished**. Corrected; the true figures are the stronger
differentiator.

**Blocking 2 — the census scope statement was false.** *"Clinical treatment was outside the scope of
the census, whose 58 are universal and workplace programmes"* — added the same morning. THIS WAY UP
is in the register and the source calls it *"a treatment service: seventeen condition-specific
programmes."* Corrected to state that the census counts one treatment service, and even it does not
publish enactment.

**Blocking 3 — "six claims tested, two held" fused two analyses.** The six-clause instrument produced
one hold, audience. The measurement finding was tested separately against the register's evidence
field. Restated, with the four failing items now accounting for five clauses so the arithmetic
closes.

**Worth fixing, applied.** The *"nobody assembling that"* sentence carries the source's fifteen-of-34
caveat. Appendix A names both live limits rather than leaving a committee member to find them.

**Supported and unchanged:** 58 across 34 organisations and the August-September window; 12 of 58
teaching a named in-the-moment technique; the enactment claim at its stated scope; the prior-art
position and *"instructions on a page"*, near-verbatim from the source.

## Strongest objection

That the corrected audience figures change a headline number in a paper Hunter has approved. They
do — in MFC's favour, and the alternative was publishing a figure taken from the wrong column of
MFC's own research.

## What would change this view

A later recode of the register's audience field, or evidence that THIS WAY UP was included in error
and the count should be 57.

## Adjacent specialist or human boundary

Whether the reworded differentiation still sells is A-08's call and has not been run. Eimear is the
person most likely to test THIS WAY UP's inclusion, and is the reason it now appears in Appendix A.

## Proposed learning

Internal consistency is not accuracy. A figure can agree with itself in four places and still
misdescribe the research. Bound into `mfc-board-paper` section 5a in the same task.
