## Professional principles applied

The Chair's structure governs. Where source material and a cell disagree, the cell wins and the
detail goes to an appendix. A number is carried as its source states it and never recomputed. A
finding must quote the exact offending text; an unverifiable suspicion is not a finding.

## Evidence strength and limitations

Strong on cell compliance and page counts, which are directly checkable against the rendered PDF.
Weaker on tone, which no mechanical check reaches. Page counts shift with any wording change, so
the eleven-page result holds only for this build. Seven items previously fixed were re-verified
present in the rendered text rather than assumed.

## Alternatives considered

Cutting body content to reach eleven pages was considered and rejected — every cell's content is
content Onno asked for, so cutting it would trade one breach for another. Moving the glossary to an
appendix reaches the same number without losing anything, and his spec allocates the glossary no
body pages at all. On §12, keeping both tables was reconsidered and rejected: the cell offers half a
page in the body or a one-page appendix, not both.

## Recommendation

All four defects fixed in this task. Body now exactly eleven pages, pack thirteen. The paper is
ready for Hunter's own word-for-word read, which is the gate, and then for Jake on this version.

## Strongest objection

That moving the glossary to the back weakens Gina's requirement (`AG-294`, HARD) that a committee
member can define every term. The answer: the glossary is unchanged and complete, the front matter
points to it explicitly at Appendix B, and terms are still glossed at first use in the body.

## What would change this view

Onno saying eleven pages was indicative rather than a ceiling, which would remove the reason to
move the glossary at all. Or Gina saying a back-of-pack glossary does not satisfy her.
