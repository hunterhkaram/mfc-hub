# A-24 adversarial review — the question set after two revision passes

## Bounded question
Are the declared verbatim pairs genuinely byte-identical; does any form break a rule in §0; does §4
claim findings the forms cannot support; are any participant-facing claims outside `K-08`; and would
a first-time reader understand each question without guessing?

## Position
**Twenty-seven findings, seven of them build-blocking.** The document's internal consistency checks
could not see the worst of them, because §3 scopes the verbatim check to stems, options, option
order, punctuation and escapes — and the two most serious failures were in *context* and in
*attribution*, neither of which that check covers.

## Professional principles applied
Verify by hashing and grep, never by the document's assertions about itself. A confirmation obtained
on a misstated premise is not a confirmation. A recorded uncertainty converted into confidence is a
claim MFC's own records contradict.

## Evidence strength and limitations
**All seven declared verbatim pairs were hashed**: item 2a across six administrations, 2b across
four, plus the takeaway, thinking-ahead, recall and taught-before pairs. **Every one is genuinely
byte-identical in its item body**, punctuation and em-dashes included. WHO-5 was the only divergence,
and only in an MFC heading line. **Zero cohorts have been delivered**, so no empirical check on any
item was possible, and no participant has seen any of these forms.

## Findings, build-blocking first
1. **The baseline pair was not comparable.** Form 1-PRE defined "everyday moments" only above item 3;
   Form 2 defined it above item 1. The pair's stems were byte-identical; the comprehension context
   was not. **A definition present on one side and absent on the other breaks a pair more thoroughly
   than a diverged word, and §3's check cannot see it.**
2. **`AG-004`'s narrowing was described to Eimear inaccurately**, and she is being asked to confirm
   it in writing. The deleted footer named **your GP** as well as Lifeline and 000; the replacement
   panel signposts nothing at all. **A qualified-human confirmation on a misstated premise.**
3. **`AG-193` was attributed to both advisors.** The register records it as `UNCERTAIN — Debra
   Fidler *or* Eimear Quigley`, and it is the sole cited basis for four rows in §4.
4. **The consent panel told participants on four of six forms that a wellbeing questionnaire is
   included when it is not.** Same class as the false mental-health statement removed in pass one —
   it was the sentence that replaced it.
5. **WHO-5 is a screen with a published poor-wellbeing cut-off**, administered while telling
   participants MFC is not assessing their mental health, with every signpost removed and no stated
   action on a low score. **The `K-08` exposure in the set**, sitting under Debra's `AG-014`.
6. **The escape rule coded the opportunity answer on the primary change score as missing** — while
   fifty lines later the document made the opposite argument for the analogous option on Form 4
   item 3. §4's flat-result sentence depends on separating *no opportunity* from *didn't act*.
7. **The consent panel contradicted §2.4 on how withdrawal works** and omitted the end date §2.4
   says it must carry.

**Material, not blocking:** the §5 open-positions list was partial (`AG-014`, half of `AG-545`);
"four of them" was two; `AG-554`'s *"validity holds"* was unsourced beyond subscale alpha; the
IMTTAQ licence is also open while only WHO-5 has a gate; `AG-555` asks the rights-holder a question
that is not the binding one, since the licence is **NC** and the client is charged; MFC headings sat
inside blocks marked *reproduced without modification*; §0.4's count was wrong; Form 5's "eight
items" is eleven; no administration of 2b was named as the change score; **a fabricated
ninety-per-cent figure appeared in a document whose first rule forbids stating results**; the
confidentiality promise exceeded what a cohort of three to ten supports, with no minimum-n
suppression anywhere; two framing blocks contradicted each other at the top of Form 1; an orphaned
helper line survived the `AG-564` edit; Form 4 item 3's bare *"No"* was ambiguous among four other
nos; Form 3's recall-first rule was not applied to Form 5 and the two recall instructions differed
in the direction that biases toward apparent decay; Form 3 item 5 used a four-point ladder where
§0.1 defines five — **`AG-550` in miniature**; Form 4 item 7 had no route for a participant who did
do a rep.

## Alternatives considered
Reporting only the seven blockers and leaving the rest — **rejected.** Several of the "material"
findings are the ones an advisor will see first, and `AG-550` is answered by exactly those.

## Recommendation
Fix all of it in the document. **None of it needs a new question.**

## Strongest objection
That this is a fourth drafting pass on a document no participant has read, and thirty minutes with
five people outranks all of it. **That objection is correct and is already written into §5.**

## What would change this view
A participant comprehension test contradicting any of it — §5 already says that test wins. An advisor
ruling that the WHO-5 collection is acceptable as designed.

## What is simply correct, and should not be softened
§4's reporting discipline — bias direction stated every time a number appears, the four numbers
never collapsed, the refusal to report "X% found it beneficial", the pre-written flat-result
sentence. The IMTTAQ items are exact including the `on the training course` preposition. WHO-5 is
verbatim and no clinical cut-off appears anywhere, correctly.

## Adjacent specialist or human boundary
The WHO-5 cut-off exposure is Eimear's and Debra's to rule on. Both licence questions are for the
rights-holders. **Nothing here is ethics approval or clinical assurance.**

## Proposed learning
Candidate: a verbatim-pair check scoped to the item body cannot see a divergence in the context that
precedes it. **The pair is what the participant reads, not what the document quotes.**
