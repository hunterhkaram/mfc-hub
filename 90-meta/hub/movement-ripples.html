Movement & ripples

Track how MFC may contribute to mental fitness becoming ordinary, practical and preventative without claiming a movement exists before people create one.

Current evidence boundary. MFC has a plausible social-change ambition and ripple theory. It does not yet have evidence that it has created a movement or changed population, workplace or community norms.

The social change we are seeking

  • Mental fitness becomes ordinary language and practice before crisis.
  • People have practical ways to notice difficult everyday moments and choose what they do next.
  • Workplaces and communities support agency without placing all responsibility on individuals.
  • Paid organisational delivery helps widen access for people who cannot pay.

How we currently think a ripple could happen

A useful ripple may begin when someone applies a mental rep in an ordinary moment, carries the language into another relationship or setting, and is supported by conditions that make repeated practice possible. This is a theory of change, not a proven causal chain.

Possible ripple pathways

Personal practice

active hypothesis

Participants recognise and apply a mental rep outside the room.

Shared language

active hypothesis

People use or share MFC language with another person or team.

Workplace and community norms

early hypothesis

Groups make proactive mental-fitness practice more ordinary and supported.

Access ripple

strategic intent

Earned revenue demonstrably enables free or reduced-cost access.

Partners and facilitators

not tested

Other people can carry the approach faithfully without depending on Hunter.

What we have genuinely observed

  • No real participant, workplace or community ripple evidence is recorded yet.
  • Internal strategy and workshop design create plausible pathways, but implementation and social-change effects remain untested.

Opportunities worth exploring

  • Test whether participants use or share the language after the workshop without prompting them to perform advocacy.
  • Explore a small community-talk format only after its purpose, audience and safeguarding boundaries are clear.
  • Track whether corporate revenue genuinely widens access rather than treating the cross-subsidy model as achieved.

What we must not overclaim

  • Do not call reach, attendance, likes or testimonials social change.
  • Do not turn individual stories into population-impact claims.
  • Do not imply people can overcome unsafe, relational or structural conditions through mindset alone.
  • Do not claim MFC has created a movement before people independently participate, adapt and carry it.

What should we test next?

Add low-burden ripple questions to the learning plan only after measurement, consent and privacy reviewers confirm the wording and purpose; begin with qualitative evidence rather than a movement score.
