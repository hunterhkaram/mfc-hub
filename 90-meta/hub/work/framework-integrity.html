The three rep types (added 2026-09-16, D-0136, provisional, on Hunter product approval)

Authority and status. D-0136, decided by Hunter 2026-09-16, named this as a consequence. Adopted under delegated product authority (K-11); committee ratification owed, as for this whole record. Verbatim source: Hunter's 2026-08-31 capture, where the three types were already articulated and never given a home.

The locked rep definition is unchanged. A moment may be caught "before it happens, while it is happening, or afterwards", and "the outcome does not determine whether it counts." This names three forms inside that definition rather than narrowing it.

Type What it is Counts?
Live The moment is caught while it is happening, and the next move is chosen in it Yes. The full form
Rehearsal The routine is run against a moment that is expected but not yet here Yes
Reflection The moment has passed and is caught afterwards, with the next move named Yes

Why only the first is called the full form, and why the other two still count. Naming a hierarchy without excluding anything is the point. Live is what the practice is for. Rehearsal and reflection are how someone gets there, and a person who only ever manages reflection is practising, not failing.

What this replaces. MFC had begun describing the practice as drilled, which imports a performance standard the permissive definition deliberately refuses, and which MFC cannot observe or measure. D-0136 retired that language. The comparative argument now rests on the practice being cued by a real moment in the person's own life, which is true of all three types and does not need the word.

Claims boundary. K-08 unchanged. None of this claims an effect; it names forms of an attempt.

Why catching comes first — attention, and what it produces (added 2026-09-16, provisional, on Hunter product approval)

Authority and status. Hunter directed this addition, 2026-09-16, in a single unbroken voice capture held verbatim at 15-inputs/hunter-notes/category-thesis/2026-09-16-founder-thesis-attention-practice-and-environment.md. Adopted under delegated product authority (K-11); committee ratification remains separately owed, as for this entire record. No D- record reserved this turn.

Why it was added. The record already explained why practice matters, through Roads, Pathways and Autopilot. It did not explain why Stop comes first. That argument existed only in Hunter's head and in the market work, nowhere in foundations.

The mechanism, in his words:

"In every single moment of our life, we have attention. Our attention is on something. And depending on what our attention's on, whether that is a thought, whether it's based on a feeling, that then leads to a behaviour. We cannot change that behaviour... if we don't catch it. If we cannot catch and realise what's happening, we won't change what's happening."

Stated for the record. Attention is always somewhere. Where it rests, on a thought or on a feeling, shapes what a person does next. A behaviour that was never noticed cannot be chosen differently. Catching is therefore not the gentle first step of a three-step routine; it is the precondition for the other two existing at all. Shift has nothing to act on and Do has nothing to redirect if the moment went by unnoticed.

"We could be in the moment and the moment just passes us by because we're in it. Sometimes you need to go, whoa, what's happening here."

What this establishes that the audience claim could not. Universality has been argued from who is eligible: open to any adult, no population membership, no requirement to identify as struggling. This is the stronger form of the same claim, because it gives a reason rather than a permission. Attention is in every moment of every life. There is no population that does not have moments, which is why the entry point is not a condition, a score or a group.

Claims boundary. This is a stated rationale for why the framework is ordered as it is, drawn from Hunter's own reasoning and consistent with attention and self-monitoring literature already in the evidence library. It is not a claim that MFC's product has been shown to produce this effect, and K-08 governs unchanged.

Why practice matters — the gap between knowing and doing (added 2026-08-05, provisional, on Hunter product approval)

Authority and status. Hunter directed this addition, 2026-08-05, D-0062, under delegated product authority (K-11); committee ratification remains separately owed, as for this entire record. This closes part of LI-10's open causal question by naming the mechanism the framework is designed around, explicitly as a stated rationale drawn from adjacent, established science — not as a claim that MFC's own product has been proven to produce this effect.

The gap, stated plainly: understanding a skill (Learn It) changes what you know. It does not by itself change what you default to under pressure. The Human Operating System's Roads / Pathways part — the routes that get easier the more you take them — names why: your existing habits and reactions are roads worn in by years of use, and Autopilot is what runs on them. A skill you learned ten minutes, or ten weeks, ago has no pathway yet. When State Check reads "too much" or "too little," access narrows toward whatever pathway is most familiar — and without practice, that is still the old default, however well you understood the new skill intellectually. This is the gap between knowing something and being able to do it when it counts.

This is exactly why Build It exists as its own stage, distinct from Learn It. A Mental Rep — one intentional pass through Stop, Shift, Do, in a real moment — is literally the act that builds a new pathway. Repetition in real moments, not further explanation, is what closes the gap.

Evidence framing, kept honest: the mechanism above (repeated, cued practice building automatic access) is well-established in adjacent literatures — habit formation, automaticity, and implementation-intentions research (see the framework-integrity-observatory's 05-...md domain 5, and R-07, which already identifies Radar as MFC's applied form of this exact literature). This is the rationale for why the framework is built the way it is. It is not a claim that MFC's specific programme has been shown to produce this effect — that remains the open causal question LI-10 already names, and nothing here closes it.

Practise low-friction moments before high-friction ones. The Radar's own guidance (choosing a few moments on purpose) carries a design principle worth stating directly: build capacity in lower-friction moments — the ones with more room to try, get it wrong, and try again — before expecting yourself to handle the genuinely difficult ones. A person who has never practised catching, shifting, and choosing in an easy moment has no real basis to expect themselves to manage that same sequence the first time it matters most. Nothing prevents someone from also trying higher-friction moments — but the expectation should be set accordingly.

The second mechanism: load. (added 2026-09-23, Hunter direct, D-0142) Roads / Pathways explains why a new skill is not there yet. It does not explain why a skill you have genuinely built can still be out of reach in a particular moment. Hunter named the missing half directly, 2026-09-23: "when we are dysregulated, when things happen... we might know something, but we can't actually do it."

These are two different reasons for the same gap, and the framework now names both:

Roads / Pathways Load
What it explains Why a newly learned skill has no route yet Why an existing route is harder to take right now
Time course Built over repetitions, changes slowly Changes within a single moment
What closes it Practice — Mental Reps in real moments A Shift — state or thinking — enough to become workable
Where it shows up in the model Roads / Pathways, with Autopilot running on them Felt in Engine and Driver, read by State Check; often sourced in Environment

Why this matters for the framework's order. If the only mechanism were pathways, the answer to every failed moment would be "practise more." Load says something different: the route may be there and still be hard to reach, and the move then is not more practice but Shift — restoring enough access to act. Shift stays the parent verb D-0076 locked: state or thinking, not state alone. This is the reason Shift sits between Stop and Do rather than being folded into either. It is also why State Check reads how much, not what: the thing that narrows access is the amount of load, not the label on it.

Evidence framing, kept honest, and it is thinner than the pathways half. Adjacent evidence supports load narrowing capacity in the moment: acute stress impairs working memory and cognitive flexibility, and the working-memory effect concentrates in high-load conditions (Shields 2016, 51 studies, 2,486 participants); and in everyday life people used reappraisal, reflection and acceptance less as stressors got more intense, while those same strategies were working better in the harder moments (Blanke 2021, N = 156). That second finding is the closest thing MFC holds to the gap Hunter is describing. Three limits stated plainly. One: not everything degrades — the same meta-analysis found stress improved response inhibition (contested by Dang 2017 and defended in reply; hold the contest), so MFC must never say "stress impairs executive function" full stop. Two: MFC does not explain this by willpower depletion or decision fatigue; that effect has failed two large preregistered multi-lab tests (Vohs 2021, N = 3,531, d = 0.06; Hagger 2016, d = 0.04), and K-08 prohibits citing it. Three: as with Roads / Pathways, this is the rationale for why the framework is shaped as it is. It is not a claim that MFC's programme has been shown to restore access in a moment. LI-10 stays open.

One word, two scopes — read this before using the term. "Load" here names the mechanism. It is not the single item of the same name in the State Check's "too much" description, which lists activation, intensity, urgency, tension, overwhelm, reactivity or load. The State Check reads the magnitude of all of it.

What this amendment does not do. It does not replace Roads / Pathways, reorder the Human Operating System, or change any locked wording. Load is named as a second mechanism for one specific thing — the knowing-doing gap — and nowhere else. Full reasoning: D-0142.

Existing practices — meditation, mindfulness, gratitude, and where they fit (added 2026-08-05, provisional, on Hunter product approval)

Established practices (meditation, mindfulness, gratitude journalling, breathwork, and similar) are genuine, independently evidenced methods that can build components of the seven deeper skills — mindfulness practice building Self-Awareness and Attention, gratitude practice building Relating to Self, breathwork building State Regulation, and so on. K-07 does not dispute or diminish that value; they sit within "what helps around the pathway" (Shift tools, exercises, anchors) below.

The distinction that matters: building a skill in general is not the same thing as being able to access that skill in a specific real-life moment that matters to you. General practice (a daily meditation session) strengthens the underlying capacity; it does not, by itself, guarantee that capacity shows up in an unrelated, unplanned moment later that day — a heated conversation, a missed deadline, a hard decision. That second gap — general capacity to moment-specific access — is what the Radar and Mental Rep are specifically designed to close: practising the actual moments a person has chosen to work on, not only building capacity in the abstract. Existing practices and MFC's approach are complementary, not competing — one builds the skill, the other practises using it where it is actually needed.

Clarifying line, added 2026-09-03 — Hunter direct ("yes to both, adopt the line"), delegated product authority under K-11; committee ratification remains owed as for this entire record. Seven parts, in two tiers. Teaching metaphors, not a literal brain map, diagnostic model or clinical formulation.

This is a map, not a label. You are not broken. You are human. Humans are complex. Understanding the basics should not be.

Primary operating parts (in the moment) — "the main three"

Part Job Recall question
Passenger The thoughts, feelings and urges that show up. "The Passenger is not bad." What is showing up?
Engine Your body state and energy. What state am I in?
Driver Your capacity to pause, steer attention and choose. Who is at the wheel right now? / What choice is available?

Wider shaping parts (around the moment)

Part Job Recall question
Autopilot The familiar pattern that is already running. What familiar pattern is running?
Roads / Pathways The routes that get easier the more you take them. What route am I practising?
Navigation What matters and where you want to head. What matters here?
Environment The people, places, cues, pressures and supports around you. What around me is shaping this?

Diagnostic question: "What is shaping access in this moment?"

The recall frame, from the model: start with the three basics, then check the wider system. The two-tier structure is not presentational — it is what makes seven parts learnable: three to hold inside a moment, four to check around it. Do not flatten it.

Autopilot and Roads / Pathways are distinct. Roads are the routes that got easier because you took them — the terrain. Autopilot is the pattern executing right now, before anything was decided. One is the road; the other is what currently has the wheel.

The Basics attach to Engine (see above): sleep, movement, food, rest in public.

Every public description is governed by the four-check rule proven necessary by today's language review, and any future change to public wording must pass all four:

  1. Label-deletion test. Remove the skill name. Does the description still read as a self-sufficient statement of the skill? Fragments fail.
  2. Verb test. Does it open with a verb governing an object? Noun and prepositional-phrase fragments fail.
  3. Clause-coverage test. Every clause of the underlying deep definition maps to a clause of the public description, and nothing appears that isn't in the definition. Loss and addition both fail.
  4. Category test. Every noun names the class, not one instance — "rigid thoughts," not "a rigid thought."

Supporting others — the same process, used with another person (added 2026-08-05, provisional, on Hunter product approval, D-0064)

Stop, Shift, Do

Stop — catch the moment. Shift — create enough space where useful and available. Do — make the next move with intention.

  • Stop commonly draws on Notice and Focus — catching the moment requires both noticing it's happening and having attention available to catch it with.
  • Shift is the parent verb — the moment you change something to become workable. You can shift your state, or you can shift your thinking. At the deep tier it commonly draws on State Regulation (body: energy, arousal, tension) and Flexible Thinking (cognitive), and the Shift toolkit's four categories reach further — its attention and connection categories also draw on Attention and Relating to Others. The toolkit's four categories are the internal taxonomy; the public menu names (Move it / Zoom / Ask a different question / Bring someone in) are what learners meet. Facilitators should use the menu names aloud, not the bare category words, since "attention" is also deep-tier vocabulary (D-0084).
  • Do commonly draws, at the deep tier, on Values-Based Action, on Relating to Others where another person is involved, and on Relating to Yourself where the next move concerns you rather than someone else.
  • "These are not fixed mappings or separate skill steps." At the public tier, Do maps to Choose; at the deep tier it draws on three (Values-Based Action, Relating to Others, Relating to Yourself) — not a defect, since acting on what matters is genuinely supported by more than one deep capability, disclosed the same way Shift's and Connect's public folds now are.
  • Choose vs Do: "Do is the moment-level step in Stop, Shift, Do. Choose is the public skill underneath that step (Values-Based Action, at the deep tier)." Restored D-0081, rekeyed D-0084.
  • Misunderstandings named in source: Stop is not freezing; Shift is not always calming down; Do is not the perfect action; SSD is not another set of skills or the whole framework.

State Check

"Is my state too much, too little, or workable for this moment?"

Language Meaning Guides Shift
Too much More activation, intensity, urgency, tension, overwhelm, reactivity or load than the moment can use well. Downshift toward workable.
Workable Enough access to choice for this moment. Does not mean perfect calm. May need no state shift.
Too little Less energy, clarity, connection, alertness or mobilisation than the moment needs. Upshift toward workable.

Lock-in: does not change Stop, Shift, Do; not a fourth step. "For this moment" is the anchor phrase.

It asks how much, not what. 03-DELIVERY-SCRIPT-INTRODUCTION-60.md, spoken:

That is a magnitude on one axis, not a category lookup. The response is invariant. Whatever the reading, the move is toward workable. The direction follows from where the person already is; it is not selected from a set. A thermostat is not a menu.

It is offered, never required. The Introduction offers it as "a version of that you can ask". 29-DELIVERY-SCRIPT-ESSENTIALS-90.md carries it as a locked workplace boundary:

never mandatory, scored, public or performance-based, never a poll, never in the chat.

Workable is defined by capacity, not by mood. "Workable is not good. Workable is: I could do the next thing from here."

And Essentials teaches what the readings feel like, because "too much and too little are not obvious from the inside": too much is fast rather than dramatic, talking over the ends of sentences, re-reading a line four times; too little is flat, "not upset, just gone", and people miss it because it feels like being tired.

What the framework currently is

MFC's mental fitness framework has three layers: a category claim (mental fitness as a developable capacity to navigate everyday moments, distinct from but complementary to mental health), an explanatory architecture (a teaching metaphor — the Human Operating System — plus five public skills simplifying seven deeper constructs, plus a simple in-the-moment method, Stop-Shift-Do), and a practice mechanism (State Check, Mental Reps, Radar). See [[90-meta/framework-integrity-observatory/02-canonical-framework-map|02-canonical-framework-map.md]] for the full picture and diagram.

The single most important fact in this whole review: none of it is committee-approved yet. Every kernel record is status: provisional. This review does not change that — it maps what exists and rates how well-supported each part is.

How the main parts connect

Category → explanatory architecture → practice mechanism, roughly top-down, with the five public skills simplifying the seven deeper ones and Stop-Shift-Do drawing loosely (not rigidly) on both. Full diagram: [[90-meta/framework-integrity-observatory/02-canonical-framework-map|02-canonical-framework-map.md]].

What is strongly supported

  • Radar (the mechanism of pre-selecting a few recurring moments to notice/practise) — this is MFC's best-evidenced component, resting on decades of well-replicated implementation-intentions research (d=.27–.66).
  • The individual seven deeper constructs each draw on genuinely evidenced adjacent psychological literatures (self-regulation, attention, cognitive flexibility, values-based action, self-compassion, relational skill) — though none has been tested as MFC's own integrated system.

What is moderately or indirectly supported

  • General trainability of psychological skills in non-clinical adults (moderate support for the components, limited support specifically for MFC's brief one-off delivery format).
  • Mental Reps as a practice unit (adjacent literatures — single-session interventions, deliberate practice — are consistent with the idea; the specific "one outcome-independent rep" mechanism itself is untested).

What is an MFC construct (not itself a scientific finding)

The public definition, the short distinction, the five-skill labels, the Stop-Shift-Do method, State Check, and the Human Operating System are all MFC's own synthesis and communication choices — legitimate product design, not claims requiring external validation in themselves, provided they are never presented as independently validated.

What remains provisional / genuinely open

Whether "mental fitness" is a genuinely distinct scientific category, or a well-branded synthesis of existing constructs — no construct-validation literature was found either way, confirmed by a live proof-run search on 30 July 2026 finding no new hits. This is this review's single most consequential open question (see 10-...md, Decision D-1).

Strongest criticisms (and MFC's current responses)

  1. The Human Operating System risks reading as pseudo-neuroscience, even with its disclaimer — external evidence shows disclaimers reduce but don't eliminate lay literalisation of brain metaphors. MFC's current disclaimer is necessary but not, on its own, fully sufficient.
  2. "Mental fitness" as a framework risks individualising problems that are actually social/structural — a mature, credible critique lineage exists. MFC's "Environment" component addresses this in principle; whether it's delivered with equal weight in practice is unverified.
  3. No causal wellbeing claim can currently be supported — MFC's own measurement (uncontrolled pre/post) cannot establish this, and MFC's own claim boundaries already correctly prohibit making such a claim. This finding confirms an existing correct boundary; it does not discover a live violation.
