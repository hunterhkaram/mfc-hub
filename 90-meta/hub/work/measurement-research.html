01 Measurement System V3.1 Advisor Asks Incorporated

Measurement System v3.1 — the advisor asks incorporated

Why the advisors want these three things

Approving instruments whose purpose you can't see is how a form becomes thirty items nobody can defend. So, plainly.

MFC's own questions have one blind spot: they only ever ask about MFC. C1–C6 ask whether a moment came up, whether you noticed it, whether you acted, what got in the way. They are all views of the same process from different angles. That is AG-193's point (HARD): MFC's bespoke questions have unknown construct validity — "We don't know whether it's measuring what it's supposed to measure" — and cannot stand alone.

The three asks each do a different job.

IMTTAQ — six items — "did their environment even let them?"

MFC's model has three parts (KN-0006): the teaching, the practice, and the environment that makes practice possible. MFC measures the first two and assumes the third.

IMTTAQ's two subscales measure reported transfer and Opportunities to Integrate — whether the workplace gave them a chance. That matters commercially as much as scientifically: if enactment is low and opportunity is low, that is the organisation's finding, not MFC's failure. Without it, every low number reads as "the workshop didn't work."

It answers Eimear's own critique (AG-184, her observation, not established fact): her read of the meta-analytic picture is that workplace wellbeing programmes don't move outcomes and that culture and workload do. The environment layer is MFC's response to that — and it is currently the one layer with no measure attached.

WHO-5 — five items — "does any of this connect to anything outside itself?"

AG-171, Eimear: "how do you know if any of the changes are associated with anything?" And AG-172: a validated instrument lets a statistics package cross-compare against enactment; MFC's own questions cannot.

With WHO-5 in, you can ask whether people reporting more Mental Reps also report better wellbeing. Association, not causation — it proves nothing about what MFC caused, and the report must never say otherwise.

Two honest limits, stated here rather than discovered later by an analyst.

  1. Common-method variance. Both measures are self-report, same person, same sitting, minutes apart. A positive correlation is therefore not clean evidence that the MFC items are valid — shared method alone can produce it. WHO-5 makes the dataset less self-referential; it does not establish construct validity on its own. Anyone presenting it as the fix for AG-193 is overselling it.
  2. A null is likely, and must be pre-registered as such. R3's warning about TYDQ applies to WHO-5 with equal force: "Adopting a measure your intervention has no mechanism to move is how you manufacture your own disconfirming evidence." A half-day workshop should not be expected to move a 14-day wellbeing screener in three weeks. Required protocol clause, written before fielding: WHO-5 is a context measure. It is not expected to move within three weeks, and a null result is not evidence the service failed. Writing that down beforehand is what makes a subsequent null honest rather than embarrassing.

And WHO-5 does not make this a clinical instrument. D-0100 imposed a "clinical-response pathway" gate on it; no advisor asked for that, and Eimear specified the opposite control on 31 August (AG-004, HARD): separate MFC from clinical risk by disclaimer, not by clinical apparatus. Closed by D-0119. What WHO-5 needs is a plain non-clinical disclaimer and a scoring note, served to everyone.

Kirkpatrick — zero items — "what shape is this in?"

Onno's ask (AG-206). It adds no questions. It is a filing structure for measures MFC already has, in a vocabulary buyers and committees recognise. Mapping in §4.

The unifying question

AG-182, Eimear, tonight — the ask that ties all three together: "I'd be super curious as to whether it maps onto other things" — do people scoring higher at the learning level implement more moments, and show a different WHO-5 at follow-up? That single analysis is the reason to have all three rather than any one. It is also the analysis that needs an analyst and a sample size, neither of which MFC currently has (§3, §7).

Kn 0006 Intervention And Measurement Current Working Direction 2026 08 24

KN-0006 — Current working direction

Read this first

This is the latest working direction as at 24 August 2026. It is the source Claude should use to understand the current logic before proposing forms, workshop measurement or researcher outreach. It does not replace [[30-decisions/D-0100-first-pilot-measurement-core]], does not make a form field-ready, and does not authorise a wellbeing, clinical, ethics or causal claim.

The central idea is simple: MFC is no longer treating the offer as one workshop that somehow causes change. It is a three-part intervention model with separate roles, and the measures must keep those roles separate.

The three parts of the intervention

Part Job in the model What it is not What we need to learn
1. Stop–Shift–Do teaching Give a person a simple, recallable process they can retrieve in an everyday moment. Proof of competence, behaviour change or wellbeing change merely because it was explained. Can they understand it, recall it without a prompt, and apply it once to a relevant moment?
2. Radar and Mental Reps practice design Help a person connect the process to personally important, recognisable moments, choose a next move and practise the loop. A habit tracker, a guarantee of repetition, or a requirement to habit-stack. Did a real moment arise, did they notice it, recall the process and attempt a Mental Rep? What made that easier or harder?
3. Environment and implementation conditions Give the team or organisation conditions that make use more possible: manager support, shared language, time, permission, resources and normal work rhythms. A claim that MFC controls the workplace environment, removes psychosocial risk, or makes individual practice public. Did the setting create opportunities and support for using what was taught?

These are connected, but not interchangeable. Someone can remember Stop–Shift–Do without using it. Someone can want to practise but have little opportunity in their environment. An organisation can create better conditions without proving that every person used the process. Keeping the parts apart is what lets MFC learn instead of telling itself one vague success story.

The minimum practice package

The current product hypothesis is that Stop–Shift–Do should not be left as conceptual information. The minimum package to test is:

  1. teach the three-step process;
  2. complete one guided Mental Rep during the session;
  3. connect it to one personally relevant, recognisable moment; and
  4. offer one optional recall or visibility support for the whole Radar.

The support is optional. A routine anchor can help some people, but it must not become a mandatory habit-stacking rule, a streak or a test of personal commitment. Structural, relational, safety and capacity constraints remain real explanations for non-use.

Measurement: one layered design, not one giant form

MFC's own questions: the mechanism layer

MFC questions are needed because no existing instrument asks the exact MFC questions. The core is about participant-reported understanding, unaided Stop–Shift–Do recall, recognisable moments, opportunity, noticing, attempted use, repetition, conditional helpfulness, barriers and supports. Those questions should be explicitly labelled MFC-designed. They are not a validated scale.

They are also the way to distinguish the three intervention parts:

Timing / question family What it tells us
Baseline and immediate post Understanding, unaided recall and one guided application of Stop–Shift–Do.
Day-21 / week-3 follow-up for offers with a return Whether a real moment arose; whether the person noticed it, recalled Stop–Shift–Do and attempted a Mental Rep; barriers and supports.
Three-month follow-up for selected case-study or research cohorts A later descriptive read on reported use and conditions. It is not proof of durability or effect.

IMTTAQ: the validated transfer-and-opportunity comparator

Current leading proposal: use the intact six-item IMTTAQ module alongside, not instead of, MFC questions: the three-item Training Transfer subscale plus the three-item Opportunities to Integrate subscale.

Why it is a strong fit:

  • it gives MFC a validated workplace-training comparator for reported transfer and environmental opportunity;
  • it makes the workplace-condition part of the model visible rather than assuming it; and

What it cannot do: prove skill acquisition, observed behaviour, wellbeing improvement, or causation. The items are workplace-worded, so they are best suited to workplace cohorts and should not be silently rewritten for family, school or community settings. A rewritten version becomes an MFC-adapted item set, not a validated IMTTAQ subscale.

Proposed timing, pending Eimear/Debra wording review and implementation decision: day-21/week-3 for Essentials and Foundations, where the participant has had a real chance to use the learning; repeat at three months for selected case-study/research cohorts. Do not use this proposal to state that baseline or immediate-post IMTTAQ transfer scores are meaningful: the transfer and opportunity items assume a post-training chance to use the learning.

TYDQ-15 / Things You Do: useful, but not the current priority

TYDQ-15 is a validated broad measure of everyday actions associated with mental health across life. For the current MFC question, adding all 15 items would increase burden without replacing the six-item IMTTAQ plus MFC mechanism questions. It therefore remains a reference and a possible future module, rather than the leading routine-form proposal.

Research and partnership implication

The most useful research question is not “does one workshop work?” It is whether a brief non-clinical programme can support reported retrieval and application in real moments, and how reported use relates to opportunity and support in the participant's environment.

That makes the relevant researcher profiles:

  • skill enactment / implementation researchers, including the ANU UVC-Lite authors;
  • training-transfer and workplace-learning researchers, who can advise on use of IMTTAQ; and
  • researchers with the capability to help MFC build a proportionate, ethical study rather than lend a name to an untested product.

No researcher or university has agreed to work with MFC. A university HREC pathway exists only if a university researcher becomes an investigator; MFC cannot independently apply through a university HREC. Internal service evaluation and publishable research remain different paths, and HREC cannot be obtained retrospectively for already-collected research data.

Non-negotiable boundaries

  • Describe data as participant-reported unless it is independently observed.

D 0100 First Pilot Measurement Core

D-0100 — First-pilot measurement core

Decision

For the routine first Foundations pilot:

  1. Retain exactly three participant timepoints: baseline before teaching, immediate post-session, and a day-21 follow-up with reminders on days 24 and 27 and closure on day 28.
  2. Use a lean core focused on comprehension, unaided Stop–Shift–Do recall, a recognisable Radar moment, a realistic intended Mental Rep, real-moment opportunity, noticing, attempted use, repetition, conditional helpfulness, barriers and supports.
  3. Use one concise past-14-day access item at baseline and follow-up. Preserve “no relevant moment,” “did not know what might help” and “not sure” as distinct from the ordinal frequency responses.
  4. Remove WHO-5 from the routine first-pilot core.
  5. Preserve WHO-5 only as a possible future research module after a named wellbeing question, confirmed licence position, analysis plan, clinical response pathway, privacy review and appropriate ethics determination exist.
  6. Move mission discovery, broad demographics and other non-core research questions into separately governed optional modules with a named decision use.
  7. Prefer a random system-generated evaluation ID with any contact-to-ID key stored separately, access-restricted and deleted under an approved schedule. Describe linked data as pseudonymous. If this cannot be governed safely, use unlinked anonymous responses and forgo within-participant analysis.

Hunter approved this direction by instructing the system to proceed after it put the exact decision to him. This approval settles the founder design gate; it does not approve the exact instrument for fielding.

Why this amends earlier decisions

D-0017 retained WHO-5 and a broader measurement architecture. The live-source and vault reconciliation found that the first routine pilot's immediate decisions concern whether the session lands and whether the intended application mechanism occurs—not whether a small uncontrolled workshop causes wellbeing change. This record therefore amends D-0017 on the routine first-pilot WHO-5 position and exact core, while retaining its three-timepoint structure and causal-claim restrictions.

D-0056 remains controlling for its separate case-study-only extended wave and implementation conditions. This record does not remove or universalise that additional case-study wave.

D 0105 Touchpoint And Case Study Cadence Strategy

D-0105 — Touchpoint and case-study cadence strategy

Decision

Extends D-0100's three-timepoint core (baseline, immediate post, day-21/day-28 close) with an organisational touchpoint layer, separate from the per-participant instrument:

  1. Touchpoint two is the existing day-21 return session (already built into Essentials and Foundations) — no new session is added for this purpose.
  2. Standard partner organisations (i.e. not selected as a case study): one further check-in after the first quarter, once that quarter's data is compiled — a scheduled meeting, not a session, where the organisation completes a light form beforehand and MFC discusses it with them. This is not repeated indefinitely. It is a single, bounded follow-up.
  3. Case-study organisations (selected, not every partner): the same light-form-plus-meeting format, but continuing quarterly for a full year (four touchpoints across 12 months).
  4. Case-study selection criteria, in descending order of justification: - Engagement/manager-support signal at the day-21 return — the best-evidenced of the four, though the evidence is modest and must be stated as such. Blume et al. (2010), full-text verified elsewhere in MFC's own evidence corpus and already the basis for the leader-session design, found modest associations between real-world training use and workplace transfer climate (r = .27) and social support (r = .21) — measured entirely within organisational training samples, establishing correlation and not causation, and not tested outside workplace settings. Correction, 2026-08-23: an earlier version of this record described these as "among the strongest predictors of whether training transfers into use." That overstated the source, which MFC's own Stage 8 synthesis explicitly characterises as modest associations. Lead with this criterion when explaining selection logic, but describe it accurately — best available, not strong. - Willingness to commit the full year — feasibility, not evidence. - Cohort size — consistency with Foundations' existing delivery floor (6+), not itself a finding. - Sector/context spread across the selected case studies — standard case-study/qualitative research practice (maximum-variation sampling), not an MFC-specific result.
  5. Individual participant forms at 3 months feed the first quarterly discussion, standard or case-study, and continue feeding subsequent ones for case-study orgs.

Why this shape, and what was rejected on the way here

An earlier version of this design proposed a monthly organisational touchpoint for every partner org, framed loosely as "a year-long case study." A bounded panel found this had two real problems, both corrected in this record: (a) a monthly participant-facing touch would have contaminated D-0100's unaided-recall measure — resolved by making the organisational touchpoint a manager-level conversation, not a participant-facing repeat of the instrument; (b) framing indefinite ongoing contact as evidence that anything survives independently was found to be backwards — the vault's own RULER research explicitly warns against exactly this pattern ("build for graduation, not renewal"; RULER's own documented weakness is dependence on ongoing provider contact for its effects). Indefinite quarterly contact for every organisation cannot answer whether anything holds up without MFC's continued involvement, because MFC never actually withdraws.

The decision below resolves this without adding process weight Hunter judged not worth carrying (his own words, in conversation: "this is kind of too much work/effort in all honesty"). Rather than engineering a deliberate silence-then-check design (which would answer the durability question more cleanly but cost more to run), the resolution is structural: standard organisations get a single bounded follow-up, not indefinite contact, so the renewal-dependency problem simply does not apply to them. Case-study organisations knowingly trade durability-proof for richer ongoing data — a legitimate, bounded choice for a small, deliberately selected group, not a claim MFC should make about organisations broadly.

What this does not do

  • Does not change D-0100's core participant instrument, timepoints, or claim boundary.
  • Does not constitute a durability/sustained-effect claim for standard partner organisations or for case-study organisations — MFC continues to hold contact with case-study organisations throughout the year, so no version of this design shows effects surviving independently of MFC's involvement. Why this is the strongest design

The first pilot's decision is not “did a 90-minute workshop improve psychological wellbeing?” It is whether the session lands, whether people can retrieve the core process in a real moment, whether they attempt a Mental Rep, and what supports or blocks use. The current Blueprint itself places these questions in stages 1 and 2 and says external outcomes belong later, once earlier stages are stable.

WHO-5 is a short, credible wellbeing instrument with a two-week recall period. That supports using it when wellbeing is a pre-specified outcome. It does not make wellbeing the right first-pilot outcome. In a small, uncontrolled, self-reported implementation pilot, baseline-to-follow-up movement would be highly confounded and easy to overinterpret. Baseline-only collection has no strong decision use. Collecting it also introduces sensitive wellbeing data, response-pathway questions and a current WHO licence whose non-commercial condition needs a qualified determination for MFC's paid corporate delivery model.

Therefore:

  1. Keep three timepoints: baseline, immediate post-session, and day-21 follow-up closing day 28.
  2. Make the follow-up application cascade the primary evidence: opportunity occurred → moment noticed → Mental Rep attempted → repetition/helpfulness/barriers.
  3. Use one concise matched access item as secondary evidence, rather than ten responses across a five-situation matrix in both baseline and follow-up.
  4. Keep unaided Stop–Shift–Do recall and plan specificity as learning/retention evidence.
  5. Remove WHO-5 from the routine first-pilot core. Preserve it as an optional future research module after a named research question, exact licence position, clinical response pathway, privacy assessment, analysis plan and appropriate ethics determination.
  6. Move mission, discovery and optional demographic questions out of the core forms. Add them only when each has a named decision use and small-cell privacy risk is controlled.

What HREC is, and why it matters here

A Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) reviews and approves research involving human participants before it starts, under the NHMRC's National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research (updated 2025). It exists to check what a well-meaning organisation can miss from the inside: whether consent is genuinely informed, whether a vulnerable participant could be harmed, whether data will be handled honestly, whether the study can answer the question it claims to.

Why MFC can't skip it and still call this "research." Ethics approval is expected prospectively, not retrospectively — university HRECs will not bless a dataset already collected. If MFC gathers WHO-5 responses now without approval, that dataset is at real risk of being unusable later for a university partnership, conference presentation, or journal submission. The workshop itself doesn't need to stop; the data collected before approval likely won't count toward the research goal.

The 2025 update, in plain terms: review is now a risk continuum rather than one-size-fits-all — a genuinely low-risk study (a short, validated, non-clinical survey is the kind of thing this was built for) can go through a faster delegated/expedited review instead of a full committee sitting, typically weeks rather than months.

Could MFC skip HREC review entirely?

Likely answer: no, not full exemption — but probably not full committee review either.

Exemption (National Statement §5.1.15–5.1.18) requires either fully de-identified data before a researcher ever sees it, or a method that's both anonymous and "highly unlikely to cause distress." Our own core items stay identical across all three services. WHO-5 returns on the case-study arc at three and twelve months, and only once the response pathway exists. This does not fix attribution, and I want to be exact: a twelve-month score across a handful of self-selected organisations, with us in contact throughout, is more confounded than a three-week one, not less. It would be descriptive context and hypothesis generation, never an effect and never attributed to us.

Worth flagging, since it changes what you last saw. The version of the forms you have seen carried WHO-5 at pre-session and again at three weeks. That is what has moved, and it is the part of the design I hold least firmly.

What would actually get us closer to attribution. A staggered start inside one organisation: team A now, team B in three months. Nobody is denied anything, it costs nothing, and team B is a genuine comparison group while they wait. Cheaper still, benchmark our TYDQ scores against Titov's published community norms from 6,070 adults, so a reference distribution does some of the work a control group would.

Two things to be straight about. A modified validated scale is no longer validated, so we either use one intact or label ours clearly as MFC's own. And we have not confirmed TYDQ's licensing terms.

One thing I would rather you hear from me than find. This instrument work was done properly in early August, and no validated instrument ever went into the forms. To be fair to the decision that removed WHO-5, it was deliberate and reasoned, and it kept WHO-5 available as a future module. The gap is not that WHO-5 came out. It is that nothing went in to replace it.

4. Two safety matters I had wrong

Disclosure risk is not specific to WHO-5. Our own core items ask what got in the way and for a real moment from someone's life, in free text, from people who chose to attend a mental-fitness session, linked to a code whose key we hold. That will surface difficult material with or without WHO-5. The clinical-response gate therefore applies to the whole core, and nothing is fielded before a named clinical owner and a written response pathway exist. That is a change in my thinking from a few days ago.

The employer context creates risks I had not named. Foundations is bought by an employer, the participants are their staff, and the organisation receives a report. Consent inside an employment relationship is not freely given by default, so opting out must be genuinely possible and invisible to a manager. Aggregate reporting can still re-identify people in a small team, so there needs to be a minimum group size below which we report nothing. And employees may under-report distress precisely because their employer commissioned the session, which makes low scores both less reliable and more concerning. Where those lines sit is not settled.

5. The research pathway

The gap we are trying to occupy is not that skill use over time in ordinary non-clinical settings is under-discussed. It is that it is not measured. Measurement here either sits inside clinical or academic intervention research, or stops at a post-session survey capturing whether people enjoyed it. Whether someone was still doing the thing three months later, in a workplace that either supported it or did not, is data that mostly does not exist.

Ethics approval. We are not pursuing HREC approval yet. Anything captured before a formal ethics relationship exists will not count toward a publishable study, and cannot be retrofitted. It remains good for our own findings and for a future Rapid Research Review building on Debra's. A future-research consent clause costs nothing now and keeps the door open.

Universities. Of 31 Australian universities checked directly, 13 will not accept an application led by an unaffiliated external organisation. The genuinely open pathways, Adelaide's being the best at no fee, still require a collaborating academic attached. The real work is finding one researcher rather than emailing ethics offices. And if a researcher asked today what we have, the answer is nothing yet, which is the argument for starting.

The obvious people are the authors of the two instruments closest to our question: the ANU group and Titov's group at Macquarie. Not for permission, but to ask whether they would review what we build or co-develop the part their instruments do not reach. The ANU centre already partners with a community organisation, Menslink, to evaluate their programme, so there is precedent. What that unlocks beyond goodwill: a named academic as investigator is what makes a university's ethics committee available at all. The collaborator is the ethics pathway.

6. What I will send in the next fortnight

The final workshop outlines for all three services, the learning outcomes for each, and the measurement forms themselves with the exact questions at pre, post, three to four weeks and three months. That last one is what I most want marked up.

7. Honest limitations

No data exists, so everything here is a plan rather than a finding. Every institutional fact is desk research, nobody at any university has been contacted, and Adelaide's no-fee pathway has not been confirmed by emailing them. The primary NHMRC guidance document could not be retrieved, so our ethics reading rests on secondary sources. MFC has no evidence that Foundations specifically works: what we have is an evidence-informed design and Debra's rapid review establishing these skills are trainable in general, which is a different claim. The quarterly check-in form does not exist yet and no organisation has been approached as a case study. Our read of the measurement gap is a bounded search rather than a systematic review.

Three further points. Our forms currently contain no validated instrument at all. Nothing in this design controls for anything, and the staggered start is the only idea here that would change that, untested. And the clinical-response gate covers every form we field, not only a wellbeing one, which I had not properly accounted for until this week.
